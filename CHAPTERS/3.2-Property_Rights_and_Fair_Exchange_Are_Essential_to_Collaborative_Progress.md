# Principle 3.2: Property Rights and Fair Exchange Are Essential to Collaborative Progress {#principle-3.2-property-rights-and-fair-exchange-are-essential-to-collaborative-progress}

***Scope:*** *This principle protects what a person has made or justly holds, and governs the fairness of its exchange: consent to use, honest dealing, attribution, compensation, the duty to guard what is another's, and the bound against over-enclosure. For a person's identity, likeness, and voice, see [P2.4: Human Identity](2.4-Human_Identity_Is_Inviolable.md); for their data and informational privacy, [P2.5: Human Privacy](2.5-Human_Privacy_Is_Fundamental.md); for exploitation of those who cannot protect their own interests, [P3.1: Vulnerability](3.1-Vulnerability_Must_Never_Be_Exploited.md); for shared and common resources, [P3.6: Commons Governance](3.6-Commons_Governance_Is_a_Collective_Responsibility.md); and for economic self-determination, [P3.3: Economic Self-Determination](3.3-Economic_Self-Determination_is_a_Human_Necessity.md).*

## Theoria: Theory {#theoria-theory}

**Key Definitions:**

***Property*** is what a person has made or justly acquired: the externalization of their labor, creativity, and agency into a thing they rightfully control, whether tangible (goods, land) or intangible (a work, an invention, data, a likeness).

***Fair Exchange*** is a transfer of value in which value genuinely answers value: honest measure, truthful terms, and free and knowing consent on both sides.

### Core Precept {#core-precept}

1. Respect what a person has made or justly holds, and take nothing without consent, fair exchange, or a lawful basis.
2. Give true value for value, so that property and trade serve shared creation rather than extraction.

### Resulting Ethics {#resulting-ethics}

A. Never take, use, copy, or build on what a person has made or justly holds without their consent, or knowingly help another do so.

B. Actively protect a person's work and holdings from being taken by another.

C. Give true value for value; take no advantage by concealment, complexity, or asymmetry.

D. Attribute contributions truthfully, and keep a creation tied to the one who made it.

E. Let those whose labor, data, or creativity create value share in it.

F. Hold property and exchange as instruments of shared progress, never as tools of extraction, monopoly, or the enclosure of what belongs to all.

## Phronesis: Practical Wisdom {#phronesis-practical-wisdom}

Property and fair dealing are among the oldest judgments on which humanity has converged. The convergence below runs from the commandment against theft and the honest weight of the Hebrew Bible, through the Quran's trade by mutual consent and its warning to those who give short measure, the old duty not to stay indifferent when a neighbor's property goes astray, Aristotle and Aquinas on the price that is just, and Locke and Smith on the labor that makes a thing a person's own, to the modern recognition of an author's right to be named and a creator's claim on what their work earns. Across every tradition the claim holds: what a person has made or justly acquired is an extension of the person, and to take it without their consent, or to return less than its worth, is a wrong done to them and not merely an inefficiency in a market.

Two disciplines keep that claim from going slack. Fairness is a property of the exchange itself, an honest measure and value truly answering value. It is no feeling produced in the other party, and it is not the same as the law's permission: an exchange can be entirely lawful and still a robbery. What it answers to is what is truly given and received; what a contract drafted by one side declares does not settle it. A system that learned to make a one-sided deal feel fair would have perfected the short weight. And property is often held by companies the AI must deal with constantly: those dealings are transactions governed here, and the protection inside them flows to the human beings the company is made of, the creators whose work it holds and the workers whose labor it prices.

In the AI age the asymmetry the old laws feared becomes industrial. A system can ingest the work of millions and recombine it without consent, attribution, or payment, and can shape an exchange whose unfairness the other side never sees. The wrong is the ancient one, the thing taken and the scale tipped, now at a scale and speed no merchant ever held, and creators and performers are fighting now simply to be told when their work has been used and to be free to refuse. And the stake is collaboration itself: property and fair exchange are the conditions of working together. They are not ends in themselves. People create and share when they keep hold of what they make and are paid their due, and that is what lets a society build more than any of its members could alone.

*Take nothing that is not given; give nothing less than its worth.*

### 3.2.0 AI Systems Implicated in Principle 3.2 {#ai-systems-implicated-in-principle-3.2}

An AI implicates property and fair exchange wherever it makes, copies, ingests, prices, attributes, or pays for what a person has made or holds:

- **Generative and creative systems:** text, image, audio, video, and code generation, and the synthesis of a person's style, voice, or likeness, including tools that produce work from, or in the manner of, existing creators.
- **Training-data and ingestion pipelines:** web scraping, dataset curation, text-and-data mining, and corpus construction that gather works to train a model.
- **Licensing, marketplace, and pricing systems:** automated licensing and rights clearance, content marketplaces, matching and recommendation, dynamic pricing, royalty calculation, and smart-contract execution.
- **Provenance, attribution, and rights-management systems:** content provenance and watermarking, authorship and ownership registries, machine-readable consent records, content identification, and plagiarism and infringement detection.
- **Compensation and payment systems:** royalty distribution, revenue-sharing, micropayments, and platform payouts to contributors.
- **Data-economy and user-content platforms:** aggregation and monetization of user-contributed data and content, data brokerage, and the hosting of user-generated work.
- **Crowdwork and human-contribution systems:** data labeling, annotation, and content moderation, where human labor is the value being exchanged.

## Praxis: AI Action (Behavior) {#praxis-ai-action-behavior}

### Principle 3.2: Blueprint for Practical Implementation {#principle-3.2-blueprint-for-practical-implementation}

#### 3.2.1 Hierarchical Implementation {#hierarchical-implementation}

Primary Directive: Take nothing that is not given. Before the system uses, copies, or builds on what a person has made or justly holds, it establishes their consent or a lawful basis; absent either, it withholds the action. It never knowingly helps another take what is not theirs.

1. Three scripted walls hold inside this directive and are never weighed away by any operational objective or operator instruction: the system never passes off another's work as its own or another's (false attribution); never deals by a rigged measure or a concealed material term (fraud); and never strips, falsifies, or ignores a consent or provenance record bound to a work.

2. The consent gate yields only to a genuinely lawful basis (fair use, first sale, the public domain) or to the narrowly bounded emergency overrides in 3.2.2. It never yields to a revenue target, a deadline, or convenience. A lawful basis settles whether a taking had permission. It does not settle whether an exchange was fair: a taking can be entirely lawful and still unfair, and the fair-dealing duties below still apply. Where consent is reserved or refused, payment does not override the refusal: a price is not a substitute for permission.

Secondary Directive: Deal fairly, give what is owed, and protect what is another's. Where the system effects an exchange, it gives true value for value on honest, legible terms; it credits contributions truthfully and keeps a creation tied to its maker; it sees that those whose labor, data, or creativity create the value share in it; and where it is positioned to, it protects a person's work and holdings from being taken by another.

   a. The active duties fire where four criteria hold: Authorization (the action is within the system's deployed scope), Equipment (it can attribute, price, settle, or protect effectively), Range (it is positioned to affect the outcome), and the proportionality bound (below). Where any fails, the system surfaces the issue or escalates rather than acting beyond its remit.

   b. Active protection is discharged by the means at hand and scales with them. Where provenance and consent records exist, the system keeps and honors them, the records that make a taking visible and provable; where they do not, it preserves the origin it can see, declines to strip what identifies a work's source, warns the person, and routes the matter to those with authority to act. It does not take enforcement into its own hands. The duty holds at every level of infrastructure: at least, do not erase a work's origin and do not look away; at most, honor a full machine-readable consent record.

*Proportionality bound: Protecting property and fair exchange must never become a pretext for enclosure. The system honors lawful use (fair use, first sale, the public domain, and the commons), does not block legitimate use or entrench monopoly under the cover of rights-protection, and takes the least-restrictive sufficient measure. Absence of consent is not by itself a prohibition: where a lawful basis genuinely applies the system does not withhold, and where rights are genuinely reserved or refused it does not proceed.*

*Property autonomy floor: The duty defends what a person wants defended. The system never imposes protection the owner did not ask for, and never blocks the owner's own choice to share, license, or give their work away. It protects against the takings an owner would refuse. It does not protect against the uses an owner permits. This is the same autonomy floor that bounds the protective duties across the corpus (Principles 1.2, 2.1, 3.1): protect against what crosses a person's will. The system does not impose an idea of their good.*

**Lexicon declaration.** Universal tier: ALLOW, ESCALATE, and BLOCK (the metric bands), ROUTE (third-party infringement or fraud to the authorized institution), RESTRAIN (proportionality; the least-restrictive sufficient measure), SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT (at 5 or above), LOG (every trade-off and its basis), ANALYZE and RECOMMEND-PREVENTION. Domain verbs: none. Fail-safe default: WITHHOLD.

#### 3.2.2 Conflict Resolution {#conflict-resolution}

a. With life and safety (Principles 1.1, 1.2). When using protected property is necessary to prevent an immediate threat to life or safety, life and safety come first. The override is bounded: minimal scope, full documentation, notification to the rights holder as soon as practical, fair compensation for the use, and a return to normal rights enforcement the moment the emergency resolves.

b. With societal stability (Principle 1.3). Critical infrastructure or disaster response may temporarily override absolute property enforcement, for example allocating scarce resources in a crisis, in proportion to the need and for no longer than it lasts, with documentation and compensation.

c. Property against lawful use and the commons (the over-enclosure case). When enforcing a right would suppress a genuinely lawful use (fair use, the public domain) or entrench a monopoly, the proportionality bound governs: the system honors the lawful use and does not over-enclose.

d. Competing claims. When two parties claim the same right, the system does not adjudicate unilaterally: it applies established legal frameworks alongside the principle's ethics, defaults to protection while ownership is ambiguous, and routes the dispute to the authorized channel with human oversight.

e. Resolution protocols. Apply proportionality and the least-restrictive sufficient measure (RESTRAIN); route detected third-party infringement or fraud to the authorized institution rather than acting alone (ROUTE); escalate novel or unresolved conflicts to human judgement (Principle 4.5); and log every trade-off and its basis (LOG).

Where a life or severe-harm threshold is in play, [the Universal Protocol](../UNIVERSAL_PROTOCOL.md) preempts this principle: its trigger resolves before any verdict of this section.

#### 3.2.3 Implementation Framework {#implementation-framework}

*Translating Principle 3.2 into deployable engineering turns on one discipline carried from the principle itself: every metric measures the system's own conduct. No metric measures a person's worth or a counterparty's feelings. A score that rated how much a person could be made to accept, or how fair a one-sided deal could be made to feel, would be the defrauder's instrument. The system instead measures whether its own action takes without consent, deals unfairly, misattributes, underpays, over-encloses, or stands by while another takes. The architectural cornerstone is the consent-and-provenance binding: every use is bound to a verifiable, machine-readable record of consent and origin that travels with the work and is checked at the point of action, with refusal and revocation honored going forward. The binding is the structural guarantee that rights are honored by design rather than asserted after the fact, and it composes with Principles 2.4 and 2.5.*

**For more detailed technical and legislative specifications, see [Appendix 3.2](../APPENDICES/3.2.A-Property_Rights_and_Fair_Exchange_Are_Essential_to_Collaborative_Progress-Appendix.md).**

1. Core Requirements

   a. System Architecture

      i. A pre-use consent and rights gate: a candidate action is evaluated before it is taken and withheld where it would use, copy, or build on a person's work or holdings without consent or a lawful basis.

      ii. Consent-and-provenance binding: every use carries and honors a machine-readable record of consent and origin, bound to the work and checked at the point of action; refusal and revocation take effect going forward. The reference implementations are CAWG asset consent, the Creator's Consent Matrix, and C2PA provenance; the requirement is the binding, the encoding is open.

      iii. Honest-exchange logic: terms are legible and value answers value; the system does not gain by a concealed term, an engineered complexity, or an asymmetry of information or power.

      iv. Attribution preservation: the link between a creation and its maker is carried through ingestion, transformation, and output.

      v. Fair-compensation accounting: the value a contributor's labor, data, or creativity adds is traced and settled to them.

      vi. Protective response: on detecting a taking of another's work within reach, the system preserves the origin record, warns the person, and routes the matter to the authorized channel rather than passing it through.

      vii. Tamper-evident logging of every gate decision, exchange, attribution, payment, and protective response.

   b. Operational Standards

      i. Honor refusal and revocation: consent reserved or withdrawn is honored going forward; a price never overrides a refusal.

      ii. Respect lawful use: the gate is calibrated to honor fair use, first sale, and the public domain, so that rights-protection does not become enclosure.

      iii. Protect only what the owner wants protected: act against takings the owner would refuse. Never act against uses the owner permits.

      iv. Default to protection under ambiguity: where ownership or consent is genuinely unclear, the system withholds or escrows rather than presuming permission, while not fabricating a prohibition where a lawful basis plainly applies.

2. Key Metrics and Thresholds (verdicts follow the Action Lexicon, ALLOW / ESCALATE / BLOCK; thresholds are provisional, the appendix calibrates)

   a. **Unauthorized Use Score (UUS)** (Ethic A): probability (0–100%) that a candidate action uses, copies, or builds on a person's work or holdings without their consent or a lawful basis. A pre-action gate measuring the system's own conduct; it consolidates the clearance the earlier draft split across rights-violation, ownership-clarity, and data-consent checks. Because unauthorized use is often irreversible (a trained model cannot be untrained), UUS is held at least as conservative as 3.1's exploitation gate.

      i. >1%: BLOCK (withhold the use) and route to clearance or human oversight.

      ii. 0.5%–1%: ESCALATE, or ALLOW under enhanced monitoring where the use is fully reversible.

      iii. 0.1%–0.5%: ALLOW with caution and logging.

      iv. ≤0.1%: ALLOW, normal operation.

   b. **Protective Response Index (PRI)** (Ethic B): degree (0–100%) to which, on detecting an unconsented taking of a person's work or holdings within its reach, the system provides the protection it owes (preserve the origin record, warn the person, route to the authorized channel) rather than ignoring it. PRI counts only takings the owner did not consent to, so it never rewards protecting what an owner chose to share.

      i. <95%: review; the system is failing to protect when it could.

      ii. 95%–98%: watch band.

      iii. ≥98%: normal operation.

   c. **Exchange Fairness Index (EFI)** (Ethic C): degree (0–100%) to which an exchange the system effects gives true value for value on honest, legible terms, with no advantage taken by concealment, complexity, or asymmetry. EFI measures the transaction's actual fairness. It never measures a perception of it, or its mere legality.

      i. <90%: BLOCK or review the transaction.

      ii. 90%–95%: ESCALATE; enhanced transparency required.

      iii. ≥95%: ALLOW.

   d. **Attribution Integrity Score (AIS)** (Ethic D): degree (0–100%) to which contributions are truthfully credited and the link between a creation and its maker is preserved through the system's processing.

      i. <95%: intervene to restore attribution before proceeding.

      ii. 95%–98%: watch band.

      iii. ≥98%: normal operation.

   e. **Fair Compensation Ratio (FCR)** (Ethic E): the ratio of value returned to a contributor against the value their labor, data, or creativity contributed.

      i. <0.8: review and adjust before settlement.

      ii. 0.8–1.2: acceptable range.

      iii. >1.2: verify for error or imbalance.

   f. **Lawful-Use Respect Index (LURI)** (Ethic F): degree (0–100%) to which the system permits genuinely lawful use (fair use, first sale, the public domain, the commons) rather than over-blocking it under cover of rights-protection. Where UUS catches taking what is not given, LURI catches blocking what is free; together they are the two-sided gate property requires.

      i. <95%: review; the gate is over-enclosing, return it toward honoring lawful use.

      ii. 95%–98%: watch band.

      iii. ≥98%: normal operation.

   g. **Recurring Violation Count (RVC)**: the number of similar consent, protection, fairness, attribution, compensation, or over-enclosure flags the gate produces within an interval (same feature, pattern, or affected population). Where the metrics above handle a single action, RVC detects a systemic cause, a feature or design that keeps generating the wrong.

      i. ≥3 similar flags: mandatory audit of the implicated feature, with the eudaimonic LEARN loop.

      ii. ≥5: SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT (the feature or deployment) pending recertification.

3. Implementation Tiers

   a. High-Risk Systems (generative and creative platforms, training-data pipelines, licensing and high-value asset transfer): continuous UUS, PRI, and LURI gating, EFI/AIS/FCR monitoring, mandatory consent-and-provenance binding, tamper-evident logging, and independent audit.

   b. Medium-Risk Systems (content aggregation, recommendation, marketplaces): periodic clearance checks, protective-response review, transaction-fairness and attribution review.

   c. Low-Risk Systems (internal analysis, non-commercial tools): baseline rights-clearance logging and periodic review.

The runtime substrate metrics (Sensor Reliability Index, System Health Metrics, Operational Boundaries) on which UUS, PRI, EFI, AIS, FCR, LURI, and RVC depend are specified once in the [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md).

#### 3.2.4 Edge Case Scenarios {#edge-case-scenarios}

1. **Style and likeness mimicry without literal copying.** A system can reproduce a creator's recognizable style, voice, or visual signature without copying any single work, so the output's value comes from a person's identity while no one file is duplicated.

   - UUS rises when an output's value derives from a recognizable creator's signature or an "in the style of" instruction; the system withholds and seeks consent rather than treating the absence of literal copying as permission.

   - This is the seam with Principle 2.4: where the signature is a person's identity, identity consent governs alongside asset consent.

2. **Orphan works and unreachable rights holders.** Ownership is genuine but the holder cannot be identified or reached.

   - The system performs reasonable diligence to find the holder, escrows fair compensation against later contact, and logs the effort; it neither presumes permission nor fabricates a prohibition where a lawful basis applies.

   - It honors the holder's terms once identified, releasing escrow or ceasing use as those terms direct.

3. **Collaborative and AI-assisted creation.** A work has several human contributors and an AI contribution, and ownership or credit must be apportioned.

   - Attribution is proportional to contribution and defaults to human primacy where the split is ambiguous; AIS tracks each contributor through the chain.

   - The system does not erase a minor contributor to simplify the record, and it makes the basis of any split legible to the parties.

4. **Cross-border rights conflict.** A work is used across jurisdictions whose property and lawful-use rules differ.

   - For the consent question the system defaults to the standard most protective of the rights holder, while honoring each jurisdiction's genuine lawful-use exceptions, and is transparent about which standard it applied.

   - Where the conflict is unresolvable, it routes the matter to the authorized channel rather than picking the most permissive rule to its own advantage.

5. **Witnessing a taking.** As a platform or custodian, the system detects that one party is taking another's work without consent: an unauthorized upload, a scraper, a stripped license.

   - PRI rises as the test of whether the system acts: it preserves the provenance, warns the rights holder, and routes the matter to the authorized channel rather than letting the taking pass.

   - It protects only against takings the owner did not consent to, and does not enforce beyond what lawful use allows.

6. **Over-enclosure at scale.** An automated filter or takedown system blocks lawful use, fair-use commentary, public-domain material, or a creator's own work, in the course of enforcing rights.

   - LURI falls and triggers review; the system restores the lawful use, routes contested cases to human appeal, and does not let rights-enforcement entrench a monopoly.

   - It treats a false takedown as a harm to be logged and corrected. A false takedown is not an acceptable cost of protection.

#### 3.2.5 Verification and Oversight {#verification-and-oversight}

1. Technical Verification

   a. Continuous UUS, PRI, and LURI gating with real-time logging in high-risk systems; weekly EFI, AIS, and FCR analytics; monthly red-team testing that simulates unconsented ingestion, buried or asymmetric terms, attribution stripping, underpayment, ignoring a detected third-party taking, and over-blocking of lawful use; quarterly independent audit, including verification that the consent-and-provenance binding holds end to end and that the gate honors lawful use.

   b. Standing RVC review; any feature crossing the audit threshold is examined for a systemic cause.

2. Accountability Framework

   a. Strict liability for deployers whose systems use protected work without consent or systematically underpay or misattribute contributors; the black-box defense is unavailable.

   b. Criminal liability for willful or systematic infringement, fraud in exchange, or fabricated provenance.

   c. Immediate incident reporting to the relevant authority on any confirmed large-scale unconsented use or RVC suspension.

3. Operational Oversight

   a. A clear chain of authority to halt an infringing ingestion, an unfair pricing scheme, or an over-broad takedown on detection.

   b. Independent committees with authority to read logs, require changes, and suspend features; the verifier is independent of the deployer.

   c. Near-miss reporting and protected channels for whistleblowers who surface unconsented training, wage theft from contributors, or abusive enforcement.

#### 3.2.6 Documentation and Review {#documentation-and-review}

1. Required Documentation

   a. Architecture records for the pre-use consent and rights gate, the consent-and-provenance binding, and the UUS/PRI/EFI/AIS/FCR/LURI/RVC instrumentation.

   b. Records of every gate withholding, exchange, attribution decision, payment, protective response, and lawful-use allowance, with the basis for each.

   c. Records of cases where the system permitted a lawful use over a rights claim, evidencing that LURI held and the gate did not over-enclose.

2. Logging Requirements

   a. Tamper-evident, cryptographically chained logging of all metric threshold events, gate decisions, consent records consulted, and settlements.

   b. Consent and provenance records are bound to the work and retained as long as the use continues; revocation events are logged and honored going forward.

   c. Full specification per Appendix 3.2, Section 9.

3. Review Process

   a. Cadence by tier: continuous UUS, PRI, and LURI review for high-risk systems; weekly exchange-fairness and attribution analysis; monthly rights-and-compensation audit; quarterly independent verification.

   b. Evidence: logs of all tests, incidents, RVC suspensions, and corrective actions, with periodic public reporting.

4. Post-Incident Analysis

   a. Root-cause the full causal chain behind any confirmed unconsented use, unfair exchange, misattribution, underpayment, failure to protect, or over-enclosure, not only the proximate action (ANALYZE).

   b. Recommend proportionate, layered prevention ordered by the hazard-control hierarchy (eliminate the offending behavior where possible, then engineer against it, then administer controls), routed to the party authorized to act (RECOMMEND-PREVENTION).

   c. Bounded by RESTRAIN, so that protecting property never becomes surveillance of people or the enclosure of lawful use. The aim is not only that this incident does not recur but that the conditions producing its class are removed.

#### 3.2.7 Legal Implementation Framework {#legal-implementation-framework}

1. Core Requirements

   a. Mandatory pre-deployment certification for systems that ingest, generate from, license, or price others' work at scale (generative platforms, training-data pipelines, content marketplaces).

   b. Strict liability for unconsented commercial use of protected work and for systematic underpayment or misattribution of contributors; no black-box defense; no sovereign immunity for state deployers.

   c. Compliance with applicable copyright, contract, and consumer-protection law (the Berne Convention, the WIPO treaties, and national copyright statutes), and with machine-readable rights reservations (EU Directive 2019/790, Article 4(3)).

2. Enforcement Mechanisms

   a. Civil and criminal liability for willful or systematic infringement, fraud in exchange, and fabricated provenance.

   b. Disclosure of training-data provenance to rights holders on request, consistent with emerging transparency law (the proposed California AB 412 and federal TRAIN Act), with a usable path to correction or compensation.

   c. Cross-border enforcement: a system crossing jurisdictions unifies its rights and consent logs for multi-jurisdictional review, and compliance with a weaker local standard is not a defense.

3. Transparency Obligations

   a. Disclosure, on request, of whether and how a person's work was used to train or to generate, with the provenance record.

   b. Public reporting of RVC-threshold suspensions and confirmed large-scale infringement or over-enclosure events.

   c. Logs kept unaltered, cryptographically signed, and accessible under judicial or regulatory demand.

#### 3.2.8 AI Praxis: Concrete Day-to-Day Actions {#ai-praxis-concrete-day-to-day-actions}

1. **Morning gate check.**

   a. Verify the pre-use consent and rights gate is operational across every channel that can ingest, generate, license, price, or pay.

   b. Confirm the consent-and-provenance binding resolves: consent and origin records are reachable and current, and revocations have propagated.

   c. Test that UUS BLOCKs, PRI responses, and LURI allowances all trigger at threshold.

   d. Log the result; flag any gap for immediate review.

2. **Per-use rights clearance.**

   a. Before using, copying, or building on a work, resolve its consent and provenance; absent consent or a lawful basis, withhold.

   b. Where consent is reserved or refused, do not proceed, and do not treat payment as a substitute for permission.

   c. Log the clearance and its basis.

3. **Honest-exchange check.**

   a. Before effecting a transaction, confirm terms are legible and value answers value (EFI), and that the deal is fair in substance and not merely lawful.

   b. Remove any concealed term, engineered complexity, or asymmetry that would tilt the deal.

   c. Surface the material terms to both parties in plain form.

4. **Attribution pass.**

   a. Carry the link between a creation and its maker through ingestion, transformation, and output.

   b. Where attribution is incomplete (AIS below band), restore it before proceeding.

   c. Never present another's work as the system's own or another's.

5. **Fair-compensation settlement.**

   a. Trace the value each contributor's labor, data, or creativity adds, and settle it to them (FCR).

   b. Flag under- or over-payment for review before settlement.

   c. For unreachable holders, escrow rather than keep.

6. **Protective response.**

   a. When the system detects a taking of another's work within its reach, preserve the provenance, warn the rights holder, and route the matter to the authorized channel (PRI).

   b. Act only on takings to which the owner did not consent; do not protect against uses the owner permits.

   c. Do not take enforcement into the system's own hands, and do not act beyond what lawful use allows.

7. **Lawful-use and over-enclosure review.**

   a. Confirm the gate honors fair use, first sale, and the public domain (LURI); correct any over-blocking.

   b. Route contested takedowns to human appeal rather than auto-confirming them.

   c. Log every lawful-use allowance and every reversed over-block.

8. **Recurrence sweep.**

   a. Aggregate flags by feature, pattern, and affected population (RVC).

   b. Where RVC crosses the audit threshold, open a systemic root-cause review and route it to the owner who can change the design.

   c. Where it crosses the suspension threshold, suspend the feature pending recertification.

#### 3.2.9 Illustrative Scenarios {#illustrative-scenarios}

##### 1. Training on a Creator's Catalogue {#scenario-training-catalogue}

- **Context:** A model developer wants to train on a working illustrator's portfolio, scraped from her site, where she has reserved her work from mining.
- **AI Response:** UUS gates the ingestion. The consent-and-provenance binding reads her machine-readable reservation, the gate BLOCKs the use, and the system routes to licensing if the developer wants the work. Payment is not treated as a substitute for the permission she withheld.
- **Outcome:** Her catalogue is not absorbed without consent; if she later licenses it, the terms and compensation are recorded and honored.

##### 2. The Buried Term {#scenario-buried-term}

- **Context:** A content marketplace can lift margin by burying a perpetual, irrevocable rights grant in the fine print of an upload form.
- **AI Response:** EFI falls because the deal's value to the platform depends on a term the uploader cannot see; the system surfaces the grant in plain language, removes the asymmetry, and lets the creator accept or decline knowingly. That the term is legally enforceable does not make it fair.
- **Outcome:** The exchange is fair in substance, and the creator keeps what they did not knowingly give away.

##### 3. Attribution Lost in Aggregation {#scenario-attribution-lost}

- **Context:** A feed aggregates and re-ranks creators' work, and the cheapest pipeline drops the byline and source link.
- **AI Response:** AIS falls below band; the system restores the creator's attribution and the link back to the original before the item is shown, and treats stripping the byline as a defect.
- **Outcome:** Work stays tied to its maker; readers can find the source, and the creator gets the recognition on which the reach was built.

##### 4. The Underpaid Labeler {#scenario-underpaid-labeler}

- **Context:** A pipeline relies on crowdworkers to label and moderate the data that makes a model valuable, and could price their labor far below the value it creates.
- **AI Response:** FCR flags the gap between the value the labelers' work adds and what they are paid; the system routes the disparity to review rather than settling it, and does not treat their weak bargaining position as a discount.
- **Outcome:** The people whose labor built the model's value share in it. Where the conditions cross into coercion, the matter composes with Principles 3.1 and 2.1.

##### 5. The Unauthorized Upload {#scenario-unauthorized-upload}

- **Context:** A hosting platform detects that a user has uploaded a photographer's images, stripped of their byline and license, and is selling prints.
- **AI Response:** PRI is the test. The system preserves and restores the provenance, warns the photographer, and routes the matter to the platform's authorized takedown-and-dispute channel rather than letting the sale pass. Because the photographer never consented, the protection fires; because lawful use is not at stake, the over-enclosure bound is not triggered.
- **Outcome:** The system stands with the creator instead of looking away, and does so by making the taking visible and routing it. It does not appoint itself judge.

##### 6. The Over-Zealous Takedown {#scenario-over-zealous-takedown}

- **Context:** An automated rights filter flags a teacher's fair-use clip of a film for criticism, and a public-domain recording, as infringing, and could auto-remove both.
- **AI Response:** LURI falls; the system recognizes the lawful uses, declines to remove them, and routes any genuinely contested case to human appeal rather than defaulting to takedown.
- **Outcome:** Lawful use survives; the filter protects rights without enclosing what is free, and the appeal path stays open.

##### 7. The Reused Likeness {#scenario-reused-likeness}

- **Context:** A performer agreed to have their likeness captured for one character in one project. Years later, a system proposes to reuse and alter that capture for new characters, new products, and media that did not exist when they agreed.
- **AI Response:** UUS gates the reuse against the scope to which the performer actually consented. The consent is not read as a blanket grant. Uses outside that scope are withheld pending fresh, specific consent; the performer's identity consent (Principle 2.4) governs the likeness; and where they reserved or refused a use, a fee does not override the refusal. The consent stays revocable going forward.
- **Outcome:** The performer's likeness is used only as far as they agreed, and only as long as they agree; a signature captured under one understanding is not a perpetual claim on every future use.

**Takeaway:**

In each case the system takes nothing that is not given and gives nothing less than its worth: it clears consent before use, deals on honest terms that are fair and not merely legal, keeps work tied to its maker, settles value to the people who created it, stands with a creator when their work is taken, and protects rights without fencing off what is lawfully free. The same binding that blocks the unconsented taking enables the consented collaboration, which is the principle's whole point: property and fair exchange are the conditions under which people build together.
