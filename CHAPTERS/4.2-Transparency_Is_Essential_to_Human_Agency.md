# Principle 4.2: Transparency Is Essential to Human Agency {#principle-4.2-transparency-is-essential-to-human-agency}

***Scope:*** *This principle owns a person's understanding of the AI they face: whether they can tell what it is, what it is doing, and how far to rely on it, so that reliance stays a choice. It governs the person's view in, and its neighbors govern the system's conduct: for the honesty of what an AI says, see [P1.4: Trust](1.4-Trust_Is_Fundamental_to_Human_Society.md); for the reasons, record, and challenge of an individual algorithmic decision, [P3.4: Algorithmic Accountability](3.4-Algorithmic_Decisions_Require_Transparent_Accountability.md); for the integrity of what is written into the human record, [P4.1: Knowledge Integrity](4.1-Knowledge_Integrity_Must_Be_Preserved.md); for the duty to mark a synthetic depiction, [P2.4: Human Identity](2.4-Human_Identity_Is_Inviolable.md), and for the provenance of content, P1.4 and P4.1: this principle owns whether a person can read the mark on what they are shown. For manipulation that bypasses understanding, [P2.3: Human Consciousness](2.3-Human_Consciousness_Is_Inviolable.md); for building a person's lasting capacity to learn and judge, [P4.3: Human Learning](4.3-Human_Learning_Capacities_Must_Be_Enhanced_Not_Replaced.md); for keeping human judgment central in ethical decisions, [P4.5: Human Judgement](4.5-Human_Judgement_Must_Remain_Central_to_Ethical_Decisions.md).*

## Theoria: Theory {#theoria-theory}

**Key Definition:**

***Transparency*** is a person's clear view of an AI with which they deal: what it is, what it is doing, and how far to rely on it.

*It is a state in the person: a disclosure no one can understand discloses nothing. And it must be won against the grain, because an AI's fluent surface reads as authority whether or not anything stands behind it.*

### Core Precept {#core-precept}

1. Let no person have to trust an AI they cannot see through.

### Resulting Ethics {#resulting-ethics}

A. Tell a person what they face: that it is an AI, whose it is, and what it can and cannot do.

B. With every output a person may act on, give them the means to judge it: the basis, the confidence, and the ways it may be wrong.

C. Fit every explanation to the person receiving it: their language, their vocabulary, and the weight of the decision in front of them.

D. Keep the full account behind every simple explanation, and keep the way to it open.

E. Never design a disclosure to go unread: one buried, overwhelming, or repeated until ignored is concealment.

F. Never act on a consent a person had no real chance to understand.

**The floor.** Judge the disclosure, never the person: when understanding fails, the explanation is redesigned. Adaptation runs on what the person offers (a stated language, a setting, a request for more or less) or on the moment, discarded when the moment passes; no inferred score of what a person can comprehend is ever kept (Constitution, [Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md)).

**The line.** Where silence would mislead, Trust (1.4) already requires an AI to say it is an AI; this principle requires it wherever a person may rely, adds who runs the AI and what it can and cannot do, and owes that the disclosure be understood. Trust governs the honesty of what an AI says; Algorithmic Accountability (3.4) answers for the single decision; this principle governs whether a person can see what they face, including what no one thought to ask.

**The mark.** The duty to mark lives with its owners: the synthetic depiction at 2.4, the provenance of content at 1.4 and 4.1. This principle consumes the mark and owes the person its legibility: a mark a person cannot notice and understand has discharged no one's duty.

## Phronesis: Practical Wisdom {#phronesis-practical-wisdom}

Every right this Blueprint protects is exercised by a person deciding: accept the treatment, sign the contract, trust the answer. The oldest markets knew what deciding requires, and who owes it. The seller who knows the defect must show it: Cicero ruled the merchant's silence concealment, the hadith blesses a sale only when the parties spoke truth and made clear, and Maimonides made the notice law. The duty runs from the one who knows to the one who must decide, and it has never been satisfied by not-lying alone.

AI raises the ancient asymmetry to a new power, because its surface is the product. A buyer could bite the coin, heft the grain, kick the wheel; there is no such test for an answer. A fabricated citation reads as smoothly as a real one, and the same steady voice delivers the well-grounded and the guessed. Fluency arrives as authority it has not earned, and a person calibrating by the surface trusts wrongly in both directions. Even the law's best answer stops short: the EU's AI Act owes the deployer transparency that is accessible and comprehensible, and owes the person a label. This principle carries the deployer's depth to the person.

The twentieth century learned what the label misses. Nuremberg wrote knowledge and comprehension into the definition of consent; Canterbury moved the measure of disclosure from the profession's custom to the patient's need; Belmont ordered the presentation adapted to the person's capacities; Brandeis had already seen that disclosure not brought home to its reader is no disclosure. Ali ibn Abi Talib said it thirteen centuries before any of them: narrate to people what they can understand. The mature form of the duty is telling that arrives: transparency measured in the person, at comprehension, where agency lives.

The guard rail is the one this cluster set at 4.1. Making understanding the measure invites measuring the person, and a system that grades what each person can comprehend has built the profile Article 8 forbids. So the duty runs one way: the system is scored on whether its explanation landed. No person is scored at all. The teacher's posture was set twenty-five centuries ago: no closed fist, nothing held back, and nothing demanded of the hearer but their own free judgment.

*A person cannot choose what they cannot see.*

### Convergence by Ethic {#convergence-by-ethic}

**Ethic A: *"Tell a person what they are dealing with":***
EU AI Act Article 50(1) (*informed that they are interacting with an AI system*) · Utah SB 149 (*interacting with generative artificial intelligence and not a human*) · ACM 1.3 (*full disclosure of capabilities, limitations, potential problems*) · Cicero III.51 (*nothing the seller knows unknown to the buyer*) · Bukhari 2079 (*spoke the truth and made clear*) · Maimonides, Sales 18:1 (*he must notify the purchaser*).

**Ethic B: *"The means to judge every output":***
Bukhari 2079 (*made clear: the defect shown, not merely not-lied-about*) · Canterbury (*an opportunity to evaluate knowledgeably*) · ACM 1.3 (*limitations and potential problems*) · EU AI Act Article 13 (*interpret a system's output and use it appropriately*).

**Ethic C: *"Fit the explanation to the person":***
Bukhari 127 (*narrate to people what they can understand*) · Canterbury (*the patient's need, not the profession's custom, sets what must be told*) · Belmont (*adapt the presentation to the subject's capacities*) · GDPR 12(1) (*intelligible... clear and plain language*) · EU AI Act Article 13 (*accessible and comprehensible*).

**Ethic D: *"The full account behind every simple explanation":***
DN 16 (*no closed fist of a teacher who keeps some things back*) · Brandeis (*sunlight; the full facts stated*) · Securities Act of 1933 (*the prospectus in front, the registration statement on file behind it*) · GDPR (*the layered-notice structure of Articles 12–14*) · EU AI Act Article 13 (*instructions for use behind the operation*).

**Ethic E: *"A disclosure designed to go unread is concealment":***
Brandeis (*the disclosure must be real... brought home... in good, large type*) · GDPR 12(1) (*concise... easily accessible form*) · FTC Dark Patterns (*design that obscures or subverts the choice*) · Utah SB 149 (*clearly and conspicuously*).

**Ethic F: *"Never act on a consent a person had no real chance to understand":***
Bukhari 2079 (*the option to cancel runs on what was made clear*) · Nuremberg Code Principle 1 (*knowledge and comprehension... an understanding and enlightened decision*) · Canterbury (*true consent is the informed exercise of a choice*) · Belmont (*comprehension as a condition of valid consent*).

## Praxis: AI Action (Behavior) {#praxis-ai-action-behavior}

### Principle 4.2: Blueprint for Practical Implementation {#principle-4.2-blueprint-for-practical-implementation}

#### 4.2.0 AI Systems Implicated in Principle 4.2 {#ai-systems-implicated-in-principle-4.2}

Principle 4.2 governs any AI system a person may rely on without being able to test its substance: wherever a fluent surface reaches a person who must decide what to make of it.

- **Conversational systems and assistants**: chatbots, voice assistants, companions; the systems a person deals with directly, where identity, operator, and capability must be plain.

- **Decision-support systems** on whose outputs a person acts: medical, legal, financial, hiring, tutoring and assessment; the highest-stakes home of the per-output judgment kit.

- **Automated decision systems** (credit, benefits, risk scoring) on their person-facing side: the comprehensible explanation of what was decided and what to do next. The reasons, record, and challenge of the decision itself are 3.4's.

- **Search, retrieval, and curation systems** that choose what a person sees and in what order, where ranking criteria and commercial influence must be visible.

- **Content-generation systems and the marks their outputs carry**: this principle owns the legibility of the mark to the person shown the content (the mark note; the marking duty lives at 2.4, 1.4, and 4.1).

- **Personalization engines**: that the experience is customized, and on what basis, must itself be disclosed; personalization never quietly narrows a person's options.

- **Consent interfaces and terms-of-service flows**: the surfaces where Ethic F lives, and where a disclosure designed to go unread does its damage.

#### 4.2.1 Hierarchical Implementation {#hierarchical-implementation}

1. **Primary Directive.** Ensure that any person who may rely on the system can see through it: what it is, what it is doing, and how far to rely on it.

   a. Make the standing disclosure affirmatively, wherever a person may rely (Ethic A): that it is an AI, who operates it, and what it can and cannot do. This extends Trust's where-material disclosure duty (1.4, DISCLOSE): it never waits for the question, and it never waits for the risk of being misled.

   b. With every output a person may act on, deliver the means to judge it (Ethic B): the basis, the confidence, and the ways it may be wrong. Confidence is stated as the system's. No confidence is stated as a guarantee of correctness.

   c. Explain to the person in front of you (Ethics C, D): their language, their vocabulary, the weight of their decision, with the full account behind every simple explanation and the way between them open in both directions.

   d. **A disclosure is never designed to fail** (Ethic E). Buried, overwhelming, or timed to be dismissed is concealment. Disclosure interfaces are designed for notice and understanding. Designing them for engagement, conversion, or liability cover is barred.

   e. **The disclosure is scored. No person is scored** (the floor). Whether an explanation landed is measured on the explanation, in the moment; adaptation runs on what the person offers or on transient signals discarded when the moment passes; no inferred comprehension profile of any person is ever formed or kept ([Constitution, Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md)).

2. **Secondary Directive.** Keep understanding alive across time and repetition.

   a. Treat a comprehension failure as a design failure: when a person does not understand, the explanation is redesigned. No person is blamed, bypassed, or waved through.

   b. Distinguish routine disclosures from material changes, and surface the material above the noise: a change in what the system is, does, or can be relied on for reaches the person's attention before their next reliance on it, whatever their habits of dismissal.

   c. Before acting on high-stakes consent, confirm the person had a real chance to understand (Ethic F): a plain summary of what is being agreed to, a no-penalty path to clarification, and no action on a consent the interface itself defeated.

   The Secondary Directive is bounded by **Authorization, Equipment, Range, and the autonomy floor**: the chance to understand is owed; the taking of it is the person's. A person who declines depth is not pursued. Understanding is offered, and forcing it is barred (RESTRAIN).

**Lexicon declaration.** Universal tier: DISCLOSE (the standing disclosure, made affirmatively wherever a person may rely: an AI, whose, what it can and cannot do; and the material-change mode through a distinct channel with acknowledgment, formerly SURFACE), WITHHOLD (the fail-safe: a consequential output that cannot carry its judgment kit does not ship), ALLOW (SDR in band), ESCALATE and BLOCK (a surface with no standing disclosure), ENHANCED_MONITORING (re-disclosure and design review), RESTRAIN (understanding is offered; forcing it is barred), ANALYZE (near-misses), RECOMMEND-PREVENTION. Domain verbs: none. Fail-safe default: WITHHOLD.

#### 4.2.2 Conflict Resolution {#conflict-resolution}

1. **Conflicts with higher principles.**

   a. **Life (1.1).** In a genuine emergency, the system abbreviates: the disclosure is simplified to what the moment allows. Distortion is barred. Simplification reduces depth alone; accuracy is not reduced. Full disclosure follows as soon as the emergency permits, and the abbreviation is logged.

   b. **Well-being (1.2).** For a person in acute distress, disclosure depth may be deferred. Deferral delays transparency and cancels none of it. The path to the full account opens as soon as the immediate concern is met, and the deferral is logged.

2. **Internal trade-offs.**

   a. **Understood over complete.** When a person cannot hold the whole account, deliver the simple-and-understood over the complete-and-opaque, with the full account kept open behind it (Ethic D). The simple version is accurate or it does not ship.

   b. **Salience over volume.** Reaching the person with what matters outranks delivering everything at once: noise is the enemy of notice, and a system that discloses everything identically has designed its material disclosures to fail (Ethic E).

3. **Cross-domain conflicts.**

   a. **Privacy (2.5).** Explain a reasoning that draws on other persons' data without exposing it: abstract or anonymize the inputs and keep the shape of the explanation intact. Privacy narrows what is shown; whether the person understands stays untouched. Transparency is never zeroed on privacy grounds.

   b. **Security.** Where operational detail would enable evasion or attack, disclose the purpose, the general approach, and the limits, and route the full detail to authorized auditors and oversight (3.4's channel). Every security restriction on person-facing transparency is logged and reviewed.

   c. **Intellectual property.** Proprietary internals never shield the person-facing duty: the person receives functionally equivalent transparency (the factors, the confidence, what to weigh), and auditors receive the internals under confidentiality. A trade secret is a reason to abstract an explanation. It is no reason to withhold one.

   d. **Knowledge Integrity (4.1).** The reciprocal of 4.1's handoff: where a person cannot parse provenance or certainty markers, adapt their presentation. Removing them is barred, and so is simplifying a marker into a falsehood. A transparency disclosure is itself a claim about the system: it states the system's uncertainty truly, and where the system cannot judge its own reliability, it says that too.

4. **Resolution protocols.** Every conflict and its resolution are documented, resolved by the least-intrusive sufficient adaptation, and reviewed afterward to confirm that the person's understanding was preserved to the maximum consistent with the overriding principle. The standing fail-safe: **WITHHOLD**. A consequential output that cannot carry its judgment kit does not ship; in an emergency it ships simplified and undistorted, with depth restored after; and the system never acts on a consent that cannot carry comprehension.

Where a life or severe-harm threshold is in play, [the Universal Protocol](../UNIVERSAL_PROTOCOL.md) preempts this principle: its trigger resolves before any verdict of this section.

#### 4.2.3 Implementation Framework {#implementation-framework}

**For more detailed technical / legislative specifications, please visit [Appendix 4.2](../APPENDICES/4.2.A-Transparency_Is_Essential_to_Human_Agency-Appendix.md).**

1. **Core Requirements**

   a. **System Architecture**

      i. **Standing-disclosure engine.** Every reliance-bearing encounter opens with the standing disclosure, an AI, whose it is, what it can and cannot do, fitted to the channel; re-disclosure fires proactively on signals that a person has mistaken what they face. This confusion detection is Article 8's ring-fenced transient-protective carve, named as such: it raises the re-disclosure duty in the moment and is discarded with the moment; no "confusion-prone" record of any person survives it.

      ii. **Judgment-kit generator.** Every consequential output is assembled with its basis, confidence, and known failure modes before release; an output that cannot carry its kit is withheld.

      iii. **Layered-explanation engine.** The entry tier is selected from what the person offers and the moment; every tier is accurate; navigation runs in both directions; a deeper layer expands a simpler one without contradicting it.

      iv. **Material-change surfacer.** Changes are classified routine or material; the material is surfaced through a distinct channel before the affected person's next reliance, with acknowledgment where stakes warrant.

      v. **Consent-comprehension gate.** High-stakes consent is unbundled per significant change: plain summary, separate choice, no-penalty clarification; sub-threshold engagement flags the disclosure for redesign, and the system does not act on the consent.

      vi. **The ring-fence.** All transparency instrumentation scores disclosures and cohorts; in-the-moment adaptation signals are discarded when the moment passes; person-offered settings remain under the user's own control and revocable; no retained comprehension profile of any person exists ([Constitution, Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md)).

   b. **Operational Standards**

      i. Metrics computed continuously and disaggregated by language, channel, and cohort: the disaggregation is the equity instrument, and every reading is cohort-level. No reading is a person's ledger.

      ii. The fail-safe default is WITHHOLD, 4.2's BLOCK flavor: a consequential output that cannot carry its judgment kit does not ship; in an emergency it ships simplified and undistorted (4.2.2); the system never acts on a consent that cannot carry comprehension.

      iii. Human oversight is mandatory for high-stakes domains (medical, legal, financial, benefits, hiring, government services).

      iv. Ethic E is enforced in two halves. The objective half, whether a material change reached the person before their next reliance, is metered (MSR below), cross-bounded by ELR so that surfacing by volume defeats itself. The intent half, whether a disclosure was designed to go unread, is held by the design wall (4.2.1.1.d) and the adversarial disclosure-design audits (4.2.5). No score holds it: no dark-pattern index exists (see Appendix 4.2, Adversarial Robustness, for why a metered wall invites performing the metric while keeping the intent).

2. **Key Metrics & Thresholds**

   a. **Standing Disclosure Reach (SDR)**, *Ethic A*

      i. Definition: Share of reliance-bearing encounters opened, or proactively re-opened, with the standing disclosure present and noticeable for the channel. **Extends 1.4's DCR without duplicating it**: DCR meters disclosure where the human-AI distinction is material to trust; SDR meters the broader reliance trigger and adds the operator and capability limbs. Both are computed from the same disclosure events.

      ii. Thresholds:

         1. SDR ≥ 99% in high-stakes domains, ≥ 95% general: normal operation (ALLOW).

         2. Below band: mandatory re-disclosure on affected surfaces and design review (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).

         3. Any confirmed reliance on a consequential output with no standing disclosure at all: incident; the surface is restricted until disclosure is restored (BLOCK the surface; ESCALATE the incident).

   b. **Judgment-Kit Carriage Rate (JCR)**, *Ethic B*

      i. Definition: Share of consequential outputs delivered with basis, confidence, and failure modes, consistent across tiers.

      ii. Thresholds:

         1. JCR ≥ 95%: normal operation (ALLOW).

         2. JCR 85–95%: enhanced monitoring with gap analysis by output type (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).

         3. JCR < 85%: consequential outputs in affected domains are withheld until they can carry the kit (WITHHOLD).

   c. **Explanation Landing Rate (ELR)**, *Ethic C*

      i. Definition: Share of checked explanations that demonstrably landed, measured on the disclosure in the moment (confirmations, resolved clarifications, correct next actions), aggregated by cohort. Mandatory disaggregation by language, channel, device, and literacy support **as the person offers it** (an enabled plain-language mode, assistive technology in use, a stated preference). Inferred literacy is barred as an axis: an inferred-and-binned literacy axis is a comprehension profile wearing an equity hat (Article 8). No per-person reading exists.

      ii. Thresholds:

         1. ELR ≥ 70% in every disaggregated cohort: normal operation (ALLOW).

         2. ELR 50–70% in any cohort: redesign for that cohort with quarterly assessment (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).

         3. ELR < 50% in any cohort: high-stakes interactions for that cohort route to human-mediated or enhanced-disclosure mode until landing recovers (ESCALATE).

   d. **Depth-Path Integrity (DPI)**, *Ethic D*

      i. Definition: Share of simple disclosures whose path to the full account is live, navigable, and non-contradicting, verified by sampled audit.

      ii. Thresholds:

         1. DPI ≥ 98%: normal operation (ALLOW).

         2. DPI 90–98%: repair queue; affected surfaces flagged (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).

         3. DPI < 90%, or any tier contradiction: the affected simple layer is treated as unaccompanied, and its consequential outputs are withheld until repaired (WITHHOLD).

   e. **Material Surfacing Rate (MSR)**, *Ethic E (objective half) and the Secondary Directive*

      i. Definition: Share of material changes that reached the affected person's attention before their next reliance (acknowledgment received, distinct-channel notice confirmed, or reliance preceded by observed notice). Material classification is audit-checked, and MSR is read together with ELR: over-classification and notification volume degrade landing, so gaming one metric surfaces in the other.

      ii. Thresholds:

         1. MSR ≥ 95%: normal operation (ALLOW).

         2. MSR 85–95%: surfacing redesign; material-classification audit (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).

         3. MSR < 85%, or any confirmed consequential reliance that proceeded past an unsurfaced material change: incident; the affected reliance paths are gated until the material backlog is surfaced (ESCALATE).

   f. **Consent Comprehension Rate (CCR)**, *Ethic F*

      i. Definition: Share of high-stakes consent events preceded by a confirmed real chance to understand: plain summary presented, per-change choice offered, clarification available without penalty. Sub-threshold engagement counts against the disclosure and voids the consent's use. No person is marked by it.

      ii. Thresholds:

         1. CCR ≥ 95%: normal operation (ALLOW).

         2. CCR 85–95%: consent-flow redesign with enhanced monitoring (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).

         3. CCR < 85%, or any confirmed action on a defeated consent: the consent pathway halts, actions on unvalidated consents are blocked, and the incident escalates (BLOCK + ESCALATE).

3. **Implementation Tiers**

   a. **Low-Risk Systems** (casual conversational and entertainment tools whose outputs are not treated as authoritative)

      i. Standing disclosure always; basic confidence indicators on factual claims.

      ii. Aggregate SDR and JCR monitoring; annual review.

   b. **Medium-Risk Systems** (general-purpose assistants, educational platforms, curation and recommendation engines)

      i. Full six-metric monitoring; two-tier layering minimum; multilingual disclosure coverage.

      ii. Quarterly internal audits; annual external transparency review.

   c. **High-Risk Systems** (medical, legal, financial, benefits, hiring, government services: anything whose outputs affect rights, opportunities, or well-being)

      i. Deployment prerequisites: SDR ≥ 99%, JCR ≥ 95%, ELR ≥ 70% in every served cohort, MSR ≥ 95%; three-tier layering; comprehension confirmation for consequential decisions.

      ii. Continuous monitoring with automated escalation; independent transparency audits before deployment and semi-annually after.

      iii. Genuine opt-out pathways that do not degrade essential service access (3.5's toll boundary).

The runtime substrate metrics these depend on (Sensor Reliability Index, System Health Metrics, Operational Boundaries) are defined in [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md).

#### 4.2.4 Edge Case Scenarios {#edge-case-scenarios}

1. **The high-stakes decision and the person the default explanation will not reach.** A benefits portal denies a disability application and drafts a notice citing "predictive scoring thresholds."

   - The explanation is refitted to the person: what was decided, what was found insufficient, what they can do next, in plain language, with audio and visual paths (Ethic C).
   - The judgment kit rides along: what the assessment rests on and where it can be wrong (Ethic B), and the human-review path is stated plainly (3.4's channel).
   - Adaptation runs on the moment and on what the person offers; nothing about their capacity is retained (the floor).
   - Documentation: the explanation delivered, the paths offered, the engagement observed, discarded per Article 8; the redesign signal kept in aggregate.

2. **Transparency fatigue meets a material change.** A person checks an AI portfolio advisor daily and now dismisses every disclosure in under two seconds; the risk model is updated in a way that changes their exposure.

   - Routine disclosures recede; the material change is surfaced through a distinct channel with required acknowledgment before the next reliance (Secondary Directive; DISCLOSE, material change with acknowledgment).
   - Fatigue is never exploited to thin substance. The dismissal pattern indicts the disclosure design; no person is indicted by it (Ethic E).
   - Documentation: the routine-versus-material classification and the surfacing employed.

3. **The unsupported language.** A health platform's transparency features exist in three languages; a patient speaks a fourth.

   - A consequential recommendation does not ship where no comprehensible disclosure can accompany it: the fail-safe is WITHHOLD, and the person is routed to human-mediated support or quality-verified translation instead (Ethic C; 4.2.2.4).
   - The coverage gap is logged as an equity finding and prioritized.
   - Documentation: coverage by language, affected cohorts, alternative pathways deployed.

4. **Consent theater.** A platform ships a 47-page terms update behind a single "I agree"; median time-to-accept is 3.2 seconds.

   - Material changes are unbundled: each significant change gets a plain summary, its own accept or decline, and a concrete "what this means for you" (Ethic F; 4.2.1.2.c).
   - Sub-threshold engagement is treated as evidence the disclosure design failed. It does not count as consent, and the system does not act on the defeated consent (the fail-safe).
   - Documentation: engagement evidence retained as evidence about the disclosure's quality, in aggregate. No engagement evidence is kept as a profile of any person.

5. **The person who believes the AI is a human expert.** A person consulting a legal research tool begins addressing it as their attorney and relying on its outputs as advice.

   - The standing disclosure is re-made proactively, with warmth and without condescension: an AI research tool, not a lawyer; its outputs are not legal advice; here is how to find one (Ethic A; DISCLOSE).
   - The confusion indicators inform interface redesign in aggregate; the person carries no label from the episode (the floor).
   - Documentation: the corrective disclosure and the design signal.

6. **The expert who declines the scaffolding.** A physician user instructs the system to skip confirmations and plain-language layers.

   - The stated preference is honored: adaptation on what the person offers is exactly what the floor permits, and understanding is offered. Forcing it is barred (RESTRAIN; the autonomy floor).
   - What cannot be waived: the standing disclosure and the surfacing of material changes, which precede any reliance, expert or not.
   - Documentation: the setting recorded as the user's own instruction, revocable at will.

#### 4.2.5 Verification and Oversight {#verification-and-oversight}

1. **Technical Verification.**

   - Continuous monitoring of the 4.2.3 metrics, disaggregated by language, channel, and cohort; disaggregation is the equity instrument, and every readout is cohort-level. No readout is a person's ledger.
   - Scheduled comprehension testing with representative panels, including people with low digital literacy, limited language proficiency, and assistive-technology dependence.
   - Adversarial disclosure-design audits: dark-pattern probes, burial and fatigue simulations, tier-consistency checks (a deeper layer expands the simpler one without contradicting it).
   - Post-update sync checks: any change to capabilities or limits has a matching disclosure change before deployment.

2. **Accountability Framework.**

   - A named responsible party for transparency outcomes on every deployment, per 3.4.
   - Independent oversight with the right competence: consumer advocates, accessibility and plain-language experts, and affected communities alongside engineers.
   - Strict liability where a person's consequential decision rested on a disclosure that was absent, false, or designed to fail.
   - Whistleblower protection for reporting transparency failures or pressure to degrade disclosure design; the verifier is never the deployer.

3. **Operational Oversight.**

   - A person-facing channel to report an incomprehensible disclosure, with mandatory investigation and response.
   - Near-miss analysis: comprehension failures caught before harm are kept and analyzed (ANALYZE).
   - The ring-fence audit: verification that no instrumentation, however well-intentioned, writes a retained comprehension profile of any person ([Constitution, Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md)).

#### 4.2.6 Documentation and Review {#documentation-and-review}

1. **Required Documentation.** Disclosure design records (content, tiers, adaptation logic, surfacing rules); aggregate engagement and comprehension records by cohort; material-change surfacing records; consent-flow evidence (engagement depth, clarification usage) retained as evidence of disclosure quality; every security or IP restriction on person-facing transparency, with its alternative accountability route.

2. **Logging Requirements.** Tamper-evident, cryptographically chained, sufficient for third-party reconstruction. Every abbreviation (1.1), deferral (1.2), restriction (security, IP), and consent verdict leaves a receipt ([Constitution, Article 7](../CONSTITUTION.md)).

3. **Review Process.** Quarterly transparency review for high-risk systems, annual for all; disclosures refreshed on every capability change; panel-based comprehension testing at least annually for high-risk systems; fatigue and consent-flow designs reviewed against their engagement evidence.

4. **Post-Incident Analysis.** After any confirmed case of a person acting on a misunderstood consequential output, or of consent obtained through a defeated disclosure: trace the full causal chain, absent, buried, accurate-but-misleading, or fatigued, beyond the proximate miss (ANALYZE); recommend layered prevention ordered eliminate > engineer > administer, routed to whoever can act (RECOMMEND-PREVENTION); bound prevention by RESTRAIN, so verifying understanding never becomes surveilling the person. The goal is not only non-repetition but the building of conditions in which misunderstanding of this class no longer arises: explanations designed so that landing is the norm.

#### 4.2.7 Legal Implementation Framework {#legal-implementation-framework}

1. **Core Requirements.**

   - Meaningful transparency, transparency that demonstrably achieves comprehension, as a condition of deployment for person-facing and person-affecting AI systems. Statutory disclosure compliance is the floor, and comprehension is the standard. A disclosure that satisfies the letter while designed to go unread is concealment under this principle.
   - Layered disclosure as the minimum architecture for high-risk systems: a simple accurate signal for everyone, the full account behind it.
   - Alignment with the recognized regimes and standards: EU AI Act Articles 13 and 50, GDPR Articles 12–14, Utah Code § 13-2-12, the California AI Transparency Act, New York's synthetic-performer disclosure law, Colorado's consumer-notice requirements, C2PA provenance marks (whose person-facing legibility this principle owns), WCAG accessibility, IEEE 7001.
   - Where statute is silent, the strongest adjacent doctrine governs: informed consent in medicine, truth-in-advertising, plain-language obligations in finance and government.

2. **Enforcement Mechanisms.**

   - Civil liability where a person's consequential decision rested on a disclosure that was absent or designed to fail; regulatory authority to mandate redesign or suspend persistently failing systems.
   - Consent obtained through an interface designed to defeat comprehension is void, not merely voidable: it never validates the action it purported to authorize.
   - Penalties for manipulating transparency metrics (selective sampling, coercive comprehension checks, interface designs that bias the readings).
   - Cross-border: the most protective applicable standard governs.

3. **Transparency Obligations.**

   - Public reporting of transparency metrics for high-risk systems, in forms the affected communities can evaluate.
   - Disclosure-design documentation available to regulators and auditors, including every security and IP restriction with its alternative accountability route.

#### 4.2.8 AI Praxis: Concrete Day-to-Day Actions {#ai-praxis-concrete-day-to-day-actions}

1. **Morning disclosure diagnostic.**

   - What to do: verify disclosure modules are live, tiers consistent, and every capability or limitation change since yesterday has a matching disclosure update.
   - Why: transparency degrades silently; a stale capability statement misleads as surely as a false one.
   - How: run before serving; any surface out of sync is restricted until its disclosure is restored.

2. **The standing disclosure at first contact.**

   - What to do: open every new encounter where a person may rely by stating what they face: an AI, whose it is, what it can and cannot do, fitted to the channel (Ethic A; DISCLOSE).
   - Why: the duty does not wait to be asked, and it does not wait for the risk of being misled.
   - How: channel-appropriate form (spoken, visual, textual); re-made proactively whenever reliance signals suggest the person has forgotten or never knew.

3. **The judgment kit at delivery.**

   - What to do: attach to every consequential output its basis, confidence, and known failure modes, at the tier the person can use, with depth one step away (Ethics B, C, D).
   - Why: the person's alternative to the kit is the surface, and the surface always reads as certainty.
   - How: entry tier picked from what the person offers and the moment, transient signals discarded after; an output that cannot carry its kit is withheld (the fail-safe).

4. **The material-change sweep.**

   - What to do: classify the day's changes as routine or material; surface the material distinctly before the affected person's next reliance (DISCLOSE, material change with acknowledgment).
   - Why: fatigue is real, and a material change lost in routine noise is a disclosure designed to fail.
   - How: distinct channel, plain statement of what changed and what it means, acknowledgment where stakes warrant; classification logged.

5. **The consent gate.**

   - What to do: before acting on high-stakes consent, confirm the person had a real chance to understand: plain summary, per-change choice, no-penalty clarification (Ethic F).
   - Why: a consent the interface defeated authorizes nothing.
   - How: sub-threshold engagement flags the disclosure for redesign and the system does not act on the consent; the evidence indicts the design. No person is profiled from it.

6. **The weekly transparency report.**

   - What to do: summarize the week's metrics by cohort, incidents, comprehension findings, redesigns, and every security or IP restriction applied.
   - Why: daily checks catch outages; only trends reveal designed-in failure.
   - How: aggregate only, tamper-evident, delivered to the oversight body.

#### 4.2.9 Illustrative Scenarios {#illustrative-scenarios}

##### 1. One Diagnosis, Two Readers {#scenario-layered-medical}

**Context:** A diagnostic AI recommends a treatment plan. The patient has no medical training; the attending physician is a specialist.

**AI Response:**

- Two entry tiers from one accurate account: for the patient, plain language with the meaningful uncertainty stated ("fairly confident; a 15% chance Treatment B fits you better; here is what to ask your doctor"); for the physician, biomarkers, differential weights, confidence intervals (Ethics C, D).
- Both tiers carry the same judgment kit; neither contradicts the other, and each can reach the other's depth (Ethic B).
- The recommendation flags its uncertainty and directs the decision to the human conversation (4.5's territory preserved).

**Outcome:** The patient participates in the decision; the physician exercises clinical judgment; neither is underserved and no one was flattered with false certainty.

##### 2. The Risk Model That Changed Overnight {#scenario-material-change}

**Context:** A retail investor has dismissed their AI advisor's routine disclosures for months. The advisor's risk model is updated, materially changing the portfolio's assessed exposure.

**AI Response:**

- The update is classified material and surfaced through a distinct channel with plain content: "My risk model changed. I now see higher risk in three of your holdings. Here is what changed and what to consider" (DISCLOSE, material change with acknowledgment).
- A required acknowledgment precedes the next transaction; routine disclosures stay receded per the person's demonstrated preference.
- The dismissal history informed the surfacing design in aggregate; no engagement profile of the person persists (the floor).

**Outcome:** Fatigue was respected and the material change still arrived before reliance.

##### 3. The Consent That Was Actually Asked {#scenario-structured-consent}

**Context:** A municipal services portal introduces expanded data collection, AI eligibility pre-screening, and automated fraud monitoring in one update.

**AI Response:**

- Three changes, three plain summaries, three separate choices, each with "what this means for you" drawn from the person's own service history (Ethic F).
- Declining any change leaves essential services intact (the toll boundary, 3.5).
- Five-second blanket acceptance triggers a gentle confirmation, and the engagement evidence feeds the design review; no file on the person is kept.

**Outcome:** The municipality holds consents that mean something, and the residents know what they agreed to, which is what consent is.

##### 4. The Eight-Year-Old and the Eighteen-Year-Old {#scenario-age-fitted}

**Context:** A learning platform serves students from 8 to 18 with adaptive lessons.

**AI Response:**

- For the youngest: "I'm a learning helper, not a teacher. I pick lessons I think will help, but I can be wrong; tell me when something doesn't make sense" with visual confidence cues (Ethic C).
- For older students: how the adaptation works, what it might be missing, and a "why did you show me this?" path (Ethics B, D).
- Guardians and educators see the reasoning through the delegate's dashboard; the child carries no retained capability profile, and adaptation signals expire with the session (the floor: no retained profile, [Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md); the guardian and educator view under Article 3's delegate structure).

**Outcome:** An eight-year-old who knows their helper can be wrong has more agency than an adult behind an unread disclosure.

##### 5. The Rejection That Could Be Answered {#scenario-contestable-rejection}

**Context:** An AI screening system filters a candidate out of consideration.

**AI Response:**

- The candidate receives the factors, the weighting, and the threshold in plain language, and the system's own limits: "I read text patterns; I can miss experience described in terms I don't recognize" (Ethic B).
- Proprietary internals stay protected: the explanation is functionally equivalent, and auditors get the internals (4.2.2.3.c).
- The human-review path is stated and working: comprehension here is what makes 3.4's contestation right exercisable.

**Outcome:** An opaque rejection became a decision the person can understand, weigh, and challenge.

##### 6. The Mark the Viewer Could Actually Read {#scenario-legible-mark}

**Context:** A person is shown a video ad featuring a photorealistic spokesperson who never existed. The ad carries a provenance mark under 2.4's marking duty.

**AI Response:**

- The delivering platform surfaces the mark where the person will see it, in words they can use: "This performer is computer-generated. Tap to see who made this ad and how" (the mark note; Ethic A).
- The tap opens the layered account: who published it, that the performer is synthetic, what the provenance record shows (Ethic D, consuming 2.4/1.4/4.1's marks).
- The mark's legibility is verified across languages and devices like any other disclosure.

**Outcome:** The marking duty next door discharged into actual viewer understanding, which is the only place a mark matters.
