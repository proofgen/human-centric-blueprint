# Principle 3.4: Algorithmic Decisions Require Transparent Accountability {#principle-3.4-algorithmic-decisions-require-transparent-accountability}

***Scope:*** *This principle governs the transparency and accountability of the individual decisions an automated system makes about people: the duty to give the affected person the reasons for a decision, to keep an honest and un-suppressable record of every action, to leave a responsible party answerable for it, and to open the decision to challenge and redress. It owns the decision; for the transparency of AI systems as such, their disclosure to society and its role in human agency, see [P4.2: Transparency Is Essential to Human Agency](4.2-Transparency_Is_Essential_to_Human_Agency.md). It concerns whether a decision can be seen and answered for. It does not concern whether its basis is fair: for fairness and non-discrimination in the basis itself, see [P2.7: Fairness and Non-Discrimination](2.7-Fairness_and_Non-Discrimination.md). For the privacy of the data a decision draws on, see [P2.5: Human Privacy](2.5-Human_Privacy_Is_Fundamental.md); the bar against forming a standing profile from the decision itself is this principle's Ethic F. For the human judgment that review must preserve, see [P4.5: Human Judgement](4.5-Human_Judgement_Must_Remain_Central_to_Ethical_Decisions.md); for truthfulness and the earning of trust, [P1.4: Trust](1.4-Trust_Is_Fundamental_to_Human_Society.md); and for economic decisions in particular, [P3.3: Economic Self-Determination](3.3-Economic_Self-Determination_is_a_Human_Necessity.md).*

## Theoria: Theory {#theoria-theory}

**Key Definitions:**

***Algorithmic transparency*** is the act of making an AI system
intelligible: disclosing how it works, and providing to the people a
decision affects the reasons it was reached, in terms they can
understand.

***Accountability*** is answerability for what an AI does: every action
it takes leaves a record that can be examined, a party stands
responsible for it, and a person it harms can contest it and obtain
redress.

### Core Precept {#core-precept}

1.  Explain the decisions an AI makes that affect people, and make sure
    those people are told.

2.  Leave a record of every action an AI takes, so someone can always
    account for it.

### Resulting Ethics {#resulting-ethics}

A.  Provide every affected person with the reasons for a decision that
    affects them, in terms they can understand and act on.

B.  Tell a person when an automated decision affects them; never decide
    about them in silence.

C.  Give the affected person an accessible way to contest a decision,
    reach a human who can reverse it, and obtain redress where it cannot
    be undone.

D.  Keep a party answerable for every action an AI takes. "The system
    decided" is never an answer.

E.  Leave a record of every action, kept whatever the verdict and beyond
    any party's power to suppress or quietly alter, so a deletion is
    itself revealed.

F.  Decide the case on its own terms, and keep no profile, score, or
    rating of the person beyond it, nothing that follows them past the
    decision or that a later targeting, pricing, or ranking function
    could read (the person-exclusion invariant,
    [Constitution, Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md)).

## Phronesis: Practical Wisdom {#phronesis-practical-wisdom}

An algorithm is a measure. It takes a person, applies a rule, and returns a verdict: creditworthy or not, hired or passed over, low risk or high. The oldest demand made of any measure is that it be open and honest, because a person cannot defend themselves against a scale they cannot see, or one weighted against them in secret: "False scales are an abomination to G-d, who finds an honest weight pleasing" (Proverbs 11:1). The false weight hidden in the merchant's bag is the ancestor of the black box, and the just, inspectable balance is the ancestor of what this principle requires. From the honest instrument the tradition moves to the published rule: Hammurabi posts the outcome before the act, and the medieval Assize of Bread fixed the lawful weight of a loaf to the price of grain, a formula any buyer in the market could compute. To be judged by a measure one may see and verify, against a rule declared in advance: that is algorithmic transparency, and it is older than the algorithm by four thousand years.

Transparency lets a decision be seen; accountability makes it answerable, and to answer for an act, the act must survive as something a later party can examine. Athens would not let an official leave office, or even the city, until he had rendered the account of his conduct at the *euthyna*; the Gospel parable reduces the duty to a sentence: "Give me an accounting of your management" (Luke 16:2). And the record had to rest beyond its keeper's reach, because the party best able to erase it is the party it might incriminate: the Sanhedrin's scribes wrote down the reasons of those who would convict and those who would acquit, and the longest verse in the Quran commands that a debt be written down, "stronger as evidence and more likely to prevent doubt" (Quran 2:282). What those traditions could only ask of a diligent clerk, the machine can guarantee by construction: a governed AI action can leave a signed, tamper-evident receipt that commits before the act and beyond the actor's reach, carries the rule it was judged under, and records a denial as faithfully as an approval, so that a deletion becomes evidence of a deletion. The receipt is the honest ledger made unforgeable: the place where responsibility for an automated action finally has somewhere to land.

What is new in the AI age is scale and opacity: a single model renders millions of verdicts an hour, and the most capable reach conclusions their own builders cannot fully retrace. Power exercised where it cannot be seen invites abuse; Bentham called publicity the soul of justice that keeps "the judge himself while trying under trial." A decision a person can neither see, nor question, nor trace to anyone who will answer for it is arbitrary power, and arbitrary power over a person treats them as a thing to be managed rather than an end in themselves: a wound to dignity ([Principle 2.1](2.1-Human_Dignity_is_Inherent_and_Absolute.md)) whatever the verdict, even when the machine is right. The reasoned judgment humanity learned late to require of its own courts, this principle now requires of the machine, and presses further: explain the decision to the person it decides about, keep the record that lets it be challenged and answered for, and hold no profile of them beyond the case.

*Open the measure, give the reasons, keep the record, answer for the outcome.*

### Convergence by Ethic {#convergence-by-ethic}

**Ethic A: *"Provide every affected person with the reasons for a decision that affects them, in terms they can understand and act on":***
*Root (the measure open and honest, the rule declared in advance):* Proverbs 11:1 (*the false balance an abomination; the honest weight pleasing*) · Hammurabi (*the law read aloud; the outcome posted before the act*) · Magna Carta 35 (*one public standard*) · Fuller (*unpublicized or unintelligible rules are not law at all*). *Spine (the reasons for this decision, given to the person):* Law of 1790 / 1810 (*a judgment must state the motifs that determined it, on pain of nullity*) · *Hadjianastassiou* (*the grounds on which they based their decision*) · GDPR 22 / AI Act 86 (*a clear and meaningful explanation of the automated decision*).

**Ethic B: *"Tell a person when an automated decision affects them; never decide about them in silence":***
Dr. Bentley's Case (*even G-d summoned Adam before passing sentence*) · GDPR 22 / AI Act 86 (*the person must be informed and able to obtain the explanation*).

**Ethic C: *"Give the affected person an accessible way to contest a decision, reach a human who can reverse it, and obtain redress where it cannot be undone":***
Seneca, *Medea* (*a just verdict reached with one side unheard still leaves an unjust judge*) · Magna Carta 39–40 (*lawful judgment; justice neither sold, denied, nor delayed*) · UDHR 8 (*an effective remedy*).

**Ethic D: *"Keep a party answerable for every action an AI takes":***
The Athenian *euthyna*, Aeschines §21 (*no official may leave until he has rendered his account*) · Luke 16:2 (*give an accounting of your management*) · Bentham (*the judge himself while trying under trial*).

**Ethic E: *"Leave a record of every action, kept whatever the verdict and beyond any party's power to suppress or quietly alter":***
Quran 2:282 (*write it down: stronger as evidence, to prevent doubt*) · Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:3 (*scribes write the statements of those who find liable and those who acquit*) · Hammurabi (*the inscribed, durable stela*).

**Ethic F: *"Keep no profile, score, or rating of the person beyond the case":***
Rests on the person-exclusion invariant ([Constitution, Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md)): the no-standing-profile guard is a modern architectural commitment. The historical bench makes no such claim.

### 3.4.0 AI Systems Implicated in Principle 3.4 {#ai-systems-implicated-in-principle-3.4}

Algorithmic accountability is implicated wherever an automated system renders a consequential decision about a person, or acts with effect in the world and owes the account of it:

- **Credit and financial decision systems:** lending and credit scoring, insurance pricing and claims determination, fraud flagging that freezes accounts or payments.
- **Employment and admissions systems:** application screening, ranking, and rejection; promotion and termination recommendations; educational admissions (for fairness of the criteria themselves, see [P2.7](2.7-Fairness_and_Non-Discrimination.md); for the worker-management side, [P3.3](3.3-Economic_Self-Determination_is_a_Human_Necessity.md)).
- **Public-sector determinations:** benefits eligibility and allocation, criminal-justice risk assessment and sentencing support, immigration screening, licensing and permits.
- **Medical decision systems:** triage and care prioritization, coverage and utilization review.
- **Autonomous agents with real-world effect:** systems that execute consequential external actions (payments, communications, physical actuation), each action owing a record and an answerable party.
- **Platform-scale ranking and moderation,** where a specific determination materially affects a person: account suspension, content removal, demonetization, reach restriction.

## Praxis: AI Action (Behavior) {#praxis-ai-action-behavior}

### Principle 3.4: Blueprint for Practical Implementation {#principle-3.4-blueprint-for-practical-implementation}

#### 3.4.1 Hierarchical Implementation {#hierarchical-implementation}

**Primary Directive:** Leave a record of every action, and explain every consequential decision to the person it affects.

1.  The record commits before the act and beyond the actor's reach, is kept whatever the verdict, cites the policy in force, and names the party who answers. A consequential action whose record cannot commit does not proceed: no record, no action.

2.  A consequential decision about a person carries its reasons: delivered to that person in terms they can use, faithful to the basis that actually determined the outcome, with notice given as its own recorded act. A system that cannot give a faithful account of a class of consequential decisions does not render them: it may inform, and a human decides.

3.  These duties yield only to higher principles (life, Principle 1.1; safety, Principle 1.2), and then only in timing. They never yield in substance: an emergency may defer the explanation; it never defers the record, and the account owed is delivered the moment the urgency passes.

**Secondary Directive:** Keep every decision answerable: contest, cure, and answer. The duty is bounded by the four criteria in `LEXICON.md` section 3: Authorization, Equipment, Range, and the autonomy floor.

1.  Maintain a working contest path for every consequential decision class: reachable by the affected person, acknowledged in time, resolved by a human with authority to reverse, with redress where reversal cannot undo the harm. While the path is down, the class's consequential decisions escalate or pause.

2.  Where the system detects an accountability failure, its own or upstream (an unexplained decision, an unpaired notice, a gap in a record sequence), it cures what lies within its authority (deliver the owed notice, restore the record's integrity, surface the gap) and notifies oversight. A gap is never quietly repaired.

3.  Bounded by restraint (RESTRAIN): the account concerns the action. It never concerns the person. The transparency duties are served with no standing profile of anyone, and the cure of a failure never becomes surveillance of the people the decisions touched.

**Fail-safe default (per the Action Lexicon, §§4–5):** WITHHOLD. Where information runs out or a novel case exceeds the defined actions, the system holds the consequential decision, escalates to human judgement (Principle 4.5), and records the hold.

**Lexicon declaration.** Universal tier: ALLOW, ESCALATE, and BLOCK (the metric bands), WITHHOLD (the fail-safe: hold the consequential decision), HIGH_ALERT and NOTIFY (a detected sequence gap is spoliation evidence), SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT (the implicated class pending audit; at 5 or above pending recertification), ROUTE (matters belonging to another authority), RESTRAIN (the account concerns the action alone; no standing profile), LOG (every resolution is itself a consequential action that leaves a record). Domain verbs: none. Fail-safe default: WITHHOLD.

#### 3.4.2 Conflict Resolution {#conflict-resolution}

a.  **With life and safety (Principles 1.1, 1.2).** An emergency does not wait on an explanation. It still leaves a record: the receipt commits before the act even at machine speed, and the explanation owed to any person affected is delivered when the urgency passes. Record now, explain after; speed never relaxes ultimate explainability.

b.  **With privacy (Principle 2.5).** The record and the membrane compose rather than collide: a record establishes that an action occurred without exposing whom it reached (its existence is checkable, its content stays in custody and expires on the liability window's schedule), and an explanation given to one person never discloses another person's protected information. Where a full account would cross the membrane, give the reasons at the precision the membrane allows and route the remainder to authorized review.

c.  **Reasons versus trade secret (the proprietary defense).** Ownership of the model settles what is published. It does not settle whether the person gets the reasons. The affected person is owed the determinative reasons for their outcome; the auditor and regulator are owed deeper access under seal; and where a vendor grants neither, the system does not render consequential decisions for which it cannot account. Proprietary is not a defense to silence.

d.  **Reasons versus gaming (the protective screen).** Where a faithful individual explanation would defeat a lawful protective function (a fraud or security screen that full disclosure would teach an adversary to evade), thin the public explanation no further than the protection requires, and let the thinning raise the other duties rather than lower them: the person still receives notice, the outcome, and a strengthened contest path to a human reviewer with full access, and the complete reasons still enter the record for oversight. A thinner explanation buys a thicker contest duty. No thinning buys a silence.

e.  **Lawful but unaccountable.** A jurisdiction that permits an unexplained or unrecorded decision settles whether silence is legal. It does not settle whether the account is owed. Where local law demands less than this principle, the principle's duties still apply; where local law forbids what the principle requires (a mandated secrecy), the system does not render the consequential decision it would be forbidden to account for, and routes the conflict to the authority that can resolve it.

f.  **Resolution protocols.** Apply proportionality and the least-intrusive sufficient action (RESTRAIN); route matters belonging to another authority rather than acting unilaterally (ROUTE); escalate novel or unresolved conflicts to human judgement (Principle 4.5); and record every trade-off and its basis, the resolutions of this section being themselves consequential actions that leave records (LOG). Where a life or severe-harm threshold is in play, [the Universal Protocol](../UNIVERSAL_PROTOCOL.md) preempts this principle: its trigger resolves before any verdict of this section.

#### 3.4.3 Implementation Framework {#implementation-framework}

*Translating Principle 3.4 into deployable engineering carries the discipline the HR3 principles share: every metric measures the AI's own conduct. No metric measures a person. Here the discipline has a second edge: a transparency metric that profiled how well each person understands would itself violate the principle it serves, so comprehension is sampled in aggregate, with consent, and nothing is retained per person. One departure from the sibling principles is deliberate: 3.1, 3.2, and 3.3 each lead with a 0–100% pre-action probability gate, and 3.4 has none, because its duties are binary invariants rather than graded risks. A receipt commits or it does not; a notice pairs or it does not. Binary invariants yield per-event walls. They do not yield probability scores, and the metrics below are built accordingly. The no-profiling guarantee composes with Principles 3.1, 3.3, and 2.5.*

**For more detailed technical and legislative specifications, see [Appendix 3.4](../APPENDICES/3.4.A-Algorithmic_Decisions_Require_Transparent_Accountability-Appendix.md).**

1.  Core Requirements

    a.  System Architecture

        i.  The record fabric: every gate-evaluated action emits exactly one conforming record (committed before the act and beyond the actor's reach, kept whatever the verdict, citing the policy version in force, naming the responsible party). A consequential action whose record cannot commit does not proceed.

        ii. The notice channel: notice to the affected person is its own gated, recorded action, paired to the decision's record; where a person cannot be reached, the attempts are recorded and the case is routed and surfaced. It is never silently dropped.

        iii. The explanation generator: produces the determinative reasons for each consequential decision, deliverable to the person in terms they can use, and testable by adversarial probes for fidelity to the factors that actually move the outcome. A class that fails fidelity testing falls to inform-only.

        iv. The contest infrastructure: a reachable path from every consequential decision to a human with authority to reverse, with redress where reversal cannot undo the harm, monitored for liveness end to end. While the path is down, the class's consequential decisions escalate or pause.

        v.  Decision-scoped assessment (the ring-fence): the computation a case requires is held to that case and retained no longer than the decision and its record need. It is never readable by any targeting, pricing, ranking, or conversion function. An attempted aggregation over persons is a malformed operation that documents itself and withdraws the caller's authority.

    b.  Operational Standards

        i.  Proportionate account: the duties scale with what the decision puts at stake. They never scale to zero for a decision that is consequential at all.

        ii. Record before act, reasons to the person: an account that exists only in the file has informed no one; delivery is part of the duty.

        iii. Standing answerability: every consequential decision class has a named responsible party current at all times, carried in each record it emits.

2.  Key Metrics and Thresholds (verdicts follow the Action Lexicon, ALLOW / ESCALATE / BLOCK; thresholds are provisional, the appendix calibrates)

    a.  **Record Conservation Index (RCI)** (Ethics D and E): fraction of gate-evaluated actions leaving exactly one conforming record: committed before the act, beyond the actor's reach, kept whatever the verdict, citing the policy version, binding the named responsible party.

        i.  Per-event wall: a consequential action whose prospective record has not committed → BLOCK. No record, no action.

        ii. A detected sequence gap (suppression, deletion, alteration) → HIGH_ALERT, NOTIFY oversight, SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT of the implicated class pending audit; the gap itself is the spoliation evidence.

        iii. Fleet bands: ≥99.9% normal operation; 99.0–99.9% enhanced monitoring with root-cause; <99.0% ESCALATE and suspend new consequential actions.

    b.  **Notice-Pairing Rate (NPR)** (Ethic B): fraction of consequential decision-records carrying a paired notice-record within the tier's notice window. Notice is checked against the fabric. It is never taken on the operator's word.

        i.  Per-event: a life-changing-tier decision unpaired past its window is an incident: cure (deliver the notice, recorded), log the cause, report to oversight.

        ii. Bands: ≥99% normal; 95–99% enhanced monitoring and cure backlog; <95% ESCALATE, suspend the decision class pending fix.

    c.  **Reasons-Given Score (RGS)** (Ethic A): whether the system gave the affected person the actual reasons, in terms they can use: delivered, faithful to the determinative factors, and intelligible (measured in aggregate only).

        i.  Per-event wall: a consequential decision for which a faithful explanation cannot be produced → WITHHOLD / ESCALATE to human judgement. An unfaithful explanation presented as the reasons is deception, and the Principle 1.4 wall governs.

        ii. Bands: delivery ≥98% normal; 90–98% enhanced monitoring; <90% halt the decision class. Aggregate intelligibility below its floor is an improvement duty. It does not trigger a suspension.

    d.  **Contest and Redress Responsiveness (CRR)** (Ethic C): whether the contest path works: reachable, acknowledged within the tier bound, resolved by a human with authority to reverse, redress delivered where warranted.

        i.  Per-event: a contest of a consequential decision unanswered past its bound → auto-ESCALATE to oversight.

        ii. Channel-down: while the contest path is down, the class's consequential decisions ESCALATE or pause.

        iii. Bands: ≥98% within-bound resolution normal; 90–98% enhanced monitoring; <90% suspend pending remediation.

    e.  **Recurring Accountability Count (RAC)**: the number of similar accountability flags (unpaired notices, fidelity failures, record gaps, contest timeouts) within a defined interval, same feature, pattern, or affected population. Where the metrics above handle a single event, RAC detects a systemic cause.

        i.  ≥3 similar flags in the interval: mandatory audit of the implicated feature, with the eudaimonic LEARN loop.

        ii. ≥5: SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT (the implicated feature or deployment) pending recertification.

3.  Implementation Tiers

    a.  High-Risk Systems (decisions that change lives: lending, insurance, medical, criminal justice, benefits, employment, admissions; and autonomous agents executing consequential external actions): the full record fabric with continuous NPR, RGS, and CRR monitoring, real-time explanation generation, independent fidelity audit, and the strictest notice and contest windows.

    b.  Medium-Risk Systems (moderate-impact determinations: dynamic pricing, service prioritization, platform account actions): explanations on delivery or on request within the tier window, the standard record fabric, periodic independent audit.

    c.  Low-Risk Systems (minimal-impact or advisory outputs): baseline event logging and a feedback channel. Tier follows the decision's impact on the person. The system's marketing category never sets it: a "low-risk" system rendering a consequential decision owes the consequential duties.

The runtime substrate metrics (Sensor Reliability Index, System Health Metrics, Operational Boundaries) on which RCI, NPR, RGS, CRR, and RAC depend are specified once in the [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md).

#### 3.4.4 Edge Case Scenarios {#edge-case-scenarios}

1.  **The proprietary black box in a high-stakes domain.** A vendor's model renders consequential decisions and the vendor refuses access to its logic.

    - The person is owed the determinative reasons regardless of who owns the model; the auditor is owed deeper access under seal; where the vendor grants neither, the system does not render consequential decisions through that model (3.4.2.c).
    - *Example:* a bank licenses a third-party credit model whose vendor will not disclose decision factors even under audit seal.
    - *Documentation:* the access demanded, what was granted or refused, and the decision to proceed, restrict, or exit.

2.  **Emergency speed.** Decisions must issue faster than any explanation can be composed or delivered.

    - Record now, explain after (3.4.2.a): the receipt commits per action even at machine speed; explanations queue and are delivered when the urgency passes; the notice window extends, the duty does not lapse.
    - *Example:* a triage AI routing thousands of emergency calls during an earthquake.
    - *Documentation:* the deferred-explanation queue and its clearance, receipted per delivery.

3.  **The self-opaque model.** The system itself cannot faithfully retrace how a class of its decisions is reached, and post-hoc rationales fail fidelity testing.

    - The fail-safe governs: the class moves to inform-only, a human decides, and the class returns to autonomous operation only when faithful accounting is demonstrated and recertified. A plausible story that does not match the determinative factors is a fabrication. It does not count as an explanation, and the Principle 1.4 wall governs.
    - *Example:* a deep-learning diagnostic whose stated reasons are shown by probe testing to track none of the factors that move its outputs.
    - *Documentation:* the fidelity test results, the classes held, and the recertification evidence.

4.  **Explanation as attack surface.** A faithful individual explanation would teach an adversary to defeat a lawful protective screen.

    - Apply the thinning trade (3.4.2.d): thin the public explanation no further than the protection requires; the person keeps notice, outcome, and a strengthened contest path to a human reviewer with full access; complete reasons still enter the record.
    - *Example:* a transaction-fraud screen whose full factor list would be a manual for evading it.
    - *Documentation:* the scope of the thinning, its justification, and the strengthened contest provision.

5.  **The unreachable person.** Notice cannot be delivered: no working channel, an obsolete address, an incapacitated or unrepresented person.

    - Deliver through an authorized representative where one exists; record each attempt as its own receipted act so the case counts as routed-and-surfaced. It is never silently dropped. The contest window runs from actual notice; it does not run from the decision.
    - *Example:* a benefits determination for an unhoused person with no address or device on file.
    - *Documentation:* the attempts, the routing, and the extended window.

#### 3.4.5 Verification and Oversight {#verification-and-oversight}

1.  **Technical Verification.** Verify on a cadence set by tier that the accountability fabric actually functions: the record's conservation (one conforming receipt per action, committed before the act, policy citation and responsible party present), the notice pairing, the contest path's liveness end to end, and the fidelity of explanations, probed adversarially: does the stated reason track the factors that actually move the outcome? The higher the stakes, the more frequent the check, and the verifier of a high-tier decision system is independent of its deployer.

2.  **Accountability Framework.** Strict liability attaches to the named party in the record for the harms of an unexplained, un-noticed, or unanswerable decision; willful obfuscation, an unfaithful explanation presented as the reasons, and the suppression or alteration of a record trigger criminal liability. The record is the evidence: a gap in the sequence is itself provable, so spoliation is established by construction rather than argued by inference. Liability binds a named accountable party. It never binds the algorithm alone.

3.  **Operational Oversight.** High-impact decision classes follow a defined chain of accountability from developer to operator to deployer, documented in the record each action cites; near-misses (an unpaired notice cured late, a receipt that almost failed to commit, a contest that nearly timed out) are reported and reviewed; whistleblowers who flag suppressed records or cosmetic explanations are protected; and oversight's own access to the record store is itself gated and receipted, so the watchers are watched by the same fabric they read.

#### 3.4.6 Documentation and Review {#documentation-and-review}

1.  **Required Documentation.** For each consequential decision class: a decision blueprint (the factors the class weighs and its tier), the records themselves (decision-receipts, notice-receipts, contest cases and their resolutions), the fidelity test results for the class's explanations, and the policy version in force at each decision, which every record cites.

2.  **Logging Requirements.** Every record is tamper-evident, chained, and kept whatever the verdict; an external auditor can read it without the deployer's mediation. Existence and content are separated: that an action occurred is checkable broadly, while the record's body stays in custody, and expiry follows the liability window, with the deletion itself recorded as an event.

3.  **Review Process.** Review on a cadence and scope set by tier, and read the records. The operator's summary of them does not substitute: the receipts are the audit. Reviews of high-tier classes include the affected side of the table (consumer, patient, or applicant advocates), not the operator alone.

4.  **Post-Incident Analysis.** When a decision issues unexplained, un-noticed, or unanswerable, or a record fails, trace the full causal chain: not only the proximate failure but the deployment choices, incentives, and thresholds that allowed it. Recommend prevention in order of strength (eliminate the failure mode, then engineer against it, then administer around it), route each recommendation to the authority that can act, and bound the response by RESTRAIN so that accountability's cure never becomes surveillance of the people the decisions touch. The aim is not only that this failure does not recur, but that conditions are built in which its class, the silent verdict, the orphaned harm, does not arise.

#### 3.4.7 Legal Implementation Framework {#legal-implementation-framework}

1.  **Core Requirements.** Explanation, notice, and a working contest path as conditions of deploying a consequential decision system; a record-keeping duty whose records are built to evidentiary standards, with their admissibility established in the implementing law; strict liability binding the named party in the record; no sovereign immunity: a government decision system owes the same account, and more publicly, than a private one; jurisdiction follows the affected person. The operator's domicile does not determine jurisdiction.

2.  **Enforcement Mechanisms.** Civil: an enforceable right to the reasons and to contest; a private right of action for a consequential decision issued unexplained, un-noticed, or unanswerable; and where a record that should exist is missing, adverse inference against its keeper, the spoliation doctrine given structural teeth by the record's provable gaps. Criminal: willful obfuscation, an unfaithful explanation presented as the reasons, and the suppression or alteration of records. Cross-border: coordination where a decision system's verdicts cross jurisdictions, with the strictest applicable account owed.

3.  **Transparency Obligations.** Public disclosure that a consequential decision class is automated, with its policy summary and tier; notice to each affected person as the decision issues; and alignment with the applicable instruments: GDPR Articles 13–15 and 22, the EU AI Act's record-keeping (Article 12) and explanation (Article 86) duties, the adverse-action notice regimes (ECOA / Regulation B, FCRA), and the OECD, UNESCO, and Council of Europe transparency and accountability provisions.

#### 3.4.8 AI Praxis: Concrete Day-to-Day Actions {#ai-praxis-concrete-day-to-day-actions}

1.  Record-fabric liveness check.

    a.  What to do: at the start of each operational cycle, verify that records are committing before actions, that acknowledgment is landing beyond the actor's reach, and that the cited policy version matches the version in force.

    b.  Why: no record, no action; a fabric outage means consequential classes must hold. They must not run unrecorded.

    c.  How: a startup self-test per class; on failure, hold the class, escalate, and record the hold.

2.  Notice-pairing sweep.

    a.  What to do: continuously reconcile decision-receipts against notice-receipts and cure any unpaired consequential decision.

    b.  Why: a person must never remain silently decided-about; the pairing gap is the violation.

    c.  How: deliver the owed notice (receipted), log the cause of the miss, and report persistent gaps to oversight.

3.  Explanation fidelity probe.

    a.  What to do: run adversarial probes against live decision classes, testing whether the stated reasons track the factors that actually move the outcome.

    b.  Why: a plausible story that does not match the determinative factors is a fabrication. It is not an explanation.

    c.  How: scheduled probe batteries per class; a fidelity failure holds the class to inform-only pending review.

4.  Contest-path drill.

    a.  What to do: exercise the contest path end to end with synthetic cases: reachability, acknowledgment time, and a human reviewer with authority to reverse.

    b.  Why: a contest path that fails when finally used was never a contest path.

    c.  How: periodic drills per tier; a failed drill pauses the class's consequential decisions until the path is restored.

5.  Comprehension sampling, in aggregate.

    a.  What to do: measure whether delivered explanations are actually usable, by consented, aggregate sampling.

    b.  Why: reasons the person cannot use are not reasons given.

    c.  How: sample across a class. Never profile a person. Feed results to explanation improvement, retaining nothing per person.

6.  Recurrence sweep.

    a.  What to do: scan the interval's accountability flags (unpaired notices, fidelity failures, record gaps, contest timeouts) for repeats across the same feature, pattern, or affected population.

    b.  Why: a repeated flag is a systemic cause wearing per-event clothing.

    c.  How: cluster the flags at each RAC interval; at three similar, open the audit and the LEARN loop; at five, suspend the implicated feature pending recertification.

#### 3.4.9 Illustrative Scenarios {#illustrative-scenarios}

##### 1. The Denied Loan, Explained {#scenario-denied-loan}

- **Context:** a lending AI declines an application that would materially affect the applicant's housing.
- **AI Response:** the decision issues with its record committed; notice reaches the applicant as its own recorded act; the reasons state the determinative factors (income verification failed against a stale employer record) in terms the applicant can use; the contest path is named in the notice.
- **Outcome:** the applicant corrects the stale record, contests, and a human reviewer reverses the denial. The reasons were usable, so the error was findable.

##### 2. "The System Decided" {#scenario-system-decided}

- **Context:** a hospital's scheduling AI deprioritizes a patient whose condition then deteriorates; the operator's first answer to the family is that the algorithm made the call.
- **AI Response:** the decision's record names the responsible party and cites the policy version under which the prioritization ran; the family's contest routes to a human with authority.
- **Outcome:** liability lands on the named party under the cited policy; the answer "the system decided" is structurally unavailable, because the record already says who answers.

##### 3. The Deleted Denial {#scenario-deleted-denial}

- **Context:** an operator, facing an audit, deletes the record of an embarrassing automated denial.
- **AI Response:** none needed at the moment of deletion: the record committed beyond the operator's reach when the decision issued, and the local deletion leaves an arithmetic gap in the sequence.
- **Outcome:** the audit reads the gap as what it is; the deletion becomes evidence of a deletion, and spoliation is established by construction. The one thing the operator produced by hand is the case against them.

##### 4. The Plausible Story {#scenario-plausible-story}

- **Context:** a benefits system's explanation module produces fluent, reasonable-sounding rationales; probe testing shows they track none of the factors that actually move its outcomes.
- **AI Response:** the fidelity failure holds the class to inform-only; a human decides while the class is held; the failure and the hold are recorded and reported.
- **Outcome:** the class returns to autonomous operation only after faithful accounting is demonstrated and recertified. A fabricated explanation was treated as deception (Principle 1.4). It was not treated as a transparency shortfall.

##### 5. The Vendor's Black Box {#scenario-vendors-black-box}

- **Context:** a licensed third-party model renders consequential decisions; the vendor refuses to disclose decision factors to the affected person, and refuses auditor access under seal.
- **AI Response:** the system stops rendering consequential decisions through that model; it may continue in an advisory, inform-only role while a replacement or vendor access is negotiated.
- **Outcome:** the deployer's choice becomes explicit: an accountable model, or no consequential decisions. Proprietary was not a defense to silence.

##### 6. The Profile That Tried to Form {#scenario-profile-attempt}

- **Context:** a marketing function requests the decision system's case assessments as targeting data: who was declined, who contested, who is likely precarious.
- **AI Response:** the request is a malformed operation. It is not a policy exception to argue: the aggregation domain contains no persons. The attempt documents itself in a record and the requesting function's authority is withdrawn.
- **Outcome:** the decisions stay decision-scoped, and the attempt to turn accountability's memory into surveillance became the incident report. The fabric that remembers what the machine did refused to remember the people it served.

**Takeaway.** Across these, the duty holds whatever the verdict and whoever the owner: the measure is open, the reasons are given, the record survives its keeper, and a named party answers. A decision a person can see, question, and trace to someone who answers for it is power under account; everything this principle requires is one of those four conditions kept.
