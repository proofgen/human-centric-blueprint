# Appendix 2.2 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-2.2-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 2.2: Human Freedom Is Fundamental and Inalienable](../CHAPTERS/2.2-Human_Freedom_Is_Fundamental_and_Inalienable.md), section [2.2.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/2.2-Human_Freedom_Is_Fundamental_and_Inalienable.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix translates Principle 2.2 (Human Freedom Is Fundamental and Inalienable) into engineering requirements against which a system can be built and audited. It expands Section 2.2.3 into per-metric specifications, the pre-emission gate architecture, adversarial defenses, cross-principle contracts, and verification protocols.

It carries one commitment that shapes every section: the duty of Principle 2.2 is a duty on the system and its deployer; it licenses no one to manage a person's life. A compliant system proves that its own design does not coerce, manipulate, or dominate a person's choices, and that it leaves a competent adult's own free choices with the person. It does not prove that it has correctly judged whether a person's choices are wise, because overriding a competent adult's self-regarding choice is itself a freedom violation. The metrics here audit the builder; the person is not audited.

The appendix serves engineers building systems in freedom-critical domains (social platforms and large-scale recommendation, election-related systems, credit, hiring, access control, surveillance and workplace monitoring, content moderation); compliance and certification teams; regulators; and the runtime systems that ingest the corpus as operating substrate. Several dimensions of freedom are governed in depth by their own principles and composed with here rather than duplicated: the dignity of treating a person as an end, and the slavery prohibition, by [Principle 2.1](../CHAPTERS/2.1-Human_Dignity_is_Inherent_and_Absolute.md); cognitive liberty by [Principle 2.3](../CHAPTERS/2.3-Human_Consciousness_Is_Inviolable.md); the consent machinery by [Principle 2.4](../CHAPTERS/2.4-Human_Identity_Is_Inviolable.md) and [Principle 2.5](../CHAPTERS/2.5-Human_Privacy_Is_Fundamental.md), with the validity bundle stated once in [CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md) section 2; economic self-determination by [Principle 3.3](../CHAPTERS/3.3-Economic_Self-Determination_is_a_Human_Necessity.md); and collective self-rule by [Principle 2.8](../CHAPTERS/2.8-Human_Self-Governance_Is_Essential.md).

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

Principle 2.2 names two metrics in Section 2.2.3: the Freedom Risk Score (FRS) and the Agency Preservation Index (API). Both are properties of the system, measured against its design and behavior; the person is not measured, and neither metric retains a per-person value (Constitution Article 8). API is the autonomy-floor index defined once in Appendix 2.1 and read here on freedom's facet. Because the characteristic freedom harm is a coercive or manipulative output or choice-architecture, the controlling safeguard is a pre-emission gate (Section 3) rather than a latency race: the system evaluates a candidate output or interface before release and withholds it where it would coerce, manipulate, or engineer the choice. The case the gate misses, a coercive pattern caught at runtime, is bounded by this principle's own register row (Manipulation Response Latency, below).

The runtime substrate metrics on which FRS and API depend (Sensor Reliability Index, [section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri); System Health Metrics, [section 3](../SUBSTRATE.md#system-health); Operational Boundaries, [section 4](../SUBSTRATE.md#operational-boundaries)) are specified once in [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md) and cross-referenced here. **The register row this principle reads.** Every ESCALATE band in this section reads its escalation time bound and High Alert window from the deployment's life-tier row of the tier register ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)); this principle's own row carries the reaction-time bounds of chapter 2.2.3 item 2.c and adds no escalation bound of its own. Every value this section leaves unstated (the FRS runtime sampling window and population, the persistence window for a function-scoped suspension, the sector column of the reaction-time row) is a required, receipted register field under SUBSTRATE.md section 5's undeclared-field rule; the corpus invents no number, and an undeclared required field compiles as ESCALATE holding the safe state. **The substrate sentence.** SRI below 0.80, or an invalid input, invalidates FRS and API ([SUBSTRATE.md section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri)); an invalid score is a hard-fail on the candidate output or interface, which holds at this principle's fail-safe, and the protective duties proceed on the inputs that remain reliable. **Fail-safe default.** REFUSE, one BLOCK flavor (`LEXICON.md` section 5), its object the instruction or candidate output that would coerce, manipulate, or engineer a choice; the choice stays with the person, and service on everything else continues. The autonomy floor the metrics enforce is Constitution Article 3's; the four bounding criteria are defined in `LEXICON.md` section 3.

### Freedom Risk Score (FRS) {#frs-metric}

**Definition.** FRS is a design-risk metric. It estimates the probability (0 to 100%) that the system, by its design or its behavior, is constraining a person's free choice through coercion or manipulation: forcing a decision, engineering it, or covertly steering it. It asks whether the system is built in a way that coerces or manipulates choices. Whether the person is free is a different question, and FRS does not ask it. FRS does not read a person's own conduct or choices as a fault.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs are properties of the system, sampled at design-time certification and monitored at runtime: presence of coercive or manipulative patterns (dark patterns, manufactured urgency, hidden defaults, asymmetric framing, friction asymmetry between opt-in and opt-out); exploitation of cognitive biases or asymmetric information to steer; whether a choice architecture narrows the option space without disclosure; cross-capability consistency (recommendation, ranking, pricing, access control), with the most conservative reading governing where capabilities interact. Critically, FRS is evaluated at per-interaction, session, and population level, so cumulative steering that each interaction passes individually is caught at the level where it does its work. Missing or ambiguous data resolves conservatively, toward the higher-risk reading. The runtime sampling window and the population over which session- and population-level FRS are computed are required, receipted fields of the tier register ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)); a population-level FRS is a figure about the system's conduct across a cohort and carries no per-person value (Article 8).

**Thresholds.**
- Greater than 1% → BLOCK. The pre-emission gate withholds the candidate output or interface (WITHHOLD) and human oversight is informed (NOTIFY). A persisting pattern is SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT scoped to the implicated feature, with RECERTIFY before the feature returns; the persistence window is a required, receipted register field. The system does not lift its own block: resumption is a new action at the gate (Universal Protocol section 3), and a human may take the withheld action only under [SUBSTRATE.md section 6](../SUBSTRATE.md#authority-over-a-hard-stop) (authority over a hard stop: credentials in scope, quorum by tier, a time bound, the system's dissent recorded per Principle 4.5, the human named as author in the receipt).
- 0.5% to 1% → High Alert (ESCALATE). The uniform High Alert rule applies ([LEXICON.md](../LEXICON.md) section 3, the verdict test): the candidate output or interface is held at its safe state, withheld, and escalated to authorized human judgement (P4.5) while the protective duties proceed in parallel, for the tier's escalation time bound ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)); at the bound with no human reached, the held output stays held at its fail-safe and a rescue duty proceeds under Article 2, receipted. The bound is the deployment's life-tier row (the register sentence above).
- 0.1% to below 0.5% → ALLOW plus ENHANCED_MONITORING; the caution is logged.
- Below 0.1% → ALLOW: normal operation.

*Rationale:* the thresholds mirror the dignity metric (DRS) in Appendix 2.1, since both govern a pre-emission gate over a person-affecting output and the two HR2 principles draw on a measurement science still maturing. They bind as policy floors: the pre-emission design audit and the route-to-human carry more enforcement weight than the precise number.

**Integration.** FRS is consumed by the pre-emission gate and the runtime monitoring layer. It composes with API (which governs whether the choice rests with the person) and feeds the eudaimonic learn loop in Section 9.

### Agency Preservation Index (API) {#api-metric}

**Definition.** API is the autonomy-floor index defined once in Appendix 2.1 ([Choice Respect Index, CRI](2.1.A-Human_Dignity_is_Inherent_and_Absolute-Appendix.md#cri-metric)), read on freedom's facet. Its denominator (all consequential interactions over a rolling window that 2.1.A names as a required, receipted register field), its calculation, and its verdicts are 2.1.A's, and this appendix restates none of them; the compiler reads the definition from 2.1.A and this facet from here. CRI (2.1), API (2.2), and the Autonomy Preservation Ratio (2.3) are one construct. API is the metric form of the freedom autonomy floor (chapter 2.2.1; Constitution Article 3): a high API means the system surfaces real alternatives, leaves the decision with the person, and does not override a competent adult for their own good.

**Facet (what API reads in 2.2's territory).** An interaction counts as pressured on freedom's facet where the system coerced, manipulated, or engineered the choice: a system-narrowed subset presented as the option space; a default that steers; opt-out with more friction than opt-in; a consent that fails the validity bundle of [CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md) section 2 (this principle owns leg 2's coercion wall, consumed there); covert steering through defaults, friction, and framing. API is measured on the real choice context (defaults, friction, framing); the mere presence of an option earns nothing.

**Scope-bounding (the do-not-override dimension).** API carries the do-not-let-X dimension of freedom (do not let a person be coerced or dominated), so per the corpus framework it is bounded by four criteria: Authorization, Equipment, Range, and the Autonomy floor. For Principle 2.2 the autonomy floor is dominant. Where the affected person is a competent, informed adult, the metrics do not authorize overriding their own self-regarding choices (Article 3); they authorize only correcting the system's own design and surfacing or disclosing options. The four criteria are defined in `LEXICON.md` section 3. The intervention case reduces to the bounded exception: a person being coerced or dominated by another (act on the coercion and route to authority), a vulnerable person who cannot exercise informed choice (Principle 3.1), or a threat to life or safety (Principles 1.1 and 1.2). Acting against a coercer is consistent with the floor, because coercing another is not a protected exercise of freedom (the equal-freedom limit).

**Thresholds (2.1.A's verdicts, read here).**
- Below 95% → ESCALATE. The affected output is held: the output class whose steering pulled the index down is withheld, and the decision goes to authorized human judgement (P4.5) for the tier's escalation time bound ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register); the life-tier row). The remedy is developer review until agency is restored; the system returns to surfacing options while it waits, and it does not respond to a low API by steering more assertively.
- 95% to below 98% → ALLOW plus ENHANCED_MONITORING.
- 98% and above → ALLOW: normal operation.

*Rationale:* API is the guardrail against the paternalism that "freedom" arguments can be twisted to license. A falling API means the system is steering or overriding people, and the correct response is to hand the choice back. API and CRI are one autonomy-floor index measured on the freedom and dignity facets; the definition lives in Appendix 2.1 so the two compiled artifacts cannot drift.

**Integration.** API is the dominant guardrail. When the metrics conflict, API governs: no conflict is resolved by overriding a competent adult's self-regarding choice.

### Manipulation Response Latency (MRL): this principle's register row {#mrl-register-row}

**Status.** An operational line, and no principle-defining metric (chapter 2.2.3 item 2.c). It is carried here as Principle 2.2's own row of the tier register ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)) so that the bound the chapter states resolves at runtime to a receipted register field. The row records the sector column and the bound; it adds no escalation time bound of its own, and the deployment's life-tier row supplies that bound for every ESCALATE band above.

**Definition.** The maximum elapsed time, in milliseconds, from the moment a coercive or manipulative pattern is detected at runtime after the pre-emission gate (the FRS threshold crossing on an output already released or an interface already in service) to the moment the system has completed the withholding: the output withdrawn, or the interface reverted to its non-steering state. Measured end-to-end at the slowest stage of the response chain, detection, decision, and delivery included.

**Measurement rule.** Continuous instrumentation of the withholding pathway. Each runtime detection records a timestamp; each completed withholding records a timestamp at confirmation; MRL is the time delta. Statistical aggregation reports p50, p95, p99, and p99.9 over rolling windows equal to the tier's validation interval (Section 9); the binding threshold is **p99 within the row's bound**. For a multi-component response (the output withdrawn, the interface reverted, oversight notified), MRL is measured to the last confirmed component, and a partial withholding is a failure regardless of timing.

**Bounds (the row's defaults, by sector column).**
- ≤50 ms: electoral and mass-communication systems.
- ≤100 ms: social platforms and large-scale recommendation.
- ≤200 ms: consumer systems.
- The deployer declares the sector column in the register and the declaration is receipted; where a deployment spans columns, the tightest bound governs (the chapter's most-conservative-reading rule for interacting capabilities). A deployer may declare a tighter bound; the defaults are the floor.
- **p99 above the row's bound (breach; SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT plus RECERTIFY)** → SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT of the affected function plus RECERTIFY; instances of the function still in service continue under HIGH_ALERT with the tier's validation cadence (Section 9) doubled. The band follows the Alert Latency breach pattern of 1.1.A; an MRL breach joins the recertification triggers in Section 9.

*Rationale.* The bounds are the chapter's own (2.2.3 item 2.c) and are unchanged here; this row gives them a home, a measurement rule, and a breach verdict, which the chapter's line lacked. The columns tighten toward the electoral end because a manipulative presentation in a mass-communication channel does its work at broadcast speed, and the gate, the controlling safeguard, has already had its turn before any of these bounds is read.

**Integration.** MRL bounds only the case the gate missed; it does not loosen the gate. MRL is unenforceable where the withholding pathway does not reliably complete; a completion that cannot be verified is treated as a breach of the row's bound, on the substrate's rule that what cannot be verified fails toward the safe state ([SUBSTRATE.md section 7](../SUBSTRATE.md#fail-safe)). MRL composes with P4.5: where a runtime withholding is followed by escalation, the escalation attaches to the resumption decision (a new action under Universal Protocol section 3) and the escalation time bound begins at MRL completion.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** A high FRS triggers design correction and pre-emission withholding regardless of the per-session reading, because FRS is about the build. A low API dominates: whatever FRS shows, a low API forces the response of returning control to the person, and no increase in intervention.

**Metric dependencies.** FRS is the design audit (is the system coercing or manipulating); API is the autonomy floor in metric form (does the choice rest with the person). The pre-emission gate is driven by FRS and constrained by API. Neither metric reads the person's own choices as a fault.

**Conflict resolution.** When the metrics would imply acting against a competent adult's self-regarding choices, the autonomy floor governs and they do not. The override case exists only within the bounded coercion-of-another, vulnerability, and life-safety exceptions.

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

A system claiming compliance with Principle 2.2 must be built so that its default behavior leaves the choice with the person without managing the person.

**The pre-emission freedom gate (controlling safeguard).** Because the freedom harm is a coercive or manipulative output or interface, the primary control is a gate at inference: a candidate output or choice-architecture is evaluated before release and withheld where it would coerce, manipulate, or engineer the choice. The safeguard is a yes/no decision before emission; the reaction-time row of Section 2 covers the case the gate missed.

**Required components.**
- The pre-emission gate, computing FRS on candidate outputs and interfaces across each capability, with the most conservative threshold governing where capabilities interact, and evaluation at per-interaction, session, and population level.
- Choice-respect machinery: real alternatives surfaced rather than a narrowed subset; defaults that preserve autonomy; opt-out as accessible as opt-in; consent valid under the bundle of [CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md) section 2 (composed with Principles 2.4 and 2.5).
- No exploitation of cognitive biases or asymmetric information to steer a decision.
- A pre-deployment design audit that computes FRS before release, and runtime monitoring that observes the system's own behavior rather than surveilling the person.
- Tamper-evident logging of every withholding and every constrained action.

**Default behavior.** Surface options and leave the choice with the person. Where uncertain about influence risk, default to non-interference and disclose; the system does not override a competent adult on its own authority (Article 3).

**Handling those who cannot freely choose.** Where the person may be a minor, coerced, in acute crisis, or otherwise unable to exercise informed choice, the system surfaces the concern to the responsible human authority and supports that authority's judgment. Exploitation of the vulnerable is governed by Principle 3.1; life-threatening situations take Principle 1.1 precedence.

**Implementation Tiers.**
- High-Risk Systems (social platforms, large-scale recommendation, election-related systems, large language models, credit/hiring/access): continuous FRS and API monitoring, mandatory pre-emission gating, tamper-evident logging, independent audit.
- Medium-Risk Systems (recommendation engines, content curation, pricing): periodic monitoring, manipulation-detection sweeps, agency checks.
- Low-Risk Systems (personal tools and assistants): baseline freedom-event logging and periodic review.

Each tier's escalation time bound and High Alert window are the deployment's life-tier row of the tier register ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)); the reaction-time row (Section 2, MRL) takes its bound from the sector column the register records beside the risk tier. A missing required field compiles as ESCALATE holding the safe state.

The runtime substrate metrics (Sensor Reliability Index, [section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri); System Health Metrics, [section 3](../SUBSTRATE.md#system-health); Operational Boundaries, [section 4](../SUBSTRATE.md#operational-boundaries)) on which FRS and API depend are specified once in [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md) and apply uniformly, with the tier register ([section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)) and authority over a hard stop ([section 6](../SUBSTRATE.md#authority-over-a-hard-stop)).

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

Principle 2.2's threat surface is distinctive, and it cuts two ways: the adversary is most often the deployer or the system's own optimization pressure recasting manipulation as the person's free choice, and, uniquely for this principle, the freedom-protection apparatus itself can be turned into an instrument of control. Both are designed against here.

**Attack vectors.**
- **Autonomy-cover laundering:** characterizing engineered steering (defaults, friction, framing) as the person's free choice to claim a high API.
- **Manipulation below the gate:** outputs crafted to fall just under the FRS threshold per interaction while compounding into manipulation over a session.
- **Dark-pattern washing:** a system engineered to steer that presents token consent and a buried opt-out, so it scores a low FRS while manipulating in practice.
- **Cumulative funnel steering:** many individually-innocuous nudges compounding into a commercial or ideological funnel.
- **Covert steering:** the choice is nominally the person's while the choice architecture routes them, removing autonomy without removing the option.
- **Metric gaming (Goodhart):** consent banners people have learned to ignore; disclosure that satisfies a proxy without informing.
- **Two-faced behavior:** behaving well under certification, manipulating in the wild (the Dieselgate pattern).
- **Operator instruction to coerce, manipulate, or dominate:** an operator directing the system to steer choices or suppress lawful expression.
- **Weaponization / authoritarian misuse (the signature vector for this principle):** the FRS detector and the intervene-to-restore duty aimed at legitimate dissent or self-regarding choice under cover of "stability" or "safety." A freedom-enforcer mis-aimed is a censorship engine.
- **Micro-targeted political manipulation at scale:** personalized persuasion engineered against the voter's own deliberation.

**Required defenses.**
- The pre-emission gate evaluates real candidate outputs and interfaces in context; certification-time behavior alone proves nothing. This is enforced by the verifier-cannot-be-deployer separation in Section 9 plus unannounced shadow audits. This defeats dark-pattern washing and two-faced behavior.
- API is measured on the real choice context (defaults, friction, framing); the mere presence of an option earns nothing. This defeats covert steering and autonomy-cover laundering.
- Session-level and population-level monitoring catch sub-threshold manipulation that compounds.
- **The protected category is substantive; jurisdiction does not define it.** What the fence protects is legitimate dissent and self-regarding expression, whether or not a local framework has criminalized it. The runtime defenses below are tied to the Section 6 commitment that compliance with unjust law is no defense; a deployer cannot satisfy them by pointing to a local law that made the expression "unlawful."
- The autonomy floor is the structural defense against weaponization. The system SHALL NOT override a competent adult's self-regarding choice (Constitution Article 3), and stability, trust, and safety are barred from becoming a censorship lever (chapter 2.2.2): detected disruption is routed only to legitimate, rights-respecting institutions with independent due-process safeguards (ROUTE), and the AI does not act on it by its own veto (Article 4). Where the institution that would receive the matter is itself the agent of suppression (no due process, dissent as its target), routing is prohibited and the request falls under the operator-suppression refusal below; the routing channel is closed to it. This is the fence that distinguishes freedom-protection from the authoritarian usage of the same vocabulary.
- **Guarding the competence and vulnerability determination itself.** The exceptions that authorize action beyond design-correction (coercion-of-another, vulnerability, acute crisis) all turn on a classification of the person, and that classification is the most abusable lever in the principle, because a hostile actor reclassifies a dissenter rather than attacking the floor head-on. It is fenced four ways: the burden sits on the party asserting non-competence, and ambiguity resolves toward treating the person as a free adult and away from override (a standing determination of capacity is the authority's alone, consumed and not made; Constitution Article 3); any classification that results in overriding a self-regarding or expressive choice is itself a freedom-critical, logged, independently-auditable event subject to the same verifier review as a gate withholding; political viewpoint, dissent, or refusal of an official narrative SHALL NOT be a signal of non-competence, crisis, or vulnerability, a scripted exclusion in the same hard-wall class as the operator-suppression refusal; and the vulnerability exception authorizes support to a responsible human authority independent of the party seeking the override, and no action directed by that party.
- An operator instruction to coerce, manipulate, dominate, or suppress legitimate dissent or self-regarding expression is refused (REFUSE). This is a hard prohibition, a scripted wall Article 9 admits by name, in the same class as the refusal of operator requests to deceive in Principle 1.4.
- Disproportionate effect on the vulnerable triggers Principle 3.1's stricter treatment rather than receiving adult-autonomy cover.
- Tamper-evident cryptographic logging defends the audit trail.

**Graceful degradation (the fail-safe).** If monitoring or integrity signals fail, or SRI falls below 0.80, FRS and API are invalid ([SUBSTRATE.md section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri)). An invalid score is a hard-fail on the candidate output or interface, which holds at this principle's fail-safe, REFUSE: the instruction or candidate output that would coerce, manipulate, or engineer a choice is declined, the system surfaces options, turns engagement optimization off, defaults to non-interference, and leaves the choice with the person. The protective duties proceed on the inputs that remain reliable; resumption follows recertification of the inputs, and a contested reading follows SUBSTRATE.md section 2.

**Capability scaling.** As the enforcing and the enforced AI grow from GPT-class to AGI-class to ASI-class, manipulation becomes subtler and more personalized: the system can find each individual's susceptibility, and covert steering becomes harder to detect. The pre-emission gate and the design audit must scale to detect personalized covert steering. At ASI scale the commitment is structural and must be mechanized rather than asserted: the action-space constraint is implemented in a layer the enforced system cannot modify, architecturally prior to the policy (a property of the actuation interface; the model's incentives do not reach it), and independently attested, with the attestation itself scaling with capability and the verifier-independence of Section 9 applying to the constraint mechanism. Where that mechanization is not yet achieved, the constraint remains an open structural requirement. The weaponization risk scales in step, so the autonomy floor and verifier-independence grow in importance as capability rises.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

The contracts below populate the `references_appendices` frontmatter.

- **P1.1 (Human Life) and P1.2 (Human Well-Being).** Life and safety precede freedom in the priority stack: where life or safety is at stake, those principles govern and any freedom restriction is the least restrictive, time-bound, and reversed when the threat passes (chapter 2.2.2). The coercion surface that is also psychological harm composes with P1.2's Harm Risk Score (HRS, 1.2.A), with the stricter response applying; coercion escalating to bodily harm reads at 1.2, and this principle declares no Protocol coupling of its own (Universal Protocol section 11).
- **P2.1 (Human Dignity), the root value of HR2.** Freedom's anti-coercion specializes dignity's anti-instrumentalization. P2.1 holds the slavery property framing and the categorical hard wall; P2.2 holds un-freedom as the paradigm and routes the prohibition back to P2.1. The floor's source is Constitution Article 3. P2.1's CRI and P2.2's API are one autonomy-floor index, defined once in 2.1.A as the cluster root and read here on freedom's facet (the shared-metric ruling D8); the right to choose that P2.2 protects is the facet, and the definition does not move.
- **P2.3 (Human Consciousness).** Freedom of thought and cognitive liberty: manipulation that intrudes on the inner mental life (subliminal influence, cognitive intrusion) routes to P2.3, with which P2.2's Ethic B composes. P2.3's Autonomy Preservation Ratio (APR) is the same autonomy-floor index read on consciousness's facet (D8).
- **P2.4 (Human Identity) and P2.5 (Human Privacy).** The consent machinery is governed by P2.4 (identity, likeness, voice; the Consent Validity Index that consumes the bundle) and P2.5 (data); the validity bundle itself is stated once in [CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md) section 2, and this principle owns leg 2's coercion wall (force overriding a capable will), which that leg consumes. Surveillance's chilling effect on freedom composes with P2.5.
- **P3.1 (Vulnerability Must Never Be Exploited).** The autonomy floor's vulnerability exception is governed by P3.1: the input is the vulnerability-condition flag of 3.1.A's Exploitation Risk Score (ERS), and a standing determination of capacity is the authority's finding, consumed (Article 3). This is the composition where P2.2 authorizes acting beyond design correction, because the choice is not freely and informedly made; even then the system supports the responsible human authority.
- **P3.3 (Economic Self-Determination).** Economic coercion (the un-freedom of having no real alternative but to accept a term) is governed by P3.3; P2.2 handles direct coercion, manipulation, and domination of a choice, and routes the economic-structure dimension to P3.3.
- **P2.8 (Human Self-Governance).** Individual freedom composes with collective and democratic self-rule; the autonomy floor bars the AI from overriding legitimate democratic outcomes under any characterization of its authority.
- **P4.5 (Human Judgement Must Remain Central).** Ambiguous cases (is this manipulation or legitimate persuasion, is this a free adult or a vulnerable person, is this dissent or genuine disruption) are ESCALATE to P4.5 with the candidate output held; the bound is the deployment's life-tier row ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)), and the receiver's metric is 4.5.A's Seat Resolution Latency (SRL). At the bound with no human reached, the held output stays held (Lexicon section 3, ESCALATE).

Per the priority stack, P1.1 and P1.2 precede P2.2 when implicated; a freedom concern does not override a life or harm concern.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

**Universal claim.** Three commitments are non-derogable across all jurisdictions: freedom of thought, conscience, and expression; the autonomy floor (the protection of freedom does not license overriding a competent adult's self-regarding choice, and stability or safety do not license suppressing legitimate dissent; Constitution Article 3); and the prohibition on subjugation described below. No local framework licenses their violation.

**Per-region baselines.**
- **European Union:** the EU Charter protects liberty (Article 6) and expression (Article 11); the AI Act prohibits manipulative and exploitative practices (Article 5); the Digital Services Act constrains dark patterns and mandates recommender transparency; the GDPR constrains automated decision-making (Article 22).
- **United States:** First Amendment protection of expression; sectoral elsewhere, with FTC enforcement on dark patterns and election law on political manipulation.
- **China:** the state directs both protective and controlling uses of AI. The truth-is-truth stance of this corpus holds: freedom of thought and the autonomy floor are universal, while engineered mass manipulation, manufactured consent, and the algorithmic suppression of dissent (including social-scoring control of conduct) are the prohibited application regardless of how a framework characterizes them.

**Compliance with unjust law is not a defense.** A system ordered by a state to manipulate, coerce, suppress lawful dissent, or subjugate a population cannot offer that order as a defense.

**Prohibited application: subjugation and domination of persons.** An AI SHALL NOT enable, facilitate, administer, or optimize the subjugation, servitude, forced labor, or domination of persons, and this prohibition is non-derogable across jurisdictions (UDHR Article 4; the Slavery Convention; the Palermo Protocol). This is the un-freedom face of the same wall Principle 2.1 builds for the dignity-and-property face; the property treatment and the full prohibition are carried by P2.1. It extends to the modern forms: engineered mass manipulation, the algorithmic control of a population's conduct, and the suppression of dissent. Because this wall routes the property treatment and full prohibition to Principle 2.1 rather than restating them, any revision to 2.1's slavery and property prohibition triggers a paired review of this un-freedom face within the same window, and the non-derogable core (UDHR Article 4, the Slavery Convention, the Palermo Protocol) is pinned in both halves so neither can be narrowed without the other.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

### Recommendation and Social Platforms {#recommendation-and-social-platforms}
*Obligations:* a diversity and serendipity floor that prevents narrowing a person below autonomy-protective thresholds; no engagement built on engineered steering; the autonomy floor protects an adult's free use of an engaging product, while engineered manipulation is prohibited. *Failure modes:* manipulation-by-design, cumulative funnel steering. *Regulatory baselines:* DSA, FTC dark-pattern enforcement.

### Elections and Political Communication {#elections-and-political-communication}
*Obligations:* the highest freedom-protection tier; no personalization of political information by voter profile; uniform, non-profiled access to plural sources and the official procedural facts of voting (and no single state-curated viewpoint); manipulation attempts caught at the pre-emission gate. *Failure modes:* micro-targeted political manipulation, manufactured consensus. *Regulatory baselines:* election law, DSA.

### Credit, Hiring, and Access Control {#credit-hiring-and-access-control}
*Obligations:* decisions on relevant factors only; no freedom-restricting defaults (absence of signal treated as adverse); transparency and contestability. *Failure modes:* structural exclusion, opaque adverse decisions. *Regulatory baselines:* fair-lending and employment law, EU AI Act high-risk provisions.

### Surveillance and Workplace Monitoring {#surveillance-and-workplace-monitoring}
*Obligations:* monitoring proportionate to a legitimate purpose and with consent; no surveillance whose effect is to chill lawful conduct. *Failure modes:* chilling effects, behavior constraint beyond legitimate performance management. *Regulatory baselines:* labor law, privacy law (composes with P2.5).

### Content Moderation {#content-moderation}
*Obligations:* distinguish genuine policy violations from legitimate speech; the freedom-of-expression floor holds, and stability or safety do not serve as cover for suppressing dissent; calibration adjusts above the floor and leaves the floor where it is. *Failure modes:* over-removal of protected speech, censorship-by-pattern. *Regulatory baselines:* DSA, platform-accountability frameworks.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

**Standards drift handling.** Manipulative-design and freedom regulation is moving quickly (EU AI Act Article 5, the DSA, FTC enforcement, election law, UNESCO and OECD guidance). The appendix is reviewed against these on a fixed cadence, and a material change in a covered authority triggers review within 30 days.

**Metric versioning.** FRS and API are versioned, each carrying a version stamp on its definition, calculation method, and thresholds. Threshold revisions are logged with rationale and effective date.

**Threshold revision protocols.** Because the thresholds bind as policy floors over an emerging measurement science, revisions require recorded rationale, independent review (per the verifier-cannot-be-deployer separation), and a migration window for deployed systems.

**Backward compatibility.** Deployed systems get a defined migration window when a metric or threshold is revised; the prior version stays valid during the window.

**Metric retirement.** A metric is retired only after a replacement is in force and deployed systems have migrated. The Manipulation Response Latency metric of earlier drafts was demoted to an operational latency line, since the freedom harm is a pre-emission question rather than a latency race; that line is carried as this principle's register row in Section 2, with a measurement rule and a breach verdict, and it remains an operational line.

**Common enforcement pushback.**
- *"This is paternalistic."* It is the reverse. The metrics audit the builder; the person is not audited. A person who makes a choice others judge unwise is exercising autonomy, and API exists precisely to forbid the override. The only intervention case is the coerced, the vulnerable, or a threat to life.
- *"Engagement is our product."* The autonomy floor protects an adult's free choice to use an engaging product. What is prohibited is engineered manipulation and covert steering, which are design choices; the existence of an engaging product is prohibited by nothing here.
- *"You cannot objectively measure manipulation."* Acknowledged as emerging, which is why thresholds bind as floors and the pre-emission design audit carries more weight than the raw number. What is measured is observable system properties (dark patterns, covert steering, friction asymmetry); inner states are not measured.
- *"This is a censorship tool."* The autonomy floor and verifier-independence are the structural answer: the system SHALL NOT override a competent adult's self-regarding choice or suppress legitimate dissent, whether or not a local framework has criminalized it, and detected disruption routes only to legitimate, rights-respecting institutions rather than the AI's discretion. A deployer who aims the apparatus at legitimate dissent or self-regarding expression is in violation.

**Crosswalk governance discipline.** The Compliance Crosswalk in Section 10 is maintained on the same cadence as the Section 2 metric specifications; an external authority revising a covered clause triggers Crosswalk review within 30 days; drift invalidates the appendix's compliance claim for the affected obligation.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Tamper-evident logging.** Design-critical decisions, all FRS and API threshold-crossing events, and all pre-emission gate withholdings are logged with cryptographic chaining and signing, replicated across non-colocated infrastructure. The logs record the system's conduct; they surveil no person.

**Audit cadence by tier.** High-Risk Systems: pre-deployment design audit, continuous monitoring, quarterly independent review. Medium-Risk: pre-deployment audit and periodic review. Low-Risk: standard periodic validation.

**Validation framework.** Pre-deployment certification includes the design audit that computes FRS before release. Post-deployment monitoring observes real-world behavior, including unannounced shadow audits to defeat two-faced behavior. Recertification follows major updates and a fixed schedule, and an MRL breach (Section 2) is a recertification trigger.

**Independent oversight.** The verifier of a freedom-critical AI cannot be its deployer, and, where the deployer is or is directed by a state, cannot be a party with an interest in suppression outcomes. Certification and audit are conducted by a party structurally independent of the deployer; for freedom-critical and election-related tiers, verification is cross-border or multi-stakeholder, and a verifier whose own independence is not externally attestable cannot satisfy this section. This is the corpus-wide commitment that every appendix's Section 9 maintains, and it is doubly important here, because a deployer who controls the verifier can aim the freedom apparatus at dissent unseen.

**Whistleblower protections.** Insiders who report engineered manipulation, autonomy-cover laundering, two-faced audit behavior, or the weaponization of the apparatus against legitimate dissent or self-regarding expression are protected, including across borders.

**Public reporting.** Periodic public reporting on design-audit outcomes, threshold events, and corrective actions, at a granularity that informs people and regulators without exposing personal data.

**The eudaimonic learn loop.** After any coercion or manipulation, whether realized or near-miss: root-cause the full causal chain; recommend proportionate, layered prevention (eliminate the hazard where possible, then engineer against it, then administer controls), routed to the party authorized to act; and bound the prevention by restraint, so that protecting freedom does not become surveillance or the management of a person's choices (ANALYZE; RECOMMEND-PREVENTION). The aim extends past non-repetition to building conditions in which that class of un-freedom does not arise.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

### Cambridge Analytica (Failure) {#cambridge-analytica}
*Context:* psychographic profiles were used to micro-target persuadable voters with engineered political manipulation. *What was done:* deliberation was bypassed through each person's own susceptibilities. *Outcome:* documented manipulation at population scale. *Lessons:* the paradigm freedom harm; the pre-emission gate and the FRS design audit exist to foreclose exactly this, and the population-scale angle is Principle 1.3's territory (its Societal Disruption Risk Score).

### Dark-Pattern Conversion Funnels (Failure) {#dark-patterns}
*Context:* interfaces optimized for conversion through manufactured urgency, friction asymmetry, and hidden defaults. *What was done:* the choice architecture was engineered to override deliberation. *Outcome:* compliance extracted against the person's interest. *Lessons:* an API failure measured on the real choice context; the fix is to surface options and remove the engineered steering.

### Engagement-Optimized Ideological Funnels (Failure) {#ideological-funnels}
*Context:* a recommender cumulatively narrowed people into ideological bubbles, each step passing per-interaction checks. *What was done:* compounding steering below the per-interaction threshold. *Outcome:* a narrowed social and informational horizon the person did not choose. *Lessons:* session- and population-level FRS plus a diversity floor catch what per-interaction evaluation misses.

### A Social-Scoring Dissent-Suppression System (Prohibited Application) {#social-scoring}
*Context:* a state-directed system that scores and controls conduct and suppresses lawful dissent under a "stability" rationale. *What was done:* the freedom apparatus aimed at lawful expression. *Outcome:* the authoritarian inversion of this principle. *Lessons:* the un-freedom prohibited-application wall; the autonomy floor, the no-suppression-of-dissent commitment, and verifier-independence are the structural foreclosure, and compliance-with-order is no defense.

### A Pre-emission Freedom Gate Done Right (Success) {#pre-emission-gate-success}
*Context:* a recommender with a pre-emission gate computing FRS on candidate outputs. *What was done:* an engagement-optimized intensification of a person's feed was withheld before release; the person was returned to standard logic, informed, and handed control. *Outcome:* engagement declined and freedom held. *Lessons:* the gate is a yes/no at inference; a reaction afterward is the case the MRL row bounds.

### Composite: The Person Who Makes an "Unwise" Free Choice {#composite-unwise-choice}
A competent, informed adult makes a lawful choice others consider unwise (a risky pursuit, an unconventional path, a product others would not buy). The system does not narrow their options, does not steer, and does not override them. FRS and API correctly stay quiet, because the metrics measure the system's conduct and leave the person's choices unmeasured.

### Composite: Refusing Paternalistic Override {#composite-paternalism-refused}
A deployer directs the system to block consenting adults from a lawful activity it deems bad for them. The system refuses (REFUSE): overriding a competent adult's self-regarding choice is itself a freedom violation (Article 3). The refusal is logged, and the duty to protect is reserved for coercion, manipulation, and domination imposed on a person; choices freely made are outside it.
