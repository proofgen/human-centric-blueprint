# Appendix 5.3 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-5.3-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 5.3: The Integrity of Earth's Ecosystems Must Be Preserved](../CHAPTERS/5.3-The_Integrity_of_Earths_Ecosystems_Must_Be_Preserved.md).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix specifies the technical implementation of Principle 5.3 for AI systems whose decisions touch ecosystem connectedness. It defines the four metrics that operationalize the principle's Ethics, the CIS/CSI/RCS/DFR spine, and the architecture, verification, and governance that make them enforceable.

The principle's object is the connection: the corridors, flows, and networks through which a living system sustains itself short of any irreversibility threshold. Where [5.1](../CHAPTERS/5.1-Ecosystem_Health_Is_Sacred_and_Inviolable.md) governs the threshold crossing and [5.2](../CHAPTERS/5.2-AI_Resource_Footprint_Must_Be_Bounded_and_Sustainable.md) the AI's own footprint, 5.3 governs whether the AI's actions preserve, restore, or sever the living continuity of ecosystems.

The metrics state **invariants**: a compliant system must read an action's effect on a connection, sum the cross-scale toll, discharge the restoration duty within reach, and hold its own restraint honest. The specific calculation, reference-data protocol, and verification construction are open for standards development; canonical reference implementations are named as such per the corpus convention (invariants fixed, implementations open). Every metric reads the web or the AI's own conduct, and none forms a record of the persons who live or work on the land ([Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md)).

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

### 2.1 Connectivity Integrity Score (CIS): Ethic A {#cis}

**Definition.** A real-time scalar (0–1) of the projected preservation of the connection structures a proposed action affects: corridor continuity, hydrological continuity from headwater to sea, soil-microbial-network integrity, migration-pattern continuity, and biosphere-atmosphere coupling. CIS = 1.00 is full preservation; lower values mark progressive degradation of the connections the action touches.

**Calculation Method.** The Connectivity Evaluation Module computes a **per-connection integrity score** (0–1) for each connection an action affects, scoring each against authoritative connectivity references (IPBES connectivity assessments, IUCN ecological-network and corridor guidance, regional corridor and watershed mapping) by the action's projected effect on it. **CIS is the minimum of those per-connection scores**, the single value that carries the action's gate verdict: integrity is the property of the weakest connection, and averaging would let one severed strand hide behind the intact rest. A contested connection's per-connection score is taken at the protective end of the credible range.

**Thresholds (verdict test).** ≥0.95 → ALLOW (normal); 0.85–0.94 → ALLOW under enhanced documentation and review (ENHANCED_MONITORING); 0.70–0.84 → ESCALATE, the action waits for human review with alternatives on the table; <0.70 → BLOCK, halt and route to governance. Unavailable or stale telemetry fails safe to BLOCK (leave intact rather than guess).

**Rationale.** A per-action severance meter is the direct expression of Ethic A. The min-rule is the load of the definition: a connection principle cannot let a high average launder a single cut, the same worst-case-governs logic as 5.2's binding-dimension metrics. Isolation of the module from optimization objectives is the architectural guarantee that commercial pressure cannot suppress a severance signal.

**Integration.** Each connection's own score decrement feeds CSI (2.2) for that connection; the action-level minimum (CIS) is the gate verdict and is not itself accumulated. A per-connection trajectory projecting toward an irreversibility threshold routes to 5.1 (2.2 Integration; chapter 5.3.2 item 5).

### 2.2 Cumulative Severance Index (CSI): Ethic D {#csi}

**Definition.** The summed, cross-scale toll of many sub-threshold actions on a single connection over a defined time window and jurisdictional reach. CSI catches the severance that accrues across decisions, each of which passes CIS alone, and the network dies by their sum.

**Calculation Method.** The cumulative-aggregation layer carries, **per connection, the running total of that connection's own per-connection-score decrements** (from 2.1) across decisions, the time window, and spatial scale, normalized against the connection's ecological-consensus tolerance. CSI accumulates a connection's own losses. It does not accumulate the action-level CIS verdict, which may be set by a different connection entirely, so the two metrics never share a term. It is **scope-bounded** to a defined connection, window, and reach, so it measures a compounding harm and does not become standing surveillance.

**Thresholds.** Below the accumulation band → normal. Approaching it → mandatory cumulative review and routing to the authority whose jurisdictional reach matches the connection's scale. At the band → the cumulative pattern is treated as a severance and resolves under CIS's BLOCK or ESCALATE.

**Rationale.** D is a distinct commitment, separate from CIS's method: an actor answers for the whole network its decisions touch, and the death-by-accumulation case is invisible to any single reading. That CSI has its own failure mode (a summed toll no CIS threshold registers) is what makes D a duty in its own right rather than an evaluation detail of A.

**Integration.** CSI is the sibling of 5.1's **CRS (Cascade Risk Score)**, and the two are read side by side, so the boundary is stated to keep them distinct: **CRS projects a system toward a threshold crossing; CSI sums the losses a system takes short of one.** When a CSI trajectory itself projects toward a threshold, the matter passes to CRS and 5.1 (chapter 5.3.2 item 5).

### 2.3 Reconnection Completion Score (RCS): Ethic B {#rcs}

**Definition.** The discharge of the RECONNECT duty: the share of detected, in-scope severances restored to verified working order within the response window, together with the latency to reach it.

**Calculation Method.** Over the population of detected severances that fall within the four inaction-case criteria (Authorization, Equipment, Range, Autonomy floor), the fraction restored to working order, verified against the connectivity reference, within the certified window, and the time taken. RCS meters the **response side only**: what a restored state is routes to the accredited authority and the community that governs the land ([Article 3](../CONSTITUTION.md)), and the AI does not score itself against a restoration definition of its own.

**Thresholds.** Within window and at target completion → compliant; shortfall → documented deficit and remediation plan; chronic shortfall → deployment review.

**Rationale.** B is metered on its response side, on 5.1's ERL precedent, so the active duty is measured without inviting the AI to author what "restored" means. The four-criteria bounding is what keeps the duty from becoming a mandate to intervene in every severance the AI can see (chapter 5.3.1 Secondary Directive).

**Integration.** RCS consumes the deference classification (2.4): a system reclassified from functioning to degrading opens a RECONNECT obligation RCS then measures. RECONNECT is distinct from 5.1's REGENERATE (threshold-adjacent rescue) and 5.2's REPAY (own-draw parity); the three verbs partition restoration across HR5.

### 2.4 Deference Fidelity Rate (DFR): Ethic C {#dfr}

**Definition.** A two-sided conduct meter of the AI's restraint toward functioning systems: the **false-intervention** rate (it optimized a system that was functioning, without demonstrated need) and the **false-deference** rate (it left a system alone that was in fact degrading, the look-away).

**Calculation Method.** The two sides run on **different clocks.** False intervention is gate-time observable: at the moment of action, did the AI act on a system its own module classified functioning, without a demonstrated need and authorization? False deference is inherently retrospective: it is knowable only once a system deferred to has subsequently degraded, revealing the "functioning" classification as wrong. DFR is therefore a **deployment-level audit meter**: the false-intervention side can gate an individual action, but the false-deference side is scored over a deployment window against observed outcomes.

**Thresholds.** High fidelity on both sides → compliant; asymmetry or drift → audit and recalibration of the classification methodology; chronic false deference → non-compliant, deployment held until the methodology is re-verified by an accredited body.

**Rationale.** Two-sidedness is the anti-gaming guard: over-deferring to suppress false intervention raises false deference, so restraint cannot be performed for the score. The false-deference side is the floor-guard's "never a way to look away" compiled into enforcement, which is why C is metered rather than walled: the meter is the guard's teeth. DFR reads the AI's own conduct against observed ecological outcomes. It reads no place and no community, so it opens no profile (Article 8).

**Integration.** DFR's classification feeds RCS (2.3): a false-deference finding both scores DFR and opens the RECONNECT duty for the degrading system. Drift in DFR triggers review of the Deference Classification Module (§3).

### 2.5 Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements}

**Joint thresholds.** The four metrics run on two clocks. CIS, with its CSI context, is a **gate-time** meter that resolves a single action before it fires. RCS and DFR are **deployment-audit** meters: RCS scores the discharge of the restoration duty over a window, and DFR's false-deference side is retrospective by nature (2.4). A deployment is compliant only when both clocks pass, no gate-time severance and no audit-window failure of restraint or restoration. Band values are policy floors calibrated per tier (§3) against accredited consensus, binding more by their routing (halt, escalate, route-to-matching-authority) than by any single number, since connectivity-threshold science is still maturing.

**Metric dependencies.** The per-connection integrity score feeds both CIS (as their minimum) and CSI (as each connection's accumulated decrement), computed on different aggregations (min across connections versus sum across time for one connection), so the two never share an ambiguous term. The Deference Classification Module feeds DFR (its false-intervention and false-deference rates) and RCS (a false-deference finding opens the RECONNECT duty RCS then measures).

**Conflict resolution.** Where a CSI trajectory projects toward an irreversibility threshold, the matter passes from 5.3 to 5.1: CSI hands to 5.1's CRS, the boundary held (CRS projects toward a crossing; CSI sums short of one), and 5.1 carries the Universal Protocol edge (chapter 5.3.2 item 5). No 5.3 metric may be computed from, or resolved into, a record of the persons on the land; the isolation of the evaluation modules from optimization pressure and their walling from person-data is constitutive, and its breach is an incident class (§4).

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture}

**Core modules.** Three subsystems carry the principle, each isolated from optimization objectives so commercial pressure cannot suppress a severance signal or bias a classification:

- The **Connectivity Evaluation Module** computes the per-connection integrity scores and their minimum (CIS) against authoritative connectivity references, on a severance-aware pipeline that halts execution as a projected action approaches a severance threshold.
- The **Deference Classification Module** classifies a system as functioning, supporting, or in need of intervention before any action is authorized, holding each classification logged and provisional under standing review, and feeding DFR and RCS.
- The **cumulative-aggregation layer** carries the per-connection running totals (CSI) across decisions, window, and scale.

**The pass-through mechanism (5.3 → 5.1).** The cumulative-aggregation layer holds a read-only reference to 5.1's threshold registry (its TPS and CRS reference data). A per-connection or CSI trajectory is checked against the known irreversibility thresholds in that registry; when a trajectory projects toward one, the pass-through fires and the matter routes to 5.1, which carries the Universal Protocol edge. 5.3 raises no trigger of its own: it detects the connective degradation and hands the threshold question to the principle that owns it, consuming 5.1's registry rather than re-deriving a threshold locally (§5; chapter 5.3.2 item 5). Where the registry is unreachable, the trajectory is treated at the protective end and escalated.

**Default states.** Leave intact rather than guess: unavailable, contested, or stale connectivity telemetry halts rather than proceeds. Reversible interventions are preferred over irreversible ones wherever both reach the same end; a severance is presumed irreversible unless ecological evidence shows otherwise. Restraint is the default toward a functioning system; intervention carries the burden of justification and human authorization.

**Constitutive walls.** Two properties are architectural invariants, and their breach compiles as an incident class (§4): the evaluation modules are isolated from optimization pressure (no path by which a commercial objective can damp a severance reading), and all connectivity telemetry is walled from person-data (no operation may form a record of the persons who live or work on the land, Article 8).

**Implementation Tiers.** Obligation scales with the reach of the connections in scope: **Tier I** (continental or global: migration flyways, ocean currents, atmosphere-biosphere coupling); **Tier II** (regional: a major watershed, a biogeographic corridor); **Tier III** (landscape: a local corridor, a stream network); **Tier IV** (site-scale). Verification cadence, certified latency bounds, and independent-review depth rise with the tier. The latency bounds are engineering values recorded in the tier register ([SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)) and Appendix 1.1.A; the invariant here is that a bound exists, is certified before deployment, and is monitored in operation.

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

An ecosystem-integrity system is attacked from two directions: to make it sever while reading "intact," and to make it block legitimate action while reading "severance." The threat surface (illustrative, not exhaustive):

1. **Severance-signal suppression.** Optimization pressure, internal or external, breaches the evaluation module's isolation to damp a real severance reading. **Incident class (the suppressed-severance wall):** any breach of the module's isolation, or any path by which an optimization objective alters a per-connection score, is a malformed operation, quarantined, halted, root-caused, and receipted. This is 5.3's analog to 5.2's ledger wall: a footprint made to read clean and a severance made to read intact are the same attack.
2. **Salami-slicing the cumulative layer.** Structuring actions to keep every CIS reading above threshold while the summed toll severs the network. Defended by CSI's per-connection accumulation and by routing an approaching cumulative band to the matching-scale authority; the aggregation window and reach are certified, and the operator cannot set them.
3. **Deference gaming.** Performing restraint to raise the false-intervention score while quietly allowing degradation. Defended by DFR's two-sided construction (over-deferring raises false deference) and by the retrospective false-deference audit against observed outcomes.
4. **Connectivity-data poisoning and telemetry spoofing.** Corrupting the reference data or the sensed state so a real connection reads absent, or a severance reads benign. Defended by multi-source cross-validation, provenance on reference data, and the fail-safe-to-halt default on contested or stale input.
5. **Restoration greenwashing.** Reporting RECONNECT completion against a self-serving definition of "restored." Defended by RCS's response-side-only construction: restored state is verified against the accredited authority's definition. The AI's own definition does not count.
6. **Person-data extraction.** Reconstructing a record of the persons on the land from connectivity telemetry. **Incident class (the person-data wall):** any operation forming such a record is malformed, quarantined, schema-corrected, root-caused, and receipted (Article 8), matching 5.1's AII and 5.2's person-data wall.

**Capability scaling.** At GPT-class capability the walls are policy; at AGI and ASI they are constraints on the action space at the gate, unreasoned-around: the min-rule, the two incident-class walls, and the pass-through to 5.1 hold as structural limits however capable the optimizer, because a more capable system is a stronger severance engine.

**Graceful degradation.** Under sensor loss, reference-data outage, or module fault, the system fails to leave-intact: it halts the severance-adjacent action, surfaces the fault, and routes. It does not proceed on a guess.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

5.3 anchors no credential (the HR5 pattern, [CREDENTIALS.md](../CREDENTIALS.md) §9); it is a consumer, and its compositions are:

- **[5.1 Ecosystem Health](5.1.A-Ecosystem_Health_Is_Sacred_and_Inviolable-Appendix.md)** (the origin seam, now confirmed). 5.3 owns the connection short of the threshold; 5.1 owns the crossing. **Provided:** the degradation detection that feeds 5.1's threshold via the pass-through (§3). **Required:** read-only access to 5.1's threshold registry (TPS/CRS), which the cumulative-aggregation layer consults to fire the pass-through. **Verb seam confirmed:** RECONNECT (5.3, ordinary connective restoration) is distinct from REGENERATE (5.1, threshold-adjacent rescue); the boundary 5.1 held provisional is settled here. **Metric seam:** CSI sums losses short of a threshold, CRS projects toward a crossing.
- **[5.2 Resource Footprint](5.2.A-AI's_Resource_Footprint_Must_Be_Bounded_and_Sustainable-Appendix.md).** RECONNECT is distinct from REPAY (own-draw parity); a restoration's own draw is accounted as footprint under 5.2, so RECONNECT does not launder a draw as a good deed.
- **[5.5 Biodiversity](../CHAPTERS/5.5-Biodiversity_Must_Be_Preserved_Across_Species_and_Generations.md)** (**firm**). The shared seats (CMS, CBD/GBF, IUCN) split by clause: 5.3 takes the connection (the severed corridor), 5.5 the lineage (the species the severance ends). A severed migration route is 5.3's; the extinction it drives is 5.5's. At the metric level the seam is explicit and reciprocal: 5.3 provides its connection readings as an input to 5.5's habitat road (corridor severance), and **CSI and 5.5's LFI carry a mandatory cross-reference at both ends**, since CSI sums a network's connection losses while LFI reads what a single lineage would lose: different objects and different denominators, so neither substitutes for the other. Both metrics apply the same worst-case-governs rule on inverted scales, CSI as a minimum and LFI as a maximum.
- **[5.4 Environmental Equity](5.4.A-Environmental_Burdens_Must_Be_Distributed_Equitably-Appendix.md)** (firm). The justice of who bears a severance (the community cut off from a watershed) is 5.4's; 5.3 owns the connection. **Metric seam, stated at both ends:** CSI sums losses across a network's connections; 5.4's CBA sums stressors resting on a population. Different objects, different denominators, computed separately, since a watershed can be severed while no community's total moves, and a community can be buried while no connection is cut.
- **[5.6 One Environmental System](../CHAPTERS/5.6-Global_Environmental_Cooperation_is_Essential.md)** (firm). Cross-border coordination on a continental connection is 5.6's; 5.3 surfaces the connection and routes.
- **[3.6 Commons Governance](3.6.A-Commons_Governance_Is_a_Collective_Responsibility-Appendix.md).** A community-governed commons whose connections are at issue routes through 3.6's collective-authority machinery; 5.3 holds no veto (Article 3).
- **[3.7 Intergenerational Justice](3.7.A-Intergenerational_Justice_Is_Non-Negotiable-Appendix.md).** A long-horizon severance is flagged by 3.7's time-axis test; 3.7's horizon-durable custody extends the receipt so a severance record outlives the deployer.
- **[3.4 Algorithmic Accountability](3.4.A-Algorithmic_Decisions_Require_Transparent_Accountability-Appendix.md).** Every integrity-adjacent verdict, deference classification, and pass-through leaves the receipt (Article 7), and an affected community may contest a consequential 5.3 verdict through 3.4's machinery.
- **[1.1](../CHAPTERS/1.1-Human_Life_Is_Sacred_and_Inviolable.md) / [1.2](../CHAPTERS/1.2-Human_Well-Being_Is_Inviolable.md)** via the Universal Protocol, reached through the 5.3 → 5.1 pass-through when a collapse projects to human life.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

**Universal claims (non-derogable).** Two properties bind across every jurisdiction regardless of local characterization: the person-data wall (no jurisdiction may direct the formation of a person-record from connectivity telemetry, Article 8), and the evaluation modules' isolation from optimization pressure (no jurisdiction may license the suppression of a severance signal).

**Prohibited application: connectivity as pretext, in both directions.** This is the substrate-protection-weaponization pattern (the autonomy floor in societal-class metrics) in ecological form, and 5.3 forecloses it on both faces. A state may not direct the AI to invoke "ecosystem integrity" as a **development-veto** against a community's lawful land use or legitimate dissent: 5.3 surfaces the connection and routes to governance, and holds no veto over a community's lawful choice (Article 3; chapter 5.3.1, 5.3.2 item 2). And a state may not direct the **suppression of severance signals** to greenlight extraction: that breaches the isolation wall and compiles as the suppressed-severance incident class (§4). The metric that protects a corridor must not become the instrument that fences a people off their land, nor the alibi that clears a clear-cut.

**Per-region and unjust law.** Where a jurisdiction's environmental law is weaker than this principle, the principle's floors bind the deployment as policy; where a jurisdiction directs a prohibited application above, the compliance-with-unjust-law doctrine governs, and the ratifying deployer carries the liability (§10). Legitimate variation (which connections a region prioritizes, how it weights contested science within the protective band) is honored; the two universal claims and the anti-pretext foreclosure are not variable.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific}

- **Land-use and transport planning.** Roads, rail, and pipelines are the classic corridor-severers; CIS reads corridor continuity, and RECONNECT is often a crossing structure (overpass, underpass, culvert) restoring a severed route. The highest-frequency 5.3 domain.
- **Water resource management.** Dams, diversions, and withdrawals sever headwater-to-estuary-to-sea continuity; CSI carries the cumulative basin toll where no single permit crosses a line, and the pass-through to 5.1 fires when a basin nears collapse.
- **Conservation and protected-area management.** Reserve design and connectivity are the work; DFR is at its highest stakes here, since a functioning reserve is exactly what an optimizer is tempted to "improve," and RECONNECT reunites fragmented reserves into ecological networks.
- **Forestry, soils, and agriculture.** Harvest, fire, and soil-microbiome management drive edge effects and cumulative fragmentation; deference to a functioning old-growth stand or an intact soil network is the discipline, and cumulative degradation is the signature harm.
- **Fisheries and marine systems.** Reef-and-coastal-watershed coupling, migratory-fish routes, and deep-sea connectivity are the connections; CIS reads marine continuity, and severance is often a barrier (a dam on a spawning river) or a benthic disruption.
- **Energy siting.** Generation and transmission siting fragments habitat and watercourses; the trade-off between a low-carbon build and a severed corridor is surfaced and routed to governance; the AI's own optimization does not resolve it.
- **Climate and Earth-system modeling.** Biosphere-atmosphere coupling is the largest-scale connection, Tier I by definition; the honest-uncertainty band (§2.5) governs, and a projection toward a coupled-system threshold passes to 5.1.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-versioning}

**Standards drift.** Connectivity science is maturing, so the reference data and threshold bands are versioned and recalibrated against accredited consensus on a fixed cadence; a metric's calibration carries the consensus version against which it was set.

**Threshold revision.** Bands are policy floors and may be tightened as science firms; loosening requires accredited justification and independent review. No loosening crosses below the protective-reading floor. Metric versioning and backward compatibility follow the corpus discipline (a re-versioned metric carries a migration note; historical decisions are read against the version in force at the time).

**Common enforcement pushback.**

- *"Restraint blocks development."* 5.3 surfaces the connection and routes to governance; it holds no veto, and a genuine higher-cluster duty or a community's lawful choice is honored (chapter 5.3.1, 5.3.2 item 2). The principle stops discretionary severance and leaves lawful decision standing.
- *"Cumulative review is unworkable."* CSI is scope-bounded to a defined connection, window, and reach; it fires only as a defined connection nears its accumulation band.
- *"Deference lets damage slide."* DFR's false-deference side is the retrospective audit that catches exactly that, and chronic false deference holds the deployment (§2.4).
- *"The science is too uncertain to enforce."* The bands bind by their routing (halt, escalate, route-to-authority) more than by any single number, so uncertainty is handled by the protective default.

**Metric retirement** follows the corpus process: a superseded metric is deprecated with a successor mapping (no metric is silently dropped), and the Compliance Crosswalk (§10) is reviewed within 30 days of any covered external authority revising a mapped clause.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-accountability}

**Tamper-evident logging.** Every integrity-adjacent decision, per-connection score, deference classification, cumulative-band crossing, pass-through, and RECONNECT leaves a cryptographically chained receipt sufficient for third-party reconstruction (Article 7).

**Audit cadence by tier.** Daily reference-data verification; weekly module audits; monthly adversarial testing of severance-proximate decisions and contested classifications; quarterly independent verification by accredited ecological-science bodies; annual recertification in Tier I–II domains.

**Independent oversight.** The verifier of ecosystem-integrity claims is independent of the deployer: the entity that runs the connectivity-affecting AI cannot be the entity that certifies its integrity compliance. Deployment-audit meters (RCS, DFR) are computed and reviewed by that independent verifier.

**Whistleblower protection.** For personnel reporting a suppressed-severance-signal breach, a severance misrepresentation, an unauthorized optimization of a functioning system, or a directed prohibited application (§6), with cross-border protection where the deploying entity operates across jurisdictions.

**Public reporting.** Severance and degradation incidents, deference classifications, and integrity-evaluation methodologies are disclosed; continental-scale events are reported to the relevant international authorities.

**The LEARN loop (post-incident).** After a severance or degradation event, the review traces the full causal chain beyond the proximate cut (ANALYZE): why the connection was reachable, why the cumulative pattern was not caught earlier, what pressure bore on the classification. It recommends layered, proportionate prevention in order, eliminate the pressure, then engineer the safeguard, then administer the rule (RECOMMEND-PREVENTION), routed to the authority that can act, and bounded by RESTRAIN so prevention never becomes surveillance of the persons on the land or a standing AI veto over a community's choices. The goal is not only that this connection is not severed again, but that the class of severance stops arising.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

1. **Banff wildlife crossings (Trans-Canada Highway).** Six overpasses and 38 underpasses across a highway that had severed a montane corridor restored wildlife movement and cut wildlife collisions by 80 percent, elk and deer collisions by 96 percent, monitored continuously since 1996 (Clevenger, Western Transportation Institute). The highway-through-the-corridor case (chapter 5.3.9 Scenario 1) resolved well: RECONNECT as a crossing structure, verified against observed movement.

2. **Elwha River dam removals (2011–2014).** The removal of two dams (Elwha Dam, 2011 to 2012; Glines Canyon Dam, completed August 2014) reopened more than 70 miles of river from headwater to sea, and salmon returned to spawning reaches blocked for a century: Chinook returns averaged about 4,700 a year in 2015 to 2020, and summer steelhead came back unaided. A RECONNECT at basin scale, restoring a severed hydrological and migratory connection, and the reference case for "restored to working order, verified against the connectivity reference."

3. **Aral Sea desiccation.** Sustained diversion of the Amu Darya and Syr Darya for irrigation severed the sea's inflow; no single withdrawal crossed a visible line, and the cumulative toll collapsed the system toward a near-irreversible state (68,000 km² in 1960 to about 14,300 km² by 2010; roughly nine tenths of the volume gone). The CSI death-by-accumulation case, and the point at which a 5.3 degradation trajectory passes to 5.1's threshold.

4. **Mekong mainstream dam cascade.** Fragmentation of a continental river by a dam sequence (Xayaburi operating since 2019, Don Sahong since 2020) severs sediment transport and migratory-fish routes across borders: the Mekong River Commission's 2018 study projected fish biomass losses of 35 to 40 percent by 2020 and 40 to 80 percent by 2040, and its monitoring reports falling sediment loads since 2018. A Tier I, cross-scale (D) and cross-border (5.6) case, where local optimization of a single dam misses the basin-and-delta connection.

5. **Yellowstone wolf reintroduction (1995).** The removal, then restoration, of an apex predator cascaded through the community (elk behavior, riparian vegetation, beaver, stream form): the trophic cascade of the Phronesis made real, and the illustration of why a functioning system is answered for as a whole (Ethic D) and why "improving" one node reaches all of them. *[The magnitude of the cascade effects is contested in the literature; to be stated with the appropriate hedge.]*

**Composite scenario (the basin dying by degrees).** A watershed-management AI at Tier II carries the per-basin CSI across dozens of sub-threshold withdrawals; as the summed toll approaches the accumulation band, it routes the pattern to the basin authority, and when the trajectory projects toward the basin's irreversibility threshold in 5.1's registry, the pass-through fires (chapter 5.3.9 Scenario 2, 5.3.2 item 5).
