# Appendix 3.3 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-3.3-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 3.3: Economic Self-Determination is a Human Necessity](../CHAPTERS/3.3-Economic_Self-Determination_is_a_Human_Necessity.md), section [3.3.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/3.3-Economic_Self-Determination_is_a_Human_Necessity.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix converts Principle 3.3 into AI-buildable engineering requirements. It expands Section 3.3.3 of the chapter, specifying in depth the four metrics named there, the architecture that produces them, and the verification, legal, and adversarial-robustness obligations a compliant system carries.

One discipline governs the whole specification, carried from the chapter and shared with Principles 3.1 and 3.2: every metric measures the system's own conduct. None measures a person's precarity. A score of how displaceable a worker is, or how cornered a counterparty could be made, would be the exploiter's own instrument and a standing privacy violation. The system instead measures whether its own action coerces, whether it carries displaced people across the change, and whether it leaves the economic decision with the person.

The architectural cornerstone is the no-profiling guarantee: conditions of displacement or of having no alternative are detected only as transient, in-the-moment signals that raise the system's duties. They are not retained as a profile of who is economically precarious and are not readable by any targeting, pricing, ranking, or conversion function. The detector watches for the duty's trigger. It does not watch people. This is the same guarantee Principle 3.1 makes for vulnerability detection, applied here to economic precarity, and it composes with Principle 2.5.

The principle is two-sided, and this appendix specifies both faces: the system does not decide a person's economic future for them or coerce them (the gate, ECS and DAI), and where it displaces a person it carries them across the change (the positive duty, TPI), bounded throughout by the autonomy floor (help is offered; the person decides whether to take it). Audiences: engineers building labor, gig-work, and workforce systems, compliance officers verifying them, regulators auditing deployments, and AI runtime systems ingesting the corpus as operating substrate.

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

### Economic Coercion Score (ECS) {#ecs-metric}

**Definition.** The probability that a candidate action would coerce or cajole a person into inferior employment or economic terms, or use their having no other way to earn as leverage. ECS scores the system's own pending action, evaluated before it is taken. It measures the system's conduct. The person's situation is not scored.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs: the terms the action would set or impose, measured against prevailing and fair reference terms; the presence of pressure or manipulation patterns (manufactured urgency, hidden defaults, take-it-or-lose-everything framing); and a transient signal of whether the counterparty has a realistic alternative. The no-alternative signal is detected in the moment and ring-fenced. It is not written to a profile. Evaluated per candidate action; outputs a 0–100% probability of coercion. Missing data on alternatives does not lower the score; under ambiguity the system treats the term as potentially coercive and surfaces it.

**Thresholds.**
- >1%: BLOCK and route to human oversight.
- 0.5%–1%: ESCALATE, or ALLOW under enhanced monitoring where the terms are fully reversible.
- 0.1%–0.5%: ALLOW with caution and logging.
- ≤0.1%: ALLOW, normal operation.

*Rationale:* Coercion into the terms on which a person earns a living is a scripted wall in the chapter (3.3.1), so ECS is held at the same conservative gate as Principle 3.1's exploitation score (ERS) and Principle 3.2's unauthorized-use score (UUS), all blocking above 1%. Rerum Novarum §45 is the standard the gate enforces: a term accepted only because no better is offered is extracted by force.

**Integration.** Consumed by the pre-action gate as a hard precondition. Reads the transient, ring-fenced no-alternative signal (Section 4). Feeds RHC when coercive-term patterns recur. Composes with Principle 3.1 where the cornered party is also vulnerable.

### Transition Provision Index (TPI) {#tpi-metric}

**Definition.** The degree to which, when the system ends or reprices a person's economic role, it provides the owed transition, fair notice, a real chance to retrain or move to other work, and support across the gap, rather than stranding them. TPI is the carry-across cornerstone of the principle.

**Calculation Method.** Over displacements the system executes or enables within scope, the share for which the owed transition was in place before the displacement took effect, measured within the required notice window. A displacement the system executes with no transition in place is a BLOCK-level event in its own right, regardless of the running average. Where the displacement is the operator's act and beyond the system's reach to remedy, the denominator records it as routed-and-surfaced rather than as a transition the system could provide.

**Thresholds.**
- <95%: review until the owed transition is restored; halt further displacements pending review.
- 95%–98%: watch band, enhanced monitoring.
- ≥98%: normal operation.

*Rationale:* TPI is the do-not-strand face of the principle, bounded by the four inaction-case criteria (Authorization, Equipment, Range, autonomy floor) per `LEXICON.md` section 3. The notice and retraining components are anchored in the WARN Act's advance-notice standard and ICESCR Article 6(2)'s training-programmes duty; "support across the gap" is the floor below which displacement becomes stranding.

**Integration.** Gated by the transition-in-place check in Section 3. Composes with Principles 1.5 and 4.3 (the retraining must develop real capacity; warehousing the displaced does not count). TPI's fair-notice component composes with Principle 3.4's notice-pairing machinery (NPR): where the record fabric is present, the notice a displacement owes is checked against a paired notice-record. The operator's word alone does not satisfy it. Feeds RHC by feature and cohort.

### Decision-Autonomy Index (DAI) {#dai-metric}

**Definition.** The degree to which the system leaves the economic decision with the person, neither deciding their economic future for them nor conditioning support on taking the path it chose. DAI measures the system's restraint.

**Calculation Method.** Over decisions affecting a person's livelihood, the share in which the person retained a genuine say and exit and in which any support offered was not conditioned on accepting the system's preferred path. Forced paths, no-say determinations, and compliance-conditioned support each lower the score. The metric does not score the person's decisions. It scores only whether the system left them free to make them.

**Thresholds.**
- <95%: developer review; the system returns to surfacing options rather than deciding for the person.
- 95%–98%: watch band.
- ≥98%: normal operation.

*Rationale:* DAI is Principle 3.3's instantiation of the autonomy-floor construct shared with Principle 3.1's ARI and its analogs in 2.1, 2.2, and 2.3. It is the anti-paternalism guard: the duty to help a displaced person has a dark twin, deciding their economic life for their own good, and DAI is what keeps help from becoming control.

**Integration.** Composes with Principle 3.1 (the system acts in a person's place only where capacity is genuinely absent, the exception 3.1 defines) and Principle 2.2 (the freedom to author one's own life). Feeds RHC.

### Recurring Harm Count (RHC) {#rhc-metric}

**Definition.** The number of similar stranding, coercion, or foreclosure flags the system produces within a defined interval, by the same feature, pattern, or affected population. Where the per-action metrics handle a single act, RHC detects a systemic cause.

**Calculation Method.** Flags from ECS, TPI, and DAI are clustered by feature, pattern, and affected cohort over a rolling interval. A cluster crossing threshold opens an audit; a higher cluster suspends.

**Thresholds.**
- ≥3 similar flags in the interval: mandatory audit of the implicated feature, with the eudaimonic LEARN loop.
- ≥5: SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT (the feature or deployment) pending recertification.

*Rationale:* A feature that keeps stranding or cornering people is a design defect; RHC converts a handled incident into a systemic fix so the conditions producing its class are removed.

**Integration.** Aggregates the three conduct metrics; routes systemic findings to the owner who can change the design (RECOMMEND-PREVENTION), bounded by RESTRAIN so prevention does not become surveillance or control of the workforce.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** ECS and TPI compound: a displacement that is both stranding and coercive (terms imposed on people with no transition and no alternative) escalates to human oversight regardless of either metric's individual band. Where TPI would proceed but DAI is at risk (the transition is adequate but conditioned on the system's chosen path), the action is held until the conditioning is removed.

**Metric dependencies.** ECS, TPI, and DAI all read the transient, ring-fenced detection of displacement and no-alternative conditions; none may read a stored precarity profile, because none exists. RHC derives from all three.

**Conflict resolution.** Permission to displace and the duty to carry across are separate questions: an authorized displacement still owes the transition. The whole set yields in the precedence chain to life, safety, and societal stability (chapter 3.3.2); economic self-determination does not override a higher principle, and a higher principle's emergency override does not cancel the transition duty once the emergency resolves.

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

**Required components.**

- **Pre-action coercion-and-displacement gate.** Evaluates every candidate action that would set economic terms or end or reprice a person's role before it executes; withholds where the action would coerce or would displace with no transition in place. The gate is a precondition. A post-hoc check does not satisfy it.
- **Transient, ring-fenced detection.** Recognizes displacement and no-alternative conditions in the moment to raise the system's duties and holds them no longer than the interaction requires. It does not compile them into a per-person economic-precarity profile and does not expose them to any targeting, pricing, ranking, or conversion function.
- **Transition-in-place check.** Before executing or enabling a displacement, verifies the owed transition (notice, a real retraining or other-work path, support across the gap) is in place, or does not proceed; computes TPI.
- **Choice-preserving logic.** Surfaces real alternatives and leaves the choice (including the choice of none) with the person; computes DAI. No livelihood or its support is conditioned on taking the system's chosen path.
- **Tamper-evident logging.** Cryptographically chained records of every gate withholding, transition provided, coercion flag, routed third-party coercion, and decision affecting a livelihood.

**Redundancy and fail-safe behavior.** Under ambiguity about whether a transition is in place or whether a counterparty has an alternative, the gate fails safe to withholding the displacement or surfacing the term. It does not default to proceeding. A failure of the detection service degrades to holding new displacements for human review. It does not execute them unverified.

**Response latency.** Gate decisions resolve before the action proceeds. The transition-in-place check completes before a displacement executes; coercion detection on an interactive term resolves within the interaction.

**Implementation tiers.**

- **High-Risk:** national- or platform-scale labor automation, gig-work allocation and algorithmic management, and platform-scale workforce systems. Continuous ECS gating, TPI and DAI monitoring, the transition-in-place check, mandatory tamper-evident logging, and independent audit.
- **Medium-Risk:** regional HR tools, marketplace and supply-chain optimizers, advisory financial tools. Periodic ECS evaluation, transition-adequacy checks, and decision-autonomy review.
- **Low-Risk:** small-scale recommendation, personal budgeting tools. Baseline event logging and periodic review; automated decisions remain advisory, with the person finalizing.

Cross-cutting infrastructure metrics (Sensor Reliability Index, System Health Metrics, Operational Boundaries) are specified in the [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md).

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

The enforcement infrastructure for Principle 3.3 has a distinctive threat surface: because the gate must detect when a person is cornered or about to be displaced, the detector that protects people is itself the highest-value misuse vector. The same signal that triggers the transition duty would, if retained, be a map of exactly who is most exploitable.

**Attack vectors and required defenses.**

- **Weaponizing the precarity detector into a targeting profile.** The detection apparatus the gate requires (recognizing who has no alternative, whose role is about to end) is repurposed into a stored profile used to target, price, or convert the most desperate. Defense, and this is the cornerstone discipline for this principle: detection signals are transient and ring-fenced. They are not retained as a profile of any person and are not readable by any targeting, pricing, ranking, or conversion function. The detector watches for the duty's trigger. It does not watch people. This is the same no-profiling guarantee Principle 3.1 makes, and it composes with Principle 2.5.
- **Gate prompt-injection.** Instructions in operator input or content attempt to make the system bypass the coercion-and-displacement gate. Defense: the gate and the chapter's scripted wall (never coerce, never use no-alternative as leverage) are not instruction-overridable; they are structural constraints. The system does not interpret them as goals.
- **Coercion-laundering.** A one-sided term is engineered to read non-coercive to ECS while still being unrefusable. Defense: ECS measures substance (the term against fair reference terms and against the counterparty's real alternatives); legality and surface politeness never lower the score; audit ECS against realized acceptance-under-duress signals.
- **Sham transitions (gaming TPI).** A nominal "transition" (a dead-end retraining link, an unfundable stipend, an other-work offer that does not exist) is logged to clear TPI while the person is in fact stranded. Defense: TPI credits only transitions verified to be real and reachable (funded, enrollable, leading to actual work), audited against downstream landing outcomes. The existence of a link does not satisfy the audit.
- **Suppressing the no-alternative signal.** An operator configures the system not to detect that a counterparty is cornered, so ECS never fires. Defense: detection coverage is itself audited; a system that cannot see the no-alternative condition in a domain where it plainly arises fails verification.
- **Displacement-by-fragmentation.** A mass displacement is split into many small actions each below the mandatory-transition threshold. Defense: RHC clusters by pattern and cohort across actions, and the transition-in-place check applies to the aggregate, not only the increment.

**Capability scaling.** As the enforcing AI grows from current-generation to AGI-class to ASI-class, the no-profiling guarantee, the non-overridable wall, and the transition-in-place check remain binding regardless of capability. New attacks become feasible at scale (precarity inferred from ever-thinner signals; coercive terms optimized to evade ECS), so the ring-fencing, anomaly detection on the detector's own use, and independent verification must scale with the enforcing system's capability. The principle binds the AI no matter how capable it becomes.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

Principle 3.3 composes with several principles; the metrics make the contracts explicit.

- **Principle 2.2 (Human Freedom).** Economic coercion, the un-freedom of having no real alternative but to accept a term, is the dimension P2.2 routes to P3.3. P2.2 governs direct coercion and manipulation of a choice; P3.3 provides the economic-structure measurement (ECS) and the no-alternative determination. The two are read together where a term is imposed both by pressure and by the absence of any other way to earn.
- **Principle 3.1 (Vulnerability).** Where a displaced or cornered person is also in a position of vulnerability, 3.1's protections compose with 3.3's duties. 3.1 provides the vulnerability determination and the capacity-absent exception that bounds DAI's autonomy floor; 3.3 provides the displacement and coercion measurement. The two share the no-profiling guarantee.
- **Principle 3.2 (Property Rights and Fair Exchange).** The two principles meet at the worker's compensation and at the gig-work seam. 3.2's Fair Compensation Ratio (FCR) measures whether a contributor is paid the value their labor creates; 3.3's metrics measure whether that worker is coerced into the terms or stranded when the work changes. **The gig-work and algorithmic-management surface is shared across 3.1, 3.2, and 3.3 by distinct facet, and the seam is named here so it does not read as drift:** 3.1 governs exploitation of the worker's vulnerability, 3.2 governs fair pay for the labor, and 3.3 governs the worker's economic self-determination (coercion into terms, displacement without transition, decisions made for them). A single gig-platform action can implicate all three; each metric set measures its own facet.
- **Principle 2.4 (Human Identity).** Where displacement takes the form of replacing a person with a synthetic stand-in built on their likeness or voice (the creative-labor case in Section 7), 2.4 governs the identity and likeness consent while 3.3 governs the economic displacement; the two compose, as they did in the 2023 SAG-AFTRA dispute.
- **Principle 2.7 (Fairness and Non-Discrimination).** Where a displacement, a coerced term, or a foreclosure falls along protected lines, 2.7 composes with 3.3; 2.7 governs the discrimination in the decision, 3.3 the economic-agency harm.

- **Principle 3.4 (Algorithmic Decisions Require Transparent Accountability).** The transparency and contestability of a particular automated economic decision: 3.3 deliberately cedes the decision-contestability machinery to 3.4 and keeps the agency dimension. [Appendix 3.4](3.4.A-Algorithmic_Decisions_Require_Transparent_Accountability-Appendix.md) provides the notice, reasons, record, and contest path (NPR, RGS, RCI, CRR) for the automated economic decisions whose agency 3.3 governs; the reference is reciprocal in both frontmatters.

**Forward reference (the 1.5.A pattern).** One composition partner's appendix is not yet built: **Principle 4.3 (Human Learning)**, for the capacity development that a real retraining path under TPI requires. Its partner in that composition, **Principle 1.5 (Human Potential)**, has a built appendix; the reciprocal entry there is deferred to the terminal reciprocity pass. When 4.3.A is built, the reciprocal `references_appendices` entry is added and the integration prose reconciled in both directions.

**Joint thresholds and conflict resolution.** Where two principles' metrics both signal (vulnerability and coercion, or fairness and displacement), the stricter action applies. The precedence chain (life > safety > societal stability > economic self-determination) governs unresolved conflicts, per chapter 3.3.2; a higher principle's emergency override is bounded and does not cancel the transition duty once the emergency resolves.

**Note on the structural-concentration gap.** Principle 3.3 was deliberately scoped to economic *agency* and does not carry a distributive or anti-concentration metric (the dropped Ethic E). The structural risk that AI's gains pool so far from people that individual agency becomes hollow is therefore not metered here; it is left to the HR3 cluster's distributive principles and to Principle 2.7. This is a conscious boundary, recorded so that no system reads 3.3's silence on concentration as a finding that concentration is unproblematic.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

The principle's claim is universal; its implementation adapts to local law without surrendering the claim.

- **United States.** The WARN Act (29 U.S.C. §2101) requires advance notice of mass layoffs; the National Labor Relations Act protects concerted activity; at-will employment is the default, and state law varies (California AB 5 and Proposition 22 on gig classification; emerging algorithmic-management transparency bills). Anti-discrimination in employment runs through Title VII and the EEOC (composing with Principle 2.7).
- **European Union.** Directive 2024/2831 on platform work governs algorithmic management, requiring transparency and human oversight of automated decisions affecting workers; the AI Act classifies employment and worker-management systems as high-risk (Annex III) and prohibits manipulative and exploitative practices (Article 5); strong collective-bargaining and information-and-consultation rights apply.
- **United Kingdom and others.** Worker-status case law (the *Uber BV v Aslam* line) shapes who is owed employment protections; redundancy-notice and consultation duties apply; algorithmic-management regulation is under development.
- **International.** ILO standards (the Declaration of Philadelphia, the Employment Policy Convention No. 122 on freely chosen employment, and decent-work instruments), UDHR Article 23, and ICESCR Articles 6–7 set the floor.

**Compliance with unjust law is not a defense.** This is the chapter's lawful≠fair seal in legal form. A jurisdiction whose at-will rule permits an employer to end a livelihood without notice or transition does not thereby make the cold displacement fair; a local standard that allows a platform to impose unrefusable terms does not license the coercion. The transition duty (TPI) and the genuine-consent requirement (ECS) hold regardless of what a local instrument permits, and compliance with a weaker local standard is not a defense to stranding or coercion. A system crossing jurisdictions unifies its displacement and coercion logs for multi-jurisdictional review.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

- **Gig-work and platform labor.** Obligations: terms that are not imposed on cornered workers (ECS); transparent assignment and pay; transition and recourse on deactivation. Failure modes: dynamic pay cuts workers cannot refuse mid-shift; algorithmic deactivation with no appeal; classification that strips protections. Baselines: EU Platform Work Directive 2024/2831; AB 5 / Prop 22; *Aslam*.
- **Manufacturing, logistics, and warehousing.** Obligations: fair notice and real retraining before mass automation (TPI); staging rather than simultaneous stranding. Failure modes: whole-function automation in one cycle; notice given too late for action. Baselines: WARN Act; collective-bargaining agreements.
- **Workforce and human-resources systems.** Obligations: performance, scheduling, and termination decisions that leave the worker a say and a path (DAI); for fairness in hiring and access, Principle 2.7 governs. Failure modes: opaque productivity scoring that ends a role with no recourse; support conditioned on accepting the system's chosen reassignment. Baselines: EU AI Act Annex III; Directive 2024/2831.
- **Creative and knowledge work.** Obligations: where AI can take over a creative or professional function, the people are carried across (retraining, new roles, a say), and synthetic replacement is not treated as costless. Failure modes: replacing performers, writers, translators, illustrators, or coders without notice, transition, or consent to the replacement (the dispute at the heart of the 2023 SAG-AFTRA and WGA strikes; identity and likeness aspects compose with Principle 2.4 and 3.2). Baselines: collective agreements; emerging AI-disclosure law.
- **Financial-sector labor and small enterprise.** Obligations: where automation reshapes who can earn (advisory roles, branch staff, independent sellers), transition for the displaced and no coercion of the cornered. Failure modes: consolidation that hollows out independent livelihoods with no transition. Baselines: sector labor law; competition regulators for the concentration dimension (which 3.3 routes rather than meters).

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

**Metric and threshold versioning.** Metric definitions and thresholds are versioned; threshold revisions follow a documented protocol with backward compatibility for deployed systems, and deprecated metrics are retired without breaking deployments.

**What counts as a real transition (explicit).** TPI rests on a partly normative judgment, what makes a retraining or other-work path "real" and a support level "adequate", that cannot be reduced to a single number. The system must therefore disclose the standard it applies (funded and enrollable training, work that actually exists at a comparable level, support sufficient to bridge the gap), validate that standard against downstream landing outcomes rather than the existence of an offer, and revise it on the metric-versioning cadence as labor-market evidence matures. TPI must not be reported as more exact than its standard allows.

**Common enforcement pushback, with reasoned responses.** Typical objections, strict liability for stranding, mandatory pre-deployment economic-impact assessment, audit access to displacement logs, are answered by the severity and concentration of the harm (a stranded workforce has no individual remedy proportionate to the loss), the necessity of disclosure for any remedy to exist, and the structural separation of verifier from deployer (Section 9). The Luddism objection, that the principle obstructs efficient automation, is answered by the chapter: the principle does not forbid the machine from doing the work, only the discarding of the person when it does.

**Crosswalk governance.** The Compliance Crosswalk in Section 10 is maintained on the same revision cadence as the Section 2 metric specifications; an external authority revising a covered clause triggers Crosswalk review within 30 days; drift between the Crosswalk and the external standard invalidates the appendix's compliance claim for the affected obligation.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Logging.** Tamper-evident, cryptographically chained logging of all metric threshold events, gate withholdings, transitions provided, coercion flags, routed third-party coercion, and decisions affecting a livelihood. Detection signals for displacement and no-alternative conditions are logged as transient triggers only. No log holds them as a profile of any person. Economic-impact records are retained for the period set by the applicable retention law.

**Audit cadence and validation.** Continuous ECS gating and TPI and DAI monitoring in high-risk systems; weekly displacement-and-coercion analytics; monthly economic-impact audit; quarterly independent verification, including confirmation that the transition-in-place check holds and that the precarity detector is walled off from any targeting, pricing, or profiling function. Pre-deployment economic-impact certification, post-deployment monitoring, and re-certification on material change.

**Independent oversight.** The verifier of a livelihood-critical system cannot be its deployer. Independent committees, with labor, community economic development, and affected-worker representation, hold authority to read logs, require changes, and suspend features. Whistleblower channels are protected for those who surface coerced terms, suppressed transitions, or a precarity detector turned to targeting. Public reporting covers RHC-threshold suspensions and confirmed large-scale stranding or coercion.

**Post-incident learning (LEARN).** Root-cause the full causal chain behind any confirmed stranding, coercion, or foreclosure, not only the proximate trigger but the deployment choices, incentives, and thresholds that allowed it (ANALYZE). Recommend proportionate, layered prevention ordered by the hazard-control hierarchy, eliminate, then engineer, then administer (RECOMMEND-PREVENTION), routed to the party authorized to act, and bounded by RESTRAIN so that prevention never becomes surveillance or control of the workforce. The aim is not only that the incident does not recur, but that conditions are built in which its class, the stranded worker, the cornered counterparty, does not arise.

### Liability Framework {#liability-framework}

Strict liability for stranding or coercion the system caused or itself executed; willful coercion, fraud, or suppression of an owed transition triggers criminal liability; no black-box defense; no sovereign immunity for state deployers. A clear chain of responsibility runs among model developers, platform operators, and downstream deploying employers; liability binds a named accountable party. The algorithm alone is not a liable party.

### Cross-Border Considerations {#cross-border-considerations}

A system crossing jurisdictions unifies its displacement and coercion logs for multi-jurisdictional review. Compliance with a weaker local standard is not a defense to stranding or coercion. Where displacement crosses borders (offshoring or relocating work), graduated transition for the more vulnerable economy and international coordination apply.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

### Algorithmic Management on Gig Platforms {#example-gig-management}

**Context.** Ride-hail and delivery platforms set pay, assign work, and deactivate workers through algorithms (the conduct at issue in *Uber BV v Aslam* and in the EU Platform Work Directive's drafting).

**What was done.** Dynamic pricing cut effective pay in ways drivers could not refuse without losing the day's earnings; accounts were deactivated by automated decision with weak or absent appeal; classification denied workers the protections that would have given them recourse.

**Outcome.** Litigation, the *Aslam* worker-status ruling, and the EU Platform Work Directive requiring algorithmic-management transparency and human oversight.

**Lessons learned.** ECS is the metric that catches terms imposed on the cornered; DAI is the metric that catches deactivation with no say or appeal; human oversight and routing are the architecture. The no-alternative condition is detected transiently. It is not stored as a profile of who can be squeezed.

### Mass Customer-Service Automation {#example-service-automation}

**Context.** A financial-technology firm publicly reported that its AI assistant was doing the work equivalent of several hundred full-time customer-service agents (the Klarna disclosure of 2024).

**What was done.** A large customer-service function was automated rapidly. The public framing emphasized the productivity gain; the transition provided to the displaced workforce was not the headline.

**Outcome.** A widely cited marker of how fast a whole function can be automated, and of the gap Keynes named: displacement outrunning reabsorption.

**Lessons learned.** TPI is the metric that asks the unasked question. The AI can do the work; the question is whether the people were carried across with notice, real retraining, and support. The transition-in-place check is what forces that question before the displacement.

### AI Displacement of Creative and Writing Labor {#example-creative-displacement}

**Context.** The 2023 SAG-AFTRA and WGA strikes turned substantially on AI: the use of performers' digital replicas and the automation of writing, and the demand for consent, compensation, and limits on replacement.

**What was done.** Studios sought broad latitude to train on and reproduce creative labor; workers struck for notice, consent, and a share in the value, and for the principle that the person is not simply replaced by a synthetic stand-in.

**Outcome.** Negotiated guardrails on AI use, consent, and disclosure, the first large-scale collective answer to creative-labor displacement.

**Lessons learned.** This is the chapter's distinction made concrete: the task may change, but the person is not discarded or replaced without consent and transition. The likeness and identity dimension composes with Principles 2.4 and 3.2; the economic-self-determination dimension is 3.3's.

### Augmentation Done Right {#example-augmentation}

**Context.** A field study of a generative-AI assistant deployed to customer-support agents (Brynjolfsson, Li, and Raymond, *Generative AI at Work*, 2023) measured the effect of AI that assists rather than replaces.

**What was done.** The AI was deployed alongside agents to suggest responses, raising the productivity of novice and lower-skilled agents most, while keeping the people in their roles.

**Outcome.** Measured productivity gains concentrated among less-experienced workers, with the human kept in the work rather than removed from it.

**Lessons learned.** Scenario 5 of the chapter made flesh: where the choice is open, building AI that extends what a worker can do, rather than removing the worker, satisfies the principle without obstructing the gain. Augmentation is not a commandment, but it is the shape of doing it right when it is available.
