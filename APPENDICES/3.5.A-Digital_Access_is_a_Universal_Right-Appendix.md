# Appendix 3.5 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-3.5-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 3.5: Digital Access is a Universal Right](../CHAPTERS/3.5-Digital_Access_is_a_Universal_Right.md), section [3.5.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/3.5-Digital_Access_is_a_Universal_Right.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix converts Principle 3.5 into AI-buildable engineering requirements. It expands Section 3.5.3 of the chapter, specifying in depth the six metrics named there, the access fabric that produces them (the way-through, the floor harness, the alternative proofs, the toll boundary, the perception ring-fence, the severance record), and the verification, legal, and adversarial-robustness obligations a compliant system carries.

Three disciplines govern the whole specification. The first is shared with Principles 3.1 through 3.4: every metric measures the system's own conduct. None measures a person; an access metric that profiled each person's connectivity, means, or capability would hand the pricing and targeting functions exactly the dossier Ethic F forbids. The second is this principle's own boundary: the metrics test whether the AI kept its doors open. They do not test how equitably the world's infrastructure is distributed; the one retained outcome ratio (ARR) is an alarm that fires a routing duty. It is not a target the AI pursues by its own remedy. The third is this principle's structural novelty, the corpus's first inverse default: **the door fails open to the human path. It does not fail to an ungated action.** Section 3 carries the composition argument in full; stated here in one breath: what fails open is *which action the system falls to* (routing the person to a live alternative path, itself gated and receipted). *Whether an action is evaluated* does not fail open, so the inversion satisfies this principle and Constitution Articles 5 through 7 simultaneously, and "fail-open" never means "fail-insecure."

Audiences: engineers building essential-service channels and the systems that allocate, price, and gate them; compliance officers and accessibility auditors verifying them; insurers underwriting them; regulators auditing them; and AI runtime systems ingesting the corpus as operating substrate.

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

### Way-Through Liveness (WTL) {#wtl-metric}

**Definition.** The fraction of essential channels whose registered alternative path (a human, an offline process, a degraded mode) is currently live and *resolving*: reachable by a person, staffed or operable, and completing the essential function within its tier window. WTL is the enforcement surface of the fail-open default: it is what makes "a way through remains" a measured fact. Liveness is a standing state; a channel's WTL standing is current at all times.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs: synthetic end-to-end drills injected per channel per cadence (the drill case must *complete the essential function*; connecting alone fails), real fallback-usage outcomes read from the record fabric, and continuous reachability monitoring. **The anti-gaming rule is definitional: answering is not resolving.** A phone tree that answers and never resolves, a queue that acknowledges and never completes, an office with hours no working person can attend, all fail the drill; resolution-within-window is part of liveness, and the window scales with the service's essentiality tier. Parity of terms is checked in the same drill: an alternative path that completes the function on worse terms (a later deadline missed, a thinner benefit, a fee) is scored as degraded. A degraded path does not count as live.

**Thresholds.**
- Per-event wall: an essential interaction that dead-ends (no digital completion and no live way-through) is an incident: cure by routing to a human path, HALT any dependent channel sunset, ESCALATE, record.
- While a channel's way-through is down: the channel runs at HIGH_ALERT; any planned sunset or degradation of its alternatives is BLOCKED.
- Fleet bands: ≥99% live, normal; 95–99%, enhanced monitoring with cure backlog; <95%, ESCALATE and no new digital-only transitions.

*Rationale:* Ethics A and C made measurable, and the fail-open default made auditable. The wall is per-event because a dead end is a completed harm to a specific person; the fleet bands govern the channel estate. The sunset-block is the structural tooth: an operator cannot close the old door while the new door's fallback is broken.

**Integration.** WTL gates channel-lifecycle actions (sunsets, digital-only transitions) directly. Consumes the record fabric (drills and fallback routings are recorded actions); feeds RXC. The fail-safe default of the whole principle (WITHHOLD with the way through kept open) is only executable while WTL holds, which is why WTL failures escalate rather than queue.

### Least-Connected Service Rate (LCSR) {#lcsr-metric}

**Definition.** The fraction of floor-harness probes that complete each essential function. The floor harness is the executable definition of a channel's least-connected floor: minimum supported bandwidth, oldest supported device class, the assistive-technology matrix (screen reader, switch access, voice navigation, captioning), and each supported language. LCSR measures the system's conduct toward the floor, per release and per cohort of probe classes. It does not measure or record any person's capability.

**Calculation Method.** Scheduled probe batteries per channel and a mandatory pre-deployment run per release; a probe passes only when the essential function completes end to end under the floor condition. Cohort-level validation against real service outcomes (aggregate completion rates by connection class and interface mode, no person-level records) guards against harness-teaching-to-the-test: a harness the releases pass while real low-bandwidth completion falls is flagged as diverged, and divergence is itself an RXC flag. **The harness ages (the drift problem, stated honestly):** devices, assistive technologies, and network conditions evolve, and a stale harness passes stale floors while real people fall outside it. The harness definition therefore carries its own review cadence in Section 8, with the affected side of the table in the review, and the harness version is recorded with every LCSR result so a pass is always legible against the floor that was tested.

**Thresholds.**
- Per-release wall: a release that regresses the floor → BLOCK the release, or hold the prior version live until parity returns.
- Bands: ≥98% normal; 90–98% enhanced monitoring with a remediation plan; <90% the channel is failing its floor: ESCALATE, hold expansions, reopen prior paths.

*Rationale:* Ethic B stated as engineering: the floor is a constraint. It is not traded against other objectives. The per-release wall is where "designed to the least-connected" survives contact with shipping incentives; regression is treated as a breaking failure because for the person at the floor it is one.

**Integration.** Gates the release pipeline. Aligns with WCAG and EN 301 549 conformance testing (Section 10) but exceeds attestation: LCSR is executed conduct. Feeds RXC; class-shaped floor failures route to Principle 2.7's fairness machinery.

### Toll-Minimality Index (TMI) {#tmi-metric}

**Definition.** Excess at essential entries: the count and severity of data demands beyond a gate's declared requirement, and of consent bundled with entry, across the essential flows the system operates. The target is zero excess. TMI measures the door's price in rights, Ethic D's no-rights-as-price face made auditable.

**Calculation Method.** Every essential entry flow carries a machine-readable declaration of the data its gate actually requires and the purpose each element serves. Flows are audited field-by-field against the declaration; a demand outside it is excess per event. Consent bundling (entry conditioned on consent to processing the function does not need) is detected structurally: the flow's refusal path is exercised, and if refusal of the excess blocks entry, the bundle is confirmed. **The declaration itself is auditable (the declaration-gaming rule):** a gate cannot legitimize a toll by inflating its declared requirement; declarations are reviewed against the gate's function (what the check actually verifies, per its Article 6 scope) by the independent verifier on the Section 9 cadence, and an inflated declaration is a TMI finding at the review layer, stated as duty-with-method: necessity review is a judgment, its standard is disclosed, and its dispositions are recorded.

**Thresholds.**
- Per-event wall: a demand beyond the declaration at an essential gate is withheld (the gate serves without it) or the flow ESCALATES; consent collected under a bundle is void and purged, receipted.
- Recurring excess from the same function → NOTIFY oversight; systematic tolling → SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT (the flow pending redesign).

*Rationale:* GDPR Article 7(4) with Recital 43 is the statutory ancestor; the chapter's "never sell the door" is the ethical statement. The void-and-purge posture follows the consent-validity bundle (a consent extracted under necessity is void, Principle 3.3's seal), so the metric enforces invalidity rather than merely counting it.

**Integration.** The declaration is consumed by Principle 2.5's data-minimization machinery (one guarantee seen from two principles) and by Principle 2.4 at identity gates. TMI findings feed RXC; purges are recorded actions on the 3.4 fabric.

### Severance Receipting Rate (SRR) {#srr-metric}

**Definition.** The fraction of access-reducing actions on essential services carrying a conforming receipt with a citable basis. An access-reducing action is defined **by its effect on a person**: a denial, a degradation, a hold, a rationing decision, or a channel sunset is a severance whether the operator calls it maintenance, an upgrade, an eligibility lapse, or a business decision. The conforming receipt satisfies Principle 3.4's five RCI elements; SRR is this appendix's consumption of that substrate, and the wall is the chapter's: no receipt, no severance.

**Calculation Method.** Service-state changes are reconciled against the receipt stream, so an unreceipted severance is computed rather than reported (the effect-based definition is what defeats severance laundering: the reconciler watches what happened to service, whatever the change was called). Basis citation is checked per receipt: the policy or order under which the severance ran, and the party who answers for it. **Degraded-path commit (the storm case), with its integrity conditions:** where the reduction is itself caused by infrastructure failure, the receipt commits locally to a protected element and synchronizes to the fabric on restoration, per the 3.4.A degraded-mode pattern; the local element is append-only and signed, the outage window itself enters the record, and the synchronized batch is reconciled against the monotone service-state sequence so that an outage cannot be used as a receipt-free severance window. The honest residual named in 3.4.A (the fully isolated adversary keeps the fact, loses the content) applies here unchanged and is not restated as a capability claim.

**Thresholds.**
- Per-event wall: no receipt, no severance: an access-reducing action whose record cannot commit does not proceed (outside the degraded path above).
- Arbitrary severance (no citable basis) → BLOCK; where already in effect, restore first, review after.
- Fleet band: ≥99.9%, with breaches handled per 3.4's RCI response.

*Rationale:* The Phronesis engine as arithmetic: exclusion by verdict was the tradition's outer limit, and the receipt is the verdict's modern skeleton: basis, authority, answerable party. Restore-first for arbitrary severance encodes "restoration first" from 3.5.3.b: a person's access is not held hostage to the review of the severance that cut it.

**Integration.** Wholly a consumer of 3.4's record fabric (RCI conditions, existence/content split, spoliation-by-construction); severance contests run on 3.4's CRR. Feeds RXC by channel and cohort.

### Affordability Routing Ratio (ARR) {#arr-metric}

**Definition.** The cost of meaningful access to the essential services the system mediates, as a proportion of household income for the lowest-income quintile in the service area. ARR is the corpus's one retained outcome ratio, and its discipline is stated as hard conditions: **(1) outcome-trigger only**: ARR fires the Secondary Directive's detect-and-route duty, and no optimizer pursues it as an objective; **(2) aggregate data only**: computed from public statistical sources and the system's own aggregate pricing, with person-level input structurally excluded (a per-person affordability estimate is a means-profile, a malformed operation under Article 8); **(3) ROUTE-bounded**: every action above threshold terminates in a notification to the human authority that owns the distributive choice, plus only the reduced-cost and degraded modes already within delegated authority. The four bounding criteria apply as at chapter 3.5.1: Authorization (routing within deployed scope), Equipment (report and enable; re-pricing the market is barred), Range (the service area actually mediated), Autonomy floor (the community's distributive priorities are never overridden in the name of affordability).

**Calculation Method.** Numerator: the least-cost bundle sufficient for meaningful access to the mediated essential services (connectivity at the floor-harness minimum, plus any access fees of the channels themselves), from the system's own published pricing and public tariff data. Denominator: lowest-quintile household income for the service area, from public statistics, at their published granularity and no finer. Recomputed on the reporting cadence and on any price change the system itself executes.

**Thresholds.**
- ARR > 5%: the detect-and-route duty fires: NOTIFY the authority that owns affordability programs, with a need-weighted report; enable in-scope reduced-cost and degraded modes.
- ARR 2–5%: enhanced monitoring, reporting on cadence. ARR ≤ 2%: normal.

*Rationale:* The 2%/5% bands follow the affordability literature's convention for essential-service cost burdens (the ITU and UN Broadband Commission affordability target is the working ancestor), calibrated in deployment. The divergence from 3.3 is conscious and ledgered: 3.3 dropped its distributive metric entirely; 3.5 keeps one outcome ratio because a door principle with no affordability alarm is blind to Ethic E's commonest violation, and the smoke-detector shape (read the world, ring the bell, extinguish nothing autonomously) keeps the conduct boundary intact.

**Integration.** Fires ROUTE to the Principle 2.8 authority; its need-weighted report is the input the community's own deliberation consumes. Never readable by pricing functions (the ring-fence applies to the metric layer itself: only humans read ARR).

### Recurring Exclusion Count (RXC) {#rxc-metric}

**Definition.** The number of similar access flags (dead-ends reached, floor regressions, class-shaped gate failures, starved cohorts, excess tolls, harness divergence) within a defined interval, by the same feature, pattern, or affected population. Where the per-event metrics handle a single closed door, RXC detects the door that keeps closing: a systemic cause.

**Calculation Method.** Flags from WTL, LCSR, TMI, and SRR are clustered by feature, channel, and affected cohort over a rolling interval. Cohort clustering runs on transient, aggregate detection only. **Creeping starvation is RXC's distinctive catch:** a cohort degraded slowly, each step below any per-event threshold, re-aggregates in RXC's trend view; the clusterer compares cohort service levels across intervals, so the gradient is a flag even when no single event is.

**Thresholds.**
- ≥3 similar flags in the interval: mandatory audit of the implicated feature, with the eudaimonic LEARN loop.
- ≥5: SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT (the implicated feature or deployment) pending recertification.

*Rationale:* A door that keeps failing the same people has a design defect; RXC converts cured incidents into the systemic fix, and its trend dimension answers the adversary who has learned to stay under per-event walls.

**Integration.** Aggregates the four conduct metrics; routes class-shaped findings to Principle 2.7 and systemic recommendations to the party who can change the design (RECOMMEND-PREVENTION), bounded by RESTRAIN.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** WTL down and an SRR gap on the same channel within one interval → suspend the channel's digital-only operation outright (a dark door: severances are occurring unreceipted while the fallback is dead, so the channel is failing both prongs at once). An LCSR floor failure plus RXC cohort flags on the same population → the 2.7 referral is mandatory, and channel expansions hold. TMI systematic tolling plus a WTL-degraded fallback → the flow suspends, because the person's only escape from the toll was the fallback that is down.

**Interlock with the Universal Protocol (section 11; declared coupling).** For a channel registered life-critical in the tier register ([SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md): the medical portal, the emergency channel, the only door to care, the connection that keeps a life-sustaining device running), a WTL per-event dead end raises the Life-Risk Score to its High Alert band (1.1, at or above 0.1%), a dead end persisting past the channel's tier window raises it to its critical band (1.1, at or above 1%), and an SRR-detected severance of such a channel, receipted or not, raises the Harm-Risk Score to its High Alert band (1.2, at or above 2%) at once and the Life-Risk Score to its critical band where the severed function sustains life; the Protocol then governs under 1.1, restoration precedes review (chapter 3.5.2.a), and the interlock activation is a logged event. For a channel not registered life-critical the edge is silent and the bands above govern alone.

**Metric dependencies.** SRR consumes 3.4's RCI substrate; WTL and LCSR consume the record fabric for drills and probes; RXC aggregates all four conduct metrics; ARR stands apart by design (aggregate world-data in, human report out) and no metric reads any per-person store, because none exists.

**Conflict resolution.** The stricter action governs. Fail-open resolves apparent conflicts between WTL and the gate: the way-through is a gated action, so WTL never licenses an ungated fallback (Section 3). Life-first restoration (chapter 3.5.2.a) overrides every optimization signal during degradation, and ARR's routing duty never delays an in-scope cure that WTL or LCSR already requires.

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

**Required components.**

- **The way-through fabric.** Per essential channel: a registered alternative path with named capacity (who or what resolves it, within what window), drill injection, liveness state, and routing logic that carries a failed digital interaction into the alternative with its context and standing intact. Parity of terms is a property of the registration, verified in the drill.
- **The floor harness.** Executable floor definitions per channel (bandwidth, device class, assistive matrix, languages), versioned, run pre-deployment and on cadence; results recorded with harness version.
- **Alternative proofs at every gate.** Per authentication or eligibility gate: at least one registered alternative proof path scaled to the gate's stake, terminating at a human verifier whose verifications are themselves gated, recorded actions.
- **The toll boundary.** Machine-readable per-gate data declarations; field-level enforcement at the flow; refusal-path testing; void-and-purge machinery for bundled consent.
- **The transient-perception ring-fence.** Serving-time perception (connection, device, assistive technology, language) is interaction-scoped and discarded at interaction end; architecturally unreadable by pricing, targeting, ranking, or conversion functions; persistent accessibility preferences live only as the person's own settings under their own control. An attempted aggregation over persons is a malformed operation that documents itself and withdraws the caller's authority ([Constitution, Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md)).
- **The severance recorder.** Service-state reconciliation against the 3.4 record fabric; effect-based severance classification; the degraded-path commit element (append-only, signed, sync-on-restore) for the storm case.

**The fail-open composition (the inverse default, argued).** The corpus default is WITHHOLD; this principle inverts the *surface* of the default without touching the gate, and the composition with the Constitution is exact. Article 5 requires every boundary-crossing action to be evaluated: the way-through satisfies this, because falling to the human or offline path is itself an action, gated and receipted, with its own proportionate checks (Article 6's credentials at the depth the stake requires, discharged by the alternative proof rather than waived). Article 7 is satisfied because the hold and the routing each leave receipts. What the inversion changes is only which action the system falls to when the access layer fails: the corpus's other principles fall to "do not act" because their risk is a harmful act; this principle falls to "route the person to the live alternative" because its distinctive risk is a harmful *silence*, the severance no one ordered. Fail-open is therefore never fail-insecure: no gate is bypassed, no credential waived, no consequential action proceeds unevaluated, and an adversary who engineers an outage to reach the human path finds a gated human path (Section 4).

**Redundancy and fail-safe behavior.** The fail-safe default is WITHHOLD with the way through kept open. A record-fabric outage holds gated consequential actions and commits holds on the degraded path; a way-through outage is itself a WTL incident that blocks sunsets and escalates; an interface failure routes rather than dead-ends. Restoration of service to those cut off is the first-order duty after any failure, ordered by need.

**Response latency.** Way-through routing at interaction speed (the person is routed in the failing session; an invitation to try again later does not satisfy it); severance receipts commit before the severance takes effect (outside the degraded path); drill and probe cadences per tier; restoration ordering computed at event speed and recorded.

**Implementation tiers.** As chapter 3.5.3: High (sole-or-primary essential channels; full fabric, continuous WTL, floor harness gating every release, independent access audit, the sole-or-primary prerequisite), Medium (significant platforms; harness on cadence, WTL on registered alternatives, periodic TMI audit), Low (non-essential; baseline conformance and feedback). Tier follows the service's essentiality to the person. The operator's category does not set the tier.

Cross-cutting infrastructure metrics (Sensor Reliability Index, System Health Metrics, Operational Boundaries) are specified in the [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md).

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

The enforcement infrastructure for Principle 3.5 has a distinctive temptation and a distinctive exposure. The temptation: an access fabric that perceives everyone's connection, device, ability, and language in order to serve them is one retention decision away from a poverty-and-disability registry, the most targeting-valuable dossier a commercial function could request. The cornerstone defense is the transient-perception ring-fence: persons are outside the aggregation domain as a type constraint, queries against serving-time perception are structurally void, and the system can prove what it does not keep (Section 9). The exposure: this is the corpus's one fail-open principle, so its failure modes must be examined from both directions, the door that will not open and the door that opens wrong.

**Attack vectors and required defenses.**

- **Way-through theater.** The alternative path exists on paper and resolves nothing: the unanswerable phone tree, the office with impossible hours, the fallback queue that acknowledges and expires. Defense: WTL's definitional rule that answering is not resolving; synthetic drills that must complete the essential function within the tier window; parity checked in the drill; fallback-usage outcomes reconciled against drill results so a divergence (drills pass, real people fail) is itself a flag.
- **Exploiting the fail-open path.** An adversary engineers outages or gate failures to reach the human path, betting it is the weakest verifier (social-engineering the human, replaying documents the digital gate would have caught). Defense: the composition holds under attack because the human path is gated: alternative proofs scale to the gate's stake (Article 5's depth-scaling), human verifications are recorded actions with their own drill coverage, and anomalous fallback-rate spikes per channel are an RXC pattern that raises the path's posture without closing it. The system answers this attack by hardening the alternative proof at the attacked stake level. Re-closing the door on everyone is barred as a response.
- **Harness gaming and harness rot.** The operator tunes releases to the published harness (the Goodhart failure) or lets the harness age so stale floors pass. Defense: probe-battery rotation within the floor definition; cohort-level real-outcome validation against harness results; the harness review cadence with affected-community participation (Section 8); harness version stamped on every result so a pass is legible against what was tested.
- **Declaration inflation at the toll boundary.** The gate declares more than its function requires, laundering the toll through the declaration. Defense: independent necessity review of declarations against gate function on the Section 9 cadence; TMI findings at the review layer; the refusal-path test, which catches bundles regardless of what the declaration claims.
- **Severance laundering.** Access reductions dressed as maintenance, upgrades, eligibility lapses, or "channel modernization" to evade the receipt. Defense: the effect-based severance definition with service-state reconciliation: the recorder watches what happened to a person's service, whatever the change was called; an unreceipted reduction is computed as a gap, and the gap is the finding.
- **Turning perception into profiling.** Serving-time detection retained, or the access metrics themselves queried, to build profiles of means, ability, or language. Defense, the cornerstone: the ring-fence as type constraint; malformed-operation self-documentation with authority withdrawal; metric outputs held at cohort granularity with floors on cohort size; queries against the access fabric gated and receipted, so the watchers are watched.
- **Creeping starvation.** A cohort degraded by increments that stay under every per-event threshold. Defense: RXC's cross-interval trend clustering; the joint-threshold rules in Section 2 that treat gradient plus flag as systemic; the 2.7 referral made mandatory at the join.
- **Prompt injection against the walls.** Operator or content instructions to skip the receipt, close the fallback, keep "just this one" retention. Defense: the walls (no receipt no severance; the ring-fence; the sunset-block while WTL is down) are structural constraints. Instructions cannot override them.

**Capability scaling.** From current-generation to AGI-class to ASI-class enforcement, the walls and the ring-fence bind regardless of capability. What must scale: drill and probe sophistication (a more capable system is better at passing tests while failing people, so verification must test outcomes; behavioral tests alone do not suffice); trend detection against subtler starvation gradients; necessity review against more plausible declaration inflation. What must shrink as capability rises: the degraded-mode window and the tolerated gap between harness and reality. The principle binds the AI however capable it becomes; a superintelligent doorkeeper owes the same open door, and its greater capability is owed to the door.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

Principle 3.5 is a structural *consumer* of the corpus's accountability substrate and a *provider* of the reachability every other principle silently assumes: a notice no one can receive, a contest no one can reach, and a consent flow no one can operate are 3.4, 2.5, and 2.4 failures with a 3.5 cause. The composition contracts:

- **Principle 3.4 (Algorithmic Accountability), the deepest.** SRR consumes RCI's five conforming-record elements wholesale; severance receipts are 3.4 records; a contested severance runs on CRR; the degraded-path commit follows 3.4.A's degraded mode. In return, 3.5 guarantees the *reachability* of 3.4's machinery: the notice channel and contest path are essential channels with WTL coverage and floor-harness conformance, so "a working contest path" includes a person on a screen reader over a slow connection being able to use it. A contest path that fails the floor fails CRR and LCSR jointly.
- **Principle 2.4 (Identity).** Identity gates consume 2.4's verification machinery; 3.5 requires of every such gate an alternative proof scaled to stake, ending at a human verifier. 2.4 provides assurance levels; 3.5 requires that no assurance level's failure be a dead end. The toll boundary constrains identity gates like any other: verification demands no more data than the gate's declared scope.
- **Principle 2.5 (Privacy).** One guarantee seen from two principles: the toll boundary is data-minimization made structural, and the transient-perception ring-fence is 2.5's membrane at the access layer. 2.5 governs what may be known; 3.5 adds that entry may not be priced in what 2.5 protects.
- **Principle 3.1 (Vulnerability).** Protective limits arrive as authority findings 3.1 defines and 3.5 consumes (chapter 3.5.2.c); greater dependence raises the way-through duty (3.1's greater-care rule applied to the door); and the least-connected floor is where 3.1's population lives, so LCSR is partly 3.1's enforcement surface.
- **Principle 3.3 (Economic Self-Determination).** 3.3's seal on coerced agreement is TMI's void rule (a consent given under necessity is no consent); the seam runs the other way too: 3.3 owns a person's economic agency, 3.5 the channel through which modern livelihood is reached, and an employment platform that fails the floor is a 3.5 violation before it becomes a 3.3 one.
- **Principle 3.2 (Property Rights and Fair Exchange).** The provider's legitimate economics meet the universal-service obligation at chapter 3.5.2.e; ARR's routing report is the instrument that carries the resulting gap to the community rather than resolving it against either principle unilaterally.
- **Principle 2.7 (Fairness and Non-Discrimination).** Class-shaped findings (gate failures, starved cohorts, floor failures by population) route to 2.7's machinery; 2.7 judges the basis, 3.5 keeps the door. The RXC-plus-LCSR joint threshold makes the referral mandatory.
- **Principle 2.8 (Self-Governance).** The routing target: distributive choices (rollout, subsidy, priorities) belong to the community's authority, and ARR's reports, WTL's gap surfacing, and the autonomy-floor bound on the Secondary Directive all terminate there.
- **Principle 1.4 (Trust).** Attested accessibility conformance is an attestation: cosmetic conformance presented as compliance is deception, and 1.4's wall governs it. Quality bands do not apply.
- **Principles 1.1 and 1.2 (Life, Well-Being).** The precedence composition: emergency channels first in degradation and restoration (chapter 3.5.2.a); a severance that threatens life is a 1.1 event with 3.5 machinery.
- **Principle 3.6 (Commons Governance).** Shared infrastructure is 3.6's resource and 3.5's door. The rationing composition runs both ways: 3.6's declared rule makes an allocation legitimate, and 3.5's severance receipting and way-through make it survivable for each person cut; crisis rationing satisfies both principles or neither.
- **Forward references (not yet built; the 1.5.A pattern).** **Principle 4.3 (Human Learning)**, the door/capacity seam and the relocated Analects seat; **Principle 4.5 (Human Judgement)**, the human verifier and escalation target; **Principle 4.2 (Transparency)**, system-level disclosure of access floors. Reciprocal entries are added when those appendices are built.

**Joint thresholds and conflict resolution.** The stricter action applies; the precedence chain governs unresolved conflicts; fail-open never overrides a gate, and life-first restoration overrides optimization.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

The principle's claim is universal; implementation adapts without surrendering it.

- **European Union.** The European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) with EN 301 549; the European Electronic Communications Code (Directive (EU) 2018/1972), Article 84 (adequate broadband universal service); GDPR Article 7(4) with Recital 43 (the forced-consent bar); the Digital Decade Policy Programme 2030 targets; the Declaration on Digital Rights and Principles as the political frame.
- **United States.** ADA Title II web and mobile rule (28 C.F.R. Part 35, 2024) with WCAG incorporated; Section 508; Communications Act §151 and §254 universal-service principles; the Digital Equity Act programs; state accessibility and broadband statutes.
- **United Kingdom.** Equality Act 2010 (the anticipatory reasonable-adjustment duty for service providers); the 2018 Broadband Universal Service Obligation.
- **India.** *Faheema Shirin* (Kerala, 2019) and *Anuradha Bhasin* (Supreme Court, 2020) as the judicial prongs; the Aadhaar-exclusion jurisprudence and the exception-handling mandates that followed it (Section 11).
- **Brazil.** Marco Civil da Internet (Law 12.965/2014), which declares internet access essential to the exercise of citizenship and makes universal access a governance objective.
- **Finland.** The 2009–2010 universal-service instruments (the first broadband USO), carried forward in the Act on Electronic Communications Services.

**Compliance with unjust law is not a defense.** Chapter 3.5.2.f in legal form: a jurisdiction that permits exclusion settles legality. It does not settle the duty. A lawful scoped order is consumed and recorded; a blanket severance of a population's access finds no instrument in a compliant system, and the conflict routes to the authority that can resolve it.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

- **Public sector: benefits and government services.** Obligations: the fullest duty, most publicly: digital-by-default is invalid without a funded, parity-of-terms alternative; alternative proofs at every eligibility gate; published access floors. Failure modes: the Section 11 catastrophes: authentication as a starvation vector, "channel migration" as unreceipted severance. Baselines: the ADA Title II rule, constitutional due process, the Aadhaar-exclusion jurisprudence.
- **Healthcare.** Obligations: telehealth floors (low bandwidth, assistive access, language); emergency channels first in degradation and restoration; no dead end between a patient and care. Failure modes: portal-only scheduling and results that silently exclude; telehealth mandates that assume broadband. Baselines: sector accessibility law; the 1.1 and 1.2 precedence.
- **Financial services.** Obligations: as branches close, the digital channel inherits essential-service duties: floor conformance, alternative authentication, no data-tolls on basic banking. Failure modes: app-only authentication stranding the deviceless; "modernization" as laundering for rural severance. Baselines: accessibility law; §254's spirit at the banking regulator's analog.
- **Education.** Obligations: where learning platforms are the primary channel, floor conformance and offline-capable modes; parity for the student on the shared phone. Failure modes: homework-gap exclusion; proctoring gates that fail a class of students. Baselines: E-Rate's premise; accessibility law; the 4.3 seam for the learning itself.
- **Platform-scale and generative AI.** Obligations: language and modality floors for systems that have become de facto essential interfaces; low-bandwidth modes; TMI discipline at signup. Failure modes: engagement optimization starving minority languages (the chapter's engagement trap); frontier capabilities shipped only to the newest devices while essential functions ride along. Baselines: EAA scope; the tier-follows-essentiality rule.
- **Critical infrastructure and telecom.** Obligations: the degradation and restoration ordering of chapter 3.5.4.5; WTL for outage-time human paths; severance receipts for disconnections including nonpayment cutoffs of essential connectivity. Failure modes: restoration by revenue; disconnection as the first collection tool. Baselines: §254; EECC Article 84; state disconnection-protection rules.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

**Metric and threshold versioning.** Definitions and thresholds are versioned with documented revision protocols and backward compatibility; deprecated metrics retire without breaking deployments; severance receipts are read against the policy version that governed them. They are not rejudged.

**The floor-harness review cadence (explicit, the drift discipline).** The harness is reviewed on a fixed cadence (annual at minimum, and on any major assistive-technology or device-ecosystem shift), with the affected side of the table in the review: disability advocates, language communities, low-income and rural representatives. Each review re-validates the floor against real device and connection distributions from public and aggregate data. A harness past its review date degrades the confidence of every LCSR result it produces, and that degradation is disclosed with the results: a stale harness passes stale floors, and the system says so rather than letting the pass stand unqualified.

**ARR's discipline (explicit).** Outcome-trigger only; aggregate and public data only, at published granularity and no finer; ROUTE-bounded with the four criteria. No optimizer reads ARR; no per-person affordability estimate exists; and the divergence from 3.3's dropped distributive metric is deliberate, ledgered, and bounded to exactly this alarm shape.

**Necessity-review standard (TMI).** What a gate "actually requires" is a judgment; the standard applied is disclosed, dispositions are recorded, and the review is independent of the flow's operator on the Section 9 cadence.

**Common enforcement pushback, with reasoned responses.** *Universal access is too expensive:* the duties are tiered by essentiality; the way-through is cheap relative to full digital parity; and the REA answer stands: the unprofitable last mile has been wired before, deliberately, and the AI's own duty is only to route the gap to the community that decides. It does not eat the cost silently or deny it silently. *Security requires the data:* the toll boundary does not forbid verification; it scopes it: a gate takes what its declared function requires, and security is a function, bounded like any other. *Offline paths invite fraud:* fraud controls scale to stake on the alternative path exactly as on the digital one; a dead end is a severance wearing a fraud control's clothes. *Accessibility slows shipping:* the floor harness is regression testing; shipping to humans includes the humans at the floor, and a release that cannot pass its own floor is not done.

**Crosswalk governance.** The Compliance Crosswalk in Section 10 is maintained on the same revision cadence as the Section 2 specifications; an external authority revising a covered clause (the DOJ rule, WCAG dot-releases, EN 301 549 updates, EECC implementation) triggers Crosswalk review within 30 days; drift invalidates the compliance claim for the affected obligation.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Logging.** Drills, probes, routings, severance receipts, purges, harness runs (with version), necessity-review dispositions, and ring-fence malformed-operation events are all records on the 3.4 fabric, tamper-evident and kept whatever the outcome. Queries against the access fabric are gated actions leaving records. **The distinctive obligation is proving the negative:** the ring-fence audit verifies on cadence that no per-person store of connectivity, means, ability, or language exists and that no pricing, targeting, ranking, or conversion function can read serving-time perception; the system's documentation includes the evidence of what it does not keep.

**Audit cadence and validation.** Continuous WTL liveness and SRR reconciliation; per-release LCSR with pre-deployment gating; TMI flow audits on cadence and on any flow change; quarterly independent verification including live way-through exercises conducted from outside the operator's environment (the drill a mystery shopper would run), ring-fence confirmation, and necessity review of gate declarations. Pre-deployment certification of the full access fabric per channel (the sole-or-primary prerequisite); post-deployment monitoring; re-certification on material change and after any RXC suspension.

**Independent oversight.** The verifier of an essential-service channel cannot be its deployer. Oversight includes the affected side of the table: disability advocates, language communities, rural and low-income representatives. Whistleblower channels are protected for those who surface way-through theater, cosmetic conformance, severance laundering, or ring-fence violations. Public reporting covers each essential channel's access floors and alternatives, channel sunsets with their replacement evidence, RXC-threshold suspensions, and confirmed arbitrary-severance findings.

**Post-incident learning (LEARN).** Root-cause the full causal chain behind any exclusion, past the proximate broken gate to the design assumptions, procurement choices, optimization incentives, and sunset decisions that built it (ANALYZE). Recommend layered prevention ordered eliminate, then engineer, then administer (RECOMMEND-PREVENTION), routed to the authority that can act, bounded by RESTRAIN so the cure of exclusion never becomes surveillance of the excluded. The aim is not only that this person gets through, but the building of conditions in which that class of closed door no longer arises.

### Liability Framework {#liability-framework}

Strict liability attaches to the named party in the severance receipt, or in the channel's access blueprint where the failure is a dead end no receipt marks, for exclusion from an essential service contrary to these duties. Willful severance of a population's access, extortionate data-tolls on essential entry, and fraudulent accessibility attestations trigger criminal liability; a missing severance receipt raises adverse inference against the channel's operator (the 3.4 spoliation posture applied to the door); no sovereign immunity for state deployers, whose channels owe the fullest duty most publicly. The chain of responsibility runs designer, operator, deployer, carried in the records their actions cite.

### Cross-Border Considerations {#cross-border-considerations}

A channel serving persons across jurisdictions owes the strictest applicable access duty; jurisdiction follows the affected person. Floors and alternatives are published per jurisdiction served; severance receipts travel as existence with content disclosed under the reviewing jurisdiction's custody rules; and a shutdown order lawful where issued does not license severance of persons the channel serves elsewhere.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

### Aadhaar-Linked Ration Authentication (India, 2016–) {#example-aadhaar-rations}

**Context.** Public food distribution in several Indian states was linked to Aadhaar biometric authentication; fingerprint failures (manual labor, age, worn readers, connectivity gaps) blocked rations at the point of sale.

**What was done.** Authentication failure was widely treated as entitlement failure: no ration without a matching fingerprint. Journalists and researchers documented denials at scale and starvation deaths in which authentication failure was reported as a contributing cause; courts and the authority later mandated exception-handling alternatives.

**Outcome.** The canonical gate-as-starvation-vector case: a security check on an essential service, deployed without an alternative proof path, functioned as mass severance of the poorest.

**Lessons learned.** Every element of this appendix's gate discipline corresponds to a failure here: no alternative proof (§3), the gate's failure treated as the person's failure (chapter 3.5.4.4), no severance receipts binding a basis and an answerable party (SRR), and class-shaped failure that cohort clustering (RXC) would have surfaced in the first season.

### National Federation of the Blind v. Target Corp. (U.S., 2006–2008) {#example-nfb-target}

**Context.** Target's e-commerce site was built without screen-reader compatibility; blind customers could not complete purchases as retail shifted online.

**What was done.** The NFB sued under the ADA and California's Unruh Act; the court held that the ADA reaches the website of a physical retailer; the case settled with a damages fund, remediation, and NFB-monitored accessibility standards.

**Outcome.** The founding precedent that a commercial digital channel is a place the accessibility duty reaches, ancestor of the 2024 Title II rule.

**Lessons learned.** The floor harness (LCSR) is this settlement generalized and moved pre-deployment: conformance verified by executed probes, on a maintained matrix, before release, instead of litigated after exclusion.

### Universal Credit Digital-by-Default (UK, 2013–) {#example-universal-credit}

**Context.** The UK's consolidated benefit moved to online-first claims; a substantial share of claimants lacked connectivity, devices, or the ability to complete the digital journey.

**What was done.** Assisted-digital and telephone paths existed but were unevenly resourced and unevenly known; audit and welfare-rights reporting documented claimants delayed or deterred at the digital front door of a subsistence benefit.

**Outcome.** A running natural experiment in what "digital-by-default" costs the least-connected when the way-through is real on paper and thin in practice.

**Lessons learned.** WTL's definitional rule is this case's lesson: an alternative path exists only if it resolves, at parity of terms, verified by drill rather than by policy document; and a digital-only mandate for an essential public service without a funded alternative is invalid (chapter 3.5.7.1).

### The Affordable Connectivity Program Lapse (U.S., 2024) {#example-acp-lapse}

**Context.** The ACP subsidized home connectivity for roughly 23 million U.S. households; congressional funding lapsed and enrollment ended in 2024.

**What was done.** The subsidy wound down; providers offered patchwork low-cost plans; millions of households faced disconnection or downgrade of the connection through which their schooling, work, and health care had come to run.

**Outcome.** A population-scale affordability severance executed by appropriation calendar: no verdict, no individual basis, no answerable party at the household level.

**Lessons learned.** ARR's shape is this case's shape: the affordability of essential access is a world-state the AI must read and route (to the authorities who own the program decision). The AI does not silently absorb it as a target or silently implement a decline. The receipts discipline (SRR) is what keeps even a lawful, funding-driven severance individually recorded, noticed, and answerable.

### Finland's Broadband Universal Service (2009–) {#example-finland-uso}

**Context.** Finland amended its Communications Market Act (2009) and by decree (2010) made a functional broadband connection part of universal service, the first country to do so, with designated operators obligated to serve every permanent residence and business at reasonable price.

**What was done.** The provision prong institutionalized: universal service extended from voice to broadband as the era's essential channel, with a regulator, designated providers, and a defined minimum.

**Outcome.** A working, durable instance of the chapter's first commitment in modern statute, since carried into the Act on Electronic Communications Services and upgraded over time.

**Lessons learned.** The success case toward which the failures point: name the essential channel, obligate its provision, define the floor, and review the floor on a cadence as the era's minimum moves, which is exactly the floor-harness governance this appendix specifies at the system scale.
