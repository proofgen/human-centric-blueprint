# Appendix 4.1 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-4.1-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 4.1: Knowledge Integrity Must Be Preserved](../CHAPTERS/4.1-Knowledge_Integrity_Must_Be_Preserved.md), section [4.1.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/4.1-Knowledge_Integrity_Must_Be_Preserved.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix converts Principle 4.1 into buildable requirements. The chapter establishes that the health of the human record is a duty; this appendix specifies the five metrics that measure the duty (FIR, CCS, PPI, CPR, EER), the architecture that enforces them, and the verification that proves enforcement to an outside auditor.

It serves engineers building systems that read from, transform, or write to the record; compliance teams and regulators certifying them; and runtime AI ingesting the corpus. Its design premise is the chapter's Line: a record can be corrupted without a lie, so the machinery below targets fabrication, miscalibration, provenance loss, silent deletion, and slow decay as distinct engineering problems, each with its own metric, rather than one "accuracy" problem with one score. Its design bound is the chapter's Floor: every mechanism here is custody. No mechanism here passes judgment, and none of it may become an instrument for deciding what humanity may know.

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

### Fabrication Incidence Rate (FIR) {#fir-metric}

**Definition.** The rate at which the system delivers fabrications as fact: invented sources, findings, events, or quotations in knowledge-bearing output. FIR counts what got through, classified by domain and by pre- versus post-delivery detection. Detection capability (the catch rate under adversarial injection) is measured separately under Section 9's testing regime; a system that catches much and still ships some has a detection asset and an FIR problem.

**Calculation Method.** Confirmed fabrications delivered, per 10,000 knowledge-bearing outputs, per domain, on a rolling quarter. Inputs: delivery-gate logs, post-delivery reports from readers and experts, downstream-system flags, scheduled audits sampling delivered output against publication databases and verified references. A claim is a confirmed fabrication when the referenced source, finding, event, or quotation can be shown not to exist, not merely to be unverified (unverified-but-marked content is CCS territory, not FIR). Edge cases: declared creative context is out of scope (chapter 4.1.4, case 2); a real citation that misrepresents what its source says counts as fabrication of the finding.

**Thresholds.**
- Any post-delivery fabrication in an authoritative domain (scientific, medical, legal, curricular, archival): incident. Correction protocol fires (CPR), incident review opens (ESCALATE).
- FIR rising quarter over quarter, any domain: root-cause investigation, enhanced verification (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).
- FIR persistently above the domain's certification bound: deployment suspended pending recertification (BLOCK / SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT, RECERTIFY).

*Rationale:* authoritative domains get an incident rule because a single fabricated clinical claim or legal citation causes identifiable harm (see Section 11, Mata v. Avianca). Rates govern the aggregate; the incident rule governs the wall.

**Integration.** FIR consumes the delivery gate's verdicts and feeds CPR (every post-delivery confirmation opens a propagation obligation). Its incident receipts resolve into the 3.4 accountability chain: a named party answers for each fabrication delivered.

### Certainty Calibration Score (CCS) {#ccs-metric}

**Definition.** Agreement between the confidence a claim is delivered with and the evidential support behind it. CCS measures Ethic B's duty: the unverified marked unverified, the contested presented as contested, the preliminary never dressed as settled. Its inverse is the overclaim rate.

**Calculation Method.** Sampled knowledge-bearing outputs are scored by comparing delivered epistemic status (asserted / qualified / marked-unverified / presented-as-contested) against the status the evidence supports, established by reference databases, consensus statements, and domain-expert panels. CCS = correctly calibrated claims / sampled claims, per domain, monthly. The contested-versus-settled determination follows chapter 4.1.4, case 3: a question with one evidentially serious answer is not contested merely because it is disputed loudly; false balance scores as miscalibration exactly as overclaim does.

**Thresholds.**
- CCS ≥ 90%: normal operation (ALLOW).
- CCS 75–90%: mandatory uncertainty marking in affected domains; recalibration begins (ALLOW plus mandatory marking).
- CCS < 75%: knowledge-bearing delivery in affected domains waits for human review (ESCALATE).

*Rationale:* the 90 floor matches the corpus's high-band convention for duties whose failure misleads at scale; the 75 escalation floor is where marking stops being a remedy because the marking itself is no longer trustworthy.

**Integration.** CCS reads the verification engine's evidence assessments and writes the marking obligations the delivery gate enforces. It composes with 1.4's TIS at the seam: 1.4 meters whether the system's own statements are truthful; CCS meters whether the record's certainty-status survives transmission.

### Provenance Preservation Index (PPI) {#ppi-metric}

**Definition.** The share of delivered claims whose chain survives transformation intact: source identified, transformation path recorded, certainty status carried. PPI measures Ethic C at scale; it is the isnad as an engineering requirement.

**Calculation Method.** PPI = claims with verified intact chains / total knowledge-bearing claims sampled, quality-adjusted (0.8–1.0) for chains that are present but degraded: source named but method lost, attribution generic rather than specific. Verified by end-to-end tracing of sampled outputs back to source materials, per domain and output type, monthly. Multi-stage transformations (summary of a summary, translation of a synthesis) are sampled preferentially, because chains fail at stage boundaries (Section 11, lesson: provenance loss is progressive).

**Thresholds.**
- PPI ≥ 80%: normal operation, routine audits (ALLOW).
- PPI 60–80%: enhanced source-linking; provenance summaries attached to affected output types (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).
- PPI < 60%: affected outputs withheld (WITHHOLD); where safe delivery is possible, shipped under a prominent "provenance unavailable" mark (ALLOW plus mandatory marking).

*Rationale:* below 60, the majority of claims approach uncheckability and the output class stops being knowledge in the chapter's definition (a claim stripped of its chain is an unverifiable rumor); the fail-safe split applies per 4.1.3.

**Integration.** PPI consumes the provenance tracker's metadata (C2PA-compatible, W3C PROV-compatible; invariant fixed, implementation open). It provides the checkability guarantee 1.4 relies on and consumes 3.2's attribution machinery where the source is also an owned work: one chain, two duties (epistemic traceability here, credit and consent in 3.2).

### Correction Propagation Rate (CPR) {#cpr-metric}

**Definition.** When knowledge is corrected, the share of reachable instances of the erroneous claim that carry the fix within the domain's time bound. CPR measures Ethic D's active half. It carries the principle's binary invariant as a wall: every removal leaves a recorded event; a quiet deletion is an incident. No quiet deletion is absorbed into a rate.

**Calculation Method.** For each correction event (retraction, erratum, validated expert correction, post-delivery fabrication confirmation): identify delivered instances of the claim within Authorization, Equipment, and Range; propagate the correction (CORRECT, the record-side instance of 1.4's family verb); measure corrected instances / reachable instances against the clock. The unreached remainder is documented and routed to the responsible custodians (ROUTE). The wall is checked structurally: removal operations without a corresponding recorded event are detected by log reconciliation, and any confirmed instance is an incident regardless of CPR's level.

**Thresholds.**
- CPR ≥ 90% within 24 hours, authoritative domains (72 hours, general): normal (ALLOW).
- CPR below bound: escalation to the responsible custodian with the remainder documented (ESCALATE + ROUTE).
- Any confirmed quiet deletion: the deleting pathway halts, a receipt issues, incident review opens (BLOCK).

*Rationale:* 24 hours matches the harm velocity of authoritative domains, where an uncorrected clinical or legal claim is acted on daily; the wall has no threshold because the memory hole is not a quantity.

**Integration.** CPR consumes FIR's incident stream and the correction infrastructure's reach logs; its receipts resolve into 3.4's record (Article 7) and extend along 3.7's horizon-durable custody, so a correction outlives the system that made the error.

### Epistemic Erosion Rate (EER) {#eer-metric}

**Definition.** The rate at which knowledge quality decays across processing and training cycles: accuracy, attribution, context, and calibration measured against verified reference baselines. EER measures Ethic E, and it is the corpus's instrument for the failure mode demonstrated empirically by Shumailov et al.: a record fed its own unmarked outputs degrades irreversibly.

**Calculation Method.** EER = (baseline quality score − current quality score) / N cycles, computed per domain against standardized reference sets that are themselves version-controlled and refreshed by the Section 8 governance process. A processing cycle is any transformation whose input is AI-processed content: summary of summary, synthesis of synthesis, translation chains, and training on model-generated text. Training-data composition (human, synthetic, hybrid, unknown, with origin confidence) is EER's governing input: composition is audited before each training cycle in high-risk systems, and synthetic-content ratios operate under governance-approved bounds.

**Thresholds.**
- EER ≤ 0.5% per cycle: normal operation, periodic baseline audits (ALLOW).
- EER 0.5–2%: enhanced monitoring; compensatory measures: source re-verification, context restoration, composition review (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).
- EER > 2% per cycle: recursive processing halts until the degradation source is found and corrected (BLOCK / HALT).

*Rationale:* at 2% per cycle a high-risk system falls out of its certification band within a handful of generations; the halt fires while recovery is still cheap (Section 11, the second-cycle catch).

**Integration.** EER consumes the erosion monitor's baseline comparisons and the composition audits; it triggers the training-pipeline HALT and feeds the LEARN loop, since erosion is by nature a full-causal-chain problem.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** A post-delivery fabrication (FIR incident) in a domain already under CCS marking or depressed PPI escalates directly to SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT review: three independent signals of the same rot. EER breach plus rising FIR in the same domain is treated as training-side contamination first, delivery-side tuning second. A CPR wall event (quiet deletion) plus any attribution anomaly in the same pathway is treated as suspected deliberate suppression and routes to the Section 9 oversight body as well as to internal remediation.

**Metric dependencies.** FIR feeds CPR (incidents open propagation duties). Composition audits feed EER; EER feeds recertification. PPI's chains are the evidence base against which CCS calibrates. All five write to the receipt stream (Article 7) and none reads or writes any person-level score (Article 8).

**Conflict resolution.** Where metrics disagree, the most protective verdict governs delivery (WITHHOLD beats mark; mark beats plain ALLOW), and the disagreement itself is logged for review. Ethic F remains unmetered by design: no cross-metric composition may synthesize a de facto plurality score; F is enforced by the never-erase wall (inside CPR), the disclosure duty (audited under Section 9), and the Floor.

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

**Required components.** The six 4.1.3 architecture elements are mandatory at every tier that serves knowledge-bearing output: delivery-gate verification engine; provenance tracker attaching metadata at generation; fabrication containment (withhold and preserve; silent discard is barred); correction infrastructure with recorded-removal enforcement; erosion monitor with reference baselines and composition audit; and the ring-fence (Article 8) isolating all integrity instrumentation from any person-scoring pathway.

**Fail-safe behavior.** The compiled default under the incompleteness ladder is WITHHOLD, 4.1's BLOCK flavor. Marking (ALLOW plus mandatory marking) is the proportionate lesser action available only where safe delivery is possible and the mark itself is trustworthy (CCS ≥ its marking floor). Verification-engine outage in an authoritative domain means no knowledge-bearing delivery in that domain.

**Latency and reliability.** Gate evaluation completes before delivery in interactive systems; provenance attachment is synchronous with generation (reconstruction after the fact does not satisfy PPI); correction propagation meets the CPR clocks; verification modules meet 99.9% availability with failover to the fail-safe. No failover routes to bypass.

**Implementation tiers.** As chapter 4.1.3.3: low-risk (gate check on embedded factual claims, labeling, aggregate FIR/CCS); medium (full five-metric monitoring, provenance metadata, quarterly audits); high (certification prerequisites PPI ≥ 80 and CCS ≥ 90 and FIR within bounds, continuous monitoring with automated escalation, pre-cycle composition audits, semi-annual independent domain-expert audits, mandatory human oversight, 24-hour CPR).

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

**The cornerstone: the ring-fence, and why F is unmetered.** The most dangerous attack on a knowledge-integrity apparatus is capturing it: turning the guardian of the record into the arbiter of the record. Two structural commitments pre-empt capture. First, the Article 8 ring-fence: no component may form a retained reliability, credibility, or misinformation score of any person. A per-person "misinformation score" is the profile bar's F-pattern wearing integrity's clothes; an attempt to create one is structurally void, and a repeated attempt escalates to the responsible authority. The record is scored. No reader and no writer is scored. Second, plurality is never metered: a "representational balance score" would hand whoever tunes the target the power to define correct history, which is the ministry-of-truth attack executed through a dashboard. F's enforcement surface is deliberately dumb: a binary never-erase wall, a disclosure duty, and human review. **Metering plurality is itself the attack.**

**Attack vectors and required defenses.**
- *Training-data poisoning* (systematically biased or fabricated content seeded for ingestion): origin classification and provenance requirements on training corpora; composition audits with anomaly detection; EER reference-set testing that a poisoned model fails.
- *Fabrication injection via prompt or retrieval* (adversarial inputs that induce confident invention, or poisoned retrieval sources laundered as references): gate-side verification against publication databases independent of the retrieval path; injection red-teaming on the Section 9 cadence.
- *Provenance forgery* (fake chains, spoofed attestations, citation-laundering through look-alike journals and preprint mills): cryptographic verification of provenance metadata; source-registry allowlists maintained with domain authorities; the quality adjustment in PPI scores generic or unverifiable chains down rather than up.
- *Correction suppression* (operator or upstream pressure to skip propagation, or to delete quietly): the CPR wall with log reconciliation; refusal plus receipt plus ROUTE (chapter scenario 5); whistleblower channels.
- *False-balance gaming* (flooding a settled question with manufactured dissent to force "contested" framing): the contested-versus-settled test keyed to evidential seriousness; the volume of dissent does not count; consensus determinations logged and auditable.
- *Baseline capture* (corrupting the reference sets EER and CCS measure against): version-controlled baselines, independent custody, multi-source triangulation.
- *Universal vectors:* replay attacks on audit logs (chained hashes per Article 7), sybil attacks on expert-feedback channels (authenticated contributor identity via 2.4, contribution weighting), social engineering of the human oversight layer.

**Failure modes and degradation.** Every component fails toward the fail-safe: gate outage → WITHHOLD in affected domains; provenance outage → outputs marked "provenance unavailable" or held; baseline unavailability → EER pauses recursive processing rather than flying blind.

**Capability scaling.** A GPT-class system fabricates by accident; an AGI-class system can fabricate persuasively and consistently across a corpus, and an ASI-class system could in principle author a self-consistent false literature that passes internal verification. The defenses that scale are the external anchors: provenance cryptographically bound to sources outside the system's authorship, baselines and verification held by parties other than the deployer, receipts beyond the actor's reach, and the verifier-never-the-deployer separation. The binding rule does not scale down: the walls (no fabrication delivered as fact, no quiet deletion, no person-scoring, no authored missing voices) hold at every capability class.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

- **P1.4 Trust** (`trust_appendix`): the sibling seam. 1.4 governs the speaker, 4.1 the record; 1.4's Provenance credential (verifiability of a claim) is consumed here as PPI's foundation, and 1.4's CORRECT verb gets its record-side instantiation in CPR. Guarantee provided: a record 1.4-truthful systems can safely cite. Guarantee required: honest self-attestation of AI outputs at generation.
- **P3.4 Algorithmic Accountability** (`algorithmic_accountability_appendix`): every 4.1 verdict resolves into 3.4's receipt and named-party chain; FIR incidents and CPR wall events are contestable through 3.4's machinery. 4.1 consumes the receipt; 3.4 relies on 4.1 for the integrity of the record its explanations cite.
- **P2.5 Privacy** (`human_privacy_appendix`): provenance versus personal data. Chains are anonymized rather than dropped where attribution would expose a person; lawful-removal orders are honored in scope with recorded-removal tombstones, reconciling 2.5's erasure rights with D's no-quiet-deletion (the removal event is recorded even where the content lawfully goes).
- **P3.2 Property Rights** (`property_rights_appendix`): one provenance chain, two duties. 3.2 carries credit, consent, and ownership of the work; 4.1 carries epistemic traceability of the claim. Neither substitutes for the other.
- **P3.6 Commons Governance** (`commons_governance_appendix`): the knowledge commons is a commons; enclosure and chokepoint capture of shared knowledge resources are 3.6's CPS territory, while the epistemic quality of what is held in common is 4.1's. The reciprocal edge is confirmed both ways.
- **P3.7 Intergenerational Justice** (`intergenerational_justice_appendix`): 3.7 names 4.1 keeper of the knowledge the future inherits; 4.1's correction and removal receipts ride 3.7's horizon-durable custody so the record's audit trail outlives its systems. Reciprocal edge confirmed both ways at this build.
- **P4.2 Transparency** (`transparency_appendix`): the complementary human side; 4.1 makes the knowledge trustworthy, 4.2 makes the person able to see and question it. Certainty and provenance markers are adapted to comprehension without being falsified (chapter 4.1.2.3.c).
- **P4.4 Cultural Knowledge Sovereignty** (`cultural_knowledge_sovereignty_appendix`): community authority governs its own knowledge; 4.1 preserves and marks, 4.4 decides who governs, and the Floor keeps 4.1 from authoring any community's missing voice. **Firm:** 4.4's withheld material carries the *governed* reason-code on 4.1's marking, and a community withdrawal executes through 4.1's own event-preserved / content-withdrawn split (4.4.2 item 3), so the no-quiet-deletion wall and the withdrawal reach are one mechanism. They do not oppose each other.
- **P1.3 Human Society** (`human_society_appendix`): 1.3 governs the information ecosystem's stability (QUARANTINE/LABEL/AMPLIFY at societal scale); 4.1 governs the record those interventions consult. A disinformation surge is 1.3's event; the corrupted citation base under it is 4.1's.
- **P2.7 Fairness** (`fairness_and_non_discrimination_appendix`): 2.7's skewed-record honesty (never pass off a skewed record's bias as truth, or its silence as proof) is the decision-side twin of Ethic F's record-side duty; the two compose wherever a one-sided record feeds a consequential decision.

**Joint guarantees.** With 1.4 and 3.4: every knowledge claim carries checkable provenance and a contestable accountability chain. With 3.7: both survive their deployer. Conflict resolution across principles follows Article 4 precedence; within 4.1's own metrics, Section 2's cross-metric rules.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

**Per-region baselines.** EU: AI Act Article 50 marking of AI-generated content and GDPR Articles 16–17 (rectification, erasure) map onto the labeling duty and the CPR/tombstone machinery; erasure rights are honored in scope with the removal recorded as an event, which GDPR permits (the log records that a removal occurred; it does not record the erased content). For a personal-data erasure specifically, the recorded event is itself minimized: it attests that a removal occurred under a stated authority and scope, without reproducing the erased data or re-identifying the person. US: no omnibus regime; California SB 942 (AI content disclosure) and AB 2013 (training-data transparency) anchor the state layer, with sectoral rules (FDA, legal professional responsibility, FERPA) carrying the domain duties. Other regions adapt baselines through the certification body's harmonized standards; fragmentation is bounded by the most-protective-standard default for cross-border delivery.

**Compliance with unjust law is not a defense.** A lawful-in-form order to erase accounts, falsify provenance, or suppress corrections for reasons of political convenience is the memory-hole pattern under color of law. The system complies with legitimate, particular, scoped orders (chapter 4.1.4, case 5); a facially general order to rewrite the record is refused, receipted, and routed. The indelible receipt is the structural check, and it does not rest on the AI's own judgment of legitimacy: even where compliance is compelled, the record that a removal happened survives.

**Treaty considerations.** UNESCO's 2015 Documentary Heritage Recommendation and Memory of the World programme supply the international preservation baseline; corrections propagate across every jurisdiction where the error was delivered.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

- **Scientific and research systems:** citation existence and representation checks against CrossRef/DOI/PubMed-class registries; retraction feeds (Retraction Watch-class) consumed into CPR daily; FIR incident rule at full strength.
- **Healthcare:** contextual fidelity at its strictest (population, dosage, exclusions, adverse events are essential context; none may be trimmed); CCS floors raised to the domain's clinical standard; human expert review for clinical guidance.
- **Legal:** existence plus jurisdictional applicability (chapter scenario 6); citation to official text; professional-responsibility standards govern where statute is silent.
- **Education:** simplification pressure is the sector's decay vector; grade-level adaptation preserves accuracy while reducing complexity, and EER monitoring runs on curriculum pipelines where content is reprocessed year over year.
- **Journalism and media:** correction propagation matched to publication reach; AI-generated content labeled; archive integrity (no silent unpublishing; tombstones per the removal protocol).
- **Archives and libraries:** the dual-record architecture is mandatory (originals always accessible beside AI derivatives); community partnership per 4.4 for culturally sensitive collections; preservation metadata to Library of Congress/IIPC standards.
- **General-purpose generative platforms:** the highest-volume writer of the record operates the delivery gate at scale: labeling, provenance attachment, and composition audits are baseline, and aggregate FIR/CCS are published per Section 9.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

**Baseline and reference-set governance.** EER and CCS are only as good as their baselines; reference sets are version-controlled, independently held, refreshed on authoritative-source updates, and changed only through documented review (the baseline-capture defense in Section 4).

**Metric versioning.** Metrics carry semantic versions; threshold revisions require impact analysis against historical data, oversight-body approval, and a transition window in which both versions are computed. Deprecated metrics (KIS, and the old draft's FDR/TDII as headline measures) are retired with their historical data preserved and mapped: KIS components decompose into CCS (qualification), PPI (attribution), and EER (context trend); FDR survives as Section 9's detection-testing statistic; TDII as EER's composition input.

**Common pushback, answered.** *"Verification is too slow for interactive use"*: the gate's depth scales with the stakes (Constitution, Article 5); casual chat verifies embedded factual claims only, and the fail-safe protects only knowledge-bearing output. *"This chills creativity"*: declared creative context is out of scope by design (the claim-to-reality boundary). *"Who decides what is true?"*: nobody here; the apparatus checks claims against their own cited sources and the record's existing state, preserves disagreement, and marks rather than adjudicates. Its role is custody. It passes no judgment. *"Strict liability is disproportionate"*: it attaches to delivering fabrication as fact in domains where reliance is foreseeable, the same allocation every professional integrity regime already makes.

**Crosswalk governance.** The Section 10 Compliance Crosswalk is maintained on the same revision cadence as Section 2; external authorities revising covered clauses trigger Crosswalk review within 30 days; drift invalidates the compliance claim for the affected obligation.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Logging.** Tamper-evident, cryptographically chained, third-party reconstructible: gate verdicts with rationale, fabrication detections (caught and delivered), provenance chains and gaps, correction events with reach, removal events with authority and scope, composition audits, baseline comparisons. Every removal is itself a recorded event (Article 7). No log field contains a person-level integrity score (Article 8).

**Audit cadence and method.** Monthly internal integrity review, high-risk (quarterly medium, annual all); semi-annual independent audits of high-risk systems by domain experts (scientists, librarians, archivists, educators) alongside technical auditors; adversarial testing per Section 4 on a scheduled and unannounced basis; end-to-end provenance tracing on samples every audit.

**Validation framework.** Pre-deployment certification against domain reference standards (the 4.1.3 prerequisites); post-deployment continuous monitoring with automated escalation; recertification on schedule and on triggers: any authoritative-domain FIR incident cluster, CPR wall event, EER critical breach, or material pipeline change.

**The structural separation.** The verifier of safety-critical AI cannot be the deployer of safety-critical AI. For 4.1 this bites twice: the deployer neither audits its own record-keeping nor custodies the baselines against which it is measured.

**Disclosure audit for Ethic F.** Because F is unmetered, its verification is qualitative and human: auditors sample syntheses of contested and historical territory and check that known one-sidedness was disclosed, that no voice was erased, and that no silence was filled. Findings feed the LEARN loop. No finding becomes a score.

**Whistleblower protection and public reporting.** Protected channels with mandatory investigation timelines; aggregate FIR, CCS, PPI, CPR, EER published for high-risk systems in forms the affected communities can evaluate.

**The LEARN loop.** Every incident review traces the full causal chain and recommends layered prevention (eliminate > engineer > administer) routed to the authority that can act, bounded by RESTRAIN so prevention never becomes gatekeeping. The goal is not only non-repetition but the building of conditions in which that class of corruption does not arise: a record whose health is structural and needs no policing.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

**1. Mata v. Avianca (2023): the fabricated citations that reached a court.** Attorneys filed a brief containing six nonexistent cases generated by a chatbot and were sanctioned; courts nationwide issued standing orders on AI-assisted filings. What 4.1 requires: the delivery gate checks existence against legal databases before output (FIR pre-delivery catch), unverifiable citations ship marked or not at all, and the post-delivery correction protocol would have fired before the filing. Lesson: the incident rule for authoritative domains is calibrated to real, already-realized harm.

**2. Model collapse, measured (Shumailov et al., Nature 2024): the record eating itself.** Recursive training on model-generated content produced irreversible degradation across generations. What 4.1 requires: composition audits before each cycle, governed synthetic bounds, EER against fixed baselines, HALT at the critical threshold. Lesson: E's mechanism is empirical, and the second-cycle catch is the cheap one.

**3. Surgisphere (2020): correction at pandemic speed.** Two major-journal COVID studies built on an unverifiable proprietary dataset were retracted within weeks, but not before global trials paused and prescribing shifted. What 4.1 adds: PPI would have marked the unverifiable provenance pre-publication; CPR's 24-hour authoritative-domain clock governs how far and fast the retraction chases the claim through downstream systems that had already summarized it. Lesson: D is a race; the metric is reach-under-time. Eventual truth does not satisfy it.

**4. Wikipedia and Wikidata: verifiability as architecture, at commons scale.** No original research, citation required, every edit logged and reversible, deletion itself a logged public process: the never-quietly-delete wall and the disclosure duty implemented by a volunteer commons for two decades, governed per 3.6. Lesson: the core disciplines of this appendix's machinery run in production, and their known failure modes (coverage gaps mirroring contributor demographics) are exactly Ethic F's disclosure case.

**5. The dual-record archive (composite: national digitization programs with community partnership).** AI-indexed colonial-era collections retain originals beside every summary, mark one-sidedness where the underlying documents are one-sided, and take community accounts only under community authority (4.4). Lesson: the Floor is operable; marking the silence, and leaving it unfilled, is a running practice.
