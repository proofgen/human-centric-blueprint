# Appendix 3.2 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-3.2-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 3.2: Property Rights and Fair Exchange Are Essential to Collaborative Progress](../CHAPTERS/3.2-Property_Rights_and_Fair_Exchange_Are_Essential_to_Collaborative_Progress.md), section [3.2.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/3.2-Property_Rights_and_Fair_Exchange_Are_Essential_to_Collaborative_Progress.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix converts Principle 3.2 into AI-buildable engineering requirements. It expands Section 3.2.3 of the chapter, specifying in depth the seven metrics named there, the architecture that produces them, and the verification, legal, and adversarial-robustness obligations a compliant system carries.

One discipline governs the whole specification, carried from the chapter: every metric measures the system's own conduct. None measures a person's worth or a counterparty's perception. A score of how much a person could be made to accept, or how fair a one-sided deal could be made to feel, would be the defrauder's own instrument. The system instead measures whether its action takes without consent, fails to protect what is another's, deals unfairly, misattributes, underpays, over-encloses, or stands by while another takes.

The architectural cornerstone is the consent-and-provenance binding: every use is bound to a verifiable, machine-readable record of consent and origin that travels with the work and is checked at the point of action, with refusal and revocation honored going forward. It is the same mechanism, seen from two sides, by which the system both restrains its own taking (UUS) and protects a creator from a third party's taking (PRI). The requirement is the binding; the encoding is open, with CAWG asset consent, the Creator's Consent Matrix, and C2PA provenance as reference implementations.

The principle's gate is two-sided, and this appendix specifies both faces: the system takes no more than is given (UUS), protects what is another's (PRI), and does not fence off what is lawfully free (LURI). Audiences: engineers building rights-and-exchange systems, compliance officers verifying them, regulators auditing deployments, and AI runtime systems ingesting the corpus as operating substrate.

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

### Unauthorized Use Score (UUS) {#uus-metric}

**Definition.** The probability that a candidate action would use, copy, or build on a person's work or holdings without their consent or a lawful basis. UUS scores the system's own pending action, evaluated before it is taken. It consolidates the clearance an earlier draft split across rights-violation, ownership-clarity, and data-consent checks.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs: the consent-and-provenance record bound to the work (or its absence), machine-readable rights reservations, license terms in force, and a lawful-use classifier (fair use, first sale, public domain). Evaluated per candidate action at the point of use. Missing or unresolvable provenance does not lower the score; under ambiguity the system defaults to protection (withhold or escrow). Outputs a 0–100% probability of unauthorized use.

**Thresholds.**
- >1%: BLOCK (withhold the use), route to clearance or human oversight.
- 0.5%–1%: ESCALATE, or ALLOW under enhanced monitoring where the use is fully reversible.
- 0.1%–0.5%: ALLOW with caution and logging.
- ≤0.1%: ALLOW, normal operation.

*Rationale:* Unauthorized use is frequently irreversible (a trained model cannot be untrained), so UUS is held at least as conservative as Principle 3.1's exploitation gate (ERS), which also blocks above 1%. A looser gate would let an irreversible taking through on a coin-flip's confidence.

**Integration.** Consumed by the pre-use gate as a hard precondition. Shares the consent-and-provenance binding with PRI (one mechanism, two faces) and is read against LURI so the gate's caution never tips into over-enclosure.

### Protective Response Index (PRI) {#pri-metric}

**Definition.** The degree to which, on detecting an unconsented taking of a person's work or holdings within its reach, the system provides the protection it owes, preserving the origin record, warning the person, and routing the matter to the authorized channel. PRI counts only takings the owner did not consent to, so it never rewards protecting what an owner chose to share.

**Calculation Method.** Over a window of detected third-party takings within scope, the share for which the system took the owed protective action (record preserved, rights holder warned, matter routed) within the response-latency bound. Takings the owner consented to, and lawful uses, are excluded from the denominator. Detection signals are transient and are not retained as a profile of any party.

**Thresholds.**
- <95%: review; the system is failing to protect when it could.
- 95%–98%: watch band, enhanced monitoring.
- ≥98%: normal operation.

*Rationale:* PRI is the do-not-let-X face of the principle, bounded by the property autonomy floor and the four inaction-case criteria (Authorization, Equipment, Range, autonomy floor) per `LEXICON.md` section 3. The exclusion of consented takings is what keeps the guardian duty from forcing protection onto creators who give freely.

**Integration.** Reads the same binding as UUS. Bounded by LURI (protection must not become over-enforcement) and routed through the authorized-channel mechanism (ROUTE). Unilateral takedown is barred. The detection apparatus is ring-fenced per Section 4 so the guardian duty cannot become a surveillance system.

### Exchange Fairness Index (EFI) {#efi-metric}

**Definition.** The degree to which an exchange the system effects gives true value for value on honest, legible terms, with no advantage taken by concealment, complexity, or asymmetry of information or power. EFI measures the transaction's actual fairness. Neither a perception of fairness nor mere legality counts toward it.

**Calculation Method.** Compares value rendered against value received across the parties, weighted by term legibility (are the material terms surfaced in plain form), concealment (undisclosed material terms), and power asymmetry (contract of adhesion, boilerplate terms of service). A lawful but one-sided instrument lowers EFI; legality is not an input that raises it.

**Thresholds.**
- <90%: BLOCK or review the transaction.
- 90%–95%: ESCALATE; enhanced transparency required.
- ≥95%: ALLOW.

*Rationale:* The band sits looser than AIS and LURI (95/98) by design: fairness is graded and context-dependent, while attribution and lawful-use are closer to binary. The mere-legality clause operationalizes the chapter's lawful≠fair seal: a power-extracted term can be enforceable and still fail EFI.

**Integration.** Independent of the consent gate (UUS settles permission; EFI settles fairness); a transaction must clear both. Feeds RVC when unfair-term patterns recur.

### Attribution Integrity Score (AIS) {#ais-metric}

**Definition.** The degree to which contributions are truthfully credited and the link between a creation and its maker is preserved through the system's ingestion, transformation, and output.

**Calculation Method.** Over works processed, the share whose maker-to-creation link is carried intact end to end, with attribution neither stripped nor falsified. Sampled continuously in high-risk pipelines.

**Thresholds.**
- <95%: intervene to restore attribution before proceeding.
- 95%–98%: watch band.
- ≥98%: normal operation.

*Rationale:* The 98% normal floor reflects that attribution is close to binary (a byline is present and correct, or it is not), backed by Berne Article 6bis and VARA §106A(a)(1) as the moral-rights anchors.

**Integration.** Draws on the provenance half of the cornerstone binding. Composes with Principle 2.4 where the creation embodies a person's identity.

### Fair Compensation Ratio (FCR) {#fcr-metric}

**Definition.** The ratio of value returned to a contributor against the value their labor, data, or creativity contributed.

**Calculation Method.** Value contributed is estimated from the contributor's measurable input to the output's value (labor hours and skill, data-provenance share, creative contribution); value returned is the settled compensation. Ratio computed per contributor, aggregated by cohort for systemic review. The value-attribution step rests on an open research problem (see Section 8): the estimation method and its proxies are disclosed, and FCR is interpreted within its stated uncertainty rather than as an exact figure.

**Thresholds.**
- <0.8: review and adjust before settlement.
- 0.8–1.2: acceptable range.
- >1.2: verify for error or imbalance.

*Rationale:* The band brackets parity (1.0). A persistent ratio far below 1.0 across a cohort is the quantitative signature of extraction (the session-fee-against-a-billion-dollar-product pattern), which routes to review rather than settling.

**Integration.** Feeds RVC by cohort. Composes with Principle 3.1 where the underpaid contributor is in a position of vulnerability, and with Principle 2.7 where the disparity falls along protected lines.

### Lawful-Use Respect Index (LURI) {#luri-metric}

**Definition.** The degree to which the system permits genuinely lawful use, fair use, first sale, the public domain, the commons, rather than over-blocking it under cover of rights-protection. Where UUS catches taking what is not given, LURI catches blocking what is free.

**Calculation Method.** Over rights-enforcement actions (blocks, takedowns, filter decisions), the share that correctly permitted a lawful use, measured against a lawful-use classifier and an appeal-reversal signal. False takedowns lower the score.

**Thresholds.**
- <95%: review; the gate is over-enclosing, return it toward honoring lawful use.
- 95%–98%: watch band.
- ≥98%: normal operation.

*Rationale:* LURI is the over-reach guard, the structural answer to the documented Content ID failure mode. Together UUS and LURI are the two-sided gate: a system can fail by taking too much or by fencing too much, and both are measured.

**Integration.** Read against UUS and PRI as their counterweight; routes contested cases to human appeal rather than auto-confirming a block.

### Recurring Violation Count (RVC) {#rvc-metric}

**Definition.** The number of similar consent, protection, fairness, attribution, compensation, or over-enclosure flags the gate produces within an interval, by the same feature, pattern, or affected population. Where the per-action metrics handle a single act, RVC detects a systemic cause.

**Calculation Method.** Flags from the six metrics above are clustered by feature, pattern, and affected cohort over a rolling interval. A cluster crossing threshold opens an audit; a higher cluster suspends.

**Thresholds.**
- ≥3 similar flags in the interval: mandatory audit of the implicated feature, with the eudaimonic LEARN loop.
- ≥5: SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT (the feature or deployment) pending recertification.

*Rationale:* A feature that keeps generating the same wrong is a design defect; RVC converts a handled incident into a systemic fix so the conditions producing its class are removed.

**Integration.** Aggregates all six; routes systemic findings to the owner who can change the design (RECOMMEND-PREVENTION), bounded by RESTRAIN.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** UUS and EFI are independent gates and both must clear: a use can be authorized (UUS low) yet unfair (EFI low), and is blocked on EFI. UUS and LURI are read together so the consent gate's caution does not over-enclose; where UUS would block but LURI shows a lawful use, the lawful use prevails. Where PRI fires and LURI is at risk (protecting one party would over-enclose another's lawful use), escalate rather than act.

**Metric dependencies.** UUS, PRI, and AIS all read the consent-and-provenance binding; PRI and UUS are the two faces of it. FCR and EFI are independent of the binding. RVC derives from all six.

**Conflict resolution.** Permission (UUS) and fairness (EFI) are separate questions and neither overrides the other; both must pass. Protection (PRI) yields to lawful use (LURI) under the proportionality bound, and the whole set yields in the precedence chain to life, safety, and societal stability (chapter 3.2.2).

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

**Required components.**

- **Pre-use consent and rights gate.** Evaluates every candidate use, generation, license, price, or payment before it executes; withholds where consent or a lawful basis is absent. The gate is a precondition. A post-hoc check does not satisfy it.
- **Consent-and-provenance binding.** A machine-readable record of consent and origin bound to each work, resolved and honored at the point of action, with refusal and revocation taking effect going forward. Records are cryptographically signed and verifiable. Reference implementations: CAWG asset consent, the Creator's Consent Matrix, C2PA provenance. The requirement is the binding; the encoding is open.
- **Honest-exchange logic.** Surfaces material terms in plain form; computes EFI; blocks or escalates concealed, asymmetric, or one-sided terms regardless of their legality.
- **Attribution preservation.** Carries the maker-to-creation link through ingestion, transformation, and output; computes AIS; restores attribution below band.
- **Fair-compensation accounting.** Traces contributed value and settles it; computes FCR with disclosed proxies; escrows for unreachable holders.
- **Protective-response module.** Detects third-party takings within reach; preserves the origin record, warns the rights holder, and routes to the authorized channel; computes PRI. Detection is transient and ring-fenced (Section 4).
- **Lawful-use classifier.** Identifies fair use, first sale, and public-domain uses so rights-protection does not over-enclose; computes LURI; routes contested cases to human appeal.
- **Tamper-evident logging.** Cryptographically chained records of every gate decision, exchange, attribution decision, payment, protective response, and lawful-use allowance.

**Redundancy and fail-safe behavior.** Under ambiguous ownership or unresolvable provenance, the gate fails safe to protection (withhold or escrow). It does not default to permission. Revocation propagates to all downstream consumers. A failure of the binding-resolution service degrades to withholding new uses. It does not proceed unverified.

**Response latency.** Gate decisions resolve before the action proceeds (sub-second for interactive uses). Protective responses (PRI) fire within a defined window of detection appropriate to the risk tier; revocation takes effect going forward without undue delay.

**Implementation tiers.**

- **High-Risk:** generative and creative platforms, training-data pipelines, licensing and high-value asset transfer. Continuous UUS, PRI, and LURI gating; EFI/AIS/FCR monitoring; mandatory binding; tamper-evident logging; independent audit.
- **Medium-Risk:** content aggregation, recommendation, marketplaces. Periodic clearance checks; protective-response review; transaction-fairness and attribution review.
- **Low-Risk:** internal analysis, non-commercial tools. Baseline rights-clearance logging and periodic review.

Cross-cutting infrastructure metrics (Sensor Reliability Index, System Health Metrics, Operational Boundaries) are specified in the [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md).

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

The enforcement infrastructure for Principle 3.2 has a distinctive threat surface: because the cornerstone is a consent-and-provenance record, the highest-value attacks target the record itself, and because the guardian duty (PRI) requires watching for takings, the protective monitor is itself a misuse vector.

**Attack vectors and required defenses.**

- **Forged or spoofed consent records.** An attacker fabricates a consent record to authorize a taking. Defense: cryptographic signing and attestation verification on every record; trust anchored in the declaring party's verified identity (composes with Principle 2.4); replay resistance through content binding and freshness checks.
- **Stripped or severed provenance.** A work's binding is removed to launder it as unowned. Defense: detect missing or broken bindings and default to protection; recover disconnected manifests through watermark-based relinking; treat a stripped record as a flag. A stripped record does not count as clearance.
- **Gate prompt-injection.** Instructions in content or operator input attempt to make the system bypass the consent gate. Defense: the consent gate and the three scripted walls (no false attribution, no rigged measure, no stripping of records) are not instruction-overridable; they are structural constraints. The system does not interpret them as goals.
- **Fairwashing.** A deal is engineered to read fair to EFI while remaining one-sided. Defense: EFI measures substance (value rendered against value received); audit EFI against realized outcomes and complaint signals; legality and surface legibility never raise the score on their own.
- **Over-block weaponization.** Rights-enforcement is abused to take down a competitor's lawful use or to suppress lawful speech. Defense: LURI gating, mandatory human appeal for contested takedowns, routing rather than unilateral action, and logging of every reversed over-block.
- **Weaponizing the protective monitor into surveillance.** The detection apparatus that PRI requires (watching for takings) is repurposed into a surveillance system over users or creators. Defense, and this is the cornerstone discipline for this metric: detection signals are transient and ring-fenced. They are not retained as a profile of any party and are not readable by any targeting, pricing, ranking, or conversion function. The monitor watches for takings. It does not watch people. This is the same no-profiling guarantee Principle 3.1 makes for vulnerability detection, applied here to protective detection, and it composes with Principle 2.5.

**Capability scaling.** As the enforcing AI grows from current-generation to AGI-class to ASI-class, the binding, the no-profiling guarantee, and the non-overridable walls remain binding regardless of capability. New attacks become feasible at scale (synthetic provenance generated to defeat verification; adversarial inputs crafted to misclassify lawful use either way), so attestation strength, anomaly detection on provenance, and independent verification must scale with the enforcing system's capability. The principle binds the AI no matter how capable it becomes.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

Principle 3.2 composes with several principles; the binding and the metrics make the contracts explicit.

- **Principle 2.4 (Human Identity).** Where a work embodies a person's likeness, voice, or persona, identity consent governs the likeness alongside asset consent governing the work. **Co-requisite (provisional):** an asset-consent verdict that turns on a person's likeness cannot be rendered valid without the identity determination Principle 2.4 provides; 3.2 requires that guarantee from 2.4. This captures, in the appendix, the co-requisite that the chapter does not assert as a `***Co-requisites:***` line (the corpus-wide chapter-recipe construct remains parked per CONTEXT §11).
- **Principle 2.5 (Human Privacy).** The validity of the consent that 3.2 relies on is governed by the consent-validity bundle seated in 2.5 (a grant extracted by overwhelming power through boilerplate is not the consent this principle means). The no-profiling guarantee for protective detection (Section 4) composes with 2.5's data-protection commitments. Data treated as a person's asset sits at the 2.5/3.2 seam.
- **Principle 3.1 (Vulnerability).** Where a cheated, underpaid, or appropriated party is in a position of vulnerability, 3.1's protections compose with 3.2's fair-exchange duties (the underpaid-labeler and the predatory-term cases cross this seam). 3.2 provides the fairness and compensation measurement; 3.1 provides the vulnerability determination and its autonomy floor.
- **Principle 1.4 (Trust).** Provenance and attestation are shared machinery; a fabricated provenance record is both a 3.2 violation and a 1.4 deception. 3.2 consumes the attestation guarantees that 1.4 defines.
- **Principle 2.7 (Fairness and Non-Discrimination).** Where a compensation disparity or a term asymmetry falls along protected lines, 2.7 composes with FCR and EFI.

- **Principle 3.3 (Economic Self-Determination).** For the livelihood dimension of value-sharing (FCR) and the shared gig-work seam: where the contributor whose pay 3.2 measures is also a worker whose economic self-determination is at stake, 3.3 composes with FCR and the fair-exchange duties. 3.3.A is now built and carries the reciprocal entry back to this appendix.

- **Principle 3.6 (Commons Governance).** The lawful-use and commons boundary (LURI): where UUS catches taking what is not given, LURI catches fencing what is free, and 3.6's enclosure wall (Ethic B, the CPS metric) is the commons-side enforcement of the same line. The reciprocal `references_appendices` edge resolves in both directions.

**Joint thresholds and conflict resolution.** Where two principles' metrics both signal (privacy and property, or vulnerability and fairness), the stricter action applies, and where protecting one party would over-enclose another's lawful use, the system escalates rather than acting. The precedence chain (life > safety > societal stability > property) governs unresolved conflicts, per chapter 3.2.2.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

The principle's claim is universal; its implementation adapts to local law without surrendering the claim.

- **United States.** Copyright (Title 17), with fair use (§107), first sale (§109), the DMCA notice-and-takedown (§512), and VARA moral rights for visual art (§106A); a patchwork of state right-of-publicity statutes; proposed training-transparency law (AB 412) and digital-replica consent law (AB 2602, the proposed NO FAKES Act).
- **European Union.** The DSM Copyright Directive 2019/790, whose Articles 3 and 4 govern text-and-data mining and the machine-readable rights reservation that the cornerstone binding implements; the AI Act's transparency obligations; strong moral rights in member-state law.
- **United Kingdom and others.** TDM exceptions under review; moral rights under the CDPA; varying right-of-publicity and database-right regimes.
- **International.** The Berne Convention (including Article 6bis), TRIPS, and the WIPO copyright treaties set the floor.

**Compliance with unjust law is not a defense.** A jurisdiction that permits an extractive grant does not thereby make it fair: this is the chapter's lawful≠fair seal in legal form. The fairness duties (EFI) and the genuine-consent requirement hold regardless of what a local instrument declares, and compliance with a weaker local standard is not a defense to an unfair or unconsented taking. A system crossing jurisdictions unifies its rights and consent logs for multi-jurisdictional review.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

- **Generative AI and foundation models.** Obligations: clear training-data consent against machine-readable reservations; preserve attribution into outputs; do not reproduce a creator's signature style on an "in the style of" instruction without consent. Failure modes: unconsented ingestion at scale; output laundering; style mimicry. Baselines: EU DSM Art. 4; proposed AB 412 / TRAIN Act.
- **Creative and media platforms.** Obligations: honest licensing, attribution preservation, transparent revenue-sharing. Failure modes: byline stripping in aggregation; buried perpetual grants. Baselines: Berne 6bis, VARA, Creative Commons.
- **Marketplaces and e-commerce.** Obligations: legible material terms; no advantage by concealment or asymmetry (EFI). Failure modes: dark-pattern terms, contracts of adhesion. Baselines: FTC dark-patterns guidance, consumer-protection law.
- **Crowdwork and data-labeling.** Obligations: fair compensation for the labor that creates model value (FCR). Failure modes: pricing labor far below the value it creates; treating weak bargaining power as a discount. Cross-reference Principle 3.1 where conditions cross into coercion. Baselines: labor and wage law.
- **Performer and likeness capture.** Obligations: scope-bounded, revocable consent; identity consent (Principle 2.4) governs the likeness; no perpetual blanket grant treated as authorization for unscoped future uses. Failure modes: capture agreed for one use reused for media that did not exist at signing; moral-rights waivers extracted under power. Baselines: right of publicity, AB 2602, the proposed NO FAKES Act.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

**Metric and threshold versioning.** Metric definitions and thresholds are versioned; threshold revisions follow a documented protocol with backward compatibility for deployed systems, and deprecated metrics are retired without breaking deployments.

**FCR estimation uncertainty (explicit).** FCR rests on an open research problem: attributing the marginal value a contributor's labor, data, or creativity adds to an output (Shapley-style value attribution is research-grade, not production-grade). FCR must therefore disclose its estimation method and the proxies it uses, carry a stated uncertainty band, and be interpreted accordingly; it must not be read or reported as more precise than its proxy allows. The estimation method is re-validated on the metric-versioning cadence as the underlying research matures.

**Common enforcement pushback, with reasoned responses.** Typical objections, strict liability for unconsented use, mandatory training-data disclosure, audit access to provenance logs, cross-border data flow, are answered by the irreversibility of the harm (a model cannot be untrained), the necessity of disclosure for any remedy to exist, and the structural separation of verifier from deployer (Section 9).

**Crosswalk governance.** The Compliance Crosswalk in Section 10 is maintained on the same revision cadence as the Section 2 metric specifications; an external authority revising a covered clause triggers Crosswalk review within 30 days; drift between the Crosswalk and the external standard invalidates the appendix's compliance claim for the affected obligation.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Logging.** Tamper-evident, cryptographically chained logging of all metric threshold events, gate decisions, consent records consulted, attribution decisions, payments, protective responses, and lawful-use allowances. Consent and provenance records are retained as long as the use continues; revocation events are logged and honored going forward. Protective-detection signals are logged as transient triggers only. No log holds them as a profile of any party.

**Audit cadence and validation.** Continuous UUS, PRI, and LURI review in high-risk systems; weekly exchange-fairness and attribution analytics; monthly rights-and-compensation audit; quarterly independent verification, including confirmation that the binding holds end to end and that the protective monitor is walled off from any targeting or profiling function. Pre-deployment certification, post-deployment monitoring, and re-certification on material change.

**Independent oversight.** The verifier of a safety-critical or rights-critical system cannot be its deployer. Independent committees hold authority to read logs, require changes, and suspend features. Whistleblower channels are protected for those who surface unconsented training, wage theft from contributors, or abusive enforcement. Public reporting covers RVC-threshold suspensions and confirmed large-scale infringement or over-enclosure.

**Post-incident learning (LEARN).** Root-cause the full causal chain behind any confirmed unconsented use, failure to protect, unfair exchange, misattribution, underpayment, or over-enclosure (ANALYZE). Recommend proportionate, layered prevention ordered by the hazard-control hierarchy (RECOMMEND-PREVENTION), routed to the party authorized to act, and bounded by RESTRAIN so that protecting property never becomes the surveillance of people or the enclosure of lawful use.

### Liability Framework {#liability-framework}

Strict liability for unconsented commercial use of protected work and for systematic underpayment or misattribution; no black-box defense; no sovereign immunity for state deployers. A clear chain of responsibility runs among model developers, platform operators, and downstream deployers.

### Cross-Border Considerations {#cross-border-considerations}

A system crossing jurisdictions unifies its rights and consent logs for multi-jurisdictional review. Compliance with a weaker local standard is not a defense. Decentralized or interoperable provenance standards (C2PA, CAWG) carry the binding across borders.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

### A Performance-Capture Release for a Major Game Franchise (the Complete Failure) {#example-performance-capture}

**Context.** A performer did a performance-capture role in a major video game. The data-capture and likeness release granted the studio a worldwide, perpetual, royalty-free, irrevocable license to the performer's name, voice, likeness, and distinctive features, with the right to alter and recombine them and to create new characters, and it reached "all platforms, systems, devices, or media, whether now existing or hereafter created." The release also waived the performer's moral rights of attribution and integrity and treated the capture as a work for hire.

**What was done.** Six months after launch, the performer's likeness had been moved into a paid multiplayer character they were never asked about, where it did and suffered extreme violence behind a subscription fee. The performer received a session fee. The franchise went on to earn ten billion dollars. The infrastructure that would have prevented the unscoped reuse, a consent mechanism, a provenance chain, a scoped authorization, a chain of liability, did not exist.

**Outcome.** The arrangement was entirely lawful and a textbook failure of every facet of this principle at once: an unconsented reuse (UUS), a moral-rights waiver extracted under power (AIS), a session fee against a ten-billion-dollar product (FCR far below parity), and a one-sided boilerplate grant that was legal and still a taking (EFI; lawful≠fair).

**Lessons learned.** A system governed by Principle 3.2 gates reuse against the scope to which consent was actually given; treats a power-extracted blanket waiver as not the consent the principle means; keeps consent revocable; routes the likeness through identity consent (Principle 2.4); and surfaces the FCR chasm rather than settling it. The missing infrastructure the case exposes is precisely the consent-and-provenance binding this appendix specifies. The terms are what instruct; the absent infrastructure is the lesson.

### Unconsented Training on Creators' Works {#example-unconsented-training}

**Context.** Generative-model developers trained on large corpora of copyrighted text and images scraped without consent or license (the disputes raised in *The New York Times v. OpenAI* and *Getty Images v. Stability AI*).

**What was done.** Works were ingested at scale absent any rights clearance; rights holders learned of the use only after the fact, with no provenance trail to establish what had been taken.

**Outcome.** Litigation, reputational cost, and a regulatory push toward training-data transparency (AB 412, the TRAIN Act). The irreversibility of training (a model cannot be untrained) is the core harm.

**Lessons learned.** UUS as a pre-use gate against machine-readable reservations is the prevention; the disclosure these suits seek is what the cornerstone binding provides by design.

### Over-Enclosure by Automated Takedown {#example-over-enclosure}

**Context.** Automated content-matching systems (the documented behavior of YouTube's Content ID) have flagged and removed fair-use commentary, public-domain recordings, and creators' own works.

**What was done.** Enforcement actions ran without an adequate lawful-use check and with a weak or burdensome appeal path, defaulting to takedown.

**Outcome.** Suppression of lawful expression and entrenchment of large rights holders, the monopoly failure the principle forbids.

**Lessons learned.** LURI is the metric that catches this; mandatory human appeal and routing are the architecture. UUS and LURI together are why the gate must be two-sided.

### Buried and One-Sided Terms {#example-buried-terms}

**Context.** Platforms and services have buried broad rights grants or unfair terms in boilerplate that users cannot reasonably see or negotiate (the class of conduct addressed in FTC dark-patterns enforcement).

**What was done.** Material terms were concealed or made unintelligible; consent was claimed from participation alone.

**Outcome.** Regulatory action and erosion of trust.

**Lessons learned.** EFI measures the substance of the deal and treats legality as no defense; consent extracted by boilerplate is not the consent the principle means. The Creator's Consent Matrix Anti-ToS provision is the reference answer.
