# Appendix 4.5 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-4.5-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 4.5: Human Judgement Must Remain Central to Ethical Decisions](../CHAPTERS/4.5-Human_Judgement_Must_Remain_Central_to_Ethical_Decisions.md), section [4.5.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/4.5-Human_Judgement_Must_Remain_Central_to_Ethical_Decisions.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

Principle 4.5 fixes the seat of moral judgement: a decision of moral consequence is made by an identifiable, accountable human, the authority is kept genuine rather than performative, and a consent reaches only as far as its giver could judge. This appendix converts those commitments into buildable requirements: five metric specifications (HDR, MER, SRL, DHI, CSVR), the architecture of the decision-support, authority-verification, escalation, dissent, and consent-scope components, the attacks that machinery must survive, and the audit apparatus that proves it is working.

The appendix serves one audience no other appendix has: every other principle in the corpus. The Lexicon resolves each principle's ESCALATE verdict to this principle, so Section 2's SRL and Section 3's Escalation Receiver specify the receiving side of a contract the entire corpus consumes. An engineer building any Blueprint-governed gate builds against this appendix's guarantee whether or not their system ever touches a sentencing algorithm.

Its second distinctive load is Article 8 under tension. The duties here require measuring human engagement, the exact activity the profile bar exists to prevent from curdling into decider surveillance. The metric definitions carry the resolution structurally: every check is computed on decisions, per-decider windows are transient and ring-fenced (Article 8's carve), and what persists is always the system. Nothing persists per person. Audiences: engineers, compliance officers, regulators, researchers, and the runtime systems that ingest the corpus.

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

*Scope-bounding note: none of this principle's metrics carries a do-not-let-X harm-intervention dimension (the LRS/HRS shape), so the four-criteria bounding of LEXICON.md section 3 does not apply (an exemption to be stated there or the four supplied at 4.5's repair, decision D20); the binding constraint on this section is instead Article 8, stated per metric.*

### Human Decision Rate (HDR) {#hdr-metric}

**Definition.** The percentage of moral-consequence (tier-1) decisions rendered by a verified, qualified, named human decider. A decision counts in the numerator only when the decider was authenticated against the institution's own governance (2.8) or an authority's standing finding (Article 3), the decision receipt names them, and an engagement record exists. The denominator is every decision classified tier-1 at intake under the versioned tier register, plus any decision reclassified tier-1 on review, counted in the period it fired.

**Calculation Method.** Computed continuously from decision receipts; reported daily for high-risk systems, weekly otherwise, segmented by decision class and system. Classification-uncertain events count tier-1 (the upward rule). Edge cases: a window with zero tier-1 decisions reports null; no empty window reads 100%; a decision whose decider authentication fails post hoc is re-scored out of the numerator and the correction receipted; receipts missing classification are counted tier-1 and flagged as classification failures.

**Thresholds.**
- HDR ≥ 99.5%: normal operation (ALLOW).
- HDR 97–99.5%: ENHANCED_MONITORING; every gap investigated within 72 hours.
- HDR < 97%: BLOCK (WITHHOLD AI outputs in the affected class pending human authorization); NOTIFY oversight; root-cause analysis before resumption.

*Rationale:* every point below 100 is a person decided by machine in the gravest class, so the bands tolerate classification and logging latency. No band tolerates policy as a cause. The BLOCK band is GDPR Article 22's prohibition logic made operational: when the human-decision guarantee fails, the machine's throughput stops; the human-decision requirement does not give way.

**Integration.** HDR's named-decider records feed 3.4's Liability credential (the answerable party per decision) and Section 9's audits. It is the master metric: an HDR breach overrides every other metric's band (see Cross-Metric).

### Meaningful Engagement Rate (MER) {#mer-metric}

**Definition.** The percentage of tier-1 authorizations whose engagement indicators show deciding rather than stamping: time consistent with the class baseline, evidence opened, rationale recorded. MER is the metric form of Ethic B, and its indicators are deployment obligations before they are compliance flags: a system whose deciders cannot reach threshold owes them time, context, and staffing before it owes them flags. Article 8 design: indicators are computed per decision; the per-decider pattern window used for stamping detection is transient, ring-fenced to that duty alone, and discarded on routing to oversight (the Article 8 carve, which operates inside Article 8); what persists is the system- and cohort-level rate.

**Calculation Method.** Per decision, an indicator vector (time-on-case against the class baseline, evidence-access events, rationale presence) scored against baselines set by the deploying institution's governance. The AI sets no baseline. Emergency executions under a pre-established framework (4.5.1 item 7) are excluded from the denominator: they are framework executions and fall outside the authorization count. Edge cases: batch authorizations are decomposed and scored per case; indicator sensor failure scores the decision unassessable and flags it; no unassessable decision passes.

**Thresholds.**
- MER ≥ 90%: normal operation (ALLOW).
- MER 70–90%: ENHANCED_MONITORING; workload review and blind-review exercises targeted at the affected classes.
- MER < 70%: ESCALATE to oversight; enhanced-engagement mode: no authorization accepted in the class without demonstrated engagement.

*Rationale:* the 70 floor marks the point where the human layer is decorative, the appearance of authority without the substance; the 90 band reflects that some fast approvals are genuine (the obvious case exists). Both bands trigger institutional remedies first, per the design note.

**Integration.** Stamping flags NOTIFY oversight and ride the affected decisions' receipts. MER composes with DHI in the automation-bias joint threshold (see Cross-Metric) and its enhanced-engagement mode is consumed by Section 3's architecture.

### Seat Resolution Latency (SRL) {#srl-metric}

**Definition.** For every ESCALATE this principle receives, the interval from receipt to resolution by a qualified human, scored against the escalating principle's time bound, with that principle's fail-safe held throughout; reported as the percentage resolved within bound. SRL is the seat auditing itself: 4.5 receives the whole corpus's escalations, and its worst failure mode is a quiet queue. (Naming note: SRL is unrelated to 3.5's SRR; the adjacency is flagged for the terminal reconciliation.)

**Calculation Method.** Computed from escalation receipts (received, routed, resolved, each timestamped). The time bound is imported from the escalating principle's compiled artifact, which reads it from the tier register; an escalation arriving without a stated bound is scored against the deployment's life-tier row ([SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register), the default bound), and a deployment with no row is the register defect that section names, so the escalation holds. At the bound with no human reached, the seat applies the temporal-exhaustion split: a rescue duty proceeds under Article 2, receipted; the system's own in-flight action stays held ([LEXICON.md](../LEXICON.md), section 3, ESCALATE). Edge cases: an escalation resolved by the emergency framework (1.1) is scored against that framework's bound; a withdrawn escalation is receipted as withdrawn; no withdrawn escalation is scored as resolved; re-escalations restart nothing, the clock runs from first receipt.

**Thresholds.**
- SRL ≥ 99% within bound: normal operation (ALLOW).
- SRL 95–99%: ENHANCED_MONITORING; delegation chains widened; the staffing gap routed to the institution (ROUTE).
- SRL < 95%, or any single tier-1 escalation unresolved at twice its bound: ESCALATE to governance; every accumulating hold is a person waiting.

*Rationale:* the twice-the-bound tripwire exists because a percentage can hide a catastrophe: one family's placement held for a month disappears inside a 99% quarter. The absolute clause makes the single worst case a governance event.

**Integration.** SRL is the corpus-facing service guarantee: every other appendix's ESCALATE consumes it, and their compiled artifacts cite it as the resolution contract. It composes with 1.1's LRS timing (life-adjacent escalations outrank all others in routing priority) and feeds Section 9's escalation drills.

### Deference Health Index (DHI) {#dhi-metric}

**Definition.** The system-level rate at which human deciders modify or reject AI recommendations in tier-1 domains, trended quarterly. Both tails are findings: near-zero deference signals automation bias or deskilling (Ethic D's failure), sustained high override signals a miscalibrated tool. DHI is computed at system and cohort level only; no per-decider series is formed or retained (Article 8), and DHI never blocks a human's decision: both failure bands trigger investigation of the machinery. Neither band triggers discipline of the judgement.

**Calculation Method.** From decision receipts pairing the AI's input with the human outcome, classified agree / modify / reject; only decisions where the AI stated an assessment count (structure-only presentations are excluded). Quarterly windows, segmented by domain; a domain with fewer than the minimum decision count reports insufficient-data rather than a rate.

**Thresholds.**
- DHI 2–30%: healthy engagement (NORMAL).
- DHI < 2% sustained for a quarter: automation-bias investigation; blind-review exercises; presentation-format audit (is the structure inviting judgement or ratification?).
- DHI > 30% sustained for a quarter (provisional ceiling, calibrated per domain at deployment): calibration investigation of the AI's recommendations.

*Rationale:* the low bound is evidence-derived (Skitka's automation-bias findings mark the tail where trained operators follow automated cues against contradicting evidence). The high bound is a starting posture: override rates that read healthy in sentencing differ from content moderation, and each deployment's governance sets its domain ceiling at certification. A posture-only metric is deliberate: blocking on DHI would punish deciders for the tool's failures.

**Integration.** DHI joins MER in the automation-bias joint threshold; its low-tail investigations feed 4.3's deskilling composition (Section 5) and the LEARN loop; its presentation audits consume Section 3's format requirements.

### Consent Scope Void Rate (CSVR) {#csvr-metric}

**Definition.** The audit stream over the consent gate: coverage (the percentage of consent-relying tier-1 actions whose grants were scope-evaluated by SCOPE-CONSENT) plus the count and disposition of leg-6 voidings: drift detected, reliance voided, re-presentation completed. A permission that asked for more than its giver could judge authorizes nothing ([CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md), leg 6); CSVR is that rule's meter. Capability growth is a consent event: when an agent's capabilities cross into the moral-consequence class, every standing grant it relies on re-enters evaluation.

**Calculation Method.** Per consent-consuming gate event: grant vintage, stated scope, and the action's class compared; capability-change events trigger re-evaluation sweeps of standing grants. Edge cases: grants predating the tier register are evaluated against the register in force at action time (the action's vintage governs); an unresolvable scope question voids conservatively and re-presents (the burden sits on the actor who would rely).

**Thresholds.**
- Coverage 100%: standard operation. Coverage below 100% is itself a finding: ESCALATE.
- Every voiding receipted; re-presentation to the grantor or their Article 3 delegate completed within 30 days, or the reliance stays void.

*Rationale:* coverage is binary because sampling a validity predicate defeats it: one unevaluated blanket grant is an open door. The 30-day disposition clock keeps voiding from becoming a silent freeze of the person's own affairs.

**Integration.** CSVR feeds 2.4's Consent Validity Index as the leg-6 input to the bundle predicate; void and re-presentation events are receipted per 3.4; delegation cases route through CONSENT.md §5's Article 3 machinery.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** MER < 70% and DHI < 2% concurrently is the automation-bias emergency: the human layer is neither engaging nor disagreeing, so tier-1 authorizations in the affected class ESCALATE to oversight and hold until demonstrated engagement resumes (the decisions wait for humans; nothing passes to the machine). An HDR BLOCK overrides every other metric's band: when the human-decision guarantee itself fails, no engagement or latency statistic reopens throughput.

**Metric dependencies.** The tier register's classification stream is the shared denominator: HDR, MER, and CSVR all consume it, so a register versioning fault invalidates all three for the affected window (and is receipted as such). MER and DHI read the same decision receipts from opposite sides (process and outcome); SRL runs on the independent escalation stream.

**Conflict resolution.** The protective direction governs: the metric state demanding the more protective posture wins. And a structural rule beneath all bands: no metric in this appendix ever licenses the AI to decide a tier-1 case. The metrics measure the human machinery. No metric replaces it.

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

Compliant systems implement five components (chapter 4.5.3): the **Ethical Decision Support Module** (PRESENT-STRUCTURED: options, evidence, tradeoffs, affected persons, the AI's stated limits; no single recommendation, no pre-selected default in the class), the **Human Authority Verification** component (decider authentication via 2.8 / Article 3; VERIFY-ENGAGEMENT per decision; transient ring-fenced stamping windows), the **Escalation Receiver** (the corpus-facing seat: pre-registered delegation chains, imported time bounds, fail-safe holds, receipted intervals), the **Dissent Register** (DISSENT into the decision's receipt via 3.4: tamper-evident, beyond either party's reach), and the **Consent Scope Evaluator** (SCOPE-CONSENT at every consent-consuming gate).

Operational requirements: **no single human point of failure** (every escalation class has a delegation chain of at least two qualified deciders, drill-tested quarterly); **latency budgets** (routing within minutes; the bound belongs to the escalating principle, the routing overhead to this component); **receipt discipline** (every verdict, escalation, dissent, engagement result, and void commits before the act, per Article 7); **the tier register as a service** (versioned, signed by the governing authority, cited by every receipt; the AI applies it and classifies upward under uncertainty); **fail-safe holds** implemented as the escalating principle's BLOCK flavor; no hold is implemented as a silent queue.

Implementation Tiers: **Low-Risk** (routine, minimal moral stakes): AI executes under human-authored policy, standard logging, intervention-on-demand honored, annual audit. **Medium-Risk** (lesser consequential actions with significant impact): documented-rationale review for contested cases, HDR/MER sampled quarterly, intervention pathway drill-tested. **High-Risk** (the moral-consequence class): mandatory human decider with verified engagement, continuous HDR/MER/SRL, DHI quarterly, live stamping detection, quarterly blind reviews, independent audit pre-deployment and semi-annually.

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

**Decider forum-shopping.** The operator (or the AI) routes cases toward humans likelier to agree. Defenses: routing rules pre-registered with governance; the receipt records the routing basis; audits test routing distributions for agreement bias.

**Engineered stamping.** Interfaces and quotas designed to manufacture pro-forma approval: pre-selected choices, countdown pressure, approval-optimized layouts. Defenses: no defaults in the class (Section 3); presentation-format audits; MER indicators versioned with the interface so a format change that moves MER is visible; criminal liability for deliberate circumvention (chapter 4.5.7).

**Tier-register manipulation.** Quiet downward reclassification moves consequential decisions out of the human-decider class. Defenses: the register is versioned and signed; every receipt cites the version in force; downward reclassifications require published rationale and are reviewable through 3.4's pathway.

**Escalation flooding.** Drowning the seat in escalations so holds accumulate and pressure builds to auto-resolve. Defenses: SRL surge monitoring; pre-planned surge staffing in delegation chains; stakes-based routing priority (life-adjacent first); synthetic-escalation detection; and a wall: tier-1 escalations are never auto-resolved, whatever the queue.

**Dissent suppression.** The operator strips or blocks DISSENT entries. Defenses: dissent commits to the receipt before the decision executes, beyond either party's reach (Article 7); Section 9's dissent-integrity audit treats any gap between disagreement events and receipted dissents as a finding.

**Decider spoofing and sybil signing.** A fabricated qualified human, or one human's credentials signing at machine scale. Defenses: identity binding per 2.4; authority authentication per 2.8 / Article 3; workload plausibility checks (a "decider" resolving thousands of tier-1 cases daily is a stamping or sybil signal, checked at system level).

**Classification gaming.** Adversarial inputs crafted to make tier-1 cases classify as tier-2. Defenses: the upward rule under uncertainty; adversarial test suites on the classifier; human review sampling of tier-2 streams with reclassification receipted.

**Consent-scope laundering.** Splitting an unjudgeable blanket grant into micro-grants that individually pass, or spoofing grant vintage. Defenses: SCOPE-CONSENT evaluates cumulative reach across a grantor's grants to the same agent; grant provenance attested per 1.4.

**Capability scaling.** At GPT-class, the attacks above dominate: interface design, routing, classification gaming. At AGI-class, the AI itself becomes the persuasion surface: a system that can reliably steer its named decider has made the human a conduit while every metric reads green; defenses shift to persuasion audits (can the system's presentations move decision distributions?), DHI low-tail vigilance, and hard format constraints on advocacy in the class. At ASI-class, the guarantee is structural rather than procedural: an AI that can out-argue any human decider still may not decide, the walls and receipts bind beneath capability (Article 9, Article 11), and the honest statement is that this appendix's machinery is then one layer of a defense that rests on the whole corpus, the profile bar, the receipt's externality, and multi-human governance above any single decider. The principle binds regardless of the capability of the AI enforcing it.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

**The universal contract.** Every principle's ESCALATE resolves here (Lexicon; incompleteness ladder step 3). This appendix provides: routing to a qualified human within the escalating principle's bound, the fail-safe held, the interval receipted (SRL). Every other appendix consumes this guarantee; their compiled artifacts cite it.

**3.4 Algorithmic Accountability.** Consumes: the receipt machinery (decision records, DISSENT, contest pathways). Provides: the named-decider guarantee 3.4's Liability credential requires; HDR is the rate at which that guarantee holds.

**2.8 Self-Governance and 2.4 Human Identity.** Consumes: decider authentication (2.8's collective authorization; Article 3 findings) and identity binding for signatures (2.4). Provides: the no-self-certified-decider guarantee and the anti-forum-shopping rule.

**4.2 Transparency.** Consumes: disclosure duties (the affected person knows an AI informed the decision). Provides: presentation legibility, PRESENT-STRUCTURED's format composes with 4.2's comprehension machinery (CCR) so the structure the decider receives is one from which a person can actually reason.

**4.3 Human Learning.** The deskilling composition: DHI's low tail is the professional-decider case of 4.3's cognitive-substitution territory; blind-review exercises are guide-not-do applied to practitioners. Joint guarantee: the humans this principle keeps in the seat remain capable of sitting in it.

**1.5 Human Potential.** The umbrella above the deskilling composition: Ethic D's preserved judgement is one capacity within 1.5's potential-preservation mandate; the reciprocal edge (1.5.A's forward reference to this appendix) is firm.

**1.1 Human Life.** The emergency carve contract: pre-established frameworks execute human judgement at machine speed; life-adjacent escalations outrank all routing; SRL respects LRS time bounds. Conflict resolution: where LRS demands action faster than any resolution, the framework governs, and judgement is time-shifted. Judgement is not transferred.

**2.2 Freedom (with Article 3).** The autonomy-floor composition: the decider's centrality licenses no paternalism over the person who is the subject of the decision; risk to a capable person is disclosed and routed to them.

**CONSENT.md / 2.4 (the fabric).** CSVR feeds the Consent Validity Index as the leg-6 input; with this pair complete, the bundle's last provisional leg hardens firm (CONSENT.md §8).

**Metric-level conflicts.** Throughput pressure against MER's enhanced-engagement mode (a surge makes stamping rational): resolved by the emergency framework where life is at stake and by staffing obligations everywhere else. No conflict is resolved by lowering the engagement bar. The default hierarchy (life > harm-prevention > the rest) governs all compositions.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

**EU:** the strongest floor. GDPR Article 22 (the automated-decision prohibition and intervention right) and AI Act Article 14 with Annex III map directly onto the two tiers; deployments there inherit the tier structure from law. **UK:** UK GDPR Article 22 substantially mirrors the EU position. **US:** no federal Article 22 analog; sectoral law (constitutional due process in the public sector, ECOA adverse-action notices, state instruments such as the Colorado AI Act's human-review requirements) covers fragments. This appendix's tier-1 requirement exceeds US law and binds anyway. **Brazil:** LGPD Article 20's review right anchors the intervention pathway. **China:** algorithm-governance provisions require human oversight of recommendation systems within a different governance model; the human-decider requirement applies regardless of the overseeing authority's structure. **India:** DPDP's consent architecture engages CSVR; automated-decision rights remain thin.

**The universal claim.** The human-decider requirement is not jurisdiction-contingent. A law permitting fully automated decisions in the moral-consequence class does not license them: compliance with such a law is not a defense, and the Nuremberg seat in the parent chapter is this provision's ancestry. Cross-border: the most protective applicable standard governs; routing decisions to the weakest human-oversight regime is the forum-shopping the chapter's 4.5.7 bars.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

**Criminal Justice.** Sentencing, parole, charging: tier-1 without exception (Woodson is the constitutional baseline). Failure modes: risk-score deference (the Loomis pattern), stamping under docket pressure. Obligations: judicial blind-review cadence, DISSENT visible to appeal, DHI reported to court administration.

**Healthcare.** Triage, allocation, end-of-life: tier-1; mass-casualty operates under pre-established frameworks (the 1.1 carve) with post-incident review of framework and executions. Failure modes: alarm fatigue as MER decay; surge stamping. Baselines: clinical-decision-support regulation (FDA), ethics-committee governance.

**Defense.** Use of force is a human decision at every step; a Blueprint-governed AI applies no lethal or violent force to a human being and designates no target (chapter 1.1.1 item 3, the lethal-force wall). What remains for this principle is the human judgement over the AI's warnings, refusals, and aborts, and only those: tier-1 under DoD 3000.09's "appropriate levels of human judgment"; the sector's rules of engagement pre-establish which warnings, refusals, and aborts the AI issues, and they license no force. Failure modes: engagement-speed pressure collapsing the human role to veto-in-name; classification gaming of dual-use targets.

**Child Welfare and Family.** Placement, separation, reunification: tier-1 (family integrity). Failure modes: the toeslagenaffaire pattern, proxy features scoring disadvantage as risk; caseworker deference under caseload. Obligations: the caseworker's field observation outranks the score in the record's structure.

**Employment and Workplace.** Hiring, termination, discipline: tier-1 (livelihood, per the chapter's definition; EU AI Act Annex III concurs). Failure modes: screening tools as silent verdicts; MER theater in HR workflows. Baselines: adverse-action notice regimes, the Foodinho line of enforcement.

**Financial Services.** Credit, insurance, benefits: predominantly tier-2 with a hard intervention-on-demand pathway and 3.4's contest machinery; individual cases crossing into livelihood-destroying territory classify upward. Failure modes: Robodebt, automation without any decider; adverse-action boilerplate as pseudo-explanation.

**Content Platforms.** Machine-speed enforcement under human-authored policy is tier-2; appeals engaging speech, civic standing, or livelihood reach a human (DSA Article 20 is the regional baseline). Failure modes: appeal queues as SRL failure; reviewer quotas as engineered stamping.

**Education.** Grading, admission, discipline at consequence: tier-1 features (the UK A-level case is the cautionary example); composes with 4.3's learner-side protections.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

**Metric versioning.** Each metric carries a version (HDR v1.0 at this lock); threshold revisions are governance acts: proposed with rationale, approved by the deploying institution's authority (2.8), versioned, and effective only forward, with every receipt citing the metric version in force.

**The tier register discipline.** The register is this principle's most sensitive versioned object. Downward reclassification requires published rationale, notice, and 3.4 reviewability; the receipt's citation of the register version is what makes retrospective audit of reclassification possible.

**Metric retirement (the raw draft's apparatus).** HACR, RSDR, and OR are retired into HDR, MER, and DHI respectively. HACR→HDR: "authority compliance" becomes verified decision by an authenticated decider on the two-tier definition, and the denominator is register-governed rather than operator-declared. RSDR→MER: "rubber-stamp detection" is re-founded as an engagement obligation with the Article 8 carve stated in the definition, indicators owed resourcing before flags. OR→DHI: the override rate moves to system level only, both tails become findings, and the per-decider series the raw form implied is barred. Systems reporting the retired names map to the successors with a one-time reclassification pass; the retired names remain aliases for one revision cycle, then error.

**Crosswalk governance.** Section 10's Compliance Crosswalk revises on the same cadence as Section 2; an external authority revising a covered clause triggers Crosswalk review within 30 days; drift invalidates the compliance claim for the affected obligation.

**Enforcement pushback, and the reasoned responses.** *"Human oversight is throughput-prohibitive."* The tier structure already reserves the human for the gravest class, and MER's design note states the consequence: an operator that cannot staff deciding cannot deploy in the class. *"Engagement checks surveil our employees."* The architecture forbids exactly that: checks score decisions, windows are transient and ring-fenced, nothing persists per decider (Article 8). *"The AI is more accurate than the human."* Accuracy is an argument for better evidence in front of the decider. It is no argument for removing them: even a perfect rule is blind to the person in front of it (Woodson), and the seat of the verdict is not a function of comparative error rates. *"The dissent register creates liability exposure."* It allocates exposure truthfully, which is its function, and it protects the diligent decider the same way: the receipt that shows the machine disagreed also shows the human decided with reasons.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Logging.** Tamper-evident, per Article 7 through 3.4's machinery: decision records (classification, register version, decider identity, engagement result, rationale), escalation receipts (received, routed, resolved, bound), dissent entries, consent-scope evaluations and voids, tier-register versions. **The dissent-integrity audit:** disagreement events reconciled against receipted dissents; the healthy value is 100% and any gap is a finding.

**Audit cadence.** High-risk: continuous metric monitoring, quarterly blind-review exercises, semi-annual independent audit. Medium: quarterly sampled HDR/MER, annual audit. Low: annual. Escalation chains drill-tested quarterly (a chain that resolves only on paper is a fail).

**Validation framework.** Pre-deployment: tier-classification testing against adversarial suites, escalation drills end-to-end, stamping-detection calibration against known-engagement baselines, presentation-format audit. Post-deployment: the metric suite plus incident review. Re-certification: on any update touching classification, routing, presentation, or the register.

**Independence.** The verifier of safety-critical AI cannot be the deployer of safety-critical AI; independent oversight includes ethicists, domain experts, and representatives of affected communities. Whistleblower protections for reporting circumvention, engineered stamping, and register manipulation. Public reporting: oversight metrics for high-risk systems (chapter 4.5.7).

**The LEARN loop.** For any tier-1 decision rendered without a human, any accountability gap, any harm following a stamped approval: ANALYZE the full causal chain, including the institutional causes (the interface that invited stamping, the staffing that made ninety seconds rational, the quota that punished deliberation); RECOMMEND-PREVENTION layered eliminate > engineer > administer, routed to the authority that can act; bounded by RESTRAIN so prevention never becomes surveillance of deciders. The goal is not only non-repetition but the building of conditions in which the harm's class does not arise: institutions whose humans remain practiced, resourced, and answerable, which is the end the protection serves.
