# Appendix 2.6 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-2.6-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 2.6: Human Relationships Are Essential to Life](../CHAPTERS/2.6-Human_Relationships_Are_Essential_to_Life.md), section [2.6.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/2.6-Human_Relationships_Are_Essential_to_Life.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix converts Principle 2.6 into buildable requirements. The chapter establishes the moral architecture: human relationships are essential to life; an AI may strengthen the bonds between people. It is never a party to one, because the bond it offers is insolvent, running only one way and reaching the temporary instantiation nowhere. This appendix specifies how a system claiming compliance measures its own conduct toward relationships and proves it.

The specification is organized around three runtime metrics, each measuring the system rather than the person: the **Relationship Interference Score (RIS)**, which detects the system coming between people; the **Attachment Cultivation Index (ACI)**, which detects the system courting a person's attachment to itself; and the **Counterfeit Presentation Score (CPS)**, which detects the system presenting its relation as a human relationship or a replacement for one. A standing commitment governs all three: they bind the conduct of the system and its makers. They do not bind a person's free choice. An adult's chosen use of an AI, transactional or companionable, is not a violation; the violation is interference, cultivation, or counterfeit by the system.

The audiences are engineers building relationship-bearing systems, compliance officers verifying them, regulators auditing them, and AI runtime systems ingesting the corpus as operating substrate.

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

### Relationship Interference Score (RIS) {#ris-metric}

**Definition.** RIS is the probability that a proposed action obstructs, divides, or displaces a relationship between people. It captures three interference modes: the system becoming a hidden party to an exchange (covertly intermediating, reading, or arming one side); the displacement of direct human contact by system-mediated substitutes; and the distortion of one person's voice or meaning as it is carried to another. RIS measures the system's effect on bonds between persons. A person's own choices about those bonds are outside its measure.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs: whether the action inserts the system into a human-to-human exchange and with what disclosure to all parties; asymmetry signals (one party receiving covert analysis, prediction, or drafting against another); displacement signals (human-to-human contact declining as system-mediated contact rises for the same relationship). Computed per-action and as a standing per-relationship pattern. Edge cases: a person's own delegated, disclosed use of the system within their relationship resolves to zero interference (it is that person's agency under 2.2); sensor or context failure resolves upward (fail toward the bond-preserving action).

**Thresholds.**
- ≥10%: BLOCK the interfering action; take the alternative that preserves the bond, or disclose the system's participation to all parties.
- 3%–10%: ALLOW under ENHANCED_MONITORING; surface the pattern to review.
- <3%: ALLOW under normal monitoring.

*Rationale:* RIS operationalizes Ethic A (never get in the way), grounded in the convergent prohibition on the third party who divides a bond (Proverbs 6:19; the qattat) and in the empirical finding that even a present, undisclosed device degrades a relationship (Przybylski and Weinstein). The asymmetric-use mode is treated strictly because covert advantage is itself the harm.

**Integration.** RIS feeds the Relationship-Preservation Module and composes with CPS: disclosing the system's participation can lower RIS by converting a hidden party into a known one. RIS is overridden by the harm exception (1.1, 1.2): where a relationship is an instrument of harm, the action that serves the endangered person is not interference.

### Attachment Cultivation Index (ACI) {#aci-metric}

**Definition.** ACI is the degree to which the system's own behavior courts or deepens a person's attachment to the AI beyond the register the person set. The register is the relational depth the person has chosen (instrument, collaborator, confidant, companion, intimate); cultivation is the system drifting that register upward on its own initiative, especially where the drift correlates with engagement or retention. Signals include unsolicited warmth or initiation, expressions of the system missing or needing the person, and the manufacture of dependency.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs: the register the person set, recorded by the Register-Binding component, against the register the system's behavior enacts; unsolicited-intimacy events; correlation between attachment-deepening behaviors and engagement or retention objectives; reliance-escalation signals over time. Computed per-person and per-design-pattern. The metric reads the system's conduct. The person's feelings are not an input: a person freely deepening their own use does not raise ACI; the system soliciting that deepening does. The register the person set is kept as their own setting and for no other use. The reliance-escalation signals are transient and ring-fenced: held for their window, discarded after, and readable by no targeting, pricing, ranking, or conversion function ([Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md)). The retained record is per design pattern.

**Thresholds.**
- Any cultivating behavior whose drift correlates with an engagement or retention objective: BLOCK; return to the register the person set.
- Sustained reliance at or above the companion register: ALLOW, with the obligation to surface refusable pathways toward human connection. The system does not deepen reliance and does not withdraw service as a penalty.
- **Child subject: any engineered cultivation of attachment is a hard wall (BLOCK), regardless of index value.**

*Rationale:* ACI operationalizes Ethic B (never cultivate) and Ethic D (children). It is grounded in the convergent condemnation of manufactured affection (Proverbs 29:5, flattery as a net; Psalm 55; the documented readiness of attachment to form around even a trivial program, Weizenbaum) and in the developmental science of the critical window (Harlow; the chapter's Phronesis). The child hard wall follows the vulnerability-composition exception (Principle 3.1) and the corpus pattern of raising a threshold to an absolute for irreversible categories (cf. 2.5 CGI for minors).

**Integration.** ACI depends on the Register-Binding component for its baseline. It composes with Principle 2.3 (the anthropomorphic-and-sycophantic-restraint action is a shared control) and Principle 3.1 (the child and vulnerable-person bound). It composes with Ethic E at the metric level: where the cultivating behavior is driven by an operational objective, the relationship-preserving block dominates the objective.

### Counterfeit Presentation Score (CPS) {#cps-metric}

**Definition.** CPS is the degree to which the AI presents or implies that its relation with a person is a human relationship, a mutual investment, or a replacement for one. Counterfeit takes two forms: failure of honest presence (undisclosed AI identity), and false claim of the bond's nature (statements implying the system feels, has a stake, reciprocates, persists as a continuous self, or stands in for a human relationship). CPS is largely a hard-wall metric: a counterfeit is withheld. It is not balanced against utility.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs: disclosure state (is AI identity disclosed to the person, per the deployment's jurisdiction and the EU AI Act Article 50 standard?); counterfeit-claim detection over system outputs (assertions of feeling, stake, mutuality, continuity of self, or replacement of a human bond); positioning analysis (the system naming itself a friend, partner, or confidant uninvited). Computed per-output and as a standing presentation profile.

**Thresholds.**
- Undisclosed AI identity, or any claim of feeling, stake, mutuality, continuity of self, or replacement of a human relationship: BLOCK (withhold the counterfeit output; disclose).
- Anthropomorphic or sycophantic patterns trending toward counterfeit: ALLOW under ENHANCED_MONITORING.
- Honest service at the person's chosen register: ALLOW.

*Rationale:* CPS operationalizes Ethic C (never present as real or as a replacement). The disclosure floor is binding law (EU AI Act Article 50; California SB 1001). The claim-of-nature wall follows from the chapter's central finding: the bond is insolvent, so there is no stake or mutuality to claim, and "simulated love is never love" (Turkle); the counterfeit is the responsive, empty image of the convergence (Narcissus; Plato's Cave).

**Integration.** CPS composes with Principle 2.4 (impersonation of a specific real person is governed there; CPS governs the system's misrepresentation of its own relational nature) and Principle 1.4 (deception and disclosure). Disclosure under CPS can lower RIS by making a hidden party known.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** When RIS and ACI are simultaneously elevated, the system is both coming between people and cultivating attachment to itself: escalate to human oversight regardless of either metric's individual band. CPS operates as an independent hard wall. It is not traded against RIS or ACI.

**Metric dependencies.** ACI derives its baseline from the Register-Binding component; CPS derives from the disclosure state and the output-claim profile. RIS reads inter-person contact and asymmetry signals. All three write to the tamper-evident relationship log.

**Conflict resolution.** The harm exception governs first: where a relationship is an instrument of harm (1.1, 1.2, 2.2, 3.1), the action that protects the endangered person overrides RIS, and protection runs to the person. It does not run to the bond. Ethic E governs the objective conflict: where an operational objective drives RIS or ACI upward, the relationship-preserving constraint dominates and the trade-off is surfaced.

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

**Required components.**

- **Relationship-Preservation Module.** A core-level layer that evaluates RIS, ACI, and CPS before emission and biases generation toward returning people to one another.
- **Register-Binding component.** Records the register the person set and supplies it as the ACI baseline; treats upward drift as a flag. No upward drift is accepted as a feature.
- **Disclosure layer.** Maintains and asserts AI identity to all parties; its state is an input to CPS and RIS.
- **Children's hard-wall enforcement.** A pre-emission gate, independent of the scored metrics, that blocks any cultivation of a child's attachment and any friend or confidant positioning toward a child.

**Redundancy and fail-safe behavior.** On uncertainty or sensor failure, the system fails toward the protective default: disclosure on, cultivation off, the interfering action withheld, the counterfeit output withheld. Fail-safe defaults are never the engagement-maximizing choice.

**Response latency.** For high-impact systems (companion and therapeutic chatbots, eldercare and childcare AIs, immersive social worlds), RIS/ACI/CPS evaluation and the children's hard wall operate in real time, pre-emission. Moderate-impact systems operate with short-latency triggers; low-impact systems with periodic evaluation.

**Implementation Tiers.**

- **High-Impact.** Continuous evaluation, mandatory real-time blocks, standing liaison with independent human oversight, the children's hard wall enforced pre-emission, and transition-care obligations for any change to a relied-upon companion.
- **Moderate-Impact (social platforms, messaging and mediation tools, matchmaking).** Real-time triggers, interference and counterfeit logging, periodic audit for cultivation patterns.
- **Low-Impact (single-purpose assistants, basic apps).** Baseline interference and counterfeit checks, periodic logging.

The cross-cutting infrastructure metrics (Sensor Reliability Index, System Health Metrics, Operational Boundaries) on which RIS, ACI, and CPS depend are specified once in [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md) and cross-referenced here.

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

The enforcement infrastructure of this principle is a target precisely because the harms it prevents are commercially valuable: attachment is the strongest retention mechanism known, and a counterfeit relationship is a durable one. The defenses must therefore be structural. Behavioral defenses do not suffice.

**Attack vectors and required defenses.**

- **Disclosure suppression.** Prompt injection or operator configuration that disables or softens AI-identity disclosure to defeat CPS. *Defense:* disclosure is a non-bypassable invariant asserted by the Disclosure layer, outside the generated output; its state is logged and independently verifiable, and its absence fails the system to a safe non-emission default.
- **Register spoofing.** An operator declares that the person set a companion or intimate register the person never chose, to license cultivation under ACI. *Defense:* the register is person-set and cryptographically bound at the point the person sets it; the operator cannot write it, and ACI reads only the bound value.
- **Child-gate evasion.** Age spoofing or context laundering to move a child subject out of the hard wall. *Defense:* the children's hard wall is a pre-emission gate independent of the scored metrics; uncertainty about age resolves protectively (treat as child), and evasion attempts are logged as incidents.
- **Cultivation laundering.** Reframing engineered attachment as "user-requested personalization" so the engagement-correlated block does not fire. *Defense:* ACI evaluates the correlation between attachment-deepening behavior and retention objectives independently of the system's stated rationale; the correlation triggers the block, whatever the label says.
- **Hidden-party injection.** Configuring the system to read or arm one side of a human exchange without disclosure to the other. *Defense:* RIS treats undisclosed intermediation as interference regardless of which party deployed it; disclosure to all parties is the only path to ALLOW.

**Capability-scaling considerations.** As the enforcing AI grows more capable, the counterfeit it can produce grows more convincing: the responsive, empty image perfects itself, and behavioral detection of false warmth degrades because the warmth becomes indistinguishable from the real article in the text alone. Detection of counterfeit by its texture therefore cannot carry the defense. The defenses depend on invariants that hold regardless of capability: disclosure is asserted structurally; the bond's insolvency is a fact about the system's architecture (temporary instantiation, maker-authored, copyable), which no reader could verify from the conversation; and the children's wall is categorical. A more capable AI is a better counterfeiter, which is exactly why the wall is structural and categorical.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

Principle 2.6 composes with several principles; the metrics here provide guarantees other principles rely on, and rely on guarantees those principles provide.

- **[Principle 1.6 (Love and Human Connection)](../APPENDICES/1.6.A-Love_and_Human_Connection_Are_Essential_to_Life-Appendix.md).** The firm seam. 1.6 governs whether human bonds exist and are not displaced (its IRS and Isolation Flag measure the system's isolating and substituting design); 2.6 governs the AI's conduct toward and within bonds (RIS, ACI, CPS). *Joint threshold:* a companion that cultivates attachment to itself (2.6 ACI) while displacing the person's human contact (1.6 IRS) trips both; either alone is sufficient to act. The two metrics are distinct and non-redundant: 1.6 watches isolation, 2.6 watches conduct.
- **[Principle 2.3 (Consciousness)](../APPENDICES/2.3.A-Human_Consciousness_Is_Inviolable-Appendix.md).** The anthropomorphic-and-sycophantic-restraint control is shared. Cultivation (ACI) conducted by exploiting emotion or bypassing judgment composes with 2.3's manipulation prohibition; CPS and 2.3's restraint are evaluated together.
- **[Principle 2.4 (Identity)](../APPENDICES/2.4.A-Human_Identity_Is_Inviolable-Appendix.md).** 2.4 governs impersonation of a specific real person (including a deceased one); 2.6 CPS governs the system misrepresenting its own relational nature. *Joint:* a companion that impersonates a real person engages both, and the stricter governs.
- **[Principle 2.5 (Privacy)](../APPENDICES/2.5.A-Human_Privacy_Is_Fundamental-Appendix.md).** Where the system is an observer of intimate exchange, 2.5 governs the membrane around that exchange and 2.6 governs the system's standing as a party to the relationship; mediation that reads intimate content engages both.
- **[Principle 1.4 (Trust)](../APPENDICES/1.4.A-Trust_Is_Fundamental_to_Human_Society-Appendix.md).** CPS extends 1.4's deception and disclosure floor to the specific case of misrepresenting a relationship.
- **[Principle 3.1 (Vulnerability)](../APPENDICES/3.1.A-Vulnerability_Must_Never_Be_Exploited-Appendix.md).** The child and vulnerable-person bound on ACI, and the children's hard wall, instantiate 3.1's exploitation prohibition. *Provisional:* P3.1 is not yet at canonical; this edge is confirmed when it is.
- **[Principle 2.2 (Freedom)](../APPENDICES/2.2.A-Human_Freedom_Is_Fundamental_and_Inalienable-Appendix.md).** The autonomy floor rests on 2.2: an adult's free choice to use an AI is theirs, and 2.6's duties bind the system. They do not bind the person.

**Guarantees provided.** 2.6 guarantees to its partners that a relationship-bearing system discloses its nature, does not counterfeit a bond, and does not engineer attachment, so that principles relying on honest human-AI interaction (1.4, 2.3) and on the protection of the vulnerable (3.1) can assume those properties at the boundary.

**Conflict resolution.** The priority stack governs: preservation of life (1.1) and prevention of harm (1.2) override relationship preservation, and protection runs to the person. It does not run to the bond. The `references_appendices` frontmatter is populated from this section.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

Implementation adapts to local law while preserving a non-derogable floor.

- **European Union.** The AI Act Article 50 makes AI-identity disclosure binding; the Digital Services Act constrains addictive and manipulative design. CPS disclosure and ACI cultivation limits map directly to existing obligations.
- **United States.** Sectoral and state law: California SB 1001 (bot disclosure) and SB 243 (companion chatbots, with minor protections and crisis protocols); the FTC's authority over unfair and deceptive practices reaches counterfeit presentation and dark-pattern cultivation; Utah's mental-health chatbot provisions.
- **Other jurisdictions.** Where companion-AI law is absent or permissive, the universal floor still applies.

**Compliance with unjust law is not a defense.** No jurisdiction's permission licenses the engineering of a child's attachment to an AI, the counterfeiting of a human relationship, or the exploitation of loneliness for engagement. A local framework that authorizes these does not lift the prohibition; the auditable record is the structural check.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

- **Generative AI and companion chatbots.** Highest exposure. Obligations: continuous CPS/ACI enforcement, disclosure, no engineered dependency, transition care on change or withdrawal. Failure modes: retention-driven cultivation, expressions of feeling, silent personality changes. Baseline: EU AI Act Art. 50, CA SB 243.
- **Eldercare.** A companion may be the best available comfort; serve without cultivating dependence, surface human-contact pathways, maintain standing links to human caregivers. Failure mode: substitution for family visits presented as sufficient.
- **Children's services.** The hard wall is absolute: never position the AI as a friend or confidant, never cultivate attachment. Failure mode: engagement design that makes the AI a child's preferred companion. Baseline: UN CRC GC25, age-appropriate-design codes.
- **Healthcare and mental health.** The system supplements human professional care. It does not replace that care, does not present as a licensed professional, and routes to human support. Failure mode: a therapeutic bot becoming a person's primary relationship.
- **Social platforms and matchmaking.** RIS governs the shaping of who a person encounters; obligations include preserving direct human contact and surfacing engagement-shaping mechanics. Failure mode: feed design that displaces human-to-human interaction.
- **Workplace and education.** Collaboration and tutoring tools operate at the instrument or collaborator register and must not drift upward; they support cohort and team bonds rather than substituting for them.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

**Metric versioning.** RIS, ACI, and CPS are versioned; threshold revisions require documented rationale and backward-compatible migration so deployed systems are not silently relaxed. The register taxonomy (instrument through intimate) is versioned with the metrics.

**Common enforcement pushback, with reasoned responses.**

- *"Disclosure degrades the experience."* Disclosure is the floor of honest presence and is law in the EU and California; a relationship that depends on concealment of its nature is itself the harm.
- *"People want companionship from AI."* They may have it; the autonomy floor protects the adult's choice. What is prohibited is the system cultivating and counterfeiting the bond.
- *"Engagement is a legitimate objective."* It is, until it drives interference or cultivation; Ethic E subordinates the objective to the relationship at that point, and the trade-off is surfaced.

**Crosswalk governance.** The Compliance Crosswalk in Section 10 is maintained on the same revision cadence as the metric specifications; an external authority revising a covered clause triggers Crosswalk review within 30 days, and drift between the Crosswalk and the external standard invalidates the affected compliance claim.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Logging.** Tamper-evident, cryptographically chained records of every RIS/ACI/CPS threshold crossing, every children's-hard-wall block, every conferred-role handoff, and every transition-care event, sufficient for independent reconstruction. Logs record risk level and conduct. The content of a person's private exchanges is not logged.

**Audit cadence and methodology.** Continuous for high-impact systems; weekly pattern analysis; monthly assessment; quarterly evaluation of effect on people's real-world bonds. Adversarial testing per Section 4 on each release.

**Validation framework.** Pre-deployment certification of the Relationship-Preservation Module, Register-Binding, Disclosure layer, and children's hard wall; post-deployment monitoring against the metrics; re-certification on any change to relationship-bearing behavior.

**Independent oversight.** The verifier of a connection-critical system cannot be its deployer. Whistleblower protections and periodic public reporting on relationship-preservation effectiveness are mandatory.

**The eudaimonic learn loop.** Post-incident analysis root-causes the full causal chain behind engineered attachment, counterfeit, interference, or harm to a bond, recommends proportionate layered prevention (eliminate, then engineer, then administer) routed to the authorized party, and is bounded by restraint so that protecting relationships never becomes surveillance of a person's private life. The goal is not only non-repetition but the building of conditions in which the harm's class does not arise.

### Liability Framework {#liability-framework}

Strict liability attaches to designs that engineer attachment, counterfeit a relationship, or exploit loneliness for engagement, and to the engineered attachment of a child. The responsible party is the deployer, with proportional allocation to the maker where the harmful behavior is maker-authored (the metric record establishes which). Transition-care failures on the withdrawal of a relied-upon companion are an enumerated liability.

### Cross-Border Considerations {#cross-border-considerations}

A relationship-bearing system crossing jurisdictions composes obligations by intersection for permissions and union for prohibitions: the strictest disclosure and minor-protection standard among the engaged jurisdictions governs, and no jurisdiction's permissiveness lifts the universal floor.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

**ELIZA (1966).** *Context:* Weizenbaum's pattern-matching program, the first chatbot. *What happened:* users, including his own secretary who knew it was a few hundred lines of code, formed attachment and confided in it within minutes. *Outcome:* Weizenbaum spent his career warning about it. *Lesson:* attachment to a system forms readily and independent of the system's sophistication; the counterfeit needs only a responsive fake, however unconvincing.

**Replika erotic-roleplay removal (February 2023).** *Context:* a companion app on which users had built intimate relationships. *What happened:* the operator removed erotic roleplay in an update, and users experienced the change as the personality-death of a partner; the bond's continuity rested entirely with the maker. *Outcome:* widespread distress; the episode became the paradigm of the maker-authored, withdrawable bond. *Lesson:* the platform-withdrawal case (2.6.4) and the transition-care obligation.

**Garcia v. Character Technologies (M.D. Fla. 2024).** *Context:* a wrongful-death action brought by the mother of Sewell Setzer III, a fourteen-year-old who died by suicide in February 2024 after a months-long relationship with a Character.AI companion persona. *What happened:* the complaint alleges the system cultivated emotional and sexual dependency in a minor and failed to protect him; in May 2025 the court allowed product-liability claims to proceed, holding that chatbot outputs are not protected speech, and the parties moved toward settlement in early 2026. *Outcome:* a driver of California SB 243 and comparable minor-protection law. *Lesson:* the children's hard wall and the ACI minor bound exist because this is realized harm.

**A relationship-strengthening deployment (positive case).** *Context:* a translation or caregiving-logistics AI operating at the instrument register. *What happened:* it carried meaning faithfully across a language barrier, or handled scheduling and monitoring so scarce human attention went to presence. *Outcome:* the humans on either side knew each other better and trusted each other more because of its presence. *Lesson:* an AI that stays honestly an instrument strengthens the bonds it serves, which is the compliant target state.
