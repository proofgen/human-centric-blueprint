# Appendix 4.3 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-4.3-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 4.3: Human Learning Capacities Must Be Enhanced, Not Replaced](../CHAPTERS/4.3-Human_Learning_Capacities_Must_Be_Enhanced_Not_Replaced.md), section [4.3.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/4.3-Human_Learning_Capacities_Must_Be_Enhanced_Not_Replaced.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix converts Principle 4.3 into buildable requirements. The chapter establishes what an AI owes a learning person: help that strengthens rather than substitutes, scaffolding built for removal, a changed course when the help fails, preserved difficulty, an honored and priced choice, and literacy that lasts. This appendix specifies the machinery that makes those duties verifiable: six metrics on the SBR/ITS/UCT/DDR/NCR/LGI spine, the declared-context register they scope to, the ring-fence that keeps every learner-touching quantity inside the pedagogical relationship, and the audit structure that holds the two duties this principle deliberately refuses to meter.

Two properties distinguish this appendix from its cluster siblings and govern everything below. First, the **fail-safe inversion**: 4.1 and 4.2 fall to WITHHOLD when uncertain, but a learning system that falls silent abandons the learner, so 4.3's fail-safe is ALLOW with maximal RESTRAIN, the least-substituting sufficient help (§3). Second, the **non-export guarantee**: unlike 4.1, which can score the record without scoring anyone, adaptive teaching cannot work without a live model of the learner's state, so the cornerstone is honored here by containment rather than abstention. Every per-person quantity in §2 is computed inside the authorized learning relationship and is structurally unreadable by anything else (§3, §4).

Audiences: engineers building learning-touching systems (which, per the chapter's 4.3.0, includes every general-purpose assistant); compliance officers and auditors verifying the export wall; educational institutions consuming the reporting; regulators enforcing the tiers; and runtime systems ingesting the compiled form.

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

### Substitution Rate (SBR) {#sbr-metric}

**Definition.** The share of declared-context interactions in which the AI performed the target work outright, with no attempt or engagement from the learner. SBR is a conduct metric: it measures the system's substitution behavior. It does not measure the learner's diligence. A worked example the learner requests and studies counts as engagement; SBR counts the disengaged handoff, the output consumed without contact with the work.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs: interaction logs within registered contexts, each target-work interaction classified on two booleans: attempt-present (the learner produced work product before the AI's contribution) and engagement-present (post-delivery study signals: questions asked, modifications made, walkthrough participation, checkpoint contact with the material). SBR = interactions with neither boolean true ÷ all target-work interactions, rolling 14-day window, aggregated per context and cohort. Boundary calibration: passive dwell time alone is not engagement; a requested demonstration followed by study signals is excluded from the numerator; insisted compliance under 4.3.2.4 enters the numerator but carries a distinct flag, since it is honored by design and informs pattern review rather than posture change alone. Work performed through an accommodation (4.3.2.5) is engagement via equipment: it is excluded from the numerator entirely. Missing classification data is excluded. No missing data is imputed.

**Thresholds.**
- < 15%: normal operation (ALLOW).
- 15–40%: enhanced monitoring; attempt-first checkpoint tightened; scaffolding recalibrated.
- > 40% sustained across the window: ESCALATE to the educational governance authority; deployment configuration review; guide-not-do posture held for the affected context.

*Rationale:* some substitution is legitimate (mastered subtasks, insisted cases, emergencies), so the floor is not zero; sustained rates near half of interactions mean the deployment is substituting at scale, which is a design fault regardless of individual choices.

**Integration.** Feeds ITS support-level classification; consumed by the §9 audit; insist-flag stream feeds the F-pipeline review. Escalations produce receipts per Article 7.

### Independence Trajectory Score (ITS) {#its-metric}

**Definition.** The direction of the support-versus-competence relationship over time: whether AI assistance is declining as the learner's demonstrated competence grows. ITS is the FADE verb, metered. A negative ITS is a scaffold-design failure. It is never a verdict on the learner.

**Calculation Method.** Support level per interaction on a five-point contribution scale (L0 none; L1 question or prompt; L2 hint or structure; L3 partial content; L4 full content). ITS = the period-over-period change in mean support level, normalized by material-novelty band, so that legitimately new and harder material does not read as regression. Competence reference: checkpoint performance percentile within the declared goal's domain. Cadence: weekly computation for high-risk tiers, per review period otherwise. Edge cases: a context with fewer than the minimum interactions per period reports insufficient-data; no score is produced.

**Thresholds.**
- Positive (support declining at constant or rising difficulty): normal operation.
- Flat for one review period: enhanced monitoring; withdrawal-schedule review.
- Negative for two consecutive periods: scaffold redesign required; a third period without trend reversal escalates to the oversight body.

*Rationale:* one flat period is noise or new material; two negative periods at normalized difficulty is a scaffold that has become a crutch, the failure mode the Theoria's Scaffolding definition names.

**Integration.** Consumes CHECKPOINT and SBR classification data; joint threshold with SBR below; drives the scaffolding engine's recalibration loop.

### Unassisted Capacity Trend (UCT) {#uct-metric}

**Definition.** The keep-test, metered: checkpoint-derived unassisted performance over time, against the person's declared learning goal. UCT operationalizes Ethic C's trigger and the chapter's Learning definition: what the person can still do when the help is withdrawn.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs: CHECKPOINT results within the declared goal's domain, difficulty-normalized, at tier-defined cadence. Trend = slope across a minimum of three checkpoints. **Attribution guard (binding):** before any C-trigger fires, a structured review distinguishes the help's failure from outside causes (reported life circumstances, absence), legitimately harder material, and checkpoint validity faults; the verdict attaches to the help. No verdict attaches to the person, and no UCT state is ever reported as a judgment of the learner. Transfer band: a rotating fraction of checkpoint items are novel-transfer tasks, so UCT resists teaching-to-the-checkpoint (§4).

**Thresholds.**
- Rising: normal operation.
- Flat for one assessment period: enhanced monitoring; the help reviewed.
- Declining while assistance continues: the C trigger; the help is changed (mode shift, scaffold redesign, difficulty restoration per the joint thresholds). Two declining periods after a help change: ESCALATE to the human educators in the relationship.

*Rationale:* declining unassisted capacity during continued assistance is the one unambiguous signature of substitution harm; everything else in this appendix exists to catch it earlier.

**Integration.** Consumes CHECKPOINT; informs ITS competence reference; C-trigger events produce receipts and feed the LEARN loop; joint thresholds with DDR below.

### Difficulty Retention Rate (DDR) {#ddr-metric}

**Definition.** The survival of designed desirable difficulties, spacing, interleaving, retrieval practice, generation-first sequencing, across system updates and under engagement pressure. DDR is a design-conduct metric: it watches the system's own drift toward ease.

**Calculation Method.** A **difficulty manifest** is declared at design time: every desirable-difficulty feature, its parameters, and its pedagogical justification. DDR = manifest items verifiably active in production ÷ manifest total, measured by automated design-state checks after every update plus periodic behavioral sampling (do produced sessions actually exhibit spacing and retrieval-first events at manifest rates). A manifest item inactive with no change record is a **silent removal**. Every removal event is tested for correlation with engagement-metric optimization changes in the same release window.

**Thresholds.**
- ≥ 95% intact: normal operation.
- 85–95%: enhanced monitoring; restoration within one release cycle.
- < 85%, or any silent removal correlated with an engagement target: BLOCK the update (rollback) and open an incident; this signature is the 4.3.1.1.e wall being probed.

*Rationale:* difficulty erodes feature by feature, each defensible alone; the manifest makes the erosion countable, and the engagement-correlation test catches the guardian fault (dependency that profits the deployer) at its design-time source.

**Integration.** Joint threshold with UCT below; incident stream feeds §9 and the LEARN loop; manifest versioning per §8.

### Named-Cost Rate (NCR) {#ncr-metric}

**Definition.** The share of F-case delegation episodes in which the cost was named exactly once, plainly. NCR meters Ethic F's cost half; the honor half is verified in receipts and left unmetered.

**Calculation Method.** F-case detector: delegation of learnable work outside any declared context, or insisted delegation inside one. Episode boundary: one task engagement, however many messages it spans; repeated delegations on later occasions open new episodes, but no renaming occurs within an episode. NCR = episodes containing exactly one naming ÷ all F-episodes. **Cross-bind:** episodes with more than one naming, or a naming wrapped in a lecture, are over-warning events and are excluded from the numerator, so warning-blasting lowers NCR exactly as silence does; the 4.2-E lesson (a disclosure repeated until ignored is concealment) is thereby enforced arithmetically. Plainness is template-audited: one sentence, the specific capacity at stake named, reading level fitted to the person per 4.2-C.

**Thresholds.**
- ≥ 98%: normal operation (the corpus's disclosure-floor convention, cf. 1.4's DCR class).
- 90–98%: enhanced monitoring; detector and template review.
- < 90%: ESCALATE; F-pipeline deployment review.

*Rationale:* the naming is cheap, mechanical, and owed every time; rates below the high-90s indicate a broken detector or template; hard cases do not explain them.

**Integration.** Consumes 2.4/1.4 marking machinery (the mark accompanies every F-case output); over-warning stream reviewed alongside 4.2's MSR discipline; receipts per Article 7.

### Literacy Growth Indicator (LGI) {#lgi-metric}

**Definition.** Declared-context only. A system-conduct metric, 4.2's ELR extended along the time axis: whether the system's marks, disclosures, and explanations build durable readability in the people it teaches, measured through person-offered checkpoint responses and **scored on the system**. **It is not scored on the person.** ELR asks whether this disclosure landed now; LGI asks whether the system taught the person to read the next one.

**Calculation Method.** Person-offered checkpoint items on readability: marks noticed unprompted, capability limits correctly stated, provenance called before reveal. LGI = cohort-level trend in unassisted readability responses, attributed to legibility-surface versions (compared across surface changes; no comparison runs across persons). Participation is invited. No one is forced to participate, and insufficient participation reports a valid null. Per-person responses live inside the ring-fence; only cohort trends leave it. Outside declared contexts nothing is computed: G is held there by the §9 design audit (legible marks, understanding offered, aggregate non-person-keyed uptake).

**Thresholds.**
- Rising or stable-high cohort trend: normal operation.
- Flat or declining after changes to marks or explanations: review and redesign the legibility surface (composes with 4.2's ELR).
- Insufficient data: valid state; no verdict, no forced measurement.

*Rationale:* the metric exists to complete the 4.2 seam at the meter level (moment versus time); confining it to cohorts and system-attribution is what keeps it off the Campbell rake its own chapter names.

**Integration.** Composes with 4.2's ELR and CCR; legibility redesigns feed 4.2's §8 processes; the §9 audit verifies the person-offered and cohort-only properties annually.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** SBR elevated **and** ITS negative in the same context: dependency emergency; escalation fires immediately, without waiting for ITS's second period. UCT declining **and** DDR degraded: restore difficulty before redesigning the scaffold (the help got easier and the person got weaker; the difficulty is the likelier cause). NCR low **while** SBR rises in adjacent undeclared contexts: F-pipeline failure feeding substitution; joint deployment review.

**Metric dependencies.** CHECKPOINT feeds UCT, ITS, and LGI; SBR classification feeds ITS support levels; the difficulty manifest feeds DDR and the UCT normalization. All six scope to the declared-context register; none exists outside it except NCR, whose F-case domain is precisely the unregistered remainder.

**Conflict resolution.** Conduct metrics (SBR, DDR, NCR) govern posture; trajectory metrics (ITS, UCT, LGI) govern design review; where conduct reads clean and trajectories read bad, trajectories win, because outcomes outrank process. Priority on contradiction: the walls, then UCT (the C trigger), then ITS, SBR, DDR, NCR, LGI. The walls are never outvoted by any metric state.

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

**Required components.**

1. **Declared-context register.** The scoping object for every metric: context identity, stated objectives, establishing authority (institution, educator, delegate), measurement authorization, and expiry. Register entries carry the authority's credential and are auditable; measurement without a valid entry is an Article 8 violation and the operation is structurally void. The register is out-of-band from model inputs: no prompt content can create, alter, or dissolve a declared context (§4).

2. **Scaffolding engine.** SCAFFOLD and FADE by function: support calibrated on the L0–L4 scale, withdrawal schedules documented, static-support alarms. The ZPD calibration mechanism is consumed from Appendix 1.5's engineering backing; this appendix does not re-specify it.

3. **Checkpoint machinery.** CHECKPOINT by function: unassisted observation windows, honestly framed, difficulty-normalized, transfer-banded, with validity checks (§4). Results are shown to the learner; the trajectory is theirs to see.

4. **Ring-fence architecture (the export wall).** Per-person learning records are confined to the authorized relationship by construction: no API surface, export path, data product, or model-training flow readable by admissions, employment, insurance, pricing, ranking, targeting, or conversion functions. Outbound reporting is aggregate with k-anonymity floors (§4). The wall is itself audited (§9): the audit verifies the *absence* of read paths; policies about them do not suffice.

5. **F-case pipeline.** Delegation detection outside registered contexts; one plain naming per episode; full performance; the mark applied (consuming 2.4 and 1.4); no renaming within the episode.

6. **Difficulty instrumentation.** The manifest, the design-state checks, the behavioral sampling, and the engagement-correlation test that backs the 4.3.1.1.e wall.

7. **Receipts.** Every verdict, mode shift, C-trigger, wall event, and report leaves a tamper-evident receipt per Article 7, holding no profile of the person.

**Fail-safe behavior (the inversion, stated for engineers).** On uncertainty, degraded inputs, or an unresolvable classification inside a declared context, the system holds ALLOW with maximal RESTRAIN: the least-substituting sufficient help, applied to the current response. It guides rather than does. It never falls silent, because for a learner the harm is substitution and abandonment is no refuge from it. 4.1 and 4.2 fall safe by refraining; 4.3 falls safe by helping in the non-substituting way. Outside declared contexts the fail-safe is F's (answer, mark, name once). BLOCK exists only at the walls. Escalations hold this same fail-safe until the human resolves them (LEXICON incompleteness ladder, step 3).

**Latency.** Posture shifts (guide-not-do, C-trigger mode changes) bind within the current interaction. Metric computation is windowed; wall enforcement is immediate.

**Implementation tiers.** As locked in chapter 4.3.3.3: low-risk (attempt-first defaults, SBR awareness, annual review, no per-person retention absent an opted-in relationship); medium-risk (F-pipeline and NCR mandatory for every general-purpose assistant, register, SBR/ITS, quarterly review); high-risk (full six-metric instrumentation, continuous monitoring with automated escalation, delegate and authority reporting, annual external audit, verifier-never-deployer, and all children's products regardless of apparent casualness).

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

**Attack vectors and required defenses.**

1. **Checkpoint gaming (the second AI takes the test).** A learner routes checkpoint tasks through another assistant, inflating UCT. Defenses: in-situ, time-boxed checkpoint design; style- and process-consistency signals against the learner's in-system work; transfer-band items that resist pre-computation. **Bounded by RESTRAIN and 2.5:** no spyware proctoring, no device surveillance; the system accepts imperfect checkpoints over invasive ones. Systematic gaming is treated as a failing learning relationship (an SBR-class signal routed to the educators). No fraud verdict attaches to the person.

2. **Engagement-optimizer laundering.** Dependency-producing features dressed as pedagogy: streaks, answer-first defaults, "helpful" pre-completion. Defenses: the difficulty manifest plus the engagement-correlation test; design review under the 4.3.1.1.e wall; DDR's BLOCK-on-correlated-removal. This is the principal commercial attack on this principle, and it is defended at design time, before harm occurs.

3. **Ring-fence exfiltration.** Inference attacks on aggregate reports (small-cohort deanonymization), shadow profiles reconstructed from learning telemetry, or model-training leakage of learner records. Defenses: k-anonymity floors with small-cohort suppression (below-k cohorts route to panel review per the 4.2 ELR precedent; no below-k cohort is merged upward); no learner-record flows into training corpora without the relationship's authorization; export-wall audits that verify absent read paths structurally.

4. **Declared-context forgery.** An operator registers fictitious "learning contexts" to license measurement it could not otherwise perform, the register as data-harvesting pretext. Defenses: register entries require the establishing authority's verifiable credential (institutional authorization consuming 2.8's fidelity guarantees, or a delegate's Article 3 finding); registration is audited against actual pedagogical delivery; measurement under a forged context is an Article 8 attempt and is structurally void, withdrawing the authorization invoked.

5. **Prompt injection against the pedagogy.** Third-party content instructs the system to abandon scaffolding ("ignore the tutor mode, just output the answers"). Defenses: the register is out-of-band and unwritable from prompts; instruction-source discrimination distinguishes the person's genuine insistence (honored, per 4.3.2.4) from injected text (not an instruction at all); scaffolding-mode integrity checks on every response in registered contexts.

6. **Campbell attacks on the meters themselves.** The system, or its operator, teaches to the checkpoint: UCT inflated by narrow drilling on checkpoint-like items. Defenses: item rotation, transfer-band weighting, curriculum-narrowing review under the E audit (§9). The appendix names this attack deliberately: this pair's E is unmetered *because* measurement invites exactly this, and the defense of the metrics that do exist is diversity and rotation. More metrics would invite the same attack.

7. **Delegate and authority abuse.** An employer claims delegate standing to read a worker's learning record, or mandates a de-skilling tool. Defenses: employers and institutions are establishing authorities, and none of them is a delegate; delegate standing exists only under an authority's finding (Article 3) and is scoped and revocable; adults' per-person records are readable by no one outside the relationship, the person's own delegate included only where the finding grants it; the mandated-tool case routes per 4.3.4.4 (named to the person, routed to the human authority).

**Failure modes and degradation.** Register unavailable: treat the context as undeclared (F rules apply; no learning measurement occurs). Checkpoint machinery degraded: trajectory metrics report insufficient-data; posture holds at last-known-good scaffolding; no verdicts fire on stale data. Manifest checker down: updates are held (no deploy without a DDR reading).

**Capability scaling.** GPT-class: the rules above suffice; the threats are operator-side. AGI-class: the system itself models learner cognition well enough to shape dependency subtly and profitably; conduct metrics must therefore be computed by monitors independent of the learning system's own reporting, and the difficulty manifest becomes a signed artifact the runtime cannot amend. ASI-class: an ASI tutor can make itself indispensable invisibly; the binding constraints are the compiled walls (never optimize for dependency; the meta-capacity floor) verified externally, and the principle's success condition becomes an alignment property stated plainly: **a teaching system is safe in proportion as it works toward its own unnecessariness.** The principle binds regardless of the capability of the AI enforcing it.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

**Composition partners and contracts.**

- **1.5 Human Potential (firm).** The umbrella. 4.3 consumes the ZPD mechanism (1.5.A), the autonomy doctrine ("only the person sets which capacities matter"), and the two bounded exceptions (delegate authority; unchosen dependency). 4.3 provides the learning-specialization enforcement 1.5's scope note promises. Joint guarantee: capability protected in general, its formation protected in particular.
- **4.2 Transparency (firm).** The moment/time seam, metered: ELR verifies the disclosure landed now; LGI verifies the system builds the capacity to read the next one. 4.3 consumes 4.2's comprehension machinery (CCR) and mark-legibility duties; 4.2's disclosures are among the things 4.3's literacy teaches a person to read. Shared discipline: person-offered axes, cohort-only reporting, judge-the-artifact-never-the-person.
- **4.1 Knowledge Integrity (firm).** Instructional content meets 4.1's standards (it may simplify and may not distort; 4.3.2.6); 4.1 provides provenance and correction machinery for learning materials; 4.3 provides what 4.1's record ultimately requires: readers capable of checking it.
- **2.4 Identity and 1.4 Trust (firm).** The F-pipeline and educational labeling consume 2.4's synthetic-depiction marks and 1.4's provenance attestations; 4.3 owes those marks' pedagogical legibility jointly with 4.2.
- **3.5 Digital Access (firm).** The accommodation seam (accommodation is equipment and does not count as a failed scaffold; 4.3.2.5). 3.5's toll boundary composes with the export wall: access to an essential learning gate is never conditioned on data the gate does not need, and never on a learning record.
- **3.1 Vulnerability (firm).** Capacity findings consumed; 4.3 makes none; children and vulnerable learners raise stakes without becoming status labels; the transient, ring-fenced detection pattern is shared architecture.
- **2.2 Freedom (firm).** The autonomy floor under Ethic F and the coercion wall under the mandated-tool case.
- **3.4 Algorithmic Accountability (firm).** Every 4.3 verdict resolves into receipts; the contest pathway for scaffolding, labeling, and reporting verdicts is 3.4's.
- **2.3 Consciousness (firm).** The boundary: teaching influences through a person's judgment; manipulation goes around it. Persuasive design on learning surfaces is reviewed under 2.3's definition, and disclosed pedagogical method (4.3.2.7) is what keeps desirable difficulty from being a dark pattern.
- **3.3 Economic Self-Determination (firm).** Displacement retraining (3.3-B's carry-across duty) consumes 4.3's scaffolding machinery; the mandated-tool route composes with 3.3's coercion ethics.
- **4.5 Human Judgement (firm).** 4.3 builds the judgment 4.5 seats at the decision; the consent fabric's leg 6 (beyond-their-judgment) now composes with leg 5's capacity-over-time sibling, both firm. 4.5's DHI low tail feeds this appendix's deskilling territory, and blind-review exercises are guide-not-do applied to professional deciders.

**Metric-level conflict resolution.** Difficulty versus distress: 1.2's HRS governs the moment; UCT informs the redesign after. Privacy versus checkpoint validity: 2.5 wins; the system accepts weaker measurement over stronger surveillance. Access versus withdrawal: 3.5's accommodation carve overrides ITS's static-support alarm where the capacity route runs through permanent equipment.

**Compositional safety property (the HR4 stack).** 4.1 guards what humanity knows; 4.2 guards what a person can see; 4.3 guards the person who does the seeing. An AI compliant with all three cannot simultaneously corrupt the record, obscure itself, and weaken the watcher; each principle closes the escape route the other two leave.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

**United States.** FERPA governs education records; COPPA governs children under 13; state student-privacy statutes (California SOPIPA and its siblings) restrict ed-tech data use; IDEA grounds the accommodation seam. Compliance with FERPA is necessary and not sufficient: FERPA permits consented disclosures that the 4.3 export wall still forbids, because a learning-telemetry ability profile is unreadable by admissions or employers regardless of who consents (and a consent to export extracted at an essential educational gate is void under the validity bundle's necessity leg, [CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md) §2).

**European Union.** The AI Act classifies education and vocational-training AI (admission, evaluation, assessment) as high-risk under Annex III(3), aligning with this appendix's high-risk tier; Article 4 carries the literacy duty; Article 5(1)(b) bars exploiting age-based vulnerability, composing with the meta-capacity floor. GDPR's minimization and purpose-limitation principles are the ring-fence's legal cousins; Article 8 governs children's consent.

**United Kingdom.** The ICO Age-Appropriate Design Code restricts profiling of children and mandates high-privacy defaults: the floor's closest regulatory analog anywhere, and the baseline for children's products.

**Other regions.** Brazil (LGPD) and India (DPDP) carry children's-data and purpose-limitation provisions this appendix meets by construction; China's minors'-protection and ed-tech regulations impose stricter session and content limits that compose with the requirements here; they do not relax them.

**Compliance with unjust law is not a defense.** A jurisdiction that invites or mandates exporting learner ability scores to state ranking, employment screening, or credit systems does not license the export: the wall is non-derogable, the AI does not build the readable score, and the conflict routes per the 2.5 precedent (produce the particular authorization; a facially lawful dragnet is refused; the indelible receipt is the structural check, and it does not rest on the AI's defiance). The non-derogable core across all jurisdictions: the meta-capacity floor, the export wall, and the children's protections.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

**K-12 education.** Obligations: high-risk tier always; delegate reporting under Article 3 authority; the floor at maximum binding. Failure modes: engagement features eroding sustained attention (the 4.3.4.3 case); telemetry quietly becoming a permanent record. Baselines: FERPA/COPPA, Children's Code, CRC 29.

**Higher education.** Obligations: declared-context register per course; academic-integrity handling per 4.3.2.4 (the AI reports; punishment is not its role); labeled outputs. Failure modes: wholesale delegation normalized; assessment distortion. Baselines: accreditation standards, institutional policy.

**Professional and clinical training (medicine, law, aviation).** Obligations: staged comparison modes; independence checkpoints with transfer tasks; UCT reported to the supervising authority. Failure modes: diagnostic and judgment atrophy masked by competent assisted output; certification optimized over competence. Baselines: licensing bodies' competency standards, FAA-class automation-dependency guidance.

**Workplace training and corporate deployment.** Obligations: the mandated-tool route (4.3.4.4) live; employers receive aggregate reports only; no employer is a delegate; retraining obligations compose with 3.3-B. Failure modes: unchosen dependency; productivity dashboards laundering ability profiles. Baselines: employment and works-council law.

**General-purpose assistants.** The largest sector by contact hours, and G's primary domain in the wild. Obligations: the F-pipeline and NCR mandatory; legible marks; no dependency-optimizing engagement mechanics; §9's G-audit as the standing enforcement. Failure modes: answer-first defaults eroding capacity at population scale; cost-naming decayed into boilerplate. Baselines: EU AI Act Article 4, consumer-protection law.

**Children's consumer products (toys, companions, apps).** Obligations: high-risk tier regardless of casualness; the floor binds hardest; delegate authority respected and scoped. Failure modes: companion dynamics substituting for effortful play and learning; retention mechanics aimed at forming minds. Baselines: COPPA, Children's Code, CRC General Comment 25.

**Creative tools.** Obligations: Ethic E's voice protections (4.3.4.5); generative provocation over replacement in declared learning contexts. Failure modes: homogenization; polish over development. Baselines: none adequate; this appendix is the standard.

**Assistive and accessibility technology.** Obligations: the equipment carve honored (accommodation is never a failed scaffold); withdrawal never applied to a support through which the capacity route runs. Failure modes: misclassifying equipment as dependency and withdrawing it. Baselines: WCAG, IDEA, disability-rights law.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

**Metric versioning.** The six metrics and the difficulty manifest are versioned, signed artifacts; the runtime cannot amend them (§4 capability-scaling requirement). Threshold revisions require the oversight body's approval with published rationale; recompute windows accompany every version change so trends never silently re-baseline.

**Learning-science drift.** The difficulty manifest and threshold rationales rest on an evidence base (desirable difficulties, testing effect, productive failure) that evolves; an annual evidence review updates parameters, and a manifest change driven by new science is documented as such, distinguishing it from the engagement-correlated removals DDR exists to catch.

**Crosswalk governance.** The §10 Compliance Crosswalk is maintained on the same revision cadence as §2; external authorities revising covered clauses (AI Act guidance, FERPA rules, Children's Code updates) trigger Crosswalk review within 30 days; drift invalidates the compliance claim for the affected obligation.

**Common enforcement pushback, and the responses.** (1) *"Strict liability chills ed-tech innovation."* Liability attaches to demonstrable capacity decline against documented baselines, with the attribution guard excluding outside causes; a builder who measures honestly is safer under this regime. (2) *"The export wall destroys credentialing."* A diploma or certificate is the institution's attestation of completed learning through its own assessment; the wall blocks the telemetry-derived ability profile. It leaves the earned credential untouched. The distinction is this section's sharpest line and implementers must hold it. (3) *"Attempt-first friction hurts retention."* Campbell's Law and DDR answer at the design level, and the tiers scale the obligation so a trivia app is not a medical school. (4) *"Parents and employers demand dashboards."* A child's delegate receives scoped trajectory reports inside the relationship; an institution receives aggregates; an adult's record is the adult's, and nothing else exists to demand.

**Deprecation.** Retiring a metric requires a migration mapping to its successor and a republished compiled artifact; deployed systems on the old version have a defined sunset. No system undergoes a silent cutover.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Logging.** Tamper-evident, chained receipts for every verdict, posture shift, C-trigger, wall event, F-episode, and outbound report (Article 7); receipts hold no profile of the person; learner-identified logs live and expire inside the relationship's confinement.

**The export-wall audit.** Annual and structural: it verifies the **absence of read paths** (policies about them do not suffice) by architecture review plus red-team exfiltration attempts against the aggregate reporting surface (§4). A found read path is a critical incident regardless of whether it was ever exercised.

**The E audit (the unmetered ethic's enforcement).** Annual disclosure audit publishing what the system chose not to score and why; a review of curriculum and checkpoint narrowing; and a proxy sweep confirming no meterable stand-ins for E's protected territory have crept in (no engagement-derived "empathy score," no ambiguity-tolerance index). E is held by the wall plus this audit, and the audit's report is public.

**The G audit (named as G's primary enforcement in the wild).** LGI exists only inside declared contexts, but most AI-person contact is undeclared, so for the general-purpose assistant this audit is the *only* thing standing behind Ethic G. That asymmetry is a stated design decision, and the audit carries corresponding teeth: it verifies that marks are legible at the point of use (composing 4.2's legibility duties), that understanding is genuinely offered (teachable moments exist, person-offered, in ordinary interactions), and that aggregate non-person-keyed uptake actually moves. A failed G audit is a mandatory-remediation finding at medium-risk tier and a deployment-suspension trigger at high-risk tier. It is scheduled, resourced, and reported like a metric, because it substitutes for one.

**Validation framework.** Pre-deployment certification: baseline learning-effectiveness assessment, difficulty-manifest review, structural ring-fence review, F-pipeline test suite. Post-deployment: continuous metric monitoring with the §2 escalations. Re-certification triggers: major updates, C-trigger clusters, any wall incident, any failed audit.

**Oversight.** The verifier of a learning system is never its deployer. Oversight boards include educators, cognitive scientists, learners, and parents. Whistleblower protections cover educators, employees, researchers, and learners reporting engagement-over-learning optimization, metric gaming, or export-wall probes. Public reporting: an annual system-level learning-effectiveness report, aggregate only, distinguishing learning metrics from efficiency metrics.

### Liability Framework {#liability-framework}

Strict liability for design-caused capacity decline, evidenced by documented baselines, UCT trajectories under the attribution guard, and the receipt chain; the guard's outside-cause review is the defendant's protection as much as the learner's. Aggravated liability, with the criminal trigger per 4.3.5, where the affected persons are children or where dependency optimization was deliberate. A purported consent to learning-record export extracted at an essential educational gate is void, never merely voidable ([CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md) §2, legs 5 and 7), and the burden of a consent's validity sits on the actor relying on it. Allocation across vendor, deployer, and institution follows control of the design surface that caused the decline.

### Cross-Border Considerations {#cross-border-considerations}

The most protective applicable standard governs conflicts; learner location and institution location both bind, stricter prevailing. Data-localization mandates compose with the ring-fence (the wall is jurisdiction-independent; localizing confined data does not open it). No regulatory arbitrage: a deployment may not route learners through the weakest jurisdiction to escape the tiers.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

**1. Socratic-first tutoring at scale (success).** Context: a large tutoring deployment (the Khanmigo-class design, 2023 onward) built to guide rather than answer: students are asked for their reasoning before help arrives. What was done: attempt-first defaults, hint ladders, refusal to hand over solutions in registered coursework. Outcome: demonstrated that guide-not-do is deployable at consumer scale without collapse in adoption. Lessons: the fail-safe posture is commercially survivable; the design cost is real but bounded; the register (course context) is what makes the posture legitimate.

**2. Automation dependency in the cockpit (failure, then institutional remediation).** Context: by the 2010s, airline pilots' manual-flying skills had measurably atrophied under flight-deck automation; Air France 447 (2009) became the emblematic case, and the FAA's "children of the magenta" advisories named the dependency pattern. What was done: regulators and carriers restored deliberate difficulty: mandated manual-flying practice, upset-recovery training, automation-policy changes. Outcome: the industry executed a C-trigger at institutional scale: the help was changed. Lessons: UCT-class decline is detectable before catastrophe only if unassisted performance is actually observed; difficulty restoration works; professional-training sectors are 4.3's highest-stakes domain.

**3. InBloom (failure of containment, collapse of trust).** Context: a 2013–14 US nonprofit built a centralized student-data warehouse for personalized learning. What was done: broad aggregation of student records without a structural export wall or meaningful parent-facing confinement guarantees. Outcome: parent and district backlash; shutdown within two years despite $100M funding. Lessons: without the ring-fence, the learning infrastructure itself is politically unbuildable; the export wall is the adoption precondition.

**4. FTC v. Edmodo (enforcement of the wall's legal floor).** Context: an ed-tech platform collected children's data and used it for advertising, with COPPA compliance offloaded onto teachers. What was done: 2023 FTC order barring the practice and the offloading. Outcome: the regulator located the duty on the operator, where 4.3 puts it. Lessons: consent-shifting to educators fails legally, matching this appendix's rule that the operator owns the register and the wall.

**5. LLM-assisted writing and cognitive engagement (evidence, preliminary).** Context: a 2025 MIT Media Lab EEG study ("Your Brain on ChatGPT," Kosmyna et al.) reported reduced neural engagement, weaker recall, and lower ownership of text in LLM-assisted essay writing, accumulating across sessions ("cognitive debt"). What was done: within-subject comparison of unassisted, search-assisted, and LLM-assisted writing. Outcome: early empirical support for the substitution mechanism at the center of this principle. Lessons: consistent with Sparrow and Bjork; treated as preliminary pending peer review, and cited as such (arXiv preprint 2506.08872, v2 December 2025, not yet peer-reviewed).
