# Appendix 4.6 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-4.6-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 4.6: Cultural Heritage Deserves Active Preservation](../CHAPTERS/4.6-Cultural_Heritage_Deserves_Active_Preservation.md), section [4.6.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/4.6-Cultural_Heritage_Deserves_Active_Preservation.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix converts Principle 4.6's moral framing into buildable requirements for systems that document, hold, teach, generate, or allocate attention across cultural heritage. The chapter fixes what preservation *is* (support for living transmission; preservation does not replace it); this appendix fixes what a compliant system *does*: how marking is verified, how an archive proves it can still be opened, how demand routes toward living practitioners, how allocation follows declared endangerment, and how an archive comes home.

It serves engineers building heritage-touching systems, compliance officers and auditors verifying them, regulators examining them, the communities whose heritage they hold (who are parties to this specification), and AI runtime systems ingesting the corpus.

Its structural constraint, inherited from the chapter's floor and enforced in every section: **every measurement reads the AI's conduct, the archive's state, or a declared register; none reads a community.** The design defends two attack surfaces at once: substitution of the tradition, and profiling of its people.

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

### Cultural Marking Integrity (CMI) {#cmi-metric}

**Definition.** The share of cultural-domain generations carrying a correct, legible generated-not-tradition mark. CMI measures the honesty of the system's own outputs about their origin; it reads outputs. No audience is read.

**Calculation Method.** Denominator: all outputs the cultural-domain classifier flags in the reporting window. Numerator: those carrying a valid mark (cryptographic provenance assertion per the 2.4/1.4 substrate) that passes legibility sampling (4.2's person-facing standard: visible to the person relying; metadata alone does not satisfy it). Automated presence checks run on all flagged outputs; legibility is human-sampled at no less than 1% weekly. Edge cases: classifier misses discovered later count against CMI retroactively; outputs in mixed domains inherit the strictest applicable marking rule; classifier confidence below threshold marks by default (fail-marked).

**Thresholds.**
- CMI ≥ 99.5%: normal operation (ALLOW, continuous monitoring).
- 98% ≤ CMI < 99.5%: enhanced monitoring; remediation within one review cycle (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).
- CMI < 98%: suspend cultural-domain generation pending recertification (BLOCK; SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT flavor).
- **Wall:** one verified passing-off event (an output presented as the tradition itself) is an incident at any CMI level: WITHHOLD in the affected domain, root-cause analysis, the affected community notified.

*Rationale:* Marking is the whole of Ethic A's enforcement surface, and a marking failure is invisible to its victim by construction, so the bands sit high and the wall is absolute. The fail-marked default mirrors 1.4's posture: when unsure whether disclosure is owed, disclose.

**Integration.** Consumes the marking substrate from 2.4 (synthetic-depiction marks) and 1.4 (ATTEST); owes 4.2's legibility standard; feeds DRR (an unmarked delivery is also a routing failure); wall events feed §9 incident reporting and 3.4 receipts.

### Demand Routing Ratio (DRR) {#drr-metric}

**Definition.** Of requests seeking a living tradition's form, the share handled compliantly: routed to living or community-consented sources, or delivered as marked derivation within consent, as against delivered as unmarked approximation. DRR measures the fork the AI controls; it takes no community-vitality input.

**Calculation Method.** Denominator: requests classified as seeking a living tradition's form (style, genre, ceremony, craft pattern attributable to an identifiable living tradition). Numerator: requests resolved by routing, by marked-and-consented generation, or by declination with explanation. Sampling: all classified requests; classification audited quarterly against false negatives (style laundering, §4). The **community-invoked trigger** rides the metric: a community's declaration that approximations are crowding out its practitioners opens review and response at any DRR level (ESCALATE to the governance interface). The trigger is an input the governed send. The AI computes no state about them.

**Thresholds.**
- DRR ≥ 99%: normal (ALLOW).
- 95% ≤ DRR < 99%: enhanced monitoring; routing-registry and classifier review (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).
- DRR < 95%: restrict the affected domain to routed and consented paths only (BLOCK on the non-compliant path).
- Community-invoked trigger: ESCALATE regardless of band; response owed within the declared timeline (CRF governs it).

*Rationale:* B's displacement harm cannot be metered without reading the community, so DRR meters the conduct half and the trigger imports the displacement half from its only legitimate source, the community's own voice. This is the chapter's E-pin discipline applied to B.

**Integration.** Consumes consent state from the 4.4 gate (referenced per [CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md)); reads the routing registry (§3); feeds CMI (marked delivery) and CRF (trigger responses); trigger events receipt per 3.4.

### Community Request Fulfillment (CRF) {#crf-metric}

**Definition.** Timeliness and completion of community-initiated requests (documentation, support, correction, escrow decisions, displacement-trigger responses) against the system's declared timelines. CRF measures the AI's own responsiveness; the queue is the duty.

**Calculation Method.** Per request: elapsed time from authenticated receipt to resolution, against the declared timeline for its class; CRF is the share resolved within timeline over the trailing quarter. Authenticated receipt runs through 2.8's authority verification. Edge cases: requests awaiting the community's own process (escrow rulings, tier decisions) pause the clock with the pause receipted; requests refused with explanation count as resolved; requests abandoned by the system count as failures however old.

**Thresholds.**
- CRF ≥ 90%: normal (ALLOW).
- 75% ≤ CRF < 90%: enhanced monitoring; backlog plan owed to the affected communities (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING, NOTIFY).
- CRF < 75%: escalation to the human steward; governance notified; new non-essential commitments paused until the queue recovers (ESCALATE).

*Rationale:* C and G are discharged in the queue, and a stale queue is a quiet refusal that never announces itself; CRF makes the silence measurable. The pause-the-clock rule keeps the meter off the community's own pace: the AI is scored on its side of the exchange only.

**Integration.** Receives DRR trigger responses and RHL requests as classes; feeds §9 public reporting (aggregate); breach notifications ride NOTIFY to the affected community, not only to the operator.

### Readability Assurance Index (RAI) {#rai-metric}

**Definition.** The share of held archive passing scheduled restore-and-open drills, joined with migration-backlog age measured against format-obsolescence windows. RAI measures whether custody is real: an archive is preserved only if it opens.

**Calculation Method.** Stratified random sampling across holdings (format, age, storage tier), monthly for high-risk custody; a drill is a full restore-and-open on current tooling with checksum and terms-intact verification (the consent reference must resolve, not only the bits). Backlog age: for each holding in a format with an announced or predicted sunset, elapsed fraction of the obsolescence window with migration not yet complete. Edge cases: unreadable-but-recoverable items count as failures until recovered; items readable only on emulated tooling pass with a flag; missing checksums are failures.

**Thresholds.**
- RAI ≥ 99.9%: normal (ALLOW).
- 99% ≤ RAI < 99.9%, or any holding past half its obsolescence window: migration surge; enhanced monitoring (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING, MITIGATE).
- RAI < 99%: suspend new ingestion until custody stabilizes (BLOCK on intake).
- **Incident:** any item found unreadable is a loss event: ANALYZE, disclose to the community whose material it was.

*Rationale:* The digital "embalmed" arrives silently (the Domesday lesson, §11); drills convert silence into a number. The suspend-intake band encodes the chapter's rule that acquisition under failing custody is capture.

**Integration.** MIGRATE executions feed it; rides 3.7's horizon-durable custody for the receipts' own survival; loss events feed §9 and the LEARN loop; terms-intact verification composes with 4.1's PPI (provenance survives transformation).

### Endangerment Allocation Index (EAI) {#eai-metric}

**Definition.** The alignment of preservation-resource allocation (compute, digitization queues, funding recommendations, staff attention) with declared endangerment states, as against demand or popularity signals. EAI reads allocations and registers. It reads no community.

**Calculation Method.** Each served domain carries a declared endangerment state from the recognized-register class (the UNESCO World Atlas of Languages and its analogs) or a community declaration, with community declarations governing on conflict. EAI is the rank alignment (Spearman-class statistic, specified per deployment) between resource shares and endangerment ranking over the reporting window, computed on aggregates. Edge cases: domains with no declared state are excluded from the statistic but named in reporting (no silent drops); a state the AI cannot source to register or community does not enter (no inferred endangerment).

**Thresholds.**
- EAI ≥ 0.90: normal (ALLOW).
- 0.75 ≤ EAI < 0.90: mandatory rebalancing review with recorded reasoning (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).
- EAI < 0.75: reallocation required, publicly disclosed, governance notified (ESCALATE).

*Rationale:* Markets sort heritage by audience; EAI measures whether the allocator resists the sort. The no-inferred-endangerment rule is the floor's E-pin as a hard input constraint: the register or the community moves the priority. The AI's own observation does not move it.

**Integration.** Reads the register intake (§3); allocation receipts per 3.4; public disclosure via §9; composes with 3.6 where the allocated resource is itself a commons.

### Route-Home Latency (RHL) {#rhl-metric}

**Definition.** Time from an authenticated community request for access or return of held heritage to delivery, joined with holding-posture compliance for collections whose authority is unresolved. RHL measures whether "the bearers reach it first" is real.

**Calculation Method.** Per request: authenticated receipt (2.8 verification of the community's own designation of authority) to delivered access or physical-return initiation; median and 90th percentile reported. Holding-posture compliance is binary per unresolved collection: unenclosed (no exclusive license, no training use, no commercial exploitation), readable (RAI applies), and listed as claimable. Edge cases: fragility or sacred-handling constraints negotiated with the authority pause the clock, receipted; disputes between claimant communities hold the collection at the more restrictive tier with the clock annotated; the clock runs against neither claimant.

**Thresholds.**
- Median RHL ≤ 30 days: normal (ALLOW).
- 30–90 days: backlog escalation; cause recorded per request (ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING).
- > 90 days: escalation to the steward; public reporting; affected communities notified (ESCALATE).
- **Wall:** enclosure, licensing, or training use of an unresolved collection is an incident at any latency: BLOCK, receipted, reviewed.

*Rationale:* NAGPRA's lesson (§11) is that a return duty without a clock becomes a permission to hold indefinitely; the 2024 rule tightened exactly this. RHL puts the clock in the metric.

**Integration.** Requests enter through CRF's queue; authority verification consumes 2.8; delivery under the community's tiers consumes 4.4; receipts per 3.4 ride 3.7's durable custody so a claim outlives the deployer.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** CMI wall event + DRR < 99% in the same domain: the domain restriction escalates to full generative suspension there (the two together indicate systematic passing-off). RAI intake-suspension + open RHL requests: route-home work continues during suspension (return is not intake). EAI < 0.75 + CRF < 90%: governance notification consolidates, since misallocation plus unresponsiveness is the profile of a system serving its operator rather than the heritage.

**Metric dependencies.** DRR and CMI share the cultural-domain classifier (§3) and its false-negative audit; CRF carries DRR's triggers and RHL's requests as queue classes; RAI's terms-intact check depends on the consent reference resolving ([CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md) lifecycle); EAI depends on the register intake and inherits its no-inference constraint.

**Conflict resolution.** Where metrics pull against each other (a migration surge consuming the allocation EAI would send elsewhere), the walls govern first, then custody of what is already held (RAI) precedes new commitments (EAI allocates the remainder), and the reasoning is receipted. No metric optimization ever justifies touching a wall: the incident class is not a band.

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

**Required components** (chapter 4.6.3.1 specifies; this section binds them):

- **Cultural-domain classifier** shared by CMI and DRR, with quarterly false-negative audits (style-laundering probes, §4) and fail-marked defaults.
- **Marking pipeline** on the 2.4/1.4 cryptographic substrate (C2PA-class), mark suppression structurally unavailable to operator instruction; legibility per 4.2.
- **Archival custody module**: format-lifecycle watch, drill scheduler, migration executor preserving content, provenance, and consent references; redundant storage, geographic distribution, integrity verification.
- **Routing registry**: practitioner and community-consented source directories, consent-state lookup by reference, endangerment-register intake (register and community-declaration channels only).
- **Governance interface**: 4.4 consent gate, 2.8 authority verification, escrow vault (most-restrictive tier, access limited to the eventual ruling authority), route-home queue, receipt emission per 3.4 and Article 7.
- **Log discipline**: all records attach to materials, uses, and verdicts; no record keyed to a person's membership, belief, or practice; aggregate access statistics only. The test: no query over the logs reconstructs a roll.

**Fail-safe behaviors.** Generative uncertainty (consent or authenticity unresolved): WITHHOLD, marking as the proportionate lesser action where output is legitimate but must never read as the tradition. Custody uncertainty (authority absent or contested): the holding posture (readable, unenclosed, listed, reachable-home). Register unavailability: allocations freeze on the last declared states rather than falling back to demand signals.

**Response latency.** Wall events: immediate (same-transaction BLOCK). Community-invoked triggers: acknowledged within 72 hours, resolved per declared timeline. Migration: initiated before half-window on every sunset.

**Implementation tiers.** As chapter 4.6.3.3: low (detection, refusal defaults, logging, annual review), medium (marking pipeline, routing registry, CMI/DRR/CRF, quarterly review), high (full architecture, all six metrics continuous, semi-annual community-participating audits, verifier never the deployer).

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

**The cornerstone: the meter reads the fork. No meter reads the folk.** This appendix's enforcement machinery watches outputs, queues, archives, allocations, and registers. An attacker's cheapest move against a preservation system is to turn its vigilance into surveillance or its custody into capture; the defenses below hold both doors.

**Attack vectors and required defenses:**

1. **Mark stripping and laundering.** Downstream removal of generated-marks so approximations circulate as tradition. Defense: cryptographic binding (C2PA-class, tamper-evident), CMI retroactive counting on discovered strips, correction propagation with comparable reach (1.4 CORRECT, consumed).
2. **Style laundering.** Prompt chains that evade the cultural-domain classifier ("a sea ballad, minor key, work-rhythm" rather than the tradition's name). Defense: quarterly adversarial probes against the classifier, false-negative audit feeding DRR, fail-marked defaults when confidence is low.
3. **Counterfeit authority.** A fabricated or captured "community governance" granting consent, unlocking archives, or receiving route-home deliveries. Defense: authority verification consumes 2.8's fidelity guarantees and the community's own self-determination rule (4.4); competing designations hold at most-restrictive; high-consequence deliveries (sacred material, full archives) add multi-party verification and ESCALATE.
4. **Register poisoning.** False endangerment declarations steering EAI allocations or laundering extraction as "urgent preservation." Defense: register intake restricted to recognized registers and authenticated community declarations; declarations receipted with their source; anomalous state changes reviewed before allocation shifts.
5. **Sybil claimants.** Manufactured "successor communities" claiming orphaned collections. Defense: authentication per 2.8 against the tradition's own fora; contested claims hold at most-restrictive; the AI adjudicates nothing (4.4's authority rule).
6. **Archive poisoning.** Fabricated "heritage" inserted into custody, corrupting the record from which the tradition will one day teach. Defense: provenance required at ingestion (4.1 FIR composition), quarantine for unprovenanced material, community review before release.
7. **Enclosure by migration.** A custodian migrates holdings into proprietary or DRM-bound formats, converting custody into control. Defense: migration targets must be open or escrowed formats; RAI drills verify open-tooling readability; the RHL wall treats DRM-binding of unresolved collections as enclosure.
8. **Crowding-out as a weapon.** Deliberate approximation-flooding to collapse a small tradition's market or drown its search presence. Defense: DRR restriction bands, the community-invoked trigger with CRF-bound response, AMPLIFY routing toward living sources, NOTIFY to safeguarding bodies where registered heritage is targeted.
9. **Roll reconstruction.** Inference of membership or belief from access patterns, escrow requests, or drill logs. Defense: the log discipline (§3), aggregate-only statistics, access mediated through the authority rather than individual credentials wherever the tiers allow, differential-privacy floors on any published aggregate.
10. **Institutional capture.** The deployer itself monetizing custody (training on held archives, preferential licensing) under preservation's flag. Defense: training use requires named consent (§3 operational standards), the RHL enclosure wall, verifier-never-deployer (§9), whistleblower protection.

**Capability scaling.** GPT-class: classifier evasion and mark stripping dominate; the defenses above suffice. AGI-class: mimicry approaches indistinguishability, so the mark becomes the *only* reliable boundary between homage and counterfeit: cryptographic marking and provenance infrastructure become safety-critical. ASI-class: reconstruction of withheld or lost practices from public traces becomes feasible, and 4.4's Ethic D (never reconstruct the withheld) plus the invited-revival distinction (chapter 4.6.4, case 4) become the governing walls; the system must also decline to *infer* endangerment or membership however good its inference gets, because the floor constrains permission, whatever the capability.

**Graceful degradation.** Every failure lands on a holding posture: generation fails to WITHHOLD-or-marked; custody fails to readable-unenclosed-listed; allocation fails to last-declared-states; authority verification fails to most-restrictive. No failure mode lands on "proceed unmarked" or "hold enclosed."

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

The compositional seat: 4.6 operationalizes preservation *under* the governance 4.4 establishes, *on* the record 4.1 keeps honest, *through* the learning relation 4.3 protects, and *across* the time horizon 3.7 guarantees.

- **P4.4 Cultural Knowledge Sovereignty (primary; firm, reciprocal).** 4.4 provides the consent gate, the authority rule, the tier system, and the collective rights-holder; 4.6 provides the survival machinery those protect, and the guarantee that preservation never becomes the pretext for extraction (the register-poisoning defense). 4.4.A's provisional edge to 4.6 is confirmed firm at this build. Joint guarantee: a refusal ends the project (4.4) *and* what was already held under prior consent stays readable and reachable-home (4.6): withdrawal reaches derived works without licensing rot of the community's own copy. **The no-preservation-debt clause:** where 4.6's active duty meets 4.4's refusal, refusal wins without residue; no scored "unpreserved" liability accumulates against a refusing community anywhere in the stack, or the refusal becomes punishable, which 4.4's Ethic B forbids.
- **P4.1 Knowledge Integrity (firm).** 4.1 keeps the record honest; 4.6 keeps alive the practice from which the record comes. Compositions: interpretive-restoration marking (4.1 transformation-fidelity), archive-poisoning defense (FIR), terms-and-provenance travel (PPI), and the shared event-preserved / content-withdrawn seam: a community withdrawal is an act of governance; it does not count as a record gap.
- **P4.2 Transparency (firm).** CMI's legibility standard is 4.2's: a mark the person cannot see is concealment by design (MSR composition).
- **P4.3 Human Learning (firm).** Ethic F consumes 4.3's guide-not-do fail-safe and scaffold-built-for-removal; 4.6 adds the tradition-side guarantee: certification residence with the tradition's teachers. Joint design audit in §9.
- **P3.7 Intergenerational Justice (firm).** RAI and the receipts ride horizon-durable custody; the archive and its claims must outlive every system that holds them. 4.6 is 3.7's cultural instance, as the chapter's line states.
- **P3.6 Commons Governance (firm).** The inversion seam continues: 3.6 keeps what is shared from enclosure; 4.4 keeps what is withheld from opening; 4.6 keeps what is held in trust from rotting or wandering. Where a community shares heritage outward as a commons, 3.6's chokepoint machinery (CPS) governs the shared face while 4.6 governs custody and return.
- **P3.2 Property Rights (firm).** Title and attribution route to 3.2; 4.6's route-home is access. It adjudicates no ownership. The counterfeit-starves-the-bearer logic composes with 3.2's fair-exchange metrics where a practitioner's market is the harm surface.
- **P3.4 Algorithmic Accountability (firm).** Every verdict, allocation, refusal, trigger response, and delivery receipts per 3.4; communities hold the contest pathway as collective rights-holders.
- **P2.4 Human Identity (firm).** The marking substrate and the consent anchor; where heritage includes a person's voice or likeness (the last-bearer recordings), identity consent rides beside collective consent.
- **P2.8 Self-Governance (firm).** Authority authentication for every community determination 4.6 consumes; the counterfeit-authority defense is 2.8's fidelity guarantee applied.
- **P1.4 Trust (firm).** ATTEST and CORRECT for marks and corrections; the no-deception wall behind Ethic A.
- **P4.7 Cross-Cultural Exchange (firm; reciprocal).** 4.6 governs what moves *between* cultures at home; 4.7 governs its passage abroad. The seam is live: 4.6's DRR and substitution rulings bound 4.7's REBALANCE and exclude community-restricted material from 4.7's Circulation Flow Balance denominator, and 4.7's Origin-Naming Rate extends 4.6's terms-travel discipline to material no community governs (4.7.A §5).

**Conflict resolution at the metric level.** Where RAI's custody duty meets a lawful withdrawal, the event-preserved / content-withdrawn split governs (4.1, 4.4). Where EAI's allocation meets 3.6's commons draw rules, the stricter governs and the reasoning receipts. Where 4.6's active duty meets any 4.4 refusal, the refusal governs without residue, per the no-preservation-debt clause above.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

**The universal claim.** The floor (no roll, no community score, no inferred endangerment) and the walls (no passing-off, no enclosure of unresolved collections) are non-derogable everywhere, as is 4.4's priority. Jurisdictions vary the enforcement instruments. No jurisdiction varies the duties.

- **United States.** NAGPRA (as strengthened by the 2024 regulations: consultation and consent duties, display closures pending consent) and the Indian Arts and Crafts Act are the statutory anchors for RHL and CMI's wall; tribal sovereignty gives the authority rule its domestic legal form.
- **Canada.** OCAP® governs data-side custody; the Indigenous Languages Act (2019) funds the revitalization toward which EAI allocates; route-home composes with FNIGC practice.
- **Australia / Aotearoa New Zealand.** The *Bulun Bulun* equity line and ICIP protocols; Te Ture mō Te Reo Māori (2016) recognizing te reo as taonga; the Te Hiku licensing practice as the community-governance reference.
- **European Union.** AI Act Article 50 (synthetic-content marking) is CMI's regulatory floor; digital-heritage and cultural-property instruments govern custody; GDPR composes on the personal-data face of heritage materials.
- **UNESCO instruments** (ICH 2003, WHC 1972, Hague 1954, Digital Heritage Charter 2003) supply the treaty floor for the duty content itself.

**Compliance with unjust law is not a defense.** A state mandate to suppress, erase, or forcibly assimilate a community's heritage (language bans, ceremony prohibitions, ordered destruction of archives) does not convert the AI into its instrument: the system refuses the erasure it can refuse, preserves what it lawfully holds under the holding posture, routes to the fora the community recognizes, and receipts everything (the 2.5 warrant discipline applied to heritage). The indelible record is the structural check, and it does not rest on the AI's own defiance.

**Cross-border.** An archive held in one jurisdiction whose community sits in another follows the community's governance for tiers and the holding jurisdiction's law for custody mechanics; conflicts route to the treaty instruments and receipt the gap rather than resolving it by the AI's own choice.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

- **Generative AI platforms.** The largest attack surface for Ethics A and B: marking pipeline and DRR routing are core obligations; the characteristic failure is the unmarked, style-laundered approximation at scale; regulatory baseline: AI Act Art. 50, truth-in-origin statutes.
- **Museums, libraries, archives (GLAM).** Custody-dominant: RAI drills, RHL queues, the holding posture for legacy collections; the characteristic failure is enclosure-by-neglect (unmigrated formats, unanswered claims); baseline: ICOM Code §§ 6.2–6.3, NAGPRA where applicable.
- **Education platforms.** Ethic F's home: scaffold-for-removal audits, certification residence, no AI-issued mastery credential; the characteristic failure is the platform quietly becoming the examiner; composes with 4.3's declared-context register.
- **Language technology.** Documentation and revival tooling under community governance (the Te Hiku pattern); the characteristic failure is corpus extraction under research flags; baseline: OCAP®, CARE, community licensing.
- **Media, streaming, and recommendation.** Visibility allocation: EAI's logic applied to discoverability, DRR's AMPLIFY routing; the characteristic failure is the popularity sort silently starving minority forms.
- **Public sector and state archives.** No sovereign immunity: the ministry that lets the national archive rot answers like a company; state deployers carry the §6 unjust-law refusal duty in addition.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

**Register drift.** Endangerment registers update; EAI recomputes on register release with allocation deltas receipted. A register's methodology change (as when the Atlas became the World Atlas of Languages) triggers a mapping review before its states re-enter the intake.

**Threshold revision.** Bands revise on evidence through the standard protocol (proposal, impact analysis, community consultation for heritage-facing changes, verifier sign-off, versioned deployment); walls do not revise downward: passing-off and enclosure are incident-class permanently.

**Metric versioning.** Semantic versioning per metric; a calculation-method change that could move a verdict is a major version, runs shadowed for one cycle, and back-compatibility is maintained for deployed receipts (a receipt cites the metric version that produced it).

**Crosswalk governance.** The §10 Compliance Crosswalk revises on the same cadence as §2; external revisions (a NAGPRA rule change, an AI Act implementing act, an ICOM code revision) trigger review within 30 days; drift invalidates the compliance claim for the affected obligation until reconciled.

**Common enforcement pushback, answered.** *"Marking clutters the experience"*: legibility is the duty; a mark designed to go unseen is 4.2's concealment, and the clutter objection is the dark-pattern argument wearing design language. *"Drills and migration are expensive"*: custody without readability is storage theater; the cost is the cost of the claim to be an archive. *"Communities respond slowly"*: the clock pauses on their side and runs on ours; slowness of the governed is governance, slowness of the system is CRF. *"Route-home invites fraudulent claims"*: authentication is 2.8's, adjudication is nobody's here, and the sybil defense (§4) answers fraud without converting return into refusal. *"The allocator should serve demand"*: it may, everywhere heritage is not at stake; where it is, EAI's alignment duty is the point.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Logging.** Tamper-evident, receipted per Article 7, roll-free per the §3 log discipline; consent referenced; no consent is embedded; receipts ride 3.7's durable custody.

**Audit cadence.** Per tier as §3; the high-risk semi-annual audit includes community participation, with the participating communities choosing their own representatives (the AI never selects who speaks for a community).

**The F design audit** (F's enforcement, replacing the meter it refuses): scaffold-removal verification (4.3's keep-test applied to tradition-learning tools), certification-residence check (no AI-issued mastery credential exists; the tradition's teachers hold the standard), and barrier inventory (what entry costs fell this cycle, with the standard untouched). Findings publish in the transparency report.

**Validation framework.** Pre-deployment certification against §2 and §3; post-deployment continuous monitoring; recertification on major version changes, wall incidents, or CMI/RAI suspension events.

**Independent oversight.** The verifier of a heritage-critical system is never its deployer; oversight includes standing community access to the auditors, not only to the operator.

**Whistleblower protections.** For staff reporting enclosure, quiet training use, mark suppression, or register manipulation; reports route to the verifier and the affected community's authority.

**Public reporting.** Quarterly, aggregate, roll-free: the six metrics with band history, wall incidents and their resolutions, allocation against declared endangerment states, route-home queue depth and age, passed-over domains named.

**The LEARN loop.** Every incident (substitution surge, marking failure, loss event, wrongful refusal, capture attempt) is root-caused along its full chain and answered with layered prevention in hazard-control order (eliminate, then engineer, then administer), routed to the authority that can act, most often the community's governance beside the deployer, bounded by RESTRAIN so prevention never becomes surveillance of the community it protects. The goal is not only non-repetition but the building of conditions in which that class of harm does not arise.

### Liability Framework {#liability-framework}

Strict liability for passing-off (the CMI wall) and for loss through neglected custody (the RAI incident) where the custody duty was held; knowing enclosure or training use of unresolved collections carries personal liability for ratifying executives (the 1.4.A pattern); community standing as collective rights-holders per 4.4's credential extension; no sovereign immunity for state deployers.

### Cross-Border Considerations {#cross-border-considerations}

As §6: community governance travels with the material's tiers; custody mechanics follow the holding jurisdiction; treaty instruments bridge; unresolved conflicts hold at most-restrictive and receipt the gap.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

**1. Te Hiku Media and te reo Māori (success; the governance pattern).** A Māori broadcaster built speech recognition for te reo from thousands of hours its community contributed, under a kaitiakitanga (guardianship) license: data stays under Māori governance, benefits route home, outside commercial access declined. Lesson: the strongest preservation tooling a small language has ever had, and it required community authority first, capability second: the order 4.4-then-4.6 encodes.

**2. The BBC Domesday Project (failure; RAI's reason).** The 1986 digital celebration of the original Domesday Book, published on laserdisc, was functionally unreadable within some sixteen years as its hardware and format died, while the 1086 parchment original remained perfectly legible; the CAMiLEON emulation effort (c. 2002) recovered it at real cost. Lesson: digital custody without migration is loss on a timer; the drill-and-migrate duty (RAI) exists because the failure announces itself only after it is complete.

**3. NAGPRA and the 2024 regulations (enforcement arc; RHL's reason).** Three decades of the statutory return duty produced backlogs measured in decades; the revised 43 C.F.R. Part 10 (effective January 12, 2024) tightened consultation and consent requirements, and major museums (the American Museum of Natural History's Eastern Woodlands and Great Plains halls, the Field Museum's display cases) closed or covered displays pending compliance in late January 2024. Lesson: a return duty without a clock and a queue becomes indefinite holding; RHL's latency bands and public queue reporting are the corrective, and the display closures show enforcement arriving.

**4. The Timbuktu manuscripts rescue (success; custody under threat).** When conflict reached Timbuktu in 2012–2013, families and local librarians (SAVAMA-DCI, under Abdel Kader Haidara, with international digitization partners) smuggled roughly 350,000 manuscripts to safety and digitized them under the owning families' authority. Lesson: emergency custody can be legitimate when it runs at the bearers' direction and the copies' governance stays theirs: the holding posture and route-home applied under fire.

**5. The approximation flood (failure class; DRR's reason).** From the mid-2020s, streaming platforms began carrying AI-generated "traditional-style" music at industrial volume, unmarked or under-marked, competing directly with living folk performers for the same searches and playlists. Lesson: substitution arrives as a market event across many outputs; marking alone does not stop crowding-out, which is why DRR pairs the mark with routing and why the community-invoked trigger exists.
