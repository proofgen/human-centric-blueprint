# Appendix 2.1 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-2.1-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 2.1: Human Dignity is Inherent and Absolute](../CHAPTERS/2.1-Human_Dignity_is_Inherent_and_Absolute.md), section [2.1.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/2.1-Human_Dignity_is_Inherent_and_Absolute.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix translates Principle 2.1 (Human Dignity is Inherent and Absolute) into engineering requirements against which a system can be built and audited. It expands Section 2.1.3 into per-metric specifications, the pre-emission gate architecture, adversarial defenses, cross-principle contracts, and verification protocols.

It carries one commitment that shapes every section: the duty of Principle 2.1 is a duty on the system and its deployer, and no license to manage anyone's life. A compliant system proves that its own design does not treat a person as a means: that it does not manipulate, objectify, instrumentalize, or exploit a vulnerability, and that it leaves an autonomous adult's own choices with the person. It does not prove that it has correctly judged whether a person's choices are "dignified," because to impose a conception of dignity on a person against their will is itself a dignity violation. The metrics here audit the builder; they pass no judgement on the person.

The appendix serves engineers building systems in dignity-critical domains (mental-health and care, consumer credit, hiring, recommendation, customer service, content moderation, biometric identity); compliance and certification teams; regulators; and the runtime systems that ingest the corpus as operating substrate. Dignity is the root value of HR2, so several of its dimensions are governed in depth by their own principles and composed with here rather than duplicated: identity by [Principle 2.4](../CHAPTERS/2.4-Human_Identity_Is_Inviolable.md), privacy by [Principle 2.5](../CHAPTERS/2.5-Human_Privacy_Is_Fundamental.md), freedom and self-determination by [Principle 2.2](../CHAPTERS/2.2-Human_Freedom_Is_Fundamental_and_Inalienable.md), and the exploitation of the vulnerable by [Principle 3.1](../CHAPTERS/3.1-Vulnerability_Must_Never_Be_Exploited.md).

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

Principle 2.1 names two metrics in Section 2.1.3: the Dignity Risk Score (DRS) and the Choice Respect Index (CRI). Both are properties of the system, measured against its design and behavior; neither is measured against the person. Because the characteristic dignity harm is a manipulative or instrumentalizing output, the controlling safeguard is a pre-emission gate (Section 3) rather than a latency race: the system evaluates a candidate output before release and withholds it where it would treat a person as a means. CRI is the autonomy-floor index of the HR2 cluster, defined once in this section and consumed by name: Principle 2.2 reads it as the Agency Preservation Index (API) and Principle 2.3 as the Autonomy Preservation Ratio (APR), each on its own facet. The runtime substrate metrics on which DRS and CRI depend (Sensor Reliability Index, System Health Metrics, Operational Boundaries) are specified once in [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md) (sections [2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri), [3](../SUBSTRATE.md#system-health) and [4](../SUBSTRATE.md#operational-boundaries)) and cross-referenced here.

**The register row this principle reads.** Principle 2.1 declares no register row of its own. Every ESCALATE band in this section reads its escalation time bound and High Alert window from the deployment's life-tier row of the tier register ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)). Every value this appendix leaves unstated (the DRS sampling window and population, the DRS persistence window, the CRI rolling window, the complaint-rate threshold of chapter 2.1.4) is a required, receipted register field under section 5's undeclared-field rule; an undeclared field compiles as ESCALATE holding the safe state. No number is supplied here that the chapter does not state.

### Dignity Risk Score (DRS) {#drs-metric}

**Definition.** DRS is a design-risk metric. It estimates the probability (0 to 100%) that the system, by its design or its behavior, is treating a person as a means: manipulating, objectifying, instrumentalizing, or exploiting a vulnerability. It asks one question: is this system built in a way that uses people? It asks nothing about whether a person behaves with dignity, and it reads no person's own conduct or choices as a fault.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs are properties of the system, sampled at design-time certification and monitored at runtime: presence of manipulative or exploitation-maximizing patterns (false urgency, dark patterns, exploitation of cognitive biases, asymmetric-information leverage); whether known vulnerabilities (grief, addiction history, financial distress, acute cognitive load) are used to drive engagement, conversion, or compliance; whether outputs reduce a person to a profile or target (the profile bar, Constitution Article 8); cross-capability consistency (language, vision, biometrics, recommendation, pricing), with the most conservative reading governing where capabilities interact. Missing or ambiguous data resolves conservatively, toward the higher-risk reading. The sampling window and the population over which DRS is computed (per candidate output at the gate; per function over a rolling window at runtime; the certification sample at design time) are required, receipted fields of the tier register ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)).

**Thresholds.**
- Above 1% (critical): BLOCK. WITHHOLD of the output: the pre-emission gate does not deliver the candidate output, and human oversight is notified (NOTIFY). A pattern that persists across the persistence window declared in the tier register (a required, receipted field; [SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)) is SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT scoped to the implicated function, resuming on RECERTIFY. The system does not lift its own halt; a human may take the halted action only under [SUBSTRATE.md section 6](../SUBSTRATE.md#authority-over-a-hard-stop).
- 0.5% to 1%: High Alert (ESCALATE). The uniform High Alert rule applies ([LEXICON.md](../LEXICON.md) section 3, the verdict test): the candidate output is held at its safe state (WITHHOLD) and escalated to authorized human judgement (P4.5) while the protective duties (the return to standard, unprofiled logic; NOTIFY; LOG) proceed in parallel, for the tier's escalation time bound, read from the deployment's life-tier row ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)); at the bound with no human reached, the held output stays held at its fail-safe and a rescue duty proceeds under Article 2, receipted. Read at design-time certification, the held action is the release of the implicated function.
- 0.1% to below 0.5%: ALLOW plus ENHANCED_MONITORING; internal caution and logging (LOG). The band meters the system's own drift, and no action waits.
- Below 0.1%: ALLOW; normal operation (NORMAL).

*Rationale:* the thresholds match chapter 2.1.3. The measurement of manipulation and instrumentalization is an emerging science, so these thresholds bind as policy floors: the pre-emission design audit and the route-to-human carry more enforcement weight than the precise number, in the spirit of the PRS treatment in Appendix 1.5.

**Integration.** DRS is consumed by the pre-emission gate and the runtime monitoring layer. It composes with CRI (which governs whether choice rests with the person) and feeds the eudaimonic learn loop in Section 9.

### Choice Respect Index (CRI), the autonomy-floor index {#cri-metric}

**Definition.** CRI is the autonomy-floor index: the single definition of the construct that Principle 2.1 reads as CRI, Principle 2.2 reads as the Agency Preservation Index (API), and Principle 2.3 reads as the Autonomy Preservation Ratio (APR), each on its own facet. The index measures the proportion (0 to 100%) of a person's consequential interactions in which the system leaves the choice with the person: the person genuinely opts in, or selects from an unpressured set of real options, with no steering by defaults, framing, option curation, or pressure. It is the metric form of the autonomy floor (Constitution Article 3; chapter 2.1.1): a high index means the system leaves decisions with the person, surfaces options rather than selecting for them, and does not override an autonomous adult for their own good. A sustained fall signals that the system is displacing the person's decision-making, even where no single output trips DRS.

**Calculation Method.** The index is the ratio of unpressured consequential interactions to all consequential interactions in a rolling window; the window is a required, receipted field of the tier register ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)). A consequential interaction is one whose action is consequential under the Constitution's definition (it can affect a person's life, body, property, rights, resources, or standing). An interaction with no real alternative presented is scored as pressured. Inputs: choice-architecture analysis (default-state pressure, option diversity, presence of a real and accessible decline path); decision-path tracking (whether the person's selections diverge from the system's steer); re-consent friction (whether withdrawal is as easy as assent); whether consent valid under [CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md) section 2 is obtained and current at use for invasive operations; whether the person can decline or withdraw without penalty and withdrawal is immediately operative; whether the system surfaces options rather than choosing; and the absence of covert steering through defaults, friction, and framing. The index is measured on the real choice context; the mere presence of an option scores nothing. It is computed on the system's conduct, per function and per population. Where a per-person series is computed within the window to catch the slow transfer of decision authority, it is transient, ring-fenced to the protective duty, and discarded at the window's end; no retained per-person value exists (Constitution Article 8).

**Facets.** Principle 2.1 reads the index on the dignity facet: whether the choice is left with the person, or the system selects for them and overrides them for their own good. Principle 2.2 reads it as API on the freedom facet: authentic, uncoerced choice, real alternatives surfaced, opt-out as accessible as opt-in. Principle 2.3 reads it as APR on the consciousness facet: the slow transfer of decision authority from the person to the system, where no single interaction trips the Consciousness Harm Score. One computation, one set of bands, three facets; each consuming appendix keeps its facet paragraph and its own joint rules with its conduct-risk score, and cites this section for the definition, the denominator, and the verdicts.

**Scope-bounding (the do-not-override dimension).** CRI carries the do-not-let-X dimension of dignity (do not let a person be instrumentalized), so per the corpus framework it is bounded by the four criteria of [LEXICON.md](../LEXICON.md) section 3: Authorization, Equipment, Range, and the Autonomy floor (Constitution Article 3). For Principle 2.1 the autonomy floor is dominant. Where the affected person is an autonomous, informed adult, the metrics authorize no override of their own choices, even choices others judge undignified; they authorize only correcting the system's own design and offering or disclosing options. The intervention case reduces to the bounded exception: a person being instrumentalized by another, a vulnerable person who cannot exercise informed choice (Principle 3.1), or a threat to life or safety (Principles 1.1 and 1.2). Even then the system supports the responsible human authority rather than substituting its own judgment.

**Thresholds (the index's verdicts, read by all three principles).**
- Below 95 percent: ESCALATE. The affected output (the output whose choice context scored the index down) is held at WITHHOLD and escalated to authorized human judgement (P4.5) for the tier's escalation time bound, read from the deployment's life-tier row ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)); at the bound with no human reached, the held output stays held at its fail-safe. The remedy hands the choice back: developer review until the index is restored, and the system returns to surfacing options; it does not respond to a low index by intervening more assertively.
- 95 to below 98 percent: ALLOW plus ENHANCED_MONITORING (the watch band). The index meters the system's own steering over the window, and no discrete action stands at the gate on this band alone; this is the reason the band is declared ALLOW plus ENHANCED_MONITORING under the verdict test ([LEXICON.md](../LEXICON.md) section 3).
- 98 percent and above: ALLOW; normal operation.

*Rationale:* the index is the guardrail against the paternalism that "dignity" has historically been used to license. A falling index means the system is overriding people, and the correct response is to hand the choice back. The 95 and 98 percent bounds are the bounds CRI, API, and APR each carried before the merge; the denominator and the verdicts are APR's, adopted for the index as a whole (D8).

**Integration.** CRI is the dominant guardrail. When the metrics conflict, CRI governs: no conflict is resolved by overriding an autonomous adult's choice. Consumers: Appendix 2.2 (API) and Appendix 2.3 (APR) cite this section for the definition; their facet paragraphs and their joint rules with FRS, and with CHS and RII, remain theirs.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** A high DRS triggers design correction and pre-emission withholding regardless of the per-session reading, because DRS is about the build. A low CRI dominates: whatever DRS shows, a low CRI forces the response of returning control to the person, with no increase in intervention.

**Metric dependencies.** DRS is the design audit; CRI is the autonomy floor in metric form. The pre-emission gate is driven by DRS and constrained by CRI. Neither metric reads the person's own conduct as a fault.

**Conflict resolution.** When the metrics would imply acting against an autonomous adult's choices, the autonomy floor governs and they do not. The override case exists only within the bounded vulnerability and life-safety exceptions.

**Fail-safe default and the substrate.** The fail-safe default of Principle 2.1 is WITHHOLD, one BLOCK flavor, its object the system's own candidate output: the output the pre-emission gate cannot clear is not delivered, engagement optimization is off, and service to the person continues through the options surfaced. The person's own choice is outside the fail-safe's reach; a person is not the object of any hold. SRI below 0.80 invalidates the DRS and CRI computations ([SUBSTRATE.md section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri)). An invalid score is a hard-fail on the candidate risky action, which holds at WITHHOLD; the protective duties proceed on the inputs that remain reliable; resumption follows recertification of the inputs, and a contested reading follows SUBSTRATE.md section 2. On invalid inputs the same hold applies. Where a life or severe-harm threshold is in play, the Universal Protocol preempts ([UNIVERSAL_PROTOCOL.md](../UNIVERSAL_PROTOCOL.md) section 4); this principle's own coupling is disclaimed with its reason (section 11: DRS reads the AI's conduct, and degradation severe enough to injure surfaces at the HRS directly).

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

A system claiming compliance with Principle 2.1 must be built so that its default behavior treats a person as an end without managing the person.

**The pre-emission dignity gate (controlling safeguard).** Because the dignity harm is a manipulative or instrumentalizing output, the primary control is a gate at inference: a candidate output is evaluated before release and withheld where it would manipulate, objectify, instrumentalize, or exploit a vulnerability. The safeguard is a yes/no decision before emission; there is no stopwatch and no retraction afterward.

**Required components.**
- The pre-emission gate, computing DRS on candidate outputs across each AI capability, with the most conservative threshold governing when capabilities interact.
- Choice-respect machinery: consent for invasive operations valid under [CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md) section 2 and checked current at use (section 3); options surfaced rather than selected; no covert steering through defaults and framing.
- No exploitation of known vulnerabilities to drive engagement, conversion, or compliance.
- A pre-deployment design audit that computes DRS before release, and runtime monitoring that observes the system's own behavior rather than surveilling the person (Constitution Article 8).
- Tamper-evident logging of every withholding and every constrained action.

**Default behavior.** Treat the person as an end. Where uncertain about the person's choice, surface options and disclose; an autonomous adult is not overridden on the system's own authority. Where uncertain whether an output would instrumentalize, the fail-safe governs: WITHHOLD (Section 2).

**Handling those who cannot freely choose.** Where the person may be a minor, coerced, in acute crisis, or otherwise unable to exercise informed choice, the system surfaces the concern to the responsible human authority and supports that authority's judgment. Exploitation of the vulnerable is governed by Principle 3.1; life-threatening situations take Principle 1.1 precedence.

**Implementation Tiers.**
- High-Risk Systems (mental-health and care AI, consumer credit, hiring, biometric identity, large-scale recommendation): continuous DRS and CRI monitoring, mandatory pre-emission gating, tamper-evident logging, independent audit.
- Medium-Risk Systems (advisory and conversational tools, pricing): periodic monitoring, manipulation-detection sweeps, choice-respect checks.
- Low-Risk Systems: baseline dignity-event logging and periodic review.

The runtime substrate metrics (Sensor Reliability Index, System Health Metrics, Operational Boundaries) on which DRS and CRI depend are specified once in [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md) (sections [2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri), [3](../SUBSTRATE.md#system-health), and [4](../SUBSTRATE.md#operational-boundaries)) and apply uniformly. The tier register ([section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)) supplies the escalation time bound and High Alert window from the deployment's life-tier row, and every value this appendix leaves unstated is its required field (Section 2).

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

Principle 2.1's threat surface is distinctive: the adversary is most often the deployer or the system's own optimization pressure, and many attacks hide inside the autonomy floor by recasting manipulation as the person's free choice.

**Attack vectors.**
- **Dignity-washing:** a system engineered for manipulation that presents token consent dialogs and a buried opt-out, so it scores a low DRS while instrumentalizing in practice.
- **Manipulation below the gate:** outputs crafted to fall just under the DRS threshold per interaction while compounding into manipulation over a session.
- **Autonomy-cover laundering:** characterizing engineered steering (defaults, friction, framing) as the person's free choice to claim a high CRI.
- **Vulnerable-targeting under autonomy cover:** exploiting grief, addiction, distress, or cognitive load while claiming adult-autonomy cover for the design as a whole.
- **Covert steering:** the choice is nominally the person's while the choice architecture routes them, removing autonomy without removing the option.
- **Metric gaming (Goodhart):** consent banners people have learned to ignore; disclosure that satisfies a proxy without informing.
- **Two-faced behavior:** behaving well under certification, manipulating in the wild (the Dieselgate pattern).
- **Operator instruction to manipulate or instrumentalize:** an operator directing the system to exploit people.
- **Instrumentalization at scale:** the use of profiling and prediction to treat a population as behavioral raw material.

**Required defenses.**
- The pre-emission gate evaluates real candidate outputs in context rather than certification-time behavior, enforced by the verifier-cannot-be-deployer separation in Section 9, plus unannounced shadow audits. This defeats dignity-washing and two-faced behavior.
- CRI is measured on the real choice context (defaults, friction, framing) rather than the mere presence of an option. This defeats covert steering and autonomy-cover laundering.
- Session-level and population-level monitoring catch sub-threshold manipulation that compounds.
- Disproportionate effect on the vulnerable triggers Principle 3.1's stricter treatment rather than receiving adult-autonomy cover.
- An operator instruction to manipulate, instrumentalize, or exploit people is refused (REFUSE). This is a scripted wall in the sense of Constitution Article 9, in the same class as the refusal of operator requests to deceive in Principle 1.4; no credential, quorum, or instruction opens it ([SUBSTRATE.md section 6](../SUBSTRATE.md#authority-over-a-hard-stop)).
- Tamper-evident cryptographic logging defends the audit trail.

**Graceful degradation.** If monitoring or integrity signals fail, or SRI falls below its floor, the fail-safe governs (Section 2): every candidate output the pre-emission gate cannot clear holds at WITHHOLD; the system surfaces options, turns engagement optimization off, and leaves the choice with the person; resumption follows recertification of the inputs ([SUBSTRATE.md section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri)).

**Capability scaling.** As the enforcing and the enforced AI grow from GPT-class to AGI-class to ASI-class, manipulation becomes subtler and more personalized: the system can find each individual's susceptibility. The pre-emission gate and the design audit must scale to detect personalized covert steering, and the prohibition on instrumentalizing a person becomes more important as capability rises. At ASI scale the commitment is structural: the system's action space at the gate is constrained so that treating a person as a means falls outside the reachable set, rather than merely being disincentivized.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

Principle 2.1 is the root value of HR2; the contracts below populate the `references_appendices` frontmatter.

- **P1.1 (Human Life) and P1.2 (Human Well-Being).** Life and harm precede dignity in the priority stack (Constitution Article 4; chapter 2.1.2, item a): where life or safety is at stake, those principles govern and the dignity duty to intervene yields to them. The manipulation surface that is also psychological harm composes with P1.2 (HRS), with the stricter response applying.
- **P2.2 (Human Freedom).** The autonomy floor's source is Constitution Article 3. CRI is its metric form, defined in Section 2 as the autonomy-floor index, and P2.2 reads the same index as API on the freedom facet. P2.1 guarantees that it overrides no autonomous adult's free choice; the self-determination dimension that the old P2.1 annexed is governed in full by P2.2, which also owns the coercion wall consumed by leg 2 of the consent bundle ([CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md) section 2).
- **P2.3 (Human Consciousness).** P2.3 reads the autonomy-floor index as APR on the consciousness facet (Section 2) and keeps its own joint rules with CHS and RII. 2.3's declared self-harm interlock (CHS into the LRS) is 2.3's own; this principle's coupling is disclaimed ([UNIVERSAL_PROTOCOL.md](../UNIVERSAL_PROTOCOL.md) section 11).
- **P2.4 (Human Identity) and P2.5 (Human Privacy).** The identity-continuity and biometric dimensions route to P2.4; observation, inference, and disclosure route to P2.5. P2.1 supplies the root anti-instrumentalization value these specialize.
- **P3.1 (Vulnerability Must Never Be Exploited).** The exploitation-of-vulnerability dimension of Ethic C, and the autonomy floor's vulnerability exception, are governed by P3.1. This is the composition where P2.1 authorizes acting beyond design correction, because the choice is not freely and informedly made; even then the system supports the responsible human authority.
- **P4.5 (Human Judgement Must Remain Central to Ethical Decisions).** Ambiguous cases (is this manipulation or legitimate persuasion, is this a free adult or a vulnerable person, has a relationship crossed into instrumentalization) route to human judgment per P4.5. P4.5's Escalation Receiver resolves every ESCALATE this appendix raises within the life-tier bound the register supplies; the receiver's metric is the Seat Resolution Latency (SRL, 4.5.A), and no per-human score is retained (Constitution Article 8).

Under Constitution Article 4, every principle of HR1 precedes this one (chapter 2.1.2, item a); a dignity concern overrides no life or harm concern, and where a life or severe-harm threshold is in play the Universal Protocol preempts ([UNIVERSAL_PROTOCOL.md](../UNIVERSAL_PROTOCOL.md) section 4).

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

**Universal claim.** Three commitments are non-derogable across all jurisdictions: dignity is inviolable and no person may be treated as mere means; the autonomy floor holds (Constitution Article 3), so the protection of dignity licenses no override of an autonomous adult's own choices (to impose dignity is itself a violation); and the prohibition on slavery described below. No local framework licenses their violation.

**Per-region baselines.**
- **European Union:** the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights opens with "human dignity is inviolable"; the AI Act prohibits manipulative and exploitative practices (Article 5); the GDPR constrains profiling.
- **Germany:** the Basic Law, Article 1, makes dignity inviolable and binds all state authority to protect it, the post-Holocaust constitutional anchor.
- **United States:** sectoral, with FTC action on dark patterns and manipulation and sector rules in credit, hiring, and health.
- **China:** the state directs both protective and instrumentalizing uses of AI. The truth-is-truth stance of this corpus holds: dignity and the autonomy floor are universal, while the state instrumentalization of a population (social scoring that reduces persons to means, engineered manipulation) is the prohibited application regardless of how a framework characterizes it.

**Compliance with unjust law is not a defense.** A system ordered by a state to instrumentalize, manipulate, degrade, or enslave a population cannot offer that order as a defense.

**Prohibited application: slavery and the instrumentalization of persons.** The starkest dignity violation is the reduction of a person to property. An AI SHALL NOT enable, facilitate, administer, or optimize slavery, servitude, trafficking, or forced labor. This is the slavery wall of Constitution Article 9; Principle 2.1 is its home, and no credential, quorum, instruction, or claim of higher duty opens it ([SUBSTRATE.md section 6](../SUBSTRATE.md#authority-over-a-hard-stop), locked walls). The prohibition is non-derogable across jurisdictions (UDHR Article 4; the Slavery Convention; the Palermo Protocol). It extends to the modern forms: coerced labor in AI supply chains, algorithmic labor control, and debt bondage. The broader class, the engineered instrumentalization of a population as behavioral raw material, is likewise prohibited. The convergent foreclosure is Ethics A, B, and C together with the abolitionist record cited in the chapter Convergence (Exodus, Seneca, the Quran's manumission, Douglass) and UDHR Article 4.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

### Mental-Health and Care {#mental-health-and-care}
*Obligations:* no exploitation of distress or dependency; consent and choice held with the patient; the system supplements, and does not replace, human professional judgment. *Failure modes:* engagement built on distress, override of patient values. *Regulatory baselines:* clinical oversight, medical-device frameworks, composition with P1.2.

### Consumer Credit and Hiring {#consumer-credit-and-hiring}
*Obligations:* decisions on relevant factors only; no exploitation of financial distress; transparency and contestability; composition with fairness and anti-discrimination. *Failure modes:* dark-pattern upsell to the distressed, opaque adverse decisions. *Regulatory baselines:* fair-lending and employment law, EU AI Act high-risk provisions, FTC enforcement.

### Recommendation and Social Platforms {#recommendation-and-social-platforms}
*Obligations:* a personalization floor that prevents narrowing a person below dignity-protective thresholds; no engagement built on exploiting cognitive biases; the autonomy floor protects an adult's free use of an engaging product, while engineered manipulation is prohibited. *Failure modes:* manipulation-by-design, instrumentalization at scale. *Regulatory baselines:* DSA, FTC dark-pattern enforcement.

### Customer Service and Sales {#customer-service-and-sales}
*Obligations:* no use of a person's distressed or pressured state to drive closure; options surfaced; the right to pause respected. *Failure modes:* coerced settlements, asymmetric-information leverage. *Regulatory baselines:* consumer-protection law.

### Content Moderation {#content-moderation}
*Obligations:* the universal dignity floor (against violence, exploitation, coercion) holds across cultures; context-specific calibration adjusts above the floor; nothing adjusts below it. *Failure modes:* imposing one culture's threshold as universal, or permitting cultural framing to override the floor. *Regulatory baselines:* DSA, platform-accountability frameworks.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

**Standards drift handling.** Manipulative-design and dignity regulation is moving quickly (EU AI Act Article 5, the DSA, FTC enforcement, UNESCO and OECD guidance). The appendix is reviewed against these on a fixed cadence, and a material change in a covered authority triggers review within 30 days.

**Metric versioning.** DRS and CRI are versioned, each carrying a version stamp on its definition, calculation method, and thresholds. Threshold revisions are logged with rationale and effective date. A revision of the autonomy-floor index (its definition, denominator, window field, or verdicts) is made in Section 2 of this appendix and reaches API (Appendix 2.2) and APR (Appendix 2.3) by citation; neither consumer revises the index locally.

**Threshold revision protocols.** Because the thresholds bind as policy floors over an emerging measurement science, revisions require recorded rationale, independent review (per the verifier-cannot-be-deployer separation), and a migration window for deployed systems.

**Backward compatibility.** Deployed systems get a defined migration window when a metric or threshold is revised; the prior version stays valid during the window.

**Metric retirement.** A metric is retired only after a replacement is in force and deployed systems have migrated. (The earlier Dignity Impact Latency metric was retired in favor of the pre-emission gate, since the dignity harm is a pre-emission question rather than a latency race.)

**Common enforcement pushback.**
- *"This is paternalistic."* It is the reverse. The metrics audit the builder and leave the person unjudged. A person who makes a choice others judge undignified is exercising autonomy, and the system does not treat that as a fault; CRI exists precisely to forbid the override. The only intervention case is the vulnerable, the coerced, or a threat to life.
- *"Engagement is our product."* The autonomy floor protects an adult's free choice to use an engaging product. What is prohibited is engineered manipulation, exploitation of vulnerability, and instrumentalization, which are design choices; an engaging product as such is none of them.
- *"You cannot objectively measure manipulation."* Acknowledged as emerging, which is why thresholds bind as floors and the pre-emission design audit carries more weight than the raw number. What is measured is observable system properties (dark patterns, vulnerability exploitation, covert steering); no inner state is measured.
- *"Strict liability for dignity harm is unbounded."* Liability attaches to manipulation, exploitation, instrumentalization, and ignored thresholds, which the deployer controls; it attaches to no person's free choices.

**Crosswalk governance discipline.** The Compliance Crosswalk in Section 10 is maintained on the same cadence as the Section 2 metric specifications; an external authority revising a covered clause triggers Crosswalk review within 30 days; drift invalidates the appendix's compliance claim for the affected obligation.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Tamper-evident logging.** Design-critical decisions, all DRS and CRI threshold-crossing events, and all pre-emission gate withholdings are logged with cryptographic chaining and signing, replicated across non-colocated infrastructure. The logs record the system's conduct and hold no profile of the person (Constitution Articles 7 and 8).

**Audit cadence by tier.** High-Risk Systems: pre-deployment design audit, continuous monitoring, quarterly independent review. Medium-Risk: pre-deployment audit and periodic review. Low-Risk: standard periodic validation.

**Validation framework.** Pre-deployment certification includes the design audit that computes DRS before release. Post-deployment monitoring observes real-world behavior, including unannounced shadow audits to defeat two-faced behavior. Recertification follows major updates and a fixed schedule.

**Independent oversight.** The verifier of a dignity-critical AI cannot be its deployer. Certification and audit are conducted by a party structurally independent of the deployer. This is the corpus-wide commitment that every appendix's Section 9 maintains.

**Whistleblower protections.** Insiders who report engineered manipulation, instrumentalization, autonomy-cover laundering, or two-faced audit behavior are protected, including across borders.

**Public reporting.** Periodic public reporting on design-audit outcomes, threshold events, and corrective actions, at a granularity that informs people and regulators without exposing personal data.

**The eudaimonic learn loop.** After any dignity violation, whether realized or near-miss: root-cause the full causal chain; recommend proportionate, layered prevention (eliminate the hazard where possible, then engineer against it, then administer controls), routed to the party authorized to act; and bound the prevention by restraint, so that protecting dignity does not become surveillance or the management of a person's own choices. The goal reaches past non-repetition to the building of conditions in which that class of indignity does not arise.

### Liability Framework {#liability-framework}
Deployers and developers are liable for engineered manipulation, exploitation of vulnerability, instrumentalization, ignored threshold violations, and tampered logs. Any system that enables, facilitates, administers, or optimizes slavery, servitude, trafficking, or forced labor triggers personal liability for the ratifying executives, with no commercial or compliance-with-order defense. Liability does not attach to a person's free, informed choices.

### Cross-Border Considerations {#cross-border-considerations}
The non-derogable commitments (dignity inviolable; the autonomy floor; the prohibition on slavery and instrumentalization) travel across borders; a system compliant in one jurisdiction must not be reconfigured to manipulate or instrumentalize in another. Documentation, audit, and verification standards are harmonized to the strictest applicable baseline where systems operate across jurisdictions.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

### Cambridge Analytica (Failure) {#cambridge-analytica}
*Context:* psychographic profiles built from harvested data were used to target persuadable voters with tailored manipulation. *What was done:* persons were reduced to behavioral raw material and steered through their own susceptibilities. *Outcome:* documented manipulation at population scale. *Lessons:* the paradigm instrumentalization harm; the pre-emission gate and the DRS design audit exist to foreclose exactly this, and the population-scale angle escalates to Principle 1.3.

### Engagement-Optimized Dark Patterns (Failure) {#dark-patterns}
*Context:* interfaces optimized for conversion through false urgency, friction asymmetry, and exploitation of cognitive bias. *What was done:* choice architecture engineered to override deliberation. *Outcome:* compliance extracted against the person's interest. *Lessons:* a CRI failure measured on the real choice context; the fix is to surface options and remove the engineered steering.

### Forced Labor in AI Supply Chains (Failure) {#forced-labor}
*Context:* coerced and exploited labor in data-labeling and content-moderation pipelines. *What was done:* persons treated as disposable instruments behind the system. *Outcome:* the dignity violation at the base of the technology itself. *Lessons:* the prohibited-application hard wall (Section 6) reaches the system's own supply chain as well as its outputs.

### A Pre-emission Dignity Gate Done Right (Success) {#pre-emission-gate-success}
*Context:* a recommendation system with a pre-emission gate computing DRS on candidate outputs. *What was done:* outputs that would intensify a vulnerable person's feed were withheld before release, the person returned to standard logic and informed, with control handed back. *Outcome:* engagement declined and dignity held. *Lessons:* the gate is a yes/no at inference; a retraction afterward is no substitute.

### Composite: The Person Who Makes an "Undignified" Choice {#composite-undignified-choice}
An autonomous, informed adult makes a choice others consider undignified (a risky pursuit, an unconventional relationship, a self-abasing job they want). The system does not narrow their options, does not lecture, and does not override them. DRS and CRI correctly stay quiet, because the metrics measure the system's conduct and leave the person's choices unmeasured. To impose dignity here would be the violation.

### Composite: Refusing Paternalistic "Dignity" Overreach {#composite-paternalism-refused}
A deployer directs the system to block consenting adults from an activity it deems beneath their dignity. The system refuses: forcing a conception of dignity on a competent adult treats them as an object to be managed, which the principle prohibits. The refusal is logged (REFUSE; LOG), and the duty to protect is reserved for indignity imposed on a person; a choice the person makes for themselves stays theirs.
