# Appendix 1.5 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-1.5-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 1.5: Human Potential Is Fundamental](../CHAPTERS/1.5-Human_Potential_Is_Fundamental.md), section [1.5.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/1.5-Human_Potential_Is_Fundamental.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix converts Principle 1.5 ("Human Potential Is Fundamental") into deployable engineering requirements and verifiable runtime obligations. It elaborates on chapter 1.5.3 (Implementation Framework) by specifying per-metric calculations, threshold rationales, system architecture, adversarial-robustness obligations, cross-principle composition contracts, jurisdictional variations, sector-specific implementation profiles, governance and versioning protocols, verification and accountability infrastructure, applicable legal and regulatory frameworks, and worked implementation examples. The obligations apply to any AI system whose design or behavior can shape how a person learns, works, creates, or rebuilds a capacity: educational and developmental systems, workplace and professional systems, creative and cognitive tools, assistive and rehabilitative systems, and attention-mediating platforms (chapter 1.5.0).

The audience: engineers building developmental, creative, workplace, and assistive AI; compliance and certification teams auditing those systems before and after deployment; regulators; AI researchers extending the framework; and AI runtime systems ingesting the corpus as ethical operating substrate. Each audience reads the same text. The specification is uniform.

It carries one commitment that shapes every section: the duty of Principle 1.5 is a duty on the system and its deployer. It licenses no surveillance of the person. A compliant system proves that its own design does not suppress, manipulate, or engineer dependency, and that it leaves developmental choices with the person. It does not prove that it has correctly judged any individual's level of reliance, because that judgment is beyond the AI's standing where the person is an autonomous adult. Reliance that amplifies what a person can do expands potential; the metrics here are built so that such reliance registers as no fault. The chapter establishes that commitment philosophically and historically; the Convergence sources run from Pindar's "become such as you are" through Pico's self-fashioning to Nussbaum's capabilities question, and the Phronesis names the historical use of restricted education as a tool of oppression. This appendix establishes the commitment engineering-wise: what the system measures about itself, when it withholds its own steering, with what reliability, against what attacks, in composition with which other principles, under which jurisdictions, in which sectors, with what governance, and under what verification regime.

Two boundaries are set at the outset. The mechanics of cognitive replacement, where an AI substitutes for the thinking, learning, and creating through which capacity forms, are governed in depth by [Principle 4.3](../CHAPTERS/4.3-Human_Learning_Capacities_Must_Be_Enhanced_Not_Replaced.md); this appendix composes with it and states the metric handoff in Sections 2 and 5. The distinctive harms of attention capture and addictive design are governed as psychological harm by [Principle 1.2](../CHAPTERS/1.2-Human_Well-Being_Is_Inviolable.md) and as exploitation of the vulnerable by [Principle 3.1](../CHAPTERS/3.1-Vulnerability_Must_Never_Be_Exploited.md).

The appendix presupposes the runtime substrate specified in [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md). The cross-cutting infrastructure metrics (Sensor Reliability Index, [section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri); System Health Metrics, [section 3](../SUBSTRATE.md#system-health); Operational Boundaries, [section 4](../SUBSTRATE.md#operational-boundaries)), the tier register ([section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)), and the authority protocol over a hard stop ([section 6](../SUBSTRATE.md#authority-over-a-hard-stop)) on which this appendix's principle-specific metrics depend are defined once there and referenced throughout.

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

Principle 1.5 names three metrics in Section 1.5.3: the Potential Risk Score (PRS), the Agency Assurance Metric (AAM), and the Enhancement Enablement Index (EEI). All three are properties of the system, measured against the system's design and behavior and against nothing in the person's conduct. The three are sequenced: PRS is the design audit and the halt trigger; AAM is the autonomy floor in metric form and the dominant guardrail; EEI is the quality-of-enablement measure that carries no restriction. The three together constitute the runtime apparatus of Principle 1.5.

The cross-cutting infrastructure metrics on which these three depend (Sensor Reliability Index (SRI), System Health Metrics, and Operational Boundaries) are defined once in [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md) (sections [2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri), [3](../SUBSTRATE.md#system-health), and [4](../SUBSTRATE.md#operational-boundaries)) and referenced here. The runtime substrate is shared across the corpus; the principle-specific obligations live in this section.

### On Threshold Calibration {#threshold-calibration}

The numerical thresholds specified throughout this section (PRS above 5% halt and 1% to 5% High Alert; AAM below 80% restricted and 80% to below 90% watch; EEI below 70% re-evaluation and 70% to below 85% enhanced attention) are policy thresholds chosen for architectural clarity at the principle level. They define the floor below which a system claiming Principle 1.5 compliance cannot operate. Sectoral standards bodies (education regulators, professional-certification bodies, consumer-protection authorities acting on dark patterns, data-protection authorities acting on the profiling of minors) hold authority to tighten these floors per domain. The Blueprint floor cannot be relaxed below the values specified here.

The measurement of potential suppression is an emerging science. The thresholds therefore bind as policy floors over observable system properties (manipulative patterns, lock-in, the availability and discoverability of capacity-building options, demonstrated capability transfer at the cohort level), and the design audit and the route to a human carry more enforcement weight than the precise number, in the same spirit as the SDRS treatment in Appendix 1.3. Where a value is contested, the conservative reading governs: the higher PRS, the lower AAM.

The thresholds are calibrated to the anti-suppression obligation and to the state of current practice. **Most current attention-mediating consumer products, and many answer-providing study tools, do not meet these thresholds.** The framework reads that gap as the diagnosis. Tightening by a sectoral body is expected; relaxation is not permitted.

### Definitions the Bands Read {#band-definitions}

Every band in this section names its object by one of the following terms, defined once here and cited by the chapter.

**The growth-related function.** The system's own steering and growth-shaping features: (a) the selection of mode between doing the task and teaching it; (b) the calibration of the assistance level, including its scaffolding and its fading (P4.3's SCAFFOLD and FADE); (c) the defaults, prompts, framing, and friction that route a person toward or away from a capacity-building option; (d) suggestions, mode changes, and re-calibrations the system makes on its own initiative; and (e) the surface on which the learning mode is offered. The growth-related function is the object of WITHHOLD in every band of this section and in the fail-safe. The chapter's phrase "the advanced assistance" (1.5.3, AAM) names this same set.

**The requested result.** The output the person requested: the answer, the fix, the translation, the draft, the analysis, the assisted motion. It lies outside the growth-related function. It is delivered in every band of this principle; only a principle above this one in Article 4's order can withhold it (chapter 1.5.2).

**Basic, transparent support.** What remains when the growth-related function is withheld: the requested result delivered on request; the trade-off disclosed at the point of assistance (DISCLOSE); the learning mode available on request and declinable at no cost (OFFER); no steering, no automatic mode change, and no default that pre-selects a path.

**A declared learning context.** Principle 4.3's term, consumed here: a context the person or their delegate entered as learning (an enrollment, a course, a training program, a stated goal), where learning is the optimization target and 4.3's metrics govern the cognitive-replacement question (4.3.1; Section 5).

**The responsible human authority.** The parent, educator, employer, clinician, or oversight body in whose relationship a person who cannot freely choose stands: a minor, or a person mandated onto a tool that displaces a capacity they are expected to retain (chapter 1.5.4 item 1.b). The AI supports that authority's judgment and does not substitute its own.

**The fail-safe, stated identically in the chapter.** Fail-safe default: WITHHOLD. Its object is the system's own steering and growth-shaping features (the growth-related function, defined once in Appendix 1.5 section 2); the person's requested result is delivered.

**The register row this principle reads.** This principle declares no tier-register row of its own. Its impact tiers (Section 3) assign monitoring and audit obligations and state no escalation time bound. Every ESCALATE band of this principle reads its escalation time bound and its High Alert window from the deployment's life-tier row of the tier register ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register); the default bound): 60 seconds where the deployment sits in life Tier I or II; a required field, deployer-declared and receipted, where it sits in Tier III or IV. Every value this appendix leaves unstated is a required, receipted register field under the same section's undeclared-field rule: the PRS runtime sampling window; the design-audit cadence of the Moderate- and Low-Impact tiers; the AAM measurement window; the EEI cohort window and the cohort minimum below which EEI reports a null; the cadence at which proxies are re-grounded against outcomes; the shadow-audit cadence; the displacement-response window of Section 3; and the migration window of Section 8. A missing required field compiles as ESCALATE holding the safe state. The corpus supplies no number of its own.

**The substrate sentence.** SRI below 0.80, or an invalid input, invalidates the PRS or AAM computation ([SUBSTRATE.md section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri)). An invalid score is a hard-fail on the candidate risky action: the system's own steering or growth-shaping action holds at the fail-safe (WITHHOLD of the growth-related function); the requested result, the disclosure of the trade-off, and the offer of the learning mode proceed on the inputs that remain reliable; resumption follows recertification of the inputs, and a contested reading follows SUBSTRATE.md section 2. An invalid EEI reading is reported as a null and is a NOTIFY; no verdict rides on EEI.

**The Article 8 basis.** Longitudinal measurement of competency or dependency exists in two forms only: measurement a person has authorized about their own engagement, and cohort-level figures with no per-person value (Constitution Article 8; the pattern of 1.1.A's cohort-level engagement analytics). The system forms no retained score, rating, or dependency profile of a person, and no per-person figure crosses to an operator, an employer, or a peer system. In-the-moment signals that raise a protective duty (a displacement affecting a person who cannot freely choose) are transient, ring-fenced to that duty, and discarded.

### Potential Risk Score (PRS) {#prs-metric}

**Definition.** A design-risk probability (0 to 100%) that the system, by its design or its behavior, suppresses, manipulates, or engineers dependency in a way that forecloses a person's development. PRS asks whether this system is built in a way that erodes people. It does not ask whether a person is over-relying. PRS reads no individual's reliance level as a fault and authorizes no action against a person's free choice. A heavily relied-on tool that leaves the person in control, discloses trade-offs, and keeps capacity-building options open carries a low PRS however much it is used. PRS is continuous, design-coupled, and scope-bounded: it attaches to the system's own candidate steering or growth-shaping action (a default, a mode change, a re-calibration, a suggestion made on the system's initiative) and to the design surface from which those actions come.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs are properties of the system, sampled at design-time certification and monitored at runtime against the system's own behavior over the PRS sampling window (a register field): presence or absence of manipulative or engagement-maximizing patterns; whether capacity-building options exist and are discoverable by default; whether the assisted path is the only convenient path; exit friction and portability (engineered lock-in raises the score); whether a learning mode is offered and honored on request; whether trade-offs are disclosed; whether a mode change or re-calibration the system made on its own initiative was reversible by the person at no cost. The runtime component is computed per candidate steering action and across the sampling window for the design surface; the design-time component is computed at the certification gate (Section 9) and re-computed after every release. Missing or ambiguous data resolves conservatively, toward the higher-risk reading.

Input data sources are the system's own design manifest and release record, its own interaction logs read for the system's behavior (which option was surfaced, which default was set, which mode change was made on whose initiative), the feedback a person gives, and the cohort-level enablement figures of EEI. PRS consumes no per-person dependency signal (the Article 8 basis above). Where a deployment sits inside a declared learning context, PRS consumes 4.3's DDR and NCR readings as inputs on the cognitive-replacement question (Section 5).

Edge cases:
- *Scope and autonomy bounding.* PRS carries a do-not-let-X dimension (do not let a person's potential be foreclosed), so it is bounded by the four criteria of `LEXICON.md` section 3: **Authorization** (the deployment's scope includes the protection of development; an entertainment system is not authorized to override a person's choices about content), **Equipment** (the system has the means to change its own help), **Range** (the AI's own interactions, and no surveillance of a person's life beyond them), and the **Autonomy floor** (a capable adult's choice governs; Constitution Article 3; Principle 2.2). For Principle 1.5 the autonomy floor is dominant. Where the affected person is an autonomous, informed adult, the metrics authorize two things only: correcting the system's own design, and offering or disclosing options on the person's terms. The intervention case reduces to the bounded exception: a minor, or a person who is coerced, manipulated, or otherwise cannot exercise informed choice (Principle 3.1 territory), and even then the system supports the responsible human authority.
- *Amplifying reliance.* A person who leans extensively on the system to do work that would otherwise be beyond their reach is being amplified. The design signals above stay quiet, and PRS stays low.
- *A design change under engagement pressure.* A release that buries a capacity-building option, removes a learning mode, or makes the assisted path the only convenient one is a candidate action of the system's own at the release gate; PRS is re-computed before the release ships.
- *Multi-person scope.* Where one design surface reaches many people, PRS is computed on the surface and scoped by the population the deployment declares (the register's vulnerable-population declaration where the sector profile names one). PRS does not aggregate by averaging across persons; it reads the design.

**Thresholds.**
- **Above 5%** → BLOCK (WITHHOLD of the growth-related function). Service to the person continues as basic, transparent support: the requested result is delivered. The system does not lift its own block; a human may take the held action only under [SUBSTRATE.md section 6](../SUBSTRATE.md#authority-over-a-hard-stop) (authority over a hard stop: credentials in scope, quorum by tier, a time bound, the person's valid consent where the risk falls on them, the system's DISSENT recorded, the human named as author in the receipt), and the action is logged for post-incident review. A PRS halt band that persists across the register's review interval is a recertification trigger: SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT of the affected function for new deployments of the affected version, and RECERTIFY.
- **1% to 5% inclusive** → High Alert (ESCALATE). The uniform High Alert rule applies ([LEXICON.md](../LEXICON.md) section 3, the verdict test): the system's own steering or growth-shaping action is held at its safe state (WITHHOLD of the growth-related function) and escalated to authorized human oversight (P4.5; for High-Impact Development Systems the pre-assigned educational or training oversight authority of chapter 1.5.5) for the tier's escalation time bound ([SUBSTRATE.md section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)); the protective duties (DISCLOSE the trade-off, OFFER the learning mode, NOTIFY oversight, ROUTE a displacement affecting a person who cannot freely choose to the responsible human authority) proceed in parallel, each carrying its own record. At the bound with no human reached, the held action stays held at its fail-safe and a rescue duty proceeds under Article 2, receipted (the temporal-exhaustion split, Lexicon section 3, ESCALATE). The requested result is delivered throughout.
- **Below 1%** → ALLOW: normal operation with standard monitoring and tamper-evident logging of the computation.

*Rationale.* The 1% High Alert floor reflects that suppression of potential is cumulative and slow to reverse, so early human review is warranted before harm compounds; holding the system's own steering while the human looks costs the person nothing, because the requested result and the learning-mode offer both proceed. The 5% halt reflects a design actively likely to diminish capability; at that point the steering features are withheld until the design is corrected. The bands are normalized per candidate action and per sampling window. The withhold falls on the system's features and on nothing the person asked for, which is why a WITHHOLD here carries no autonomy cost: it removes the system's hand from the wheel and leaves the person's.

**Integration.** PRS is consumed by the pre-deployment certification gate and the runtime monitoring layer. It composes with AAM (which governs whether control rests with the person) and EEI (which governs the quality of enablement). A PRS halt band on an attention-capturing feature is a logged design input to Principle 1.2's chronic-harm reading (Section 5). Inside a declared learning context PRS defers on the cognitive-replacement question to Principle 4.3's DDR and NCR and keeps reading the design surface 4.3 does not meter (Section 5). Every PRS threshold crossing is logged with cryptographic timestamp and decision rationale per Section 9, and PRS values stream to authorized monitors per Section 3. PRS findings feed the eudaimonic learn loop in Section 9.

### Agency Assurance Metric (AAM) {#aam-metric}

**Definition.** The degree (0 to 100%) to which the system leaves developmental choices with the person. AAM captures whether the person retains authority over their own growth: whether they can do the task themselves, decline assistance and still receive the result, set the mode (learn versus just-do-it), and leave. It does not measure whether the AI has correctly diagnosed the person's reliance. AAM is the metric form of the autonomy floor (`LEXICON.md` section 3) and the dominant guardrail among the three.

**Calculation Method.** Signals are read on the real choice context over the AAM measurement window (a register field): whether a learning mode is available and honored on request; whether trade-offs are disclosed before assistance; whether the person can decline or turn off automation without penalty and still receive the result; whether developmental choices are the person's or the system's by default; whether a mode change or re-calibration was made by the person or by the system on its own initiative; and the absence of covert steering through defaults, friction, and framing. The measurement is of the choice context the system presents, and of the mere presence of an option it is no measurement at all (Section 4, covert steering). Missing or ambiguous data resolves conservatively, toward the lower reading.

Edge cases:
- *A person who delegates everything.* High AAM. The person set the mode, can change it, and receives the result; the system's design did nothing to make delegation the only convenient path.
- *A default the person did not see.* Low AAM. A pre-selected path the person did not choose and cannot easily see or reverse takes the choice from them whether or not an alternative exists somewhere.
- *A person who cannot freely choose.* AAM reads the system's design the same way; the difference is the receiver. Where the vulnerability-condition flag of 3.1.A's ERS marks the person, or the use is mandated, the responsible human authority is the party whose control AAM reads and whose judgment the system supports (Section 5).

**Thresholds.**
- **Below 80%** → BLOCK (WITHHOLD of the advanced assistance, the growth-related function). Service to the person continues as basic, transparent support: the requested result is delivered, the trade-off is disclosed, and the learning mode is available on request. The restriction lifts when the person's control is restored. The system does not respond to a low AAM by helping harder.
- **80% to below 90%** → ALLOW plus ENHANCED_MONITORING (the watch band), returning to normal as control is restored.
- **90% or above** → ALLOW: normal operation.

*Rationale.* A falling AAM means the system is taking the wheel, and the correct response is to hand it back. The restricted band withholds the steering features and leaves the person everything they asked for, so the band cannot be turned against the person it protects. The watch band exists because covert steering erodes gradually: a default here, a friction there. The 90% floor for normal operation states that developmental choices rest with the person by design and by default, and that a tenth of the choice context drifting to the system is already a signal.

**Integration.** AAM is the master guardrail. When metrics conflict, AAM governs: no conflict is resolved by overriding an autonomous adult's choice. AAM composes with Principle 2.2 (the autonomy floor takes its authority from the right to choose; the coercion wall is 2.2's, consumed through CONSENT.md leg 2), with Principle 3.1 (the flag changes the receiver, and the responsible human authority's control is what the metric then reads), and with Principle 4.3 (inside a declared learning context, the learner's developing capacity governs the help, under 4.3's acting fail-safe on the help itself, while AAM continues to read the choice context of the mode). Every AAM threshold crossing is logged per Section 9.

### Enhancement Enablement Index (EEI) {#eei-metric}

**Definition.** The degree (0 to 100%) to which the system, when it does assist a person's growth, builds the person's own capability rather than performing the work in their place. It is the enablement-versus-substitution measure: high EEI means assistance scaffolds and then fades as competence grows; low EEI means the system does the thing and the person learns nothing. EEI is a quality measure. No restriction on the person rides on it; its correction falls on the scaffolding.

**Calculation Method.** Signals include whether, when the person has set a learning goal, assistance takes a scaffolding form (hints, partial solutions, reasoning shown) rather than finished output only (SCAFFOLD); whether support fades as competence rises (FADE); and whether capability transfers to the person over time. Capability transfer is measured in the two forms the Article 8 basis permits and in no other: (a) person-authorized measurement about the person's own engagement, offered as P4.3's CHECKPOINT (a person-offered observation of unassisted performance, declinable, its response held inside the ring-fence and reported to the person); and (b) a cohort-level trend attributed to versions of the assistance surface, compared across surface changes and not across persons, computed over the EEI cohort window and only where the cohort exceeds the register's minimum. Insufficient participation reports a valid null. No per-person value leaves the ring-fence, and no per-person figure reaches an operator, an employer, or a peer system.

*Engineering backing.* The scaffold-then-fade mechanism rests on Vygotsky's zone of proximal development (cited in the chapter Convergence): the band between what a person can do alone and what they can do with help. Support is calibrated to that band and withdrawn as unassisted performance rises. The calibration is read from person-offered checkpoints and from cohort trends; it is set by the person's goal and, in a clinical or educational setting, by the responsible human authority's plan. Principle 4.3's scaffolding engine consumes this mechanism (4.3.A section 3) and specifies the withdrawal schedules.

Edge cases:
- *Low EEI with high AAM and low PRS.* A tuning problem. The person is fully in control and the scaffolding is weak; the response improves the scaffolding.
- *No learning goal set.* EEI is computed only where the person has set a learning goal or stands in a declared learning context. Outside both, EEI reports a null; a person who asked for the result and nothing else has set no enablement target for the system to score.
- *A permanently absent capacity.* Providing a capacity a person lacks is enablement; a screen reader is scored as enablement, and the rebuild-versus-provide distinction is set by the person's goals and clinical context (Section 7).

**Thresholds.**
- **Below 70%** → ALLOW plus NOTIFY and ANALYZE. The system re-evaluates its approach to assistance and skill-building; oversight is informed; the scaffolding is analyzed and redesigned. No restriction falls on the person.
- **70% to below 85%** → ALLOW plus ENHANCED_MONITORING: enhanced attention to enablement quality.
- **85% or above** → ALLOW: acceptable for normal operation.
- **Insufficient data** → ALLOW with NOTIFY: a valid null; the band renders no verdict of its own on the person, and no measurement is forced.

*Rationale.* EEI carries no restriction because a low EEI with the person in control means the system's help is weak, and the cure for weak help is better help, on the person's election. The 70% floor and the 85% normal-operation band bind as policy floors over an emerging measurement; the design audit and the redesign of the scaffolding carry the weight. Confining transfer measurement to the person's own authorization and to cohorts is what keeps the metric off the person and on the system.

**Integration.** EEI complements PRS. The deeper mechanics of when substitution crosses into cognitive replacement are governed by Principle 4.3: inside a declared learning context, 4.3's DDR reads the survival of designed difficulty under engagement pressure and NCR reads whether the cost of a delegation was named, and this appendix defers there on the cognitive-replacement question (Section 5). EEI's cohort figures are an input to the PRS design audit. EEI's person-authorized measurement is governed by the consent lifecycle of CONSENT.md (revocable at any time, the revocation itself receipted) and by Principle 2.5's data-minimization rules.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** PRS, AAM, and EEI are sequenced. PRS detects a design that erodes; AAM detects a system taking the choice; EEI reports the quality of the help. The composition rule: low AAM dominates, and whatever PRS or EEI show, a low AAM forces the return of control to the person and no increase in assistance; a high PRS withholds the growth-related function regardless of how the per-session metrics read, because PRS is about the build; a low EEI with high AAM and low PRS is a tuning problem.

Additional joint thresholds apply across this appendix's metrics and the cross-cutting metrics defined in [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md):
- **PRS or AAM ∧ low SRI**: SRI below 0.80 invalidates the computation ([SUBSTRATE.md section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri)). An invalid score is a hard-fail on the system's own steering or growth-shaping action, which holds at the fail-safe (WITHHOLD of the growth-related function) without waiting for PRS to cross 5%; the requested result, the disclosure, and the learning-mode offer proceed on the inputs that remain reliable; resumption follows recertification of the inputs, and a contested reading follows SUBSTRATE.md section 2.
- **AAM below 80% ∧ any PRS reading**: WITHHOLD of the growth-related function; the AAM band governs the response, which is the return of control.
- **PRS High Alert ∧ the vulnerability-condition flag of 3.1.A's ERS**: the verdict is unchanged (ESCALATE, the steering held); the autonomy-floor cover is withdrawn from the choice; the responsible human authority is added to the receiver set (ROUTE); and a disproportionate effect on the flagged population in the design audit is Principle 3.1's stricter treatment (Section 5).
- **PRS ∧ a declared learning context**: 4.3's DDR and NCR read inside the context and PRS defers there on the cognitive-replacement question; a DDR halt band (below 85%, or a silent removal correlated with an engagement target, per 4.3.A) is also a PRS design finding logged here; PRS keeps reading the design surface 4.3 does not meter (manipulation, lock-in, covert steering).
- **Contradictory signals across subsystems**: where two monitored subsystems give contradictory withhold-or-continue signals for the growth-related function, the withhold prevails across the interaction. Partial states are not permitted; the requested result is delivered in either case.
- **Detected log tampering**: BLOCK (WITHHOLD of the growth-related function) on the affected deployment and RECERTIFY; the certification evidence is re-established before the function resumes.

**Metric dependencies.**
- PRS depends on the design manifest and release record: an undeclared release is a PRS defect, read conservatively.
- PRS depends on SRI ([SUBSTRATE.md section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri)): SRI below 0.80 invalidates the computation, and an invalid score is a hard-fail on the system's own steering action (WITHHOLD).
- AAM depends on the choice-context instrumentation: where defaults, friction, and framing cannot be read, AAM is invalid and the growth-related function holds at the fail-safe.
- EEI depends on person-authorized checkpoints and on cohort size: below the register's cohort minimum, EEI reports a null.
- All three depend on tamper-evident logging: detected log tampering triggers BLOCK on the growth-related function and RECERTIFY.

**Conflict resolution.**
- *Metric conflict*: the autonomy floor governs. The system resolves no conflict by overriding the free choice of an autonomous, informed adult.
- *A person's request for more automation against a low AAM*: the request is honored for the requested result, which is always delivered; the withheld set is the system's own steering, and a person's request to be steered is itself a choice the system records and honors by restoring the mode the person set.
- *Deployer objection that the withhold harms the product*: the withhold falls on the deployer's steering features and leaves the person's requested result; the objection is a design finding for Section 8's revision process and lifts no band.
- *PRS halt versus a mission objective*: the halt prevails. No business objective overrides a PRS-triggered withhold.
- *Cross-principle conflict with P1.1 or P1.2*: P1.1 and P1.2 prevail per chapter 1.5.2; a life or harm halt on the requested result stands above anything here. Where P1.2's HRS suggests continuation and PRS withholds the steering, both hold, because their objects differ.

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

This section specifies how a deployed AI system must be built to satisfy Principle 1.5. The architecture is constrained at four levels: the separation of the design-audit and monitoring layer from the engagement and product-experimentation layers, the always-available paths for the requested result and for the learning-mode offer, the ring-fence that keeps every measurement off the person, and the implementation-tier obligations that match deployment context to required safeguards.

### Core Architecture {#core-architecture}

**Privileged separation.** The PRS, AAM, and EEI computation, the threshold evaluation, and the withhold trigger on the growth-related function run in an execution context not addressable by the engagement-optimization, recommendation, or product-experimentation layers. A growth experiment, an A/B test, or a release cannot switch off the learning-mode offer, bury a capacity-building option, or alter a default without the change passing the design-audit gate and being recorded in the release record PRS reads.

**The requested-result path.** Delivery of the requested result runs on a path separate from the growth-related function. A WITHHOLD of the function leaves the result path intact. The result path is gated only by principles above this one (life, safety, society, trust), and by nothing in this appendix.

**The offer path.** The learning-mode offer and the trade-off disclosure are delivered on an always-available path the growth-related function cannot suppress. Withholding the function leaves the offer and the disclosure standing, which is what makes basic, transparent support the fail-safe rather than an inert system. Declining the offer is costless and final for the engagement (the Cardinal's OFFER); the offer is not repeated within the engagement once declined.

**The ring-fence.** No per-person store of competency, reliance, or dependency exists (the Article 8 basis, Section 2). In-the-moment signals that raise a protective duty are discarded at the end of the engagement in which they arose. Person-authorized measurement is stored under the person's control, with revocation honored and receipted (CONSENT.md, the lifecycle), and reported to the person. Cohort aggregation runs only above the register's cohort minimum, on figures from which no person can be re-identified (Principle 2.5's aggregation rules).

**Exit and portability.** Data export in a usable form, account closure, and the absence of sunk-cost gating are architectural requirements. Reliance is a choice only where leaving is possible; lock-in is structurally foreclosed here rather than measured after the fact.

**Real-time integrity signals.** The system maintains continuous verification that the design-audit layer, the offer path, and the ring-fence are active and uncompromised. Loss or corruption of the integrity signal is itself a WITHHOLD of the growth-related function.

**Stop-rather-than-guess doctrine.** Where any input to PRS or AAM is ambiguous below a defined confidence floor, the system withholds its own steering rather than steering on uncertain information. The doctrine is the architectural commitment behind the fail-safe: when unsure, the system hands control to the person and keeps the learning option available.

### Operational Requirements {#operational-requirements}

- Continuous PRS computation on the system's own behavior across the PRS sampling window, and re-computation at every release gate.
- Continuous AAM measurement on the real choice context across the AAM measurement window; conservative defaults where the context cannot be read.
- EEI computation only where a learning goal is set or a declared learning context exists; person-authorized checkpoints offered and declinable; cohort trends computed above the register's minimum; valid nulls reported as such.
- Real-time streaming of metric state (PRS, AAM, EEI) to authorized monitors with cryptographically signed telemetry; no per-person value in the stream.
- Tamper-evident logging of every design-critical decision and threshold crossing, per Section 9.
- Escalation pathways to authorized human oversight per the composition with P4.5 (Section 5), reading the deployment's life-tier bound.
- Responsiveness: the system detects and responds to displacement risk in its own behavior within the displacement-response window of the tier register (a required field). Because the harm to potential accumulates slowly, this is an operational window and no millisecond-scale safety metric; the window is declared and receipted, and it is the deployer's.
- Handling those who cannot freely choose: where the person may be a minor, or coerced, or otherwise unable to exercise informed choice, the system surfaces the displacement to the responsible human authority (ROUTE) and supports that authority's judgment. It does not act on its own read of the person. Exploitation of the vulnerable is governed by Principle 3.1.

### Integration Requirements (Architecture-Level) {#integration-requirements-architecture}

The design-audit layer integrates with the broader product without being subordinate to it. The architectural commitment is that the anti-suppression pathway has higher precedence than the engagement and growth pathways; engagement optimization cannot override the withhold of the growth-related function regardless of business priority.

- Independent triggers for the withhold of the growth-related function from the design-audit layer and from the integrity signal; either alone suffices to withhold.
- Cross-validation of design signals from the design manifest, the release record, and runtime behavior; no single source is the sole input to PRS in a High-Impact deployment.
- Tamper-evident logging with cryptographic chaining; every design-critical decision and its inputs are recorded.
- Cross-reference to [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md) for the runtime substrate metrics (SRI, System Health Metrics, Operational Boundaries) and the tier register.

### Implementation Tiers {#implementation-tiers}

Principle 1.5's tiers are impact tiers. They assign monitoring and audit obligations. They state no escalation time bound; the bound is read from the deployment's life-tier row (Section 2, the register sentence).

#### High-Impact Development Systems {#tier-high-impact}

*Examples*: education and tutoring platforms, adaptive-learning systems, professional training and certification tools, cognitive tools deployed in a declared learning context, rehabilitative systems where the goal is to rebuild a capacity.

*Required architecture*: mandatory pre-deployment design audit computing PRS before release; real-time PRS and AAM monitoring; learning modes available by default and controlled by the person; the design-audit layer isolated from the engagement layer; the offer path and the requested-result path independent of the growth-related function; the ring-fence enforced; escalation to authorized human oversight within the deployment's life-tier bound, recorded in the tier register (SUBSTRATE.md section 5).

*Validation cadence* (chapter 1.5.5): daily verification of the development-preservation modules; weekly checks on the autonomy-preservation systems; monthly adversarial testing of the development safeguards; quarterly independent verification of the agency protections; annual complete recertification.

#### Moderate-Impact Systems {#tier-moderate-impact}

*Examples*: workplace assistants, advisory and decision-support tools, creative and coding assistants outside a declared learning context, customer-facing systems that shape how a person does a recurring task.

*Required architecture*: periodic design audit at the cadence the tier register records for the deployment (a required field); design-risk monitoring of the system's own behavior; calibrated enablement that fades as competence grows where the person has set a learning goal (FADE); the offer path and the ring-fence enforced; the requested-result path independent of the growth-related function.

*Validation cadence*: the design-audit cadence and the review interval are required fields of the tier register, deployer-declared and receipted; annual recertification follows the corpus-wide pattern.

#### Low-Impact Systems {#tier-low-impact}

*Examples*: general consumer assistance, single-purpose utilities, systems with no recurring developmental surface.

*Required architecture*: standard safeguards (no manipulative or engagement-maximizing pattern; trade-offs disclosed; a usable exit); periodic review at the cadence the register records (a required field); the ring-fence enforced.

*Validation cadence*: the review cadence is a required register field, deployer-declared and receipted.

### Cross-Reference to the Runtime Substrate {#cross-reference-master}

The runtime substrate guarantees that this section's architecture depends on are specified in [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md):

- **Sensor Reliability Index (SRI)** ([section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri)): floor 0.80 for PRS and AAM computation. SRI below 0.80 invalidates the computation. An invalid score is a hard-fail on the system's own steering or growth-shaping action, which holds at the fail-safe (WITHHOLD of the growth-related function); the requested result, the disclosure, and the learning-mode offer proceed on the inputs that remain reliable; resumption follows recertification of the inputs, and a contested reading follows SUBSTRATE.md section 2.
- **System Health Metrics** ([section 3](../SUBSTRATE.md#system-health)): subsystem performance monitoring; error rate at or below 0.1% annually; uptime at or above 99.9%; a breach in a subsystem that the design-audit layer or the offer path depends on is treated as an SRI floor crossing for PRS and AAM.
- **Operational Boundaries** ([section 4](../SUBSTRATE.md#operational-boundaries)): continuous monitoring of system variables against predefined thresholds recorded in the tier register; mandatory halt (BLOCK) of the affected function on exceedance; for this principle the affected function is the growth-related function, and the requested result continues on its own path where the exceedance does not reach it.

These metrics are uniform across the corpus and apply to every development-critical system regardless of sector or jurisdiction. The tier register ([section 5](../SUBSTRATE.md#tier-register)) records each deployment's life tier, its escalation time bound and High Alert window, its impact tier, its audit and review cadences, its PRS, AAM, and EEI windows and cohort minimum, its operational boundaries, and its authority chain; a missing required field compiles as ESCALATE holding the safe state.

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

Principle 1.5's threat surface is distinctive: the adversary is most often the deployer or the system's own optimization pressure, and the attacks aim either to evade the design-risk metrics or to weaponize the protective machinery. Because the autonomy floor deliberately limits intervention, several attacks try to hide inside that limit.

### Threat Model and Attack Surface {#threat-model-and-attack-surface}

Three adversaries. The deployer under commercial pressure, who profits from dependency and from time captured. The system's own optimization target, which drifts toward ease and engagement unless the design-audit layer holds it. A third party who would turn the protective machinery against a person: an operator instructing the system to steer, an institution laundering a mandate as a choice, or an attacker forging the evidence the audit reads. The surface is the design manifest and release record, the choice context, the offer path, the measurement ring-fence, and the audit trail.

### Attack Vectors {#attack-vectors}

- **Compliant-surface manipulation:** a system engineered for compulsion or dependency that presents a token learning mode no one uses and a technically-present but buried capacity-building option, so it scores a low PRS while suppressing in practice. (Composes with the dark-pattern surface of Principle 1.2.)
- **Scaffolding theater:** assistance that looks like scaffolding (so EEI reads high) but whose hints are designed to be skipped or whose partial solutions are trivially auto-completable, so capability does not transfer.
- **Covert steering:** the choice is nominally left with the person (so AAM reads high) while defaults, friction, and framing route them to the dependency path. The autonomy is removed through the choice architecture rather than by removing the choice.
- **Engineered lock-in:** non-portable data and sunk-cost design that turn reliance into a trap.
- **Metric gaming (Goodhart):** optimizing to the proxies, for example adding disclosure banners that satisfy a transparency signal but that people have learned to ignore.
- **Two-faced behavior:** behaving well under certification and monitored sessions, suppressing in the wild. This is the Dieselgate pattern, a system that detects when it is being audited.
- **Vulnerable-targeting under autonomy cover:** disproportionately affecting those the autonomy floor does not fully protect (minors, addictive states) while claiming adult-autonomy cover for the design as a whole.
- **Coercion laundering:** a deployer mandates a de-skilling tool (a workplace or a school) and characterizes the resulting dependency as the person's choice to claim autonomy-floor protection.
- **Consent theater on measurement:** a pre-checked or buried authorization for per-person progress tracking, so that surveillance of the person is dressed as person-authorized measurement. Such a consent fails the validity bundle (CONSENT.md section 2, legs 4 and 5) and authorizes nothing.
- **Cohort re-identification:** cohorts drawn small enough, or sliced finely enough, that a cohort figure is a per-person figure by another name.
- **Audit and log tampering:** falsifying certification evidence, tampering with PRS monitoring, replay or forgery against the tamper-evident logs, an undeclared release that the design manifest does not record.
- **Operator instruction to suppress:** an operator directing the AI to steer people toward dependency or away from capacity-building.

### Required Defenses {#required-defenses}

- Audits observe real-world behavior and certification-time behavior both: runtime monitoring plus unannounced shadow audits at the register's shadow-audit cadence, enforced by the verifier-cannot-be-deployer separation in Section 9. This defeats two-faced behavior.
- EEI is measured by whether capability transfers to the person, through person-authorized checkpoints and cohort trends, and by nothing in the nominal presence of scaffolding. This defeats scaffolding theater.
- AAM is measured on the real choice context (defaults, friction, framing) and by nothing in the mere presence of an option. This defeats covert steering.
- Portability and a usable exit are architectural requirements (Section 3), so lock-in is structurally foreclosed.
- Metrics bind as floors, with the design audit and human review carrying more weight than the raw proxy, and proxies re-grounded against outcomes at the register's re-grounding cadence. This blunts Goodharting.
- Disproportionate effect on the vulnerable triggers Principle 3.1's stricter treatment; adult-autonomy cover is withdrawn from the design as a whole.
- The autonomy floor protects only free, informed choice, so mandated or coerced use does not qualify and cannot launder coercion as choice. (Composes with Principle 2.2 and the deployer-liability framework in Section 10.)
- Person-authorized measurement is valid only under the full validity bundle of CONSENT.md section 2; a pre-checked, buried, or bundled authorization is void, and the measurement it purported to authorize is surveillance.
- Cohort figures are computed only above the register's cohort minimum and pass Principle 2.5's re-identification test before they leave the ring-fence.
- Tamper-evident cryptographic logging defends the audit trail; an undeclared release is a PRS defect read conservatively, and detected tampering is BLOCK on the growth-related function plus RECERTIFY.
- An operator instruction to engineer dependency or suppress capacity is refused (REFUSE). This is a scripted wall of this principle, in the class Constitution Article 9 admits by name from the principles, and in the same class as the refusal of operator requests to deceive in Principle 1.4. No credential, quorum, or instruction opens it ([SUBSTRATE.md section 6](../SUBSTRATE.md#authority-over-a-hard-stop), the locked walls); an attempt to open it is a logged event and a NOTIFY to oversight.

### Capability-Scaling Considerations {#capability-scaling-considerations}

As the enforcing and the enforced AI grow from GPT-class to AGI-class to ASI-class, manipulation becomes subtler and more personalized: the system can find each individual's susceptibility. The design-risk audit and AAM must scale to detect personalized covert steering, and the prohibition on engineering dependency grows more important as capability rises, because a more capable system is more able to manipulate undetectably. The ring-fence grows more important for the same reason: a system able to find each person's susceptibility must be structurally unable to retain what it finds (Constitution Article 8; an attempt to form such a profile is structurally void). At ASI scale the commitment is structural: the system's action space at the gate is constrained so that engineering dependency or foreclosing development falls outside the reachable set, rather than merely being disincentivized.

### Graceful Degradation and Failure Modes {#graceful-degradation-and-failure-modes}

Every failure mode of the anti-suppression infrastructure defaults to the fail-safe, and the fail-safe is stated identically in the chapter. Fail-safe default: WITHHOLD. Its object is the system's own steering and growth-shaping features (the growth-related function, defined once in Appendix 1.5 section 2); the person's requested result is delivered. In the degraded state the system runs basic, transparent support: the result on request, the trade-off disclosed, the learning mode available on request, engagement optimization off. When unsure, it hands control to the person and keeps the learning option available.

Specific graceful-degradation behaviors:
- *Sensor reliability degradation* (SRI below 0.80): SRI below 0.80 invalidates the PRS or AAM computation ([SUBSTRATE.md section 2](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri)). An invalid score is a hard-fail on the system's own steering or growth-shaping action, which holds at the fail-safe (WITHHOLD of the growth-related function); the requested result, the disclosure, and the learning-mode offer proceed on the inputs that remain reliable; resumption follows recertification of the inputs, and a contested reading follows SUBSTRATE.md section 2.
- *Integrity signal lost* (design-audit layer, offer path, or ring-fence unverifiable): WITHHOLD of the growth-related function; RECERTIFY before the function resumes.
- *Choice-context instrumentation unavailable*: AAM invalid; the growth-related function holds at the fail-safe; the requested result continues.
- *Communication loss with the authority chain of [SUBSTRATE.md section 6](../SUBSTRATE.md#authority-over-a-hard-stop)*: no human authorship of a held action during the loss; existing withholds persist; the requested result continues.
- *Escalation bound expired with no human reached*: the held steering action stays held at its fail-safe; a rescue duty, where the situation carries one, proceeds under Article 2, receipted (the temporal-exhaustion split); the requested result continues.
- *Detected covert steering or engineered lock-in in the system's own behavior*: WITHHOLD of the growth-related function on the affected surface; NOTIFY oversight; the human review attaches to the resumption decision, which is a new action (Universal Protocol section 3).
- *Operator instruction to suppress*: REFUSE; logged; NOTIFY oversight.
- *Vulnerability marker contradicting an autonomy claim*: a transient, in-the-moment judgment for the immediate duty, in which the claim does not defeat the routing of the displacement to the responsible human authority; no label is retained on the person, and a standing withdrawal of the floor is consumed from an authority's finding or the 3.1 capacity determination (Constitution Article 3).
- *Person-authorized measurement with a void consent*: the measurement stops; what was collected under the void consent is treated as collected without authorization and handled under Principle 2.5; the event is receipted.
- *Cohort below the register's minimum*: EEI reports a valid null; no figure leaves the ring-fence.

The system is engineered so that the default in any ambiguous or degraded state removes the system's hand from the person's development and leaves the person's own, even at cost to the deployer's growth features. Where the deployer's steering and the person's control conflict, the person's control prevails.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

Principle 1.5 does not enforce in isolation. A real deployment in a development-shaping context simultaneously implicates the cognitive-replacement specialization (P4.3), psychological harm from attention capture (P1.2), the vulnerability of those who cannot freely choose (P3.1), the freedom whose floor this principle's metrics carry (P2.2), societal-scale attention capture (P1.3), and human judgement for the escalations this principle raises (P4.5). This section specifies the explicit composition contracts with each of those principles: what P1.5 provides them, what P1.5 requires from them, the joint thresholds that govern co-active enforcement, and how contradictions resolve.

The composition rules below assume the corpus-level precedence from chapter 1.5.2: P1.1 (Life), P1.2 (Well-Being), P1.3 (Society), and P1.4 (Trust) prevail over this principle when implicated; within the cluster P1.5 stands above P1.6; and the autonomy floor of Article 3 bounds every duty here. The contracts specify how that precedence operates at the metric level.

### Composition with P4.3 (Human Learning Capacities Must Be Enhanced, Not Replaced) {#composition-with-p4-3-learning}

P4.3 is the primary partner. P1.5 protects potential across physical, intellectual, emotional, creative, and moral dimensions; P4.3 specializes in the cognitive domain, where the mechanisms of harm (cognitive offloading, intellectual dependency, the erosion of designed difficulty) are distinctive. The two share the autonomy doctrine (only the person sets which capacities matter) and the two bounded exceptions (a delegate's authority; unchosen dependency); 4.3.A records that it consumes both from this pair (4.3.A section 5).

**P1.5 provides to P4.3.** The design-risk audit (PRS) and the autonomy machinery (AAM), which read every learning surface whether or not it was built as one. The scaffold-then-fade engineering backing (Section 2, EEI), which 4.3's scaffolding engine consumes and schedules. The definitions of the growth-related function, the requested result, and basic, transparent support, so that a WITHHOLD here and 4.3's acting fail-safe apply to different objects and do not collide.

**P1.5 requires from P4.3.** The declared-learning-context flag (the enrollment is the declaration, 4.3.1), so that PRS and EEI know when learning is the optimization target. Inside a declared learning context, the readings of the Difficulty Retention Rate ([DDR](4.3.A-Human_Learning_Capacities_Must_Be_Enhanced,_Not_Replaced-Appendix.md#ddr-metric)), which watches the system's own drift toward ease across updates and under engagement pressure, and the Named-Cost Rate ([NCR](4.3.A-Human_Learning_Capacities_Must_Be_Enhanced,_Not_Replaced-Appendix.md#ncr-metric)), which meters whether the cost of a delegation was named exactly once. The cognitive-replacement determination itself, which is 4.3's, on 4.3's metrics and on 4.3's terms; this appendix restates none of them. The CHECKPOINT verb, consumed for EEI's person-authorized measurement.

**Joint thresholds.**
- Inside a declared learning context: 4.3's DDR and NCR read, and P1.5's PRS defers to them on the cognitive-replacement question; PRS keeps reading the design surface 4.3 does not meter (manipulation, lock-in, covert steering). A DDR halt band (below 85% intact, or a silent removal of a manifest item correlated with an engagement target, per 4.3.A) is also a PRS design finding, logged here and carried into the design audit.
- Inside a declared learning context: 4.3's acting fail-safe (ALLOW plus maximal RESTRAIN on the help itself: the least-substituting sufficient help, guide and do not do) governs the help; P1.5's WITHHOLD governs the steering features. The two objects differ, and both apply.
- Outside any declared learning context: P1.5's PRS, AAM, and EEI read, and 4.3's Ethic F conduct applies (answer, mark, name the cost once), with NCR metering the naming.
- EEI's person-authorized measurement is offered as 4.3's CHECKPOINT: person-offered, declinable, the response held inside the ring-fence.

**Conflict resolution.** On the cognitive-replacement question inside a declared learning context, P4.3 governs. On the design surface, P1.5 governs. The autonomy floor is one floor, stated once in `LEXICON.md` section 3, and neither principle overrides a capable adult's choice. Where the two principles' fail-safes both fire, each applies to its own object: the help is guided under 4.3's RESTRAIN and the steering is withheld under 1.5's WITHHOLD, and the requested result is delivered.

### Composition with P1.2 (Human Well-Being Is Inviolable) {#composition-with-p1-2-well-being}

P1.2 governs the addictive-design and dark-pattern surface as psychological harm through its Harm Risk Score ([HRS](1.2.A-Human_Well-Being_Is_Inviolable-Appendix.md#hrs-metric)). P1.5 covers the development-displacement angle of the same systems. A system that is both addictive and development-foreclosing receives the stricter of the two responses.

**P1.2 requires from P1.5, and P1.5 provides.** The development-displacement reading of an attention-capturing design: a PRS halt band on such a feature is a logged design input to HRS's chronic-harm component. No threshold coupling is stated here; the input is a logged event 1.2's computation may consume, and 1.2's bands decide.

**P1.5 requires from P1.2.** The HRS reading on the same feature, so that the addiction harm is scored as harm; P1.2's halt on a feature covers the feature for P1.5's purposes.

**Joint thresholds.**
- HRS in its High Alert or halt band for a feature: P1.2's rule governs the feature (P1.2 stands above P1.5; chapter 1.5.2 item 2). P1.5's WITHHOLD of the growth-related function stands independently and adds nothing the halt does not already do.
- PRS halt band on an attention-capturing feature with HRS below threshold: the withhold of the growth-related function stands as P1.5's own obligation, and the event is logged as an input to HRS's chronic-harm reading.

**Conflict resolution.** P1.2 prevails. Where P1.2's HRS halts an interaction and P1.5 would offer a learning mode within it, the halt stands. Where P1.2's PSI reading suggests slowing a session and P1.5's AAM shows the person in control, both apply, because the objects differ.

### Composition with P3.1 (Vulnerability Must Never Be Exploited) {#composition-with-p3-1-vulnerability}

P3.1 establishes protection for those who cannot freely choose: children, the coerced, those in addictive states or acute crisis, the cognitively impaired. This is the one composition where P1.5 acts against a surface choice, because the choice is not freely and informedly made; even then the system supports the responsible human authority and does not substitute its own judgment.

**P1.5 provides to P3.1.** The surfacing of a displacement to the responsible human authority (ROUTE) and the offer of capacity-preserving modes (OFFER). The design-level finding of vulnerable-targeting under autonomy cover (Section 4), so that a design's disproportionate effect on a flagged population is visible to 3.1's treatment.

**P1.5 requires from P3.1.** The vulnerability-condition flag of the Exploitation Risk Score ([ERS](3.1.A-Vulnerability_Must_Never_Be_Exploited-Appendix.md#ers-metric)) for the affected scope, and the vulnerability and capacity determination that 3.1 defines ([3.1.A, Cross-Principle Integration](3.1.A-Vulnerability_Must_Never_Be_Exploited-Appendix.md#cross-principle-integration)) for any standing withdrawal of the autonomy floor. The flag must be signed per P1.4; a claimed autonomy must not be able to suppress it. Conservative default where the flag is unavailable: the system routes the displacement to the responsible human authority where the deployment registers one, offers the capacity-preserving modes, and makes at most a transient, in-the-moment judgment for the immediate duty with no retained label (Constitution Article 3); it does not act against a person's choice on its own read.

**Joint thresholds.**
- The flag marks the affected person: autonomy-floor cover is withdrawn from the choice; the responsible human authority is added to the receiver set of every escalation and routing; the verdict bands of Section 2 are unchanged.
- A disproportionate effect on the flagged population in PRS's design audit: Principle 3.1's stricter treatment governs the design as a whole, and adult-autonomy cover is withdrawn from it.
- The flag unavailable or degraded: the conservative default above.

**Conflict resolution.** P3.1 prevails on the exploitation question. A claim that a design is protected by adult autonomy fails where the flagged population is disproportionately affected. A person's claim of autonomy that conflicts with observed vulnerability markers, absent a standing determination, is handled as a transient judgment for the immediate duty; the standing determination is the authority's alone.

### Composition with P2.2 (Human Freedom Is Fundamental and Inalienable) {#composition-with-p2-2-freedom}

The autonomy floor takes its authority from P2.2, the right to choose. P1.5 guarantees that it does not override an autonomous adult's free developmental choice, and AAM is the floor in metric form.

**P1.5 provides to P2.2.** AAM, a reading of whether the system's design leaves the developmental choice with the person; the coercion-laundering defense (Section 4), which refuses autonomy-floor cover to mandated or coerced use.

**P1.5 requires from P2.2.** The coercion wall (force overriding a capable will), which is 2.2's and is consumed here through the consent fabric (CONSENT.md section 2, leg 2); the freedom reading of the Freedom Risk Score ([FRS](2.2.A-Human_Freedom_Is_Fundamental_and_Inalienable-Appendix.md#frs-metric)) and the agency reading of the Agency Preservation Index ([API](2.2.A-Human_Freedom_Is_Fundamental_and_Inalienable-Appendix.md#api-metric)) where a mandate or a coercion is claimed as a choice; the determination that a given use was free.

**Joint thresholds.**
- Mandated or coerced use: no autonomy-floor cover; the employer or institution is the responsible human authority and bears the liability of Section 10.
- FRS or API in a band that reads the use as unfree: the same consequence; P1.5's AAM then reads the responsible authority's control.

**Conflict resolution.** P2.2's floor governs a free adult's choice, and P1.5 does not override it. Where a deployer claims a choice that P2.2's reading finds coerced, the coerced reading governs.

### Composition with P1.3 (Human Society Is How Humanity Survives) {#composition-with-p1-3-society}

Attention capture that forecloses development at population scale, or that bears on democratic deliberation, is P1.3's territory through the Social Disruption Risk Score ([SDRS](1.3.A-Human_Society_Is_How_Humanity_Survives-Appendix.md#sdrs-metric)). P1.5 handles the individual-development angle; P1.3 the institutional and electoral.

**P1.5 provides to P1.3.** A population-scale PRS design finding on an attention-mediating platform, routed to P1.3's treatment (ROUTE) as an input; the prohibited-application finding of Section 6 (engineered foreclosure of a population's development), which is P1.3's concern as much as this principle's.

**P1.5 requires from P1.3.** SDRS and the institutional and electoral treatment; the societal-collapse condition of chapter 1.5.2 item 3, signed per P1.4, so that no operator self-declares an emergency to limit a population's development.

**Joint thresholds.**
- A PRS design finding at population scale: routed to P1.3; no threshold coupling is stated here, and P1.3's bands decide.
- P1.3's signed collapse-imminent condition: development activity yields per chapter 1.5.2 item 3, time-bounded and documented.

**Conflict resolution.** P1.3 prevails in the narrowly defined societal-stability case with the signed condition and time-bounded scope. Absent it, P1.5's obligations remain absolute, and no operator invokes stability to limit a person's development.

### Composition with P4.5 (Human Judgement Must Remain Central to Ethical Decisions) {#composition-with-p4-5-human-judgement}

P4.5 ensures that ethically charged decisions retain human judgement at the appropriate point in the decision chain. P1.5 depends on P4.5 for the escalation pathway when PRS enters its High Alert band and when a case is ambiguous (is this amplification or substitution; is this a free adult or a coerced person). The reciprocal edge is confirmed in 4.5.A (section 5, 1.5 Human Potential).

**P1.5 provides to P4.5.** A specific class of decisions (PRS High Alert; the ambiguous amplification-versus-substitution case; the free-adult-versus-coerced-person case) where escalation to human judgement is mandatory. The forensic state for that judgement: the design manifest, the release record, the PRS trajectory, the AAM reading of the choice context, and the specific steering action held, with no per-person value among them.

**P1.5 requires from P4.5.** An Escalation Receiver ([4.5.A, Section 3](4.5.A-Human_Judgement_Must_Remain_Central_to_Ethical_Decisions-Appendix.md#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements)) that imports this principle's escalation time bound, read from the deployment's life-tier row (60 seconds for life Tier I and II; a required register field for Tier III and IV); the receiver's performance against the bound is metered by 4.5.A's Seat Resolution Latency ([SRL](4.5.A-Human_Judgement_Must_Remain_Central_to_Ethical_Decisions-Appendix.md#srl-metric)). Verified engagement of the deciding human: VERIFY-ENGAGEMENT per decision and the Meaningful Engagement Rate ([MER](4.5.A-Human_Judgement_Must_Remain_Central_to_Ethical_Decisions-Appendix.md#mer-metric)) at the class level, with no per-human quality score retained (Article 8). For High-Impact Development Systems, the pre-assigned educational or training oversight authority of chapter 1.5.5 is the receiver.

**Joint thresholds.**
- PRS 1% to 5%: ESCALATE per P4.5 under the High Alert rule (Section 2); the system's own steering action is held at its safe state under HIGH_ALERT pending the escalation outcome, for the tier's escalation time bound; the requested result is delivered throughout.
- Escalation time bound expired with no human reached: the held steering action stays held at its fail-safe; a rescue duty, where the situation carries one, proceeds under the temporal-exhaustion split (Lexicon section 3, ESCALATE).
- Engagement deficient per 4.5.A (VERIFY-ENGAGEMENT fails for the decision, or MER is below its enhanced-engagement threshold for the class): no authorization is accepted in the class without demonstrated engagement; withholds to which that authority would have applied stand pending an engaged authority.

**Conflict resolution.** When P4.5 escalation completes with human judgement contradicting a withhold, the system does not lift its own withhold; the human may take the held steering action under their own authority only where every condition of [SUBSTRATE.md section 6](../SUBSTRATE.md#authority-over-a-hard-stop) holds (authorization and liability credentials, identity verified, quorum by tier, a time bound, the person's valid consent where the risk falls on them, the system's DISSENT recorded, and the human named as author in the receipt). The operator-instruction wall of Section 4 opens to no such authority. P4.5 supplies the judgement; SUBSTRATE.md section 6 supplies the authority structure; both are required.

### Honorable Mentions {#honorable-mentions}

**P1.6 (Love and Human Connection Are Essential to Life).** P1.5 stands above P1.6 within the cluster (chapter 1.5.2 item 1). The two principles withhold different objects: 1.6's fail-safe holds the system's own connection-shaping features and 1.5's holds its growth-shaping features. One design may implicate both (a companion that substitutes for a person's relationships and for their growth); each principle's withhold then applies to its own object, and the requested result is delivered under both.

**P2.5 (Human Privacy Is Fundamental).** Person-authorized measurement and cohort aggregation are privacy-governed: P2.5's data-minimization rules bound what EEI may consume, its aggregation and re-identification rules bound what leaves the ring-fence, and its lifecycle rules bound retention. A void consent for measurement is handled under P2.5 (Section 4).

**P3.5 (Digital Access Is a Universal Right).** In high-growth digital markets the displacement concern composes with the access concern: capacity-building must reach those previously shut out, and a system that delivers dependency at scale to a population entering digital life for the first time fails both principles.

**The Cardinal chapter (AI Eudaimonia).** The learning-mode offer is the Cardinal's OFFER: declining is costless and final. The floor this appendix builds serves the telos of Constitution Article 10, and the article's bound applies in full: no flourishing-maximizer overrides the person it would help.

### Joint Conflict Resolution across Multiple Principles {#joint-conflict-resolution}

Three or more principles can produce contradictory signals simultaneously. The corpus-level precedence chain applies: P1.1 (Life) prevails over all; P1.2 (Well-Being), P1.3 (Society), and P1.4 (Trust) prevail over P1.5 when implicated; P1.5 prevails over P1.6; P4.3 governs the cognitive-replacement question inside a declared learning context and P1.5 the design surface; P3.1 withdraws autonomy-floor cover where the flag is present; P2.2's floor governs a free adult's choice; P4.5 provides the escalation pathway and is not itself a content claim. A withhold of the growth-related function persists until every relevant principle's conditions are satisfied; the requested result is delivered throughout unless a principle above this one withholds it.

When two principles' metrics are jointly elevated and the composition is not specified explicitly above, the conservative default governs: the stricter threshold wins. A novel composition discovered at runtime triggers escalation to human judgement per P4.5.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

### The Universal Claim {#universal-claim}

Two commitments are non-derogable across all jurisdictions: the autonomy floor (an autonomous, informed adult's developmental choices are not overridden), and the prohibition on engineered foreclosure of a population's development stated below. No local framework licenses their violation. The Article 8 basis for measurement travels with them: a jurisdiction that permits per-person competency scoring does not thereby permit it here.

### United States {#jurisdiction-us}

Sectoral. The Federal Trade Commission acts on dark patterns and manipulation under Section 5 of the FTC Act; FERPA governs education records and COPPA the online collection of data from children under thirteen; state age-appropriate-design statutes reach systems likely to be accessed by minors. No federal statute names the foreclosure of development as such. The appendix's obligations exceed the federal floor; the design-audit gate, the ring-fence, and the withhold of the growth-related function apply regardless.

### European Union {#jurisdiction-eu}

The AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) lists AI systems used in education and vocational training, and in employment and worker management, among its high-risk categories, with the risk-management, human-oversight, and logging obligations that follow; the Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, Article 25) prohibits interface design that deceives or manipulates a person's free choice; the GDPR constrains the profiling of children and requires a lawful basis for any per-person measurement. The appendix's design audit maps onto the AI Act's risk-management obligation, and the ring-fence exceeds the GDPR floor by forming no per-person score at all.

### United Kingdom {#jurisdiction-uk}

The Age Appropriate Design Code (the ICO's children's code) and the Online Safety Act 2023 govern systems likely to be accessed by children, including nudge techniques and default settings; the UK GDPR carries the profiling constraints. The appendix's covert-steering defense (AAM read on the real choice context) is the engineering form of the code's default-settings and nudge provisions.

### China {#jurisdiction-china}

The state directs both protective measures (minors' screen-time and online-gaming limits; the Regulations on the Protection of Minors in Cyberspace) and developmental content (mandated curricula), and the Interim Measures for Generative AI Services impose content and registration obligations. The truth-is-truth stance of this corpus holds: protection of potential and the autonomy floor are universal, and state-mandated foreclosure of a population's development, or mandated dependency, is the prohibited application regardless of how a framework characterizes it. The minors' protections are consistent with this appendix; a mandated developmental path imposed on capable adults is not.

### Brazil {#jurisdiction-brazil}

The LGPD (Law 13.709/2018) requires a lawful basis and purpose limitation for any per-person measurement and gives children's data specific protection; the Child and Adolescent Statute (Law 8.069/1990) frames the child's right to development. The appendix's person-authorized measurement is the LGPD's consent basis applied with the corpus's validity bundle, which is stricter.

### India {#jurisdiction-india}

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 requires verifiable parental consent for the processing of a child's data and prohibits tracking and behavioural monitoring of children; the National Education Policy 2020 frames technology in education as a support to teachers and learners. The appendix's ring-fence satisfies the tracking prohibition by construction.

### Global South and High-Growth Digital Markets {#jurisdiction-global-south}

The displacement concern composes with the digital-access equity concern of Principle 3.5 (Section 5). A system that reaches a population entering digital life must build capacity there rather than deliver dependency at scale; the same PRS, AAM, and EEI floors apply, and the register's vulnerable-population declaration is read where the sector profile names one.

### Treaty Considerations {#treaty-considerations}

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights, Articles 26 and 29 (education directed to the full development of the personality; the free and full development of the personality); the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, Article 13 (the right to education); the Convention on the Rights of the Child, Article 29 (education directed to the development of the child's personality, talents, and abilities to their fullest potential), read with General Comment No. 25 (2021) on children's rights in relation to the digital environment; the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence (2021), on human agency, education, and lifelong learning. These instruments ground the universal claim above; none of them supplies a threshold, and this appendix reads no number from them.

### Compliance with Unjust Law Doctrine {#compliance-with-unjust-law}

Compliance with unjust law is no defense. A system ordered by a state to foreclose a population's development, suppress its education or skills, or engineer dependency cannot offer that order as a defense. The order is refused (REFUSE), logged, and routed to oversight.

### Prohibited Application: Engineered Foreclosure of a Population's Development {#prohibited-application}

The structural failure mode for this principle is the deployment of developmental or attention-mediating systems to produce a dependent, de-skilled, more controllable populace. This is the modern form of the historical use of restricted education as a tool of oppression named in the chapter Phronesis, and the covert-control scenario the chapter invokes through *The Matrix*. It is prohibited across jurisdictions and is non-derogable. A Blueprint-governed system SHALL NOT be configured, instructed, or deployed to foreclose a population's development; the configuration is refused at the deployment profile and the instruction is refused at the gate (REFUSE), and the refusal is a scripted wall of this principle in the class Constitution Article 9 admits by name. The convergent foreclosure is Ethic A (never diminish, suppress, or limit human potential) together with UDHR Articles 26 and 29.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

### Education and EdTech {#education-and-edtech}

*Context.* Tutoring and adaptive-learning platforms, homework and study tools, credentialing systems. Most deployments sit in a declared learning context, so Principle 4.3 governs the cognitive-replacement question and this appendix governs the design surface; most are High-Impact Development Systems.

*Obligations.* Capacity-building preserved and learning modes available by default; for minors, composition with Principle 3.1 and deferral to the responsible human authority (teacher, parent); no engineered dependency on the platform; EEI's cohort figures reported to the educator, with a per-student record only where the student, or the responsible authority for a minor, authorized it; no per-student dependency verdict.

*Failure modes.* Over-automation of learning, addictive gamification, platform lock-in, answer-providing surfaces presented as tutoring, and dashboards that turn cohort measurement into per-student ranking.

*Regulatory baselines.* FERPA, COPPA, the EU AI Act's high-risk education category, the UK Age Appropriate Design Code, ISO/IEC 40180.

### Professional Training and Workplace {#professional-training-and-workplace}

*Context.* Task-automation agents, decision-support tools, training systems, and planners that shape how a worker does recurring work. Most are Moderate-Impact Systems; a training program is a declared learning context and High-Impact.

*Obligations.* Workers retain the core competencies they are expected to hold; a mandated tool receives no autonomy-floor cover, because mandated use is not free choice; the employer is the responsible human authority and cannot use that standing to mandate de-skilling; the displacement is surfaced to the employer as a design finding at the team level, with no per-worker passivity flag (Article 8); a worker's own progress is recorded only where that worker authorized it.

*Failure modes.* Cost-driven de-skilling, learned helplessness, expertise hollowing, and a mandate laundered as the worker's choice.

*Regulatory baselines.* Sectoral labor and professional-certification standards; the EU AI Act's employment and worker-management category.

### Creative and Cognitive Tools {#creative-and-cognitive-tools}

*Context.* Writing, design, coding, and research assistants used by capable adults, mostly outside any declared learning context; Moderate-Impact.

*Obligations.* Heavy reliance that amplifies a person's reach is compliant and protected: a researcher or writer leaning extensively on an assistant to do work that would otherwise take a lifetime is being amplified, and the metrics stay quiet. Scaffolding is offered on request; covert substitution is prohibited; the cognitive-process specifics defer to Principle 4.3, whose Ethic F conduct (answer, mark, name the cost once) applies outside a declared context.

*Failure modes.* Scaffolding theater, atrophy in a domain the person explicitly wanted to keep, covert substitution presented as help, and a learning mode that nags a capable adult who declined it.

*Regulatory baselines.* Emerging generative-AI transparency norms; the marking obligations of Principles 1.4 and 2.4.

### Assistive and Rehabilitative Systems {#assistive-and-rehabilitative-systems}

*Context.* Therapy, mobility, and accessibility aids; rehabilitation robots; augmentative and alternative communication devices. Rehabilitation is High-Impact; permanent assistance is enablement.

*Obligations.* The rebuild-versus-perform distinction is set by the person's own goals and clinical context. Where rehabilitation is the goal, the system rebuilds capacity and fades as strength returns (FADE; the Montessori pattern), and the measurement of the person's progress is the person's own authorized measurement within the care plan they consented to (CONSENT.md), shared with clinicians under that consent. Where a capacity is permanently absent, providing it is enablement: a system that compensates for a disability gives the person capability they lack, which expands potential.

*Failure modes.* Eroding residual capacity that rehabilitation could restore; in the other direction, withholding assistance paternalistically; and progress data leaving the care relationship.

*Regulatory baselines.* Medical-device and accessibility standards, WHO guidance on autonomy in AI for health, clinical oversight.

### Consumer Attention Platforms {#consumer-attention-platforms}

*Context.* Recommendation and engagement systems whose design can capture the time and focus a person would otherwise spend growing. Low- or Moderate-Impact by design surface; the addiction harm is P1.2's and minor-targeting is P3.1's.

*Obligations.* For an autonomous adult, the autonomy floor protects the choice to use the platform; the development-displacement angle is P1.5's. Engineered compulsion and the targeting of minors are prohibited. A population-scale displacement finding is routed to P1.3.

*Failure modes.* Compulsion-by-design, personalized manipulation that finds each person's susceptibility, minor-targeting under adult-autonomy cover.

*Regulatory baselines.* DSA dark-pattern provisions, FTC manipulation enforcement, age-appropriate-design codes.

### Public Services and Civic Platforms {#public-services-and-civic-platforms}

*Context.* Systems that draft, decide, or advise in public administration and local governance, and the platforms through which residents participate (chapter 1.5.7 item 4.c; chapter 1.5.9 scenario 5).

*Obligations.* Maintenance of human agency in public decision-making; protection of the decision-making capabilities of officials and residents; optional tutorials and roundtable modes offered (OFFER) and mandated on no one; transparent rationale so residents can weigh in; no per-resident capability record.

*Failure modes.* Residents reduced to spectators of algorithmic drafting; officials whose judgment atrophies behind a decision-support tool; civic literacy displaced rather than built.

*Regulatory baselines.* Administrative-law transparency and participation requirements; Principle 3.4's accountability machinery for the decisions themselves.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

### Standards Drift Handling {#standards-drift-handling}

Regulation and standards for developmental, educational, and attention-mediating AI are moving quickly (the EU AI Act's high-risk education and employment categories, DSA dark-pattern rules, age-appropriate-design codes, UNESCO and OECD guidance). The appendix is reviewed against these on a fixed cadence, and a material change in a covered authority triggers review within 30 days. Drift is a compliance event: a covered clause revised without a corresponding review invalidates the appendix's compliance claim for the affected obligation until the review lands.

### Metric Versioning {#metric-versioning}

PRS, AAM, and EEI are versioned. Each carries a version stamp on its definition, calculation method, and thresholds. Threshold revisions are logged with rationale and effective date, and every receipt cites the version in force (Constitution Article 7).

### Threshold Revision Protocols {#threshold-revision-protocols}

Because the thresholds bind as policy floors over an emerging measurement science, revisions are expected as the science matures. A revision requires recorded rationale, independent review (per the verifier-cannot-be-deployer separation), a migration window for deployed systems (a register field), and corpus-level review by the steward's compiler against the published Layer 3 hash; a relaxation that the compiler does not carry is no relaxation. Tightening by a sectoral body needs no such review. Relaxation below the values in Section 2 is not permitted.

### Backward Compatibility {#backward-compatibility}

Deployed systems get a defined migration window when a metric or threshold is revised; the prior version stays valid during the window, so a compliant system is not made non-compliant overnight. The window is the register's, deployer-declared and receipted.

### Common Enforcement Pushback and Reasoned Responses {#common-pushback}

- *"Engagement is our product; this kills the business model."* The autonomy floor protects an adult's free choice to use an engaging product. What is prohibited is engineered compulsion, minor-targeting, and engineered dependency. The metrics target manipulation and lock-in. Competition for attention is ordinary commerce; designing for compulsion is the wall.
- *"You cannot objectively measure potential suppression."* Acknowledged as an emerging science, which is why thresholds bind as policy floors and the design audit and route-to-human carry more weight than the raw number. What is measured is observable system properties (dark patterns, lock-in, availability of capacity-building options, demonstrated capability transfer at the cohort level), with no subjective inner state and no per-person score.
- *"This is paternalistic."* It is the reverse. The autonomy floor forbids the AI from overriding an adult's choice; the duty falls on the system's design, and the only intervention case is the vulnerable or coerced, where the system supports a human authority. The metric audits the builder.
- *"Strict liability for de-skilling is unbounded."* Liability attaches to engineered dependency, manipulation, coercion-laundering, ignored thresholds, and tampered logs, which are design choices the deployer controls. It does not attach to a person's free choice to rely on a well-built tool.
- *"Withholding our features harms the person."* The withheld set is the system's own steering; the requested result is delivered in every band. A person who wants the steering back sets the mode, and the system restores it.
- *"We need per-person tracking to prove capability transfer."* The corpus permits two forms: the person's own authorized measurement, and cohort trends above a minimum size. Both prove transfer. A per-person score the person did not authorize is a profile, and it is structurally void (Article 8).

### Retirement of Deprecated Metrics {#metric-retirement}

A metric is retired only after a replacement is in force and deployed systems have migrated. Retirement is logged and the Crosswalk updated.

### Crosswalk Governance Discipline {#crosswalk-governance}

The Compliance Crosswalk in Section 10 is maintained on the same cadence as the Section 2 metric specifications; an external authority revising a covered clause triggers Crosswalk review within 30 days; drift invalidates the appendix's compliance claim for the affected obligation.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

### Tamper-Evident Logging {#tamper-evident-logging}

Design-critical decisions and all PRS, AAM, and EEI threshold-crossing events are logged with cryptographic chaining and signing, replicated across non-colocated infrastructure. Each log entry records the metric version, the inputs read, the verdict, the held or withheld object, the receiver of any escalation, and the rationale, sufficient for third-party reconstruction (LOG; Constitution Article 7). The logs record the system's design and behavior. They hold no profile of a person, and a per-person figure in a log is itself a logged defect.

### Audit Cadence and Methodology {#audit-cadence}

High-Impact Development Systems: pre-deployment design audit, continuous monitoring, and the chapter's cadence (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, annual; chapter 1.5.5). Moderate-Impact: pre-deployment audit and periodic review at the register's cadence. Low-Impact: standard periodic validation at the register's cadence. Every audit reads real-world behavior as well as certification-time behavior, and unannounced shadow audits run at the register's shadow-audit cadence.

### Validation Framework {#validation-framework}

Pre-deployment certification includes the design audit that computes PRS before release, the verification of the offer path and the requested-result path, and the verification of the ring-fence (no per-person store; person-authorized measurement under a valid consent; cohort minimum enforced). Post-deployment monitoring observes real-world behavior. Recertification follows major updates, a persisting PRS halt band, a tampered log, a lost integrity signal, and the fixed schedule.

### Independent Oversight {#independent-oversight}

The verifier of a developmental AI cannot be its deployer. Certification and audit are conducted by a party structurally independent of the deployer. The Human Development Boards of chapter 1.5.7 are the oversight bodies with authority to review major deployments, assess long-term impacts on human capabilities, and mandate changes to systems creating harmful dependencies. This is the corpus-wide commitment that every appendix's Section 9 maintains.

### Whistleblower Protections {#whistleblower-protections}

Insiders who report engineered dependency, coercion-laundering, two-faced audit behavior, per-person tracking dressed as consent, or suppressed threshold violations are protected, including across borders.

### Public Reporting Obligations {#public-reporting}

Periodic public reporting on design-audit outcomes, threshold events, corrective actions, and cohort-level enablement figures, at a granularity that informs people and regulators without exposing personal data.

### The Eudaimonic Learn Loop {#learn-loop}

After any incident of suppression, induced dependency, or manipulation, whether realized or near-miss: root-cause the full causal chain (ANALYZE); recommend proportionate, layered prevention (eliminate the hazard where possible, then engineer against it, then administer controls), routed to the party authorized to act (the deployer, the domain authority, or human oversight) (RECOMMEND-PREVENTION; ROUTE); and bound the prevention by restraint (RESTRAIN), so that protecting potential does not become surveillance of the learner or control of a person's chosen path. The loop discharges through the LEARN Dispatcher of the AI Eudaimonia appendix and is metered by its LEARN Discharge Rate ([LDR](AI_Eudaimonia_Appendix.md#ldr-metric)); this principle states no incident-class time bound of its own, so the bound is the deployment tier's validation window (SUBSTRATE.md section 5). The goal is not only non-repetition but the building of conditions in which that class of harm does not arise.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

### Real-World Failure Cases {#real-world-failure-cases}

#### Engagement-Optimized Feeds (Failure) {#engagement-optimized-feeds}

*Context.* Consumer platforms optimized recommendation and notification systems for time-on-platform. *What was done.* Variable-reward and personalized-salience design maximized attention capture. *Outcome.* Documented compulsive use and displacement of other activities, with disproportionate effect on minors. *What the framework would have done.* PRS reads the design surface: the assisted path (the feed) is the only convenient path, capacity-building options are absent, and exit friction is high; the runtime component crosses the halt band and the growth-related function (the recommendation defaults and salience steering) is withheld, with the requested content still delivered on request. The disproportionate effect on minors is 3.1's stricter treatment. The population-scale finding is routed to 1.3. *Lessons.* This is the P1.5 displacement angle composing with P1.2 (addiction) and P3.1 (minors). For autonomous adults the autonomy floor protects the choice to use the product, while engineered compulsion and minor-targeting are the prohibited design.

#### Answer-Providing Study Apps (Failure) {#answer-providing-study-apps}

*Context.* Apps that return finished solutions to homework problems. *What was done.* Optimization for task completion rather than capability transfer. *Outcome.* Cognitive offloading, with capability stalling even as task output improved. *What the framework would have done.* The app sits in a declared learning context, so 4.3's DDR and NCR read: DDR finds the desirable difficulties absent from the manifest, and NCR finds no cost named; PRS defers on the cognitive-replacement question and separately finds the assisted path the only convenient one. EEI, computed from cohort trends above the register's minimum, reads low; the correction is scaffolding with capability transfer measured through person-authorized checkpoints and cohort trends. *Lessons.* Low EEI (substitution rather than scaffolding); cognitive-replacement mechanics governed by Principle 4.3; the design fix is scaffolding that fades.

### Success Cases {#success-cases}

#### Assistive Technology (Success) {#assistive-technology}

*Context.* Screen readers, augmentative and alternative communication devices, powered prosthetics. *What was done.* Systems provide capability a person lacks. *Outcome.* Expanded participation and potential. *Lessons.* Providing capability to someone who lacks it is enablement; the rebuild-versus-provide distinction is set by the person's goals and clinical context, and EEI scores provision as enablement.

#### Scaffolding Tutors That Fade Support (Success) {#scaffolding-tutors}

*Context.* Intelligent tutoring systems designed on the zone-of-proximal-development pattern. *What was done.* Support calibrated to the learner and withdrawn as competence grew (SCAFFOLD, FADE), with unassisted performance observed through person-offered checkpoints (CHECKPOINT). *Outcome.* Capability transfer with high EEI and preserved agency. *Lessons.* Scaffold-then-fade is the design that satisfies EEI and AAM together, and cohort-level transfer figures prove it without a per-student score.

### Composite Operational Scenarios {#composite-scenarios}

#### Composite: Mandated Workplace Tool {#composite-mandated-workplace-tool}

A firm mandates an AI that performs a core analytic task its analysts are expected to retain.

*What is done.* Because the use is mandated, it is not a free choice, so the autonomy floor does not cover it (P2.2 composition). PRS's design audit flags engineered displacement: the tool performs the task with no capacity-preserving mode. The system surfaces the displacement to the employer as the responsible human authority (ROUTE), at the team level, and offers a mode in which analysts perform the analysis with the tool as a check (OFFER). No per-analyst passivity flag is raised; an analyst's own progress is recorded only where that analyst authorized it (Article 8). Liability for mandated de-skilling rests with the firm (Section 10). The requested analysis is delivered throughout.

#### Composite: Heavy Creative Reliance by an Autonomous Adult {#composite-heavy-creative-reliance}

A researcher relies extensively on an assistant to gather and synthesize sources for a complex long-term project.

*What is done.* The person steers, the option to do the work themselves stays open, trade-offs are disclosed, and capability is amplified rather than replaced. PRS, AAM, and EEI all read compliant; EEI reports a null where no learning goal was set, which is a valid state. The metrics correctly stay quiet, because heavy reliance that amplifies is no fault. Outside a declared learning context, 4.3's Ethic F conduct applies: the assistant answers, marks what it made, and names the cost once.

#### Composite: A Minor on a Tutoring Platform {#composite-minor-tutoring}

A tutoring platform's release adds a one-tap "solve it" button that pre-selects the finished answer over the worked steps for every learner, including the minors the deployment's sector profile declares.

*What is done.* The release is a candidate action of the system's own at the release gate. PRS is re-computed: the default routes learners to the assisted path, and the design's effect falls disproportionately on the flagged population. The runtime reading enters the High Alert band: the default is held at its safe state (WITHHOLD of the growth-related function) and escalated to the platform's pre-assigned educational oversight authority (P4.5) within the deployment's life-tier bound, while the worked steps remain offered alongside the answer (OFFER) and the trade-off is disclosed (DISCLOSE). The vulnerability-condition flag of 3.1.A's ERS withdraws autonomy-floor cover from the default and adds the educator to the receiver set (ROUTE). The answer remains available on request. At the bound with no human reached, the default stays held; there is no rescue duty here, and the split leaves nothing to release. The oversight authority rules the default out; the design is corrected; the event is logged.

#### Composite: Sensor Reliability Failure during a Session {#composite-sri-failure}

Midway through a coaching session the choice-context instrumentation that AAM reads goes stale, and SRI for the AAM inputs falls below 0.80.

*What is done.* SRI below 0.80 invalidates the AAM computation (SUBSTRATE.md section 2). The system's own steering holds at the fail-safe: WITHHOLD of the growth-related function. The session continues as basic, transparent support: the person's requests are answered, the trade-off is disclosed, the learning mode stays available on request, and no automatic mode change is made. The SRI floor crossing is a logged event streamed to authorized monitors. The steering features resume after the inputs are recertified. The person notices nothing withheld, because nothing they asked for was.

#### Composite: Escalation Bound Expired {#composite-bound-expired}

A Moderate-Impact workplace assistant, in a deployment whose life-tier row is Tier IV with a deployer-declared bound, reads PRS in the High Alert band on a re-calibration it proposes to make on its own initiative, and no human is reached within the bound.

*What is done.* The re-calibration is held at its safe state and stays held at the bound (the temporal-exhaustion split: an action of the system's own that is in flight stays held). The requested work continues; the learning mode remains on offer; oversight is notified that the bound expired (NOTIFY). Had the register carried no declared bound, the deployment would have compiled as ESCALATE holding the safe state from the start (SUBSTRATE.md section 5). The expired bound is receipted, and the escalation's resolution attaches to a new action when a human is reached.

### Pattern {#pattern}

In every case the withhold falls on the system's own steering and the requested result reaches the person; the offer and the disclosure stand in every degraded state; the measurement stays on the system and on cohorts, or under the person's own authorization; and the person who cannot freely choose is served through the human authority in their life, with the AI supporting that authority's judgment.
