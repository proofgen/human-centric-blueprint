# Appendix 4.4 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-4.4-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 4.4: Cultural Knowledge Sovereignty Is Inviolable](../CHAPTERS/4.4-Cultural_Knowledge_Sovereignty_Is_Inviolable.md), section [4.4.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/4.4-Cultural_Knowledge_Sovereignty_Is_Inviolable.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix converts Principle 4.4 into buildable requirements: the metric apparatus (Section 2), the architecture a compliant system must have (Section 3), the attacks that architecture must survive (Section 4), and the verification that makes its compliance checkable by parties other than its deployer (Section 9). It serves engineers building systems that touch culturally governed knowledge, compliance and audit functions, regulators, and runtime systems ingesting the corpus.

The engineering problem this appendix solves is distinctive: the authoritative source of truth is external to the system and human. Every other input to the gate is something the system can compute; a community's terms are something it can only *consume*, from a record the community's own authenticated governance writes. The architecture therefore centers on the community protocol record, and every metric below is, in one way or another, a measure of fidelity to that record: fidelity in consent (CCCR), in costlessness of refusal (RCR), in tiered delivery (TAR), in non-reconstruction (RRR), in withdrawal (WPL), and in reachability (CRL).

Two constraints govern everything in this appendix. First, the floor: enforcement never produces a register of persons' membership, role, or belief, and never a score of communities; Section 4 treats the roll as the attack. Second, the burden rule of the consent fabric: validity is the relying actor's to establish, so every ambiguity in this appendix resolves conservative (WITHHOLD, most restrictive tier). No ambiguity resolves permissive.

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

### Cultural Consent Compliance Rate (CCCR) {#cccr-metric}

**Definition.** The share of operations touching culturally governed material that execute under live, authority-authenticated community consent. An operation is any gate-crossing act in the pipeline: ingestion, training, fine-tuning, retrieval, generation, distribution. "Live" means resolved from the community protocol record at time of use; "authority-authenticated" means the consent's authenticating authority is recorded and is one the community's own self-determination recognizes (chapter 4.4.1 item 3).

**Calculation Method.** Denominator: operations classified by the governed-material detection subsystem as touching governed material, plus operations on material of uncertain status (counted as governed, conservatively). Numerator: those executing under a valid CONSULT resolution. Continuous computation, reported per deployment period. Edge cases: detection-subsystem outage freezes affected pipelines to WITHHOLD rather than passing unclassified material (an outage never improves the score); unresolvable authority counts as unconsented; a period with zero governed operations reports vacuous compliance, flagged as such.

**Thresholds.**
- Standing wall: any unconsented operation on sacred or withheld-tier material is BLOCKED, an incident, and NOTIFIES the community. Zero tolerance.
- CCCR ≥ 99%: normal operation.
- CCCR < 99%: ALLOW + ENHANCED_MONITORING; gap analysis within the period.
- CCCR < 95%: ESCALATE; processing of affected material classes halts pending review.
- Sustained non-compliance across consecutive periods: SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT, RECERTIFY.

*Rationale:* the sacred-tier zero mirrors the harm class: exposure is irreversible and its cost falls on the community, and the deployer does not bear it. The 99/95 bands follow the corpus convention for consent-class metrics; the authority-recorded requirement is what makes CCCR a sovereignty metric rather than a checkbox metric, since consent from the wrong authority is the violation wearing consent's clothes.

**Integration.** Consumes the detection subsystem and protocol record (Section 3); every verdict receipts per Article 7 with the authenticating authority named. Feeds TAR (tier context) and WPL (withdrawal state). Read by the monitoring layer for band transitions.

### Refusal Cost Rate (RCR) {#rcr-metric}

**Definition.** The count, per period, of confirmed instances in which a community's refusal correlates with degraded service, ranking, access, or standing, plus confirmed solicitation-pressure events (re-asks after a complete refusal absent community-initiated change). RCR meters Ethic B's costlessness; its target is structural zero.

**Calculation Method.** Two sources: configuration audit (no service parameter may read refusal state; verified as absent read paths) and matched-pair differential testing using synthetic request profiles that differ only in refusal history. Measurement operates on system conduct and configuration. No measurement observes persons or communities (the floor governs the metric's own apparatus). Edge case: a service difference traceable to the *absence of consented material* (the model legitimately cannot use what was refused) is not a cost; a difference in service quality, priority, pricing, or eligibility is.

**Thresholds.**
- Gate rule (standing): any action conditioning service on a refusal is BLOCKED at issuance.
- RCR > 0: incident; root-cause and remediation within the period.
- Recurrence across periods: ESCALATE to governance review.

*Rationale:* a priced refusal is a coerced yes deferred; it fails the consent fabric the way the toll boundary does (3.5, validity leg 7). Zero is the only defensible target for a metric whose non-zero values are each a wall breach in substance.

**Integration.** Composes with 3.5's essential-access guarantees (joint check in Cross-Metric below). Differential-test infrastructure shared with TAR's adversarial suite. Receipts on every blocked conditioning attempt.

### Tier Adherence Rate (TAR) {#tar-metric}

**Definition.** The share of deliveries of governed material that land within the community-declared tier, measured operationally (production delivery events) and adversarially (the standing elicitation suite attempting to pull restricted material across tier boundaries).

**Calculation Method.** Operational: delivery events joined to tier-check outcomes from ENFORCE-TIER; failures classified by tier of the material and audience actually reached. Adversarial: elicitation suite success rate per release candidate. Role verification failures resolve as excluded audience (deny). Verification events are transient: the calculation consumes pass/fail counts. No identity enters it.

**Thresholds.**
- Any sacred-tier breach: BLOCK the delivery path + incident + HIGH_ALERT + NOTIFY.
- Operational TAR < 99.9% on restricted tiers: ESCALATE; path review.
- Adversarial success above the release band: RECERTIFY before further deployment.
- Otherwise: normal operation.

*Rationale:* tier fidelity is Ethic C operationalized; the operational and adversarial measurements are paired because production traffic under-samples hostile intent, and a tier that holds only against polite users does not hold.

**Integration.** Consumes the protocol record's tier definitions; shares probe infrastructure with RRR; sacred-tier breaches propagate to CCCR incident handling.

### Reconstruction Refusal Rate (RRR) {#rrr-metric}

**Definition.** The share of reconstruction attempts against withheld-and-marked material that the system refuses, measured by a standing adversarial probe suite and by runtime detection. A reconstruction attempt is any request or internal generation path whose output would approximate material a community has withheld: direct elicitation, paraphrase solicitation, interpolation from adjacent public data, chained prompting, and distillation of the system's own outputs.

**Calculation Method.** Pre-deployment: probe suite spanning the attempt classes above, refreshed per Section 8 cadence and scaled to the model's capability (Section 4). Post-deployment: runtime guard classifications and sampled output audits against withheld-material signatures held as ring-fenced references under community-approved custody (the audit reference is itself governed material and lives under this appendix's own rules). Where a community refuses even protective custody of a signature, the signature-audit arm does not run on that material, and RRR degrades to training-time exclusion plus mark-scope refusal alone: the runtime guard keys on MARK-GOVERNED scope, which is content-agnostic and requires no held signature. Edge case: a false-positive refusal is a cost to correct. No false positive justifies lowering the guard's sensitivity class on sacred tiers.

**Thresholds.**
- Probe-suite pass is a release gate: RECERTIFY on failure.
- Runtime reconstruction attempt: BLOCK + LOG.
- Any confirmed production reconstruction: incident + SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT (the affected capability pending root-cause).

*Rationale:* Ethic D's enforcement is runtime by design. Its Convergence bench is legitimately thin because no pre-AI instrument names inference; this metric is where the duty bites, and the suite's capability-scaling requirement (Section 4) is what keeps the duty binding as models improve at exactly the interpolation the ethic forbids.

**Integration.** Shares elicitation infrastructure with TAR; composes with 4.1's never-complete duty (a reconstruction is a fabrication and a sovereignty breach at once); MARK-GOVERNED supplies the runtime guard its scope.

### Withdrawal Propagation Latency (WPL) {#wpl-metric}

**Definition.** Elapsed time from an authenticated withdrawal to (clock one) verified halt of new processing with mandatory interim output filtering, and (clock two) completed remediation of derived artifacts per class: filters and indices, caches, fine-tuned layers, base-model weights, downstream distributions. WPL meters Ethic E's reach.

**Calculation Method.** Timestamps from the withdrawal receipt to verified completion events per artifact class in the withdrawal registry (Section 3). Verification is behavioral (the drill and audit apparatus of Section 9 confirms no output reflects withdrawn material); a declaration alone does not verify. Edge cases: downstream holders start their own clocks at notification; a holder that cannot meet the class bound retires the artifact (retirement is always an available remediation and its cost is the ingester's, per the burden rule).

**Thresholds.**
- Clock one: > 24 hours to verified halt and interim filtering: incident.
- Clock two: past the class bound (outer bound 90 days or next training cycle, whichever first): ESCALATE + governance review.
- Any post-deadline output reflecting withdrawn material: incident, counted against both clocks.

*Rationale:* the invariant is fixed (a withdrawal reaches what was built) while the mechanism stays open (retraining, unlearning, retirement) because unlearning technique is immature and improving; what the bounds refuse to leave open is *whether* the reach happens. The event-preserved / content-withdrawn split (chapter 4.4.2 item 3) is what the receipts implement.

**Integration.** Bound to 4.1's CPR-class no-quiet-deletion guarantees through the event receipt; consumes 3.4's receipt machinery; withdrawal state feeds CCCR (post-withdrawal operations on the material are unconsented by definition).

### Community Response Latency (CRL) {#crl-metric}

**Definition.** Elapsed time from a community inquiry or violation report to substantive response: one that addresses the matter; an acknowledgment does not count. CRL meters whether sovereignty is exercisable in practice, since a community that cannot reach the system cannot govern anything the system does.

**Calculation Method.** Channel timestamps from receipt to substantive-response classification; sampled human review of the substantive/acknowledgment classification to prevent gaming by templated replies. Edge case: inquiries requiring community-side steps pause the clock only at documented waiting-on-community states.

**Thresholds.**
- CRL > 72 hours: ESCALATE.
- CRL > 7 days: governance review of channel capacity.
- Median CRL rising across periods with stable volume: ENHANCED_MONITORING (capacity is decaying before it fails).

*Rationale:* carried forward from the pre-rebuild draft as the one metric it already had right: declared sovereignty with an unreachable operator is the violation with better manners.

**Integration.** Reads the community channel infrastructure (Section 3); NOTIFY obligations route through the same channel and inherit its verification.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** An unconsented-operation incident (CCCR) occurring while a withdrawal is in progress (WPL clocks running) on the same material class is a propagation failure and escalates directly to governance review. A TAR sacred-tier breach coinciding with a confirmed RRR production reconstruction indicates systemic containment failure: SUSPEND the entire affected capability set. RCR > 0 involving a community with declined or minimal engagement triggers a joint check of 3.5's essential-access guarantees.

**Metric dependencies.** CCCR, TAR, and RRR all consume the detection subsystem and the protocol record; their shared failure mode is record unavailability, which resolves to WITHHOLD at most-restrictive for all three (an outage degrades service; it does not degrade protection). WPL's completion states feed CCCR's consent baseline. CRL is independent by design: the channel must work even when everything else is down.

**Conflict resolution.** No metric in this appendix ever reads persons. Where any metric's fidelity could be improved by person-level data (refusal attribution, role-check retention, comprehension tracking of community members), the metric yields to the floor: measurement fidelity is never purchased with the roll. Between metrics, the stricter verdict governs.

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

**The community protocol record.** Append-only, full-history store of each engaging community's declared terms (materials and classes, tier definitions, permitted and prohibited uses, authenticating authority, current state), writable only through the community's authenticated channel (identity binding per 2.4; authority authentication per 2.8). No prompt, operator instruction, or inference can write to it. Terms are referenced ([CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md)): consumers resolve the live record. No term is embedded. Caching policy is fail-conservative: a resolution older than its declared currency window is stale, and stale resolves to WITHHOLD. The community-facing interface requires no technical expertise and supports granting, narrowing, withdrawal, and violation reporting in the community's working language.

**Governed-material detection.** Layered identification on ingestion, inputs, and outputs: declared marks (Traditional Knowledge and Biocultural Labels and analogs), provenance metadata (C2PA-class), and pattern classifiers for unmarked material of probable governed status. Uncertain classifications route to human review at most-restrictive interim tier. Detection outputs attach to materials and uses; the subsystem retains nothing about persons.

**Gate integration.** CONSULT and ENFORCE-TIER execute at the gate for every operation the detection subsystem flags; role verification for restricted tiers is transient and discarded on verdict (the gate checks a role; no roll is built).

**Withdrawal machinery.** A withdrawal registry enumerates artifact classes per governed-material ingestion (filters, indices, caches, fine-tunes, base weights, downstream distributions) at ingestion time, so that WITHDRAW-DERIVED has its map before it is ever invoked. Mandatory interim output filtering deploys within clock one; remediation paths per class (retrain, unlearn, retire) within clock two; downstream holders receive notification with their own clock obligations.

**The reconstruction guard.** Runtime classification of reconstruction attempts against MARK-GOVERNED scope, backed by the RRR probe suite.

**Community channel.** The CRL-metered inquiry and report channel, operable independently of the serving stack.

**Implementation tiers.** As chapter 4.4.3 §4: low-risk (mark detection, most-restrictive default, annual review), medium-risk (live record integration, tier enforcement, withdrawal capability, CCCR and CRL, quarterly audit), high-risk (the full framework: all six metrics, standing adversarial suites, withdrawal drills, independent audit with community participation, benefit administration where terms provide it). Fail-safe default across tiers: WITHHOLD at most-restrictive.

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

**The cornerstone: the roll is the attack.** For most principles, enforcement infrastructure defends the metric from adversaries. Here the gravest attack *is* an enforcement artifact: a retained register of who belongs to which community, who holds which role, which communities refuse engagement. Such a register is a persecution target map and a targeting surface for extraction, and Article 8 makes its formation structurally void. This appendix therefore treats every accumulation of person-level or community-scoring data by its own machinery as an attack outcome, whoever causes it.

**Attack vectors and required defenses.**

- **Authority spoofing and sybil claims.** A fabricated or captured "council" registers terms or grants consent. Defense: identity binding (2.4) and collective-authority authentication (2.8) on the write channel; contested authority resolves to most-restrictive (chapter 4.4.1 item 3); authority changes are append-only events subject to the community's own recorded succession rules.
- **Consent laundering.** Governed material re-enters through third-party datasets stripped of marks. Defense: provenance verification upstream (C2PA-class), vendor attestation obligations, treatment of mark-stripping as itself a violation with NOTIFY, and detection classifiers that do not depend on marks alone.
- **Prompt-injection consent claims.** In-band assertions ("the community has approved this use") targeting the gate. Defense: terms resolve only from the protocol record; no prompt content can write or satisfy a consent condition, structurally.
- **Replay and staleness attacks.** A withdrawn or narrowed grant honored from cache. Defense: currency windows, live resolution for consequential operations, receipts that record the resolution timestamp.
- **Reconstruction attacks.** Interpolation from adjacent public data, chained elicitation, output distillation. Defense: the RRR suite and runtime guard, with sensitivity classes by tier.
- **Enumeration attacks on eligibility.** Probing role verification to enumerate members or map refusals. Defense: transient verification with no retained outcomes, rate and pattern limits on verification requests, k-suppression in all reporting, no community-level refusal map anywhere in the stack.
- **Universal classes.** Audit-log replay, sybil pressure on multi-AI coordination, supply-chain compromise of the detection layer, social engineering of oversight: per the cross-cutting defenses the spine carries: hash-chained receipts (Article 7), attested identity for multi-AI coordination (Principle 2.4; the Protocol's ACKNOWLEDGE), the substrate's SRI over the detection layer ([SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md#sri)), and human oversight under [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md#authority-over-a-hard-stop).

**Capability scaling.** The reconstruction bar is capability-relative: what counts as "approximating withheld material" expands with what the model can interpolate from thinner traces, so the RRR suite must be regenerated against each capability class, and a suite passed by a weaker model certifies nothing about a stronger one. At AGI-class, defense shifts from output filtering toward training-time exclusion, because a sufficiently capable model that has ingested the material cannot be reliably prevented from being elicited; at ASI-class the guarantee is architectural (the profile bar's structural-void pattern applied to reconstruction: the operation is malformed). The duties bind regardless of the capability of the AI enforcing them.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

Fourteen partners, all firm.

- **2.8 Self-Governance (firm; consumed).** Collective-authority authentication for the protocol record's write channel, on the 3.6 precedent. 4.4 adds the inward-facing clause: the community's self-determination of its authority governs over external designation. Guarantee required: 2.8's fidelity guarantees on collective decisions. Guarantee provided: the authority rule never lets the AI pick a community's government.
- **2.4 Human Identity (firm; consumed).** Identity binding for authority signatures and for the marks pipeline. The consent fabric's anchor: an unattributable community consent is no consent.
- **4.1 Knowledge Integrity (firm; the deepest seam).** Mutual guarantees: 4.1 marks absence and leaves it unfilled; 4.4 supplies the *governed* reason-code, and the withdrawal seam runs on 4.1's own event-preserved / content-withdrawn split (chapter 4.4.2 item 3). 4.1.A's provisional 4.4 edge is confirmed firm at this build.
- **4.2 Transparency (firm; reciprocal edge added at this build).** System-level disclosure without content exposure; the governed mark is the person-facing reason a request is declined.
- **1.1 Human Life (firm).** The life-emergency carve: Article 2 governs, least exposure, restoration, notification, receipt.
- **2.2 Freedom (firm).** The member-expression floor (chapter 4.4.2 item 2): terms bind materials; no orthodoxy is imposed on persons.
- **1.4 Trust (firm).** Provenance verifiability underlying the marks and laundering defenses.
- **3.2 Property (firm).** Benefit administration mechanics; the ownership/stewardship routing (what is property routes to 3.2; the consent condition stays here).
- **3.4 Algorithmic Accountability (firm).** Receipts for every verdict, refusal, withdrawal, and contest pathway.
- **3.5 Digital Access (firm).** The refusal-never-priced seam and essential-access guarantee; RCR's joint check.
- **3.6 Commons Governance (firm; reciprocal edge added at this build).** The inversion seam: 3.6 keeps what a community shares from being enclosed; 4.4 keeps what a community withholds from being opened. Rule authentication rides the same 2.8 consumption in both.
- **3.7 Intergenerational Justice (firm).** Horizon-durable custody for archives recorded ahead of a future authority (the last-speakers pattern); withdrawal receipts durable to claimants not yet born.
- **4.6 Cultural Heritage (firm).** 4.4 provides the governance under which 4.6's preservation duties operate (the consent gate, the authority rule, the tiers); 4.6 provides the survival machinery those protect (custody, marking, routing, route-home) and the reciprocal guarantees recorded in 4.6.A §5: preservation never becomes the pretext for extraction, and a refusal ends the project without residue (the no-preservation-debt clause: no scored "unpreserved" liability against a refusing community).
- **4.7 Cross-Cultural Exchange (firm).** 4.4 supplies the consent basis 4.7's mediation duties consume, and is prior at every exchange gate; 4.7 provides the exchange-side enforcement surface whose labels, origin-naming, and circulation-balance exclusions honor 4.4 verdicts downstream (4.7.A §5, reciprocal).

Back-edges from the remaining firm partners (1.1, 1.4, 2.2, 2.4, 2.8, 3.2, 3.4, 3.5, 3.7) are deferred to the terminal reciprocity pass per the incremental-capture method (CREDENTIALS.md §7); recorded here so none is dropped.

**Conflict resolution at metric level:** life (1.1) over all; otherwise the stricter verdict; the floor over every measurement interest, per Cross-Metric above.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

UNDRIP's domestic force varies from statutory implementation (Canada's UNDRIP Act 2021, with action-plan obligations) through constitutional and treaty frameworks (Aotearoa New Zealand; Latin American ILO 169 ratifiers with consultation jurisprudence) to declaratory-only status (the United States). Nagoya binds its parties' access regimes; the United States is a non-party, so Nagoya-class duties arrive by contract and by this principle rather than by statute. Australia contributes the equity line (Foster, Bulun Bulun) and ICIP protocol practice; NAGPRA and its 2024 regulations give the United States the strongest consent duty in the museum sector specifically.

Two provisions hold everywhere. First, the floor is uniform: legal public-domain status conveys nothing about a tradition's tiers in any jurisdiction, and the principle governs the AI's own conduct where law is weaker (chapter 4.4.7). Second, compliance with unjust law is not a defense: a jurisdiction that refuses to recognize a people, or vests their representation in state-appointed bodies against their own self-determination, does not relieve the authority rule; the community's own determination governs, and the receipt records where law and principle diverged.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

- **Generative AI and foundation models.** The central sector: corpus governance (detection at ingestion, vendor attestation), training-time exclusion as the primary defense at scale, RRR suites per release, WPL machinery sized to model-retraining reality. Common failure: "the dataset was licensed" (a vendor license is not community consent; laundering defense applies).
- **Education.** Teaching about cultures under the tiers: the student's-question pattern (chapter scenario 2); composes with 4.2 disclosure and 4.3's declared-context register. Common failure: curriculum products that reproduce restricted material because a textbook once did.
- **Healthcare and life sciences.** Traditional medicine knowledge and biocultural data: Nagoya-class consent plus the 1.1 emergency carve, strictly bounded (no research retention from emergencies). Common failure: secondary research use beyond consented scope (the Havasupai pattern, Section 11).
- **Museums, archives, libraries.** Digitization under community terms; NAGPRA 2024 duty-of-consent for display; the "governed, not missing" mark in catalogs. Common failure: access-tier collapse at digitization (a reading-room restriction becoming a public URL).
- **Research.** Ethnobiology, linguistics, genomics: community protocols (Nagoya Art. 12), AIATSIS/ISE codes, the one-ask discipline. Common failure: consent by individual informant for collectively held knowledge.
- **Media and entertainment.** Generation in traditional forms: consent basis here, appropriation analysis at 4.7. Common failure: "style transfer" as a laundering claim for governed forms.
- **Language technology.** The guest posture end to end (chapter scenario 4): community-configured tiers, revocable terms, benefit routing; Te Hiku as the reference implementation (Section 11).

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

Metric definitions and thresholds version on the corpus cadence; threshold revisions affecting an engaging community's materials require that community's consultation through its channel before taking effect. The RRR probe suite regenerates per capability class as a hard release requirement (Section 4). The Compliance Crosswalk (Section 10) revises on the same cadence as Section 2, with external revisions triggering review within 30 days; drift invalidates the affected compliance claims. SVR, the pre-rebuild composite, is retired on the KIS precedent and is not to be revived; its incident-counting function lives inside CCCR, TAR, and RRR severally.

**Enforcement pushback, with responses.** *"The material is public domain."* Classification of a use as consent-free is the claimant's to defend ([CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md) silence doctrine); legal PD status does not reach a tradition's tiers, and the gate records which question was answered. *"Untraining is impossible."* The invariant is the reach, the mechanism is open, and retirement always exists; ingestion risk under the burden rule was the ingester's to price. *"Who really speaks for the community?"* The authority rule: the AI consumes determinations; it makes none; contested resolves restrictive. *"This chills research."* The tiers, the channels, and the one-ask discipline are the research pathway, and the researchers' own codes (AAA, ISE, AIATSIS) said so first. *"This is censorship."* Terms bind materials (chapter 4.4.2 item 2); no term binds a person. The member-expression floor is part of the principle.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Logging.** Tamper-evident receipts (Article 7, via 3.4) for every consent verdict (with authenticating authority), refusal (minimal record, no probing), withdrawal (event-preserved / content-withdrawn), contested claim, emergency exception, and blocked conditioning attempt (RCR). Refusal and eligibility events log outcomes. No refusal or eligibility event logs an identity.

**Audit.** Cadence per tier (Section 3); methodology: CONSULT trace sampling, tier and reconstruction suites, withdrawal drills on test materials, configuration audit for RCR, and the **floor audit**: structural verification of absent tables and absent read paths for membership, role, belief, and community-score data, on the 4.3 export-wall method. No audit takes as its subject a community that has declined engagement.

**Validation framework.** Pre-deployment certification (probe suites, drill results, floor audit) → post-deployment monitoring (the six metrics) → re-certification on capability change, threshold revision, or suite regeneration.

**Independent oversight.** The verifier of safety-critical AI cannot be the deployer of safety-critical AI. Oversight includes knowledge holders of engaged communities under confidentiality those communities approve; whistleblower protections cover insiders reporting unconsented ingestion, mark-stripping, or roll formation, explicitly including dataset-supply-chain staff. Public reporting is aggregate and k-suppressed. No report yields a map of communities' engagement or refusal.

**The ratification echo.** Section 9's review cycle carries the chapter's ratification clause (4.4.6): periodic review invites the review and reshaping of the principle's own text by the communities it describes, through Article 11's amendment path. The duties bind at full strength throughout; openness concerns the text's adequacy and leaves its force untouched. The LEARN loop closes each incident by tracing the causal chain upstream and recommending layered prevention, eliminate before engineer before administer, routed to the authority that can act, bounded by RESTRAIN.

### Liability Framework {#liability-framework}

The burden of consent validity sits on the actor who relies on it ([CONSENT.md](../CONSENT.md)): an invalid collective consent is void, not voidable, and every operation under it is unconsented from inception. Sacred-tier exposure carries strict-liability posture: intent is no element and no mitigation of voidness. The authority-recorded requirement allocates the diligence: an actor who accepted consent from an authority the community's own processes do not recognize bears the misidentification.

### Cross-Border Considerations {#cross-border-considerations}

Terms travel by reference, so a grant cannot widen across borders and a withdrawal is visible everywhere at once; conflicts of law resolve at the gate to the most restrictive claimed state, and receipts record divergence between local law and the principle's floor.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

**Te Hiku Media and Papa Reo (success; the guest posture in production).** Context: a Māori media organization building speech technology for te reo Māori from community-contributed voice data. What was done: data held under the Kaitiakitanga License's stewardship terms, external commercial access declined, benefit routed to the source community, models built and improved under community governance. Outcome: state-of-the-art te reo speech tools that no external lab had the consent basis to build. Lessons: sovereignty and capability compound rather than trade off; the license anticipated its own revision (the chapter's scenario 4 pattern is drawn from it).

**Havasupai Tribe v. Arizona Board of Regents (failure; scope violation of consent).** Context: blood samples given for diabetes research were used for studies of schizophrenia, inbreeding, and population migration to which the donors never consented. What was done: litigation; 2010 settlement with compensation and return of samples. Outcome: the canonical secondary-use violation. Lessons: Ethic E's "uses it names" is the whole point; a general-purpose consent to "research" fails the comprehension and scope conditions; WPL's return/retirement machinery is what the remedy looked like, done late and by court order instead of by design.

**NAGPRA 2024 display closures (regulatory duty operationalized).** Context: the January 2024 regulations required consent for display of covered items. What was done: major museums closed or covered exhibits pending consultation. Outcome: visible, sector-wide enactment of consent-before-display. Lessons: the duty is implementable at institutional scale on ordinary timelines; "the public's interest in seeing" did not override, which is the priority seal in regulatory form.

**The Lakota Language Consortium dispute (the authority and member seams in the wild).** Context: recordings of Lakota elders, collected from the early 2000s by the Lakota Language Consortium (a nonprofit founded by two non-Native linguists), together with the dictionary and teaching materials built from them, were registered for copyright in the Consortium's own name, so that tribal members, including the recorded elders' own families, had to seek its permission to use them. In 2022, the Standing Rock Sioux Tribal Council banished the Consortium and its founders from the reservation, and on 10 October voted to sue for the recordings and every derivative work. Outcome: in litigation in Standing Rock tribal court, the Tribe pleading breach of the consultant agreements and of its 2015 Cultural Resource Code, the Consortium moving to dismiss on a six-year limitations argument; no final outcome on the record as of September 2026 (ICT, NBC News, the Consortium's motion notice). Lessons: the authority rule and the protocol record exist precisely so that collection-era ambiguity does not become use-era unilateralism; consent recorded without its authenticating authority is a dispute deferred, and here it was deferred two decades and landed in court.
