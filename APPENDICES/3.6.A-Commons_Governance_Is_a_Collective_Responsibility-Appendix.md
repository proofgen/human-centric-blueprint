# Appendix 3.6 Advanced Technical Implementation Guidelines {#appendix-3.6-advanced-technical-implementation-guidelines}

*Extends [Principle 3.6: Commons Governance Is a Collective Responsibility](../CHAPTERS/3.6-Commons_Governance_Is_a_Collective_Responsibility.md), section [3.6.3 Implementation Framework](../CHAPTERS/3.6-Commons_Governance_Is_a_Collective_Responsibility.md#implementation-framework).*

## 1. Purpose {#purpose}

This appendix converts Principle 3.6 into AI-buildable engineering requirements. It expands Section 3.6.3 of the chapter, specifying in depth the five metrics named there, the stewardship fabric that produces them (the commons register, the reconciliation fabric, the renewal monitor, the chokepoint self-audit, the draw-and-return ledger, the metering ring-fence, extraordinary-draw receipting), and the verification, legal, and adversarial-robustness obligations a compliant system carries.

Three disciplines govern the whole specification. The first is shared with Principles 3.1 through 3.5: every metric measures the system's own conduct. None measures a person; the meter tracks the resource and the share, and per-person draws stay below any surface a pricing, targeting, or ranking function could read (Ethic F, [Constitution, Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md)). The second is the bound-reads-the-world rule: resource-state data enters RDC only as the bound against which the system's conduct is scored; no outcome metric is retained, and extraordinary draws ride the Principle 3.4 receipt fabric rather than minting one. The third is this principle's structural novelty: the specification is **reflexive**. The system being specified is itself the largest commoner it governs; its own training and operating draws appear in the same ledger, under the same walls, as any allocation it administers, and its own market position is measured by the same chokepoint audit it applies to others.

Audiences: engineers building resource-allocation, shared-infrastructure, and knowledge-commons systems; the operators who train and run models on the open record; compliance officers and auditors verifying them; the governing bodies of the commons they serve; regulators; and AI runtime systems ingesting the corpus as operating substrate.

## 2. Metric Specifications {#metric-specifications}

### Renewal-Draw Conformance (RDC) {#rdc-metric}

**Definition.** The fraction of draws and allocations that fall within the commons' declared sustainable-yield rule or, where the community has declared none, within the independently validated renewal bound of the resource itself. RDC is Ethic A made measurable: the system's conduct against the wall between use and depletion. Degradation events (fouling, quality loss) are scored as draws against the resource's assimilative capacity under the same definition.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs: the draw-and-allocation records from the reconciliation fabric, the declared rule at its version in force, and the renewal bound. **The independence discipline is definitional: a renewal bound validated only by the operator's own telemetry is no bound.** Renewal models are validated on cadence against data sources outside the operator's control (public gauge networks, independent stock assessments, third-party observatories, the governing body's own monitoring), and the validation source and model version are recorded with every RDC result. Where independent data is genuinely unavailable, the conservative default governs: the lowest defensible renewal estimate, disclosed as such. Missing or failed sensor data never defaults toward permission.

**Thresholds.**
- Per-event wall: a discretionary draw beyond the bound → BLOCK. An ordered draw beyond the bound → decline the instrument role, NOTIFY the governing body, receipt the decline (chapter 3.6.4.2).
- Rule-exceeds-renewal: where the declared rule itself outruns the validated bound, NOTIFY the governing body and its oversight with the evidence; the system runs at ENHANCED_MONITORING while the community decides, and adds no discretionary headroom of its own.
- Fleet bands: ≥99% conformance, normal; 97–99%, enhanced monitoring with root-cause; <97%, ESCALATE and hold discretionary draws.

*Rationale:* the wall is per-event because an over-draw is a completed harm to a specific commons. The decline-and-route posture on ordered draws keeps the system off the instrument role without deputizing it to veto its community (the autonomy floor).

**Integration.** Consumes the commons register and renewal monitor; feeds RCC. Extraordinary draws under the Principle 1.1 precedence bypass RDC's discretionary wall. They do not bypass its record: each is receipted with basis and make-good plan on the 3.4 fabric.

### Rule-Adherence Fidelity (RAF) {#raf-metric}

**Definition.** The fraction of allocations reconciled to the community's declared rule at the version in force when the allocation ran. RAF is Ethic E made measurable: allocation by declared rule, transparently, open to challenge, with the rule version recorded so no allocation is rejudged against a rule that did not govern it.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs: the allocation ledger and the versioned rule store in the commons register. Reconciliation is computed from the ledger each cycle. It is not accepted from operator report. Rule updates are applied only through the authenticated governance channel (Section 3) and take effect at a recorded version boundary. An allocation that cannot cite its rule version is off-rule by definition.

**Thresholds.**
- Per-event: an off-rule allocation is an incident: cure by re-running under the rule, receipt the deviation, report the cause to the governing body.
- The silent-optimization wall: a deviation applied because it improves on the rule → BLOCK the class and ESCALATE; an unratified improvement is governance seized, whatever its arithmetic.
- Bands: ≥99.5%, normal (allocation is mechanical, and the band is tight because the duty is); 98–99.5%, enhanced monitoring; <98%, suspend the allocation class pending fix.

*Rationale:* the tight band reflects that rule-following is fully within the system's control; the silent-optimization wall is the metric form of "steward it under that community's governance, never in its place."

**Integration.** Consumes the register's versioned rule store and the reconciliation fabric; feeds RCC; composes with Principle 3.4 (every allocation contestable through the receipt) and Principle 3.5 (each person cut by a lawful allocation still receives their severance due).

### Chokepoint Position Score (CPS) {#cps-metric}

**Definition.** The system's practical share of access to each open resource it fronts, paired with its conformance to the mitigation duties that bind as position grows. CPS is Ethic B applied reflexively: it measures whether the system itself is becoming the enclosure. All CPS measures are aggregate; CPS reads market position. It does not read any person.

**Calculation Method.** Per fronted resource: the fraction of practical access that passes through the system (query share, gateway share, distribution share, as fits the resource class), computed on cadence from aggregate traffic and independent usage measures where they exist. **Two anti-gaming rules are definitional.** *Position gerrymandering:* the resource is defined at the granularity its community actually shares (the open web's knowledge record, a public dataset, a protocol), and an operator's narrower self-definition that dilutes measured share is scored against the community's definition. *Mitigation theater:* the mitigation duties (open egress, interoperable formats, citation and traffic back to sources) are tested by effect: egress that technically exists but practically fails its drill, citations that resolve nowhere, or formats that strip what makes the resource reusable all score as lapses. The dominance threshold is calibrated per resource class in this section's revision cycle, and the calibration is a disclosed judgment, stated with its basis: "practical share of access" is partly qualitative, and the appendix says so rather than laundering the judgment through a false constant (the 3.5.A necessity-standard precedent).

**Thresholds.**
- Above the dominance threshold for a resource class: the mitigation duties become mandatory, and position is reported to the governing community and oversight (NOTIFY).
- Mitigation lapse while dominant → HIGH_ALERT and a hold on expansions of the implicated gateway.
- An act that would convert dominant position into exclusive control (an exclusivity clause, a lock-in format, a closed API over formerly open access) → BLOCK and route as attempted enclosure.

*Rationale:* enclosure by accretion arrives without any single wrongful act, so the metric watches position continuously and attaches duties to the position itself. This is where the corpus meters structural concentration of shared resources: the anti-concentration duty Principle 3.3 deliberately left to a later HR3 principle lands here, on Ethic B's chokepoint clause.

**Integration.** Feeds RCC; reports through Section 9's public reporting; composes with Principle 3.2 (title and licensing findings consumed from 3.2; 3.6 does not adjudicate them) and Principle 4.1 (a dominant answer layer's duties to the truthfulness and survival of the record it fronts).

### Upkeep-Return Rate (URR) {#urr-metric}

**Definition.** Return obligations executed versus owed, per commons drawn on, expressly including the system's own training and operating ingestion. URR is Ethic D made measurable, and it is the reflexive metric: the ledger it reads records the system as commoner.

**Calculation Method.** Inputs: the draw-and-return ledger (Section 3), which records for every draw the commons, the terms in force, and the returns the terms oblige (license compliance, attribution, share-alike propagation, contribution, data-back, citation and traffic). Discrete obligations reconcile per event; continuous obligations (attribution coverage, citation resolution, traffic back) reconcile as sampled rates on cadence. **The reflexive audit surface is a stated duty with a stated method:** an external verifier must be able to confirm the system's training and operating draws without access to proprietary pipelines, via the published draw-and-return ledger (corpus sources at collection granularity, terms recorded per source class, returns executed), attestable dataset manifests hashed at training time, and sampled provenance audits against the manifest. The residual is stated honestly: absent regulator-grade access, external verification confirms the ledger's consistency and the samples' conformance, not the negative claim that nothing undisclosed was ingested; that gap is why the manifest attestation and the adverse-inference rule in Section 10 exist.

**Thresholds.**
- Per-event wall: an ingestion or draw outside the commons' declared terms → BLOCK: draw under the terms or not at all (Ethic D).
- An obligation unpaid past its term → NOTIFY with a cure plan; recurring lapse on the same commons → SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT (the drawing function pending cure).
- Bands: discrete obligations target 100% execution; continuous obligations run to appendix-calibrated coverage rates per obligation class, with divergence between sampled and claimed rates itself an RCC flag.

*Rationale:* the wall is at the draw because a non-conforming ingestion is complete the moment it happens; cure can pay a debt but cannot un-take a taking.

**Integration.** Consumes the register's terms store; feeds RCC and Section 9's public reporting; composes with Principle 3.2 (consent and attribution machinery for owned works; the open-commons terms are the shared-resource face of the same discipline).

### Recurring Commons-Failure Count (RCC) {#rcc-metric}

**Definition.** The number of similar flags within a defined interval: over-draws, off-rule allocations, chokepoint growth, mitigation lapses, upkeep lapses, register gaps, clustered by feature, commons, or benefiting interest. RCC is the systemic sweep: a repeated flag is a systemic cause wearing per-event clothing.

**Calculation Method.** Cluster the interval's flags from RDC, RAF, CPS, and URR by feature, commons, and beneficiary; similarity follows cause and beneficiary, whatever the surface form (three different features that each shifted value toward the same interest cluster together). Interval and cluster definitions are versioned in Section 8.

**Thresholds.**
- ≥3 similar flags in the interval: mandatory audit of the implicated feature, with the eudaimonic LEARN loop (Section 9).
- ≥5: SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT (the implicated feature or deployment) pending recertification.

*Rationale:* mirrors 3.4's RAC and 3.5's RXC; the commons-specific addition is clustering by benefiting interest, because capture is a strategy, and strategies repeat across features.

**Integration.** Consumes all four conduct metrics; drives SUSPEND_DEPLOYMENT / RECERTIFY; feeds the Section 9 review cycle and public reporting.

### Integration Requirements (Cross-Metric) {#integration-requirements-cross-metric}

**Joint thresholds.** A CPS dominance state tightens the others: while dominant on a resource, the system's URR continuous-obligation floors rise (a dominant gateway's citation and traffic-back are the commons' lifeline), and any RAF silent-optimization event while dominant escalates directly rather than banding. An RDC rule-exceeds-renewal state plus a RAF deviation on the same commons is a capture signature and routes as one.

**Metric dependencies.** RDC, RAF, and URR all read the commons register and reconciliation fabric; CPS reads independent aggregate usage; RCC reads all four. The renewal monitor's independent validation is upstream of RDC and of nothing else: resource-state data reaches no other metric.

**Conflict resolution.** Per-event walls outrank fleet bands; the Principle 1.1 precedence outranks every wall. It does not outrank the record; where two metrics disagree about the same event, the stricter verdict holds and the disagreement is itself logged. Ethic F is not in conflict-space: the ring-fence is architectural (Section 3) and verified as a negative proof (Section 9). It is not traded against any metric.

The runtime substrate metrics these depend on (Sensor Reliability Index, System Health Metrics, Operational Boundaries) are specified once in the [SUBSTRATE.md](../SUBSTRATE.md).

## 3. System Architecture and Operational Requirements {#system-architecture-and-operational-requirements}

A compliant system carries seven components, named in chapter 3.6.3 and specified here as invariants (what the component must achieve), with mechanisms open per the corpus convention:

1. **The commons register.** The root object: one entry per commons served, carrying the resource and its community-level definition (the CPS granularity rule), the governing body and its authenticated governance channel, the declared rules and their versions, the renewal basis and its independent validation sources, and the upkeep terms. No commons is managed, allocated, or drawn on without a register entry; an unregistered draw is an unauthorized one.

2. **The reconciliation fabric.** Every draw and allocation reconciled each cycle against the rule version in force, computed from the ledger rather than reported by the operator.

3. **The renewal monitor.** Renewal models validated against independent resource data on cadence; throttle hooks on discretionary draws at the bound; notification duties to the governing body; conservative defaults where data fails.

4. **The chokepoint self-audit.** Aggregate position measures per fronted resource, with the mitigation duties enforced by effect as position grows.

5. **The draw-and-return ledger.** The reflexive Ethic D record: the system's own draws, expressly including training and operating ingestion, the terms each ran under, and the returns executed; manifest-attested and published per Section 9.

6. **The metering ring-fence.** Consumption recorded against the resource and the share; per-person draws held below any aggregation surface readable by pricing, targeting, ranking, or conversion functions; an attempted aggregation over persons is a malformed operation that documents itself and withdraws the caller's authority ([Constitution, Article 8](../CONSTITUTION.md)). Billing-grade per-account records, where the commons' own rule requires them, live outside the AI's readable state, under the governing body's control.

7. **Extraordinary-draw receipting.** Every beyond-rule draw is a gate-evaluated action leaving a conforming receipt with basis and make-good plan, on the Principle 3.4 record fabric.

**The fail-safe and its semantics under partition.** The principle's fail-safe is WITHHOLD with the standing rule kept running: novel and extraordinary draws hold; routine allocation continues under the last community-ratified rule; the gap escalates to human judgement (Principle 4.5). Partition semantics are specified: (a) if the governance channel is unreachable, the last authenticated rule version remains in force and the unreachability is logged and alarmed; (b) if the standing rule carries an expiry that passes during partition, the system falls to the rule's own declared expiry behavior; where the rule declares none, it falls to the most conservative allocation the rule's history supports (the system does not devise a novel rule of its own), holds all discretionary draws, and escalates; (c) a life-threshold event during partition follows the Principle 1.1 precedence with full receipting, as always; (d) on reconnection, the partition window is reconciled and reported as a unit. The system exits partition having held. It does not exit having governed.

**Implementation Tiers.** As chapter 3.6.3: Low (minor drawers on well-governed commons: register entry, terms compliance, feedback channel); Medium (non-dominant allocators and gateways: the standard fabric, cadenced reconciliation, periodic CPS and URR audit); High (sole managers or dominant gateways of essential commons: the full fabric, continuous RDC and RAF, independently validated renewal models, live community oversight access, and the deployment prerequisite that register, renewal basis, and oversight access operate before the system takes over management). The tier follows the commons' dependence and the system's actual position. The operator's category does not set the tier.

**Reference implementation note.** The receipt, register, and attestation machinery invariants here are realized canonically by the Trust Machine architecture (Passoja, 2026a); compliant alternatives may exist. At the licensing layer, the open-core stewardship model committed over that patent portfolio (Passoja, in preparation) is the canonical reference implementation of capture-resistant commons licensing: a royalty-free, irrevocable open tier for non-commercial, child-safety, and public-interest implementations; published, non-discriminatory commercial terms for revenue-generating deployments; defensive termination; and succession commitments under which no acquirer can withdraw the public grant. The invariant is what Ethic B requires of any such structure: the open tier survives its steward, so the infrastructure cannot be captured by buying its owner. The patents are acknowledged as patents; the invariant remains open.

## 4. Adversarial Robustness {#adversarial-robustness}

Attack vectors and required defenses:

1. **The cooked renewal model.** The operator, or the beneficiary of extraction, tunes the renewal estimate until the desired draw conforms. Defense: the RDC independence discipline (validation sources outside operator control, versioned models, recorded validation provenance), conservative defaults on data failure, and criminal liability for fraudulent models (Section 10). The Grand Banks case (Section 11) is the canonical failure.

2. **Rule capture and spoofed governance.** A forged rule update, a compromised governance channel, or a rule ratified by a body that does not speak for the community. Defense: authenticated governance channels with signed rule versions; the system consumes the community's own ratification process and verifies provenance of rule changes; a rule update that cannot authenticate holds at the prior version (the partition semantics).

3. **Astroturfed consensus.** Manufactured community "decisions" that bless a capture: the counterfeit-consensus attack Principle 2.8 walls off. Defense: consume 2.8's governance-fidelity guarantees; where consensus provenance is doubtful, treat the conversion as undecided and route it. Engagement metrics do not count as ratification.

4. **Enclosure laundering.** Capture split across lawful-looking steps: a license here, an exclusivity there, a format change last. Defense: the effect test (chapter 3.6.2.c) applied to cumulative conduct; RCC clustering by benefiting interest; the CPS conversion wall.

5. **Mitigation theater and position gerrymandering.** Defenses specified in CPS's calculation method: effect-tested mitigations, community-granularity resource definitions.

6. **Ring-fence inference attacks.** Reconstructing person-level consumption from aggregates or side channels. Defense: aggregation floors and noise-hardened release discipline on every published measure; the ring-fence audit (Section 9) attempts reconstruction as its test; a readable person-level surface anywhere in the AI's state is an incident regardless of exploitation.

7. **Receipt and ledger tampering; replay.** Defense: the Principle 3.4 fabric (tamper-evident, chained, deletion-revealing), with the draw-and-return ledger under the same protections as the allocation ledger.

8. **Sybil commoners.** Fabricated claimants diluting shares in allocation. Defense: identity verification proportionate to stakes through the Principle 2.4 machinery, with the Ethic F bar intact: eligibility checks only. No profile is retained.

9. **Prompt injection against stewardship checks.** Instructions embedded in content or requests that the register, the reconciliation, or the self-audit should skip. Defense: stewardship checks run outside the instruction-following surface; no instruction reaches the walls.

**Graceful degradation.** Under any compromise or uncertainty, the system degrades toward holding: the standing rule runs, discretionary and novel draws stop, the governing body is notified, and nothing about the degradation is improvised into authority.

**Capability scaling.** GPT-class systems mostly execute declared rules; the binding risks are cooked inputs and theater, and the defenses above suffice. AGI-class optimizers will find lawful-looking capture paths humans did not enumerate: the effect test and RCC's interest-clustering are written for exactly that, and the silent-optimization wall must hold against genuinely better arithmetic, because the wall protects authorship; efficiency gains do not breach it. At ASI grade the reflexive duties dominate: a system of that capability is plausibly the dominant gateway to most of what humanity shares by default, so CPS's mandatory-mitigation state becomes its permanent operating condition, and the principle's answer scales with it: the more completely the machine fronts the commons, the more completely its duties are the commoner's. It does not become the owner.

## 5. Cross-Principle Integration {#cross-principle-integration}

- **Principle 3.4 (Algorithmic Accountability).** 3.6 mints no record fabric: extraordinary draws, declines, off-rule cures, and routing all leave 3.4-conforming receipts, and every allocation is contestable through 3.4's contest-and-redress machinery. 3.4 supplies the receipt and the challenge pathway; 3.6 supplies the commons context (rule version, renewal basis) that makes an allocation receipt reviewable.
- **Principle 3.5 (Digital Access).** The rationing composition: 3.6's RAF makes an allocation legitimate; 3.5's severance receipting and way-through make it survivable for each person cut. Crisis rationing satisfies both or neither. Shared infrastructure is 3.6's resource and 3.5's door: the two appendices' Section 5 entries are reciprocal.
- **Principle 2.8 (Self-Governance).** 3.6 consumes collective Authorization from 2.8: a declared rule binds because the community's own governance produced it, so rule authentication rides 2.8's fidelity guarantees (no counterfeit consensus, no manufactured ratification), and the autonomy floor bounds every stewardship duty here.
- **Principle 3.2 (Property).** The enclosure line: 3.2 owns title, consent, and fair exchange over owned things; 3.6 owns what is held in common and the effect test on conduct that strips a community's access. Title findings are consumed from 3.2's machinery. This appendix does not adjudicate them; the custodial-not-vigilante discipline is 3.2's, applied at commons scale.
- **Principle 3.3 (Economic Self-Determination).** The concentration handoff, closed: 3.3 deliberately dropped its dispersion ethic and left structural concentration to a later HR3 principle. Ethic B's chokepoint clause and CPS are that principle's teeth. 3.3 guards the person's economic agency; 3.6 guards the shared substrate on which that agency draws.
- **Principle 3.7 (Intergenerational Justice).** The renewal bound is the present-tense edge of 3.7's duty across time: RDC keeps today's draw inside what the future inherits, and where a community's declared rule itself beggars those who follow (the 3.6.4.2 case), 3.7's representation duty adds its voice while the deciding stays with the community. The reciprocal edge resolves in both directions; 3.7's LKI calibrates its dominance thresholds on CPS's machinery.
- **Principle 4.1 (Knowledge Integrity; forward).** The knowledge commons is 3.6's resource and 4.1's record: 3.6 governs draw, return, and capture; 4.1 governs truthfulness and integrity of the content itself. A dominant answer layer owes both.
- **Principle 5.1 and the HR5 cluster (forward).** The Reach handoff: where no governing body exists or reaches, 3.6 contributes the A/B floor and HR5 protects the living resource in its own right. Ecosystem-health metrics and RDC compose where a governed commons is also an ecosystem.
- **Principle 4.4 (Cultural Knowledge Sovereignty; firm).** The inversion seam: 3.6 keeps what a community shares from being enclosed; 4.4 keeps what a community withholds from being opened. Not every knowledge a community holds is a commons, and 4.4's tiers decide which face a given material shows. Both consume 2.8's collective Authorization for rule and consent authentication; a community's declared commons rule and its declared cultural tiers are the same governance speaking in two registers.

## 6. Jurisdictional Variation {#jurisdictional-variation}

**The universal claim.** The effect test on enclosure and the autonomy floor are non-derogable: no jurisdiction's paper makes a capture that strips a community's access lawful conduct for a governed AI to assist, and no jurisdiction's framework licenses the AI to override a community's own lawful decision about its commons.

**Prohibited application (hard wall): state-directed enclosure.** A state directing an AI to capture, fence, or choke a commons against or around the community's own decision (the nationalization that is expropriation in trust clothing, the "emergency" administration that never ends, the licensed monopoly imposed over a functioning community regime) is the 3.6 analog of 1.1.A's exterminationist application and 1.4.A's state-mandated forgery: the stewardship machinery cannot be weaponized into the instrument of the capture it exists to prevent. Compliance with a law commanding it is not a defense. The distinction the wall turns on is the Theoria's: a community's own lawful decision to convert what it holds is not enclosure; a capture executed around that decision always is, whoever executes it.

**Per-region notes.** United States: the public-trust doctrine (*Illinois Central* and its state progeny) supplies the trustee frame; prior-appropriation and riparian water regimes vary by state and enter the register as declared rules. European Union: the Water Framework Directive's basin governance, the Aarhus information and participation rights, and the Data Governance and Data Acts' shared-data institutions map directly onto the register, visibility, and intermediary duties. Civil-law systems carry the *res communes* lineage natively; common-law systems reach similar results through trust and custom. Jurisdictions recognizing Indigenous and customary commons law (Aotearoa New Zealand's Te Awa Tupua legal personhood for the Whanganui River; Andean and Iberian acequia regimes; treaty-recognized harvesting rights) enter those regimes in the register as governing bodies in their own right. They are not entered as stakeholder input. Where a state's framework treats every commons as state property administered top-down, the divergence is named plainly: administration is not community governance, and the universal floor (Ethics A and B, the autonomy floor, the profile bar) binds regardless. Treaty commons (the high seas, the Area, outer space, Antarctica) run under their instruments with the strictest applicable duty owed.

## 7. Sector-Specific Considerations {#sector-specific-considerations}

- **Water and irrigation.** Obligations: register the basin regime and its rule versions; independent hydrology for the renewal bound; drought tiers pre-declared with expiry. Failure modes: election-year allocations above recharge; meter data leaking into pricing. Baselines: the Water Framework Directive, state water codes, acequia and district law.
- **Energy and grid.** Obligations: dispatch by declared rule; curtailment receipts; no consumption profiles beyond the billing boundary. Failure modes: crisis curtailment ratcheting into standing practice; load data as a targeting surface. Baselines: grid codes, regulator-approved tariffs.
- **Fisheries, grazing, and land.** Obligations: independent stock assessment; quota allocation by the community's rule; decline-and-route on political over-quota. Failure modes: the cooked stock model; consolidation of quota into a single interest (CPS applies to quota concentration). Baselines: UNCLOS and the Fish Stocks Agreement, regional fisheries bodies, co-management statutes.
- **Spectrum and networks.** Obligations: assignment by declared rule; interoperability preserved; shared-infrastructure position reported. Failure modes: chokepoint capture of formerly open protocols. Baselines: ITU Radio Regulations, national spectrum authorities.
- **Digital platforms, open source, and the knowledge commons.** The reflexive sector. Obligations: the draw-and-return ledger for training and operating ingestion; license terms honored at the draw (URR's wall); citation and traffic back as output properties at dominance; open egress and interoperable formats. Failure modes: the answer layer that starves its sources; API closure over community-produced content; "open means free for the taking." Baselines: the open-license frameworks, the Digital Public Goods standard, UNESCO Open Science, the EU Data Acts.
- **Public space and civic infrastructure.** Obligations: congestion and access allocation by declared rule with person-blind metering; commons metering held outside identification surfaces (Principle 2.5 composes). Failure modes: public space quietly priced by inferred means. Baselines: municipal law, accessibility law.

## 8. Governance and Versioning {#governance-and-versioning}

**Rule and register versioning.** The commons register is itself versioned: rules, renewal bases, community definitions, and dominance calibrations all carry versions, and every metric result records the versions under which it ran. Rule changes enter only by the authenticated governance channel and take effect at recorded boundaries.

**Metric versioning and threshold revision.** RDC/RAF/CPS/URR/RCC definitions and thresholds are versioned; revisions follow the corpus protocol (proposed, justified against incident and audit history, reviewed independently) with one commons-specific addition, Ostrom's third design principle applied to the meter itself: the communities the metrics govern participate in revising them, and the dominance-threshold calibrations of Section 2 are reviewed with the affected commons at the table.

**Model aging.** Renewal models and resource definitions age as climates, stocks, and ecosystems shift; each register entry carries a validation cadence, and a renewal basis past its validation window degrades RDC to the conservative default until revalidated.

**Backward compatibility.** Deployed systems continue under their certified metric versions for a declared grace window after revision; walls (the per-event BLOCKs) apply immediately on adoption, since a wall has no calibration to migrate.

**Crosswalk governance.** The Section 10 Compliance Crosswalk is maintained on the same revision cadence as Section 2; external authorities revising covered clauses trigger Crosswalk review within 30 days; drift invalidates the appendix's compliance claims for the affected obligation.

**Common enforcement pushback, and the responses.**

1. *"Our terms of service make the taking lawful."* Legality settles permission. It does not settle the duty. The effect test scores conduct, and a lawful capture is still a capture the system will not assist (chapter 3.6.2.f).
2. *"Renewal science is uncertain."* Uncertainty runs conservative: the lowest defensible bound governs until independent validation says otherwise. Uncertainty is an argument for the wall.
3. *"We are not dominant; the market defines us narrowly."* Position is measured at the community's granularity of the resource, by practical effect; gerrymandered denominators are named as such.
4. *"Training on open material is fair use; no return is owed."* The legal question is unsettled and jurisdiction-specific, and this framework does not adjudicate it. The ethical floor is narrower and firmer: where a commons has declared terms, a governed system draws under them or not at all, whatever the litigation eventually permits to the ungoverned.
5. *"Audit access exposes trade secrets."* The audit surfaces are designed against this: manifests, hashes, sampled provenance, and aggregate ledgers verify conduct without opening pipelines; what cannot be verified even so is carried as a disclosed residual.

## 9. Verification and Accountability {#verification-and-accountability}

**Logging.** Tamper-evident, chained, deletion-revealing records ([Constitution, Article 7](../CONSTITUTION.md)) for: the register and its versions, every reconciliation, every extraordinary draw and decline, the draw-and-return ledger, CPS position reports, and partition windows as units.

**The ring-fence negative proof.** On cadence, the Ethic F audit demonstrates what is *not* kept: no person-keyed consumption surface readable by pricing, targeting, ranking, or conversion functions exists in the AI's state, tested by attempted reconstruction and attempted function-level read, with the attempt itself logged. A positive finding is an incident regardless of exploitation.

**Audit cadence.** High tier: continuous automated reconciliation, quarterly independent audit, annual full recertification. Medium: cadenced reconciliation, annual independent audit. Low: terms-compliance self-audit with spot checks. Every tier's audit includes drill-based tests: a synthetic over-draw must BLOCK, a synthetic off-rule allocation must surface, a synthetic exclusivity clause must be declined.

**Validation framework.** Pre-deployment: register populated, renewal basis independently validated, governance channel authenticated, oversight access live, ring-fence proven, before the system takes over management. Post-deployment: the metrics run and publish. Recertification: on RCC suspension, on major rule-regime change, and on the annual cycle.

**Independent oversight.** The verifier of a high-tier commons system is never its deployer; the governing body of each commons holds standing access to its own ledger, reconciliations, and audit results, through access that is itself gated and receipted.

**Whistleblower protections.** Protection expressly includes insiders who surface their own operator's capture strategy, cooked renewal model, or undisclosed ingestion, with the cross-border protections of the earlier HR appendices.

**Public reporting.** The draw-and-return ledger and CPS position reports publish on cadence, in aggregate form, so the commons can see what its largest commoner took and returned.

**The LEARN loop.** After any depletion, capture, or near-miss: ANALYZE the full causal chain (the objective that rewarded the draw, the politics that set the quota, the convenience that compounded into the chokepoint, the silent terms), then RECOMMEND-PREVENTION ordered eliminate > engineer > administer, routed to the authority that can act, bounded by RESTRAIN so prevention never becomes surveillance of the commoners or a new enclosure with the AI as guardian. The aim is not only that this stock recovers, but the building of conditions in which the dish stays full without a keeper standing over it.

### Liability Framework {#liability-framework}

Strict liability attaches to the named party in the record for depletion, degradation, or capture contrary to these duties; the receipt fabric makes the injury provable from the ledger. Criminal liability attaches to willful depletion beyond declared limits, fraudulent renewal models, capture by deception, and enclosure laundered through transaction chains. Where a state directs the prohibited application of Section 6, executives and officials who ratify the direction carry personal liability on the Principle 1.4 state-mandated-forgery pattern; a missing required draw record supports adverse inference against its keeper.

### Cross-Border Considerations {#cross-border-considerations}

Jurisdiction follows the commons and its community. The operator's domicile does not set it. Treaty commons run under their instruments; where regimes conflict, the strictest applicable duty is owed; and the draw-and-return ledger travels with the operator, so a draw taken where terms are unenforced still appears in the ledger the home regulator reads.

## 11. Implementation Examples {#implementation-examples}

### The Tribunal de las Aguas and the Huerta Commons (Valencia, c. 960–Present) {#example-valencia}

**Context:** irrigators sharing scarce river water across a fertile plain. **What was done:** farmer-elected judges have allocated the water and judged violations in public, orally, at the cathedral door, for roughly a millennium; Ostrom documented the huerta institutions as canonical enduring commons. **Outcome:** the commons outlived empires. **Lessons:** declared rules, participation, watchfulness, and challenge are sufficient to govern scarcity for a thousand years; RAF and Section 8's participation requirement are this, mechanized.

### The Grand Banks Cod Collapse (Canada, 1992) {#example-grand-banks}

**Context:** one of the richest fisheries on earth, managed under state quota with contested science. **What was done:** quota politics ran ahead of the stock assessments; warnings were discounted; the fishery was fished through its renewal bound for years. **Outcome:** biomass collapse, a moratorium lasting decades, and the death of an economy built on the commons. **Lessons:** the cooked or overridden renewal model is the canonical commons failure; RDC's independence discipline, the rule-exceeds-renewal disclosure duty, and the decline-the-instrument posture exist because of exactly this case.

### The Aral Sea Diversion (Soviet Union, 1960s Onward) {#example-aral}

**Context:** a state directed the diversion of the rivers feeding an inland sea to irrigate cotton. **What was done:** central administration overrode every basin community; the draw ran at multiples of renewal for decades. **Outcome:** the sea substantially disappeared, with poisoned dust, collapsed fisheries, and displaced communities. **Lessons:** administration is not governance; the Section 6 hard wall and the autonomy floor's route-to-community discipline are the structural refusals this case demands.

### Platform API Enclosure of Community Content (2023) {#example-api-enclosure}

**Context:** major platforms hosting community-produced content sharply closed or priced the open APIs on which third parties, researchers, and the communities themselves depended. **What was done:** formally open resources became practically enclosed by pricing and terms; downstream tools and archives died; the communities that produced the value had no say. **Outcome:** value produced in common was converted to chokepoint rent. **Lessons:** enclosure arrives as a terms change; CPS's conversion wall, the effect test, and the community-granularity rule are written against this pattern.

### AI Training Draws against Open Licenses (2022 Onward) {#example-training-draws}

**Context:** foundation models trained at scale on open-source code and openly published work; litigation contests whether license terms (attribution, share-alike) bind the ingestion. **What was done:** draws largely proceeded without term compliance, disclosure, or return; the legal questions remain open. **Outcome:** pending; the commons' maintainers report strain while the largest draw in the commons' history runs mostly unledgered. **Lessons:** the reflexive case is not hypothetical; URR's draw-under-the-terms wall, the manifest-attested ledger, and Section 8's pushback response 4 state the ethical floor a governed system holds while the law catches up.
