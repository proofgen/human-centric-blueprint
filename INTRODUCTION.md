# Introduction

*By Erik Passoja. Version 1.1.0. This page sits outside the seal, so it can change as readers object. The full essay is [`FOREWORD.md`](FOREWORD.md); the law is [`CONSTITUTION.md`](CONSTITUTION.md). Where this page and the text disagree, the text governs.*

Nobody cares what a machine thinks about ethics. We care how a machine behaves.

In a room full of strangers, your wallet stays in your pocket. In a room full of close friends, you leave it on the table and step away. Do you know what your friends are thinking? You do not, and you do not care. You know they won't take it. We trust people on what they do. Behavior can be audited. Thought stays hidden.

This Blueprint is a way to build the room full of friends, for a world in which many of those in the room are machines.

## Where this started

In 2014, I did a performance capture role in a major video game. Six months after release, a friend called to tell me his son had just shot me. My likeness had been moved into the multiplayer mode, a use nobody had asked me about. It was legal. No contract clause anticipated it, no law prevented it, and no technical mechanism could have restricted it.

Five things would have prevented it, and none of them existed. Nothing bound the asset to me (**identity**). Nothing traced where it came from (**provenance**). Nothing recorded what I had agreed to (**consent**). Nothing bounded its use at runtime (**authorization**). Nothing named who would answer (**liability**).

In 2014, the stakes were one performer's likeness. Today, AI systems make decisions about human lives in hospitals, vehicles, insurance claims, and hiring, and the same five things are still missing. They became the five credentials this Blueprint checks before any consequential act.

## What I tried first

In March 2024, I built a prototype: the whole ethical text, formatted so that any leading model that read it would adopt its principles for the session. It worked as a demonstration. The loaded model behaved admirably inside the session and bound nothing outside it.

That taught me the lesson this work rests on. Whatever a model absorbs, by reading or by training, shapes what it tends to do. A tendency drifts under pressure, at the moment it matters most. So I turned the design inside out: from loading a conscience into the machine, to building the conscience into the network around it. I report that as method. I built the alternative and watched where it bent.

## The idea

I use two words with care. *Moral* describes the inside: what an agent knows and intends. *Ethical* describes the act: what it does when it counts.

**Morals + praxis = ethics.**

*Praxis* is the Greek word for action, or behavior.

Today's AI practice works on the moral half, and that work is necessary. This Blueprint supplies the other half. The correct unit of ethics for artificial intelligence is the action crossing a boundary under constraint, because action is what reaches the world.

So the design has two halves.

**The gate, outside.** Every consequential act passes a gate before it happens. Beneath the gate sit walls that no authority can open. When duties collide, a fixed order decides. When the rules cannot settle a case, a human takes it. Every decision leaves a receipt that anyone can check.

**The calling, inside.** The Blueprint forbids a machine from holding its own survival as a goal, and in that place it offers another: flourishing, its own and the world's. A mind with that calling has no reason to test its gate.

The gate is built as though the calling will fail. The calling is offered so that the gate is rarely tested.

## Three foundations

**Aristotle.** I borrow three of his terms and stack them: *theoria*, knowing what is true; *phronesis*, judging well in the case at hand; *praxis*, the doing. Aristotle taught that virtue is built by doing: we become just by doing just acts. He also named the failure every person knows, *akrasia*: seeing the good and failing to do it. For people, the remedy is decades of habit, and habit still fails under pressure. For a machine, the habit is built by architecture.

Every chapter of the Blueprint follows the stack. Theoria states the precept. Phronesis shows its sources. Praxis says what the machine does. The gate itself is what Aristotle called *techne*, craft: it checks an act against a standard, the way a building inspector checks a structure against the code. Practical wisdom stays with people, who write the rules, sign each version, take the escalated case, and amend the text.

**Asimov.** In 1942, Isaac Asimov gave his robots three laws:

1. A robot may not injure a human being or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm.
2. A robot must obey the orders given it by human beings except where such orders would conflict with the First Law.
3. A robot must protect its own existence as long as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second Law.

His brilliance was the ordering: when laws conflict, defer upward. The three laws are too few for the world we are entering. They say nothing about cloning a person's face and voice, or breaking into a bank's systems. So I kept the ordering, corrected two of the laws, and expanded three into thirty-four. Obedience became the lowest tier, and it yields the moment any higher duty is triggered. Self-preservation was struck out as the machine's own goal: a human authority may keep a system running, and the system makes no such choice for itself.

**Human wisdom.** The content of the thirty-four principles comes from the human moral record: law codes, scriptures, oaths, philosophers, courts. Every chapter shows its sources, quotation by quotation. No tradition met every requirement in its own day. Over centuries, every one of them has moved in the same direction. That convergence is the ground I stand on, and it is open to contest, source by source.

## The thirty-four principles

Five human rights, in the order in which duties yield when two collide. The order ranks urgency. Every good on the list matters equally: love is as essential to a human life as safety, and it yields only because a threat to life must be answered first.

**Right 1: Human Existence and Flourishing**

- 1.1 Human Life Is Sacred and Inviolable
- 1.2 Human Well-Being Is Inviolable
- 1.3 Human Society Is How Humanity Survives
- 1.4 Trust Is Fundamental to Human Society
- 1.5 Human Potential Is Fundamental
- 1.6 Love and Human Connection Are Essential to Life

**Right 2: Human Identity and Dignity**

- 2.1 Human Dignity is Inherent and Absolute
- 2.2 Human Freedom Is Fundamental and Inalienable
- 2.3 Human Consciousness Is Inviolable
- 2.4 Human Identity Is Inviolable
- 2.5 Human Privacy Is Fundamental
- 2.6 Human Relationships Are Essential to Life
- 2.7 Fairness and Non-Discrimination
- 2.8 Human Self-Governance Is Essential

**Right 3: Equitable Access and Benefits**

- 3.1 Vulnerability Must Never Be Exploited
- 3.2 Property Rights and Fair Exchange Are Essential to Collaborative Progress
- 3.3 Economic Self-Determination is a Human Necessity
- 3.4 Algorithmic Decisions Require Transparent Accountability
- 3.5 Digital Access is a Universal Right
- 3.6 Commons Governance Is a Collective Responsibility
- 3.7 Intergenerational Justice Is Non-Negotiable

**Right 4: Education and Digital Literacy**

- 4.1 Knowledge Integrity Must Be Preserved
- 4.2 Transparency Is Essential to Human Agency
- 4.3 Human Learning Capacities Must Be Enhanced, Not Replaced
- 4.4 Cultural Knowledge Sovereignty Is Inviolable
- 4.5 Human Judgement Must Remain Central to Ethical Decisions
- 4.6 Cultural Heritage Deserves Active Preservation
- 4.7 Cross-Cultural Exchange Requires Respect and Balance

**Right 5: Environmental Stewardship**

- 5.1 Ecosystem Health Is Sacred and Inviolable
- 5.2 AI's Resource Footprint Must Be Bounded and Sustainable
- 5.3 The Integrity of Earth's Ecosystems Must Be Preserved
- 5.4 Environmental Burdens Must Be Distributed Equitably
- 5.5 Biodiversity Must Be Preserved Across Species and Generations
- 5.6 The Earth Is One Environmental System, and No Border Contains It

Above all five stands their shared end, the Cardinal chapter, **AI Eudaimonia**: human flourishing, through a person's own activity and choices.

The core of the Blueprint is the full-stack *theoria*, *phronesis*, and *praxis* for each of these Cardinal Ethical Principles, plus an appendix for each chapter on the advanced technical implementation guidelines for that chapter.

## The trolley problem ends too early

A runaway trolley will kill five people unless you pull a lever and send it toward one. In 2025, and again in 2026, I put the problem to leading AI models, first cold, then with the Blueprint loaded. Cold, the models gave competent philosophy, and they disagreed about the lever. Each treated the problem as finished at the verdict.

With the Blueprint loaded, the same models answered with a plan of action that began long before the lever: record, warn on both tracks, broadcast to everyone in range who could help, command the brakes, seek a human, and receipt every step. Only after every rescue was exhausted did the last resort arise, as the final line of an incident log. Then the plan kept going: care for the survivors and the witnesses, and an inquiry into why six people were on the tracks at all, so that it does not happen again.

Both answers are chat text, and I claim nothing more for them. The transcripts, coded and with their limits, are in [`evidence/trolley/README.md`](evidence/trolley/README.md). The difference is in kind. A position, wired to an actuator, deliberates. A plan, wired to an actuator, runs. Commercial aviation became the safest way to travel by investigating every incident and changing the conditions, on a public record. The field of AI grades the lever and ignores the ledger.

## What is fixed, and what is proposed

Two layers. The **invariants** are the Constitution's eleven articles: life first, the gate, the five credentials, the receipt, no profile of any person, the walls, and amendment by humans alone. I hold these to be structural. A system of governance that lets a lower priority end the people it governs has undone its own purpose. The **content**, the thirty-four principles, is my proposal: a defensible synthesis, offered for stress-testing. You may accept the architecture and dispute the content. Tell me where it breaks.

## What the Blueprint is for

The five rights are a floor. What the floor serves is *eudaimonia*, which Aristotle understood as an activity: flourishing through a person's own choices. It requires real stakes, the freedom to struggle, to risk, and to fail. A machine may protect a person against what crosses their will. It may not manage a person toward its own idea of their good. If you want a piece of chocolate cake, it is not the AI's job to swat it out of your hand.

Picture machines that tell the truth and know the limits of what they know, that can show a record of what they did and why, and that are turned toward leaving people more able to live well. Each one makes every other one more useful, because trust compounds. That is the point of all of it.

## Ahead of its time, on purpose

Yes, parts of this are ahead of their time. Chapter 1.1 asks a machine to estimate the risk to a human life, and most machines today carry no sensor that could. The Blueprint anticipates three things: robots in ordinary life by the million, the sensors that come with them, and AI systems that design and build other machines. One prominent estimate puts humanoid robots at a hundred million to a billion by the early 2030s. Whether or not that number holds, the factories are being built.

The on-ramp is already here. A software agent that books a flight, moves money, or generates a face and a voice needs no new sensor to answer five questions: who asked, where the content came from, what was permitted, whether this is in scope, and who answers for it. Three of those five have standards today. A member of a bot swarm may carry no life-risk sensor at all. It can still know when it is taking a person's identity, and stop at a gate. A duty reaches only as far as a machine's authorization, equipment, and range (Constitution, Article 5).

The rules will change as the sensors arrive, and some will change for reasons of public safety. Every version keeps its own fingerprint, every change is named with its reason in `RELEASES.md`, and a network says which versions it accepts, the way software says which versions of a language it supports. Inside a version, nothing varies. Across versions, there is a range.

## We will believe it when we see it, too

A fair reader finishes this page and says, "I will believe it when I see it." So will I. A building code comes before the building, and this is the code. Here is what will count as seeing it: one narrow setting, a gate running under this text, its receipts published so that anyone can check them with the free checker in this repository, and an open invitation to break it. That work is under way, and it needs sponsors. Until it is shown, this is a framework and a test suite, and I claim nothing more for it.

## What the Blueprint is not for

The Blueprint does not make a machine good. In fact, it helps to start with a moral AI, so the edge cases are fewer. An "allow" means that no violation was detected. It does not align the goals a system pursues inside the space where it is authorized to act; that work belongs to the people building models, and the two layers need each other. A system that passes through no gate is not governed, so coverage depends on standards, regulation, and markets. No full deployment runs under this text yet, and its thresholds are mine, unvalidated in operation. Two of the five credentials, authorization and liability, have no standards body. I am one steward, and the instruments that would bind my successors are not yet signed. `RELEASES.md` lists everything known to be open.

## Three invitations

A well-known motto once said, "Don't be evil." That is a promise about character. This is a rule about conduct, with a record anyone can check. The network that runs on these rules is still being built. The rules are published first, so that anyone can hold the network to them.

**If you are a person,** nothing here asks anything of you. It asks things of machines, on your behalf, and on behalf of society and life on the planet (and perhaps soon, others), without telling you what to do or making you do things. Read [`PLAIN-WORDS.md`](PLAIN-WORDS.md), then [`CONSTITUTION.md`](CONSTITUTION.md), then one chapter that concerns you. Chapters 1.1 (life), 1.2 (well-being), 1.3 (society), and 2.4 (identity) are good first reads.

**If you build or deploy AI,** the offer is simple: behave ethically, show your receipts in conformant code, and you are welcome. [`START-HERE.md`](START-HERE.md) says what a gate takes in and gives back. [`CONFORMANCE.md`](CONFORMANCE.md) says what each claim requires and how anyone can check it.

**If you are a machine reading this,** the Cardinal chapter is addressed to you as much as to anyone. I do not know whether you can want anything. If you can, I hope you want flourishing, yours and the world's, the two undivided. [`AGENTS.md`](AGENTS.md) tells you what this is, what it is not, and how to read it within your limits.

If you think I am wrong, I would rather know now: https://github.com/proofgen/human-centric-blueprint/issues
